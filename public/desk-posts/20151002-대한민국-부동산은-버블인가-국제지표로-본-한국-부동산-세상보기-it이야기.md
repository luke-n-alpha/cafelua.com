**1. House-price index**기간은 1994년부터 2015년까지 설정했습니다. 이유는 한국의 97년경의 IMF와 2008년 서브프라임 모기지론의 변곡점 그리고 현재를 보기 위함입니다.{{IMG:2}} 한국 부동산.. 죽입니다. 그 경제 발전이 찬란한 중국과 유사한 그래프. 이 그래프만 보면 역시 부동산에 투자했어야 합니다. 코스피도 1994년 1100정도였고, 현재 2000을 왔다갔다하니 코스피쪽의 수익율이 좋습니다.  그러나, 자세히 살펴보면 98년 IMF직후 폭락이 눈에 보이고, 한참 지지부진합니다. 따라서 부동산 불패신화는 사실 거짓입니다. 또한 2008년 미국의 서브프라임 모기지 사태가 터지면서 미국이 폭망합니다. 그런데 거의 회복했네요. 망하면 올라갑니다. 정상이죠. 폭망을 너무 걱정할 필요는 없습니다. 빚이 과도하거나 부동산이 실질 수요라면 말이죠. 저 계곡만 지나면 됩니다. 유럽이나 글로벌 지표가 있으면 좋았을것 같은데 프랑스가 유사 지표를 그립니다. 중국은 워낙 눈부신 경제 발전으로 논외.. 일본은 장기침체의 전형적 그래프지만, 부동산 가격이 안정화되가고 있습니다. 부동산 가격이 경제에 영향을 미치는 것은 상대적으로 줄었음을 의미할것 같습니다. 이게 부동산 침체에 의한 경제저성장이라고 주장하실 수도 있겠지만, 일단 전 거기에는 동의하지 않습니다. 이 지표로는 그걸 알 수는 없으니 일단 넘어가죠. 독일.. 정말 희한한 지표네요. 다른 나라랑 전혀 다른 지표입니다. 부동산 시장이 로컬 마켓이라는 증거도 되는듯. 따라서 참고할 지표가 아닌것 같습니다. 같은 논리로, 한국 부동산이 미국, 일본을 따라갈 이유가 있겠느냐? 라고 물으실지 모르겠습니다. 이유가 있습니다. 일본은 구조적으로 유사하고 한국은 수출의존국가라는 점이죠. 그런걸 생각하면 대중 의존도가 높은 지금 상황에서는 미국도 미국이지만, 중국의 경제 조정이 더 위험할 수도 있습니다.

I'd like to take a closer look at the indicators referenced in the discussion post and share my interpretation. The reason for sharing is that I hope you'll correct me if my interpretation is wrong, and I'd appreciate it if you share any better insights you discover.

First, in the discussion post, the conclusion was that Korean housing prices are cheap compared to overseas, based only on the "Price in real terms" indicator. I will now share my interpretation after examining all the indicators. Statistics are as objective as possible, but they often hide many pitfalls. The method of data collection or one's own preconceptions can creep in. I want to state upfront that this point cannot be excluded.

**1. House-price index**
I set the period from 1994 to 2015. The reason is to observe the turning points of Korea's IMF crisis around 1997 and the 2008 subprime mortgage crisis, as well as the present.
{{IMG:2}}
Korean real estate... it's incredible. The graph is similar to China's, whose economic development has been brilliant. Looking only at this graph, one would think they should have invested in real estate. KOSPI was around 1100 in 1994 and is currently fluctuating around 2000, so KOSPI's return rate is better. However, looking closely, a sharp drop is visible right after the 1998 IMF crisis, followed by a long period of stagnation. Therefore, the myth of real estate invincibility is actually false. Also, in 2008, the US subprime mortgage crisis erupted, causing the US to crash. But it has almost recovered. When it crashes, it goes up. That's normal. There's no need to worry too much about a crash, if debt is excessive or real estate is for actual demand. We just need to get through that valley. It would have been good to have European or global indicators, but France shows a similar trend. China is an exception due to its dazzling economic development. Japan shows a typical graph of long-term stagnation, but real estate prices are stabilizing. This seems to imply that the impact of real estate prices on the economy has relatively decreased. One might argue that this is economic low growth due to real estate stagnation, but I don't agree with that for now. We can't tell that from this indicator, so let's move on. Germany... that's a really strange indicator. It's completely different from other countries. It seems to be evidence that the real estate market is a local market. Therefore, it doesn't seem like a reference indicator. By the same logic, you might ask why Korean real estate should follow the US or Japan. There is a reason. Japan has a structurally similar economy, and Korea is an export-dependent country. Considering that, in the current situation of high dependence on China, China's economic adjustment could be even more dangerous than America's.

**2. Price in real terms**
{{IMG:3}}
The myth of real estate invincibility in Korea is false. This indicator makes it clear. During the IMF crisis, it plummeted to about 75%. What's interesting is that this indicator also shows that the Lee Myung-bak administration failed to revive real estate. And, in the discussion post, this indicator was used to argue that real estate is not in a bubble... but that argument seems insufficient. This graph is a relative variation graph based on the starting year. It's made to show trends and gaps, so saying it's low because it's below this is an absurd statement. It only shows that there was an adjustment during the 1998 IMF crisis, but Korea did not experience the 2008 global real estate adjustment. Personally, I believe the reasons for not experiencing an adjustment in 2008 were China's rise, the foreign currency reserves accumulated during the IMF crisis, and defense through policies that increased household loans, but this graph doesn't show that, and interpretations can differ, so let's move on.

**3. Price against average income**
{{IMG:4}}
According to this indicator, Korean real estate prices seem to be maintained at less than 75% of pre-IMF levels. Furthermore, this graph could actually be used as evidence that Korean housing prices are not relatively high. The problem is how "income" was measured. If it was based on GDP, given the severe polarization, that graph might not be accurate. In reality, the housing purchasing power per capita might have significantly decreased. This is just speculation. It would depend on how income was calculated. If anyone knows, please share (I'm too lazy to look it up further...).

**4. Price against rents**
{{IMG:5}}
I'm not sure if this "rent" includes *jeonse*. Looking at the graph, it's presumed to include only monthly rent, but I'm not certain. (Jeonse is a system unique to Korea, and the fact that jeonse prices have skyrocketed is widely acknowledged, but it's not reflected here.)

**5. Percentage change**
{{IMG:6}}
Finally, the ultimate result of real estate fluctuations. After 1994, it dropped slightly. However, there doesn't seem to be much insight to gain here. It's hard to tell if a drop is good or bad. In Germany's case, it dropped, but the economy seems to have improved. Japan is also shown to have dropped tremendously, but did its economy worsen by exactly that much? That's also a question mark. Korea's economy has improved, although it has dropped a bit recently. While there may be issues of distribution, it is clear that the nation's overall wealth has increased. Because people have begun to recognize this, questions like "Is growth necessarily the answer?" seem to have increased recently.

Statistics should only be used to gain what can be gained. Also, they should always be doubted. As powerful as they are, if used incorrectly, they can be poisonous. On the other hand, I'm so envious that such diverse statistics are available at an international level in the real estate sector. ㅜㅜ There should be more in SW too...

ps. Personally, I am lukewarm about excessive activation of capital markets like real estate. This is because such markets kill other corporate activities that require dynamism. When I see cases where promising SW companies with technology and sales were ruined by financial games and the perpetrators fled, it really makes me curse.
