When the iPad was released, there was a lot of fuss as if the tablet era would immediately begin, but it seems the tablet era hasn't fully opened yet. What's noteworthy recently is rather the tabletization of PCs, specifically Windows tablet PCs. However, looking at the broader trend of tablets, it seems difficult for tablets to truly feel like an 'innovation' to users yet. Regarding this, I'd like to compare tablets with smartphones first, and then discuss the current direction of tablets to explore when tablets will become complete.

**1. The End of Smartphone Evolution is the iPhone**

The evolution of smartphones, in fact, ended with the iPhone. It's said that humans didn't evolve from chimpanzees, but rather chimpanzees also evolved in a different direction from humans to become their complete form. From that perspective, all phones we call smartphones today are, in fact, successors to the iPhone. If we compare it to humans, the iPhone can be considered the beginning of modern humanity. However, Windows CE or Symbian, which existed before it, showed direction but were not perfect devices themselves. And that direction was continuously refined, leading to an explosive increase when the iPhone emerged at a certain technological threshold.

And that direction was precisely in network speed and OS versatility. Network speeds only became usable after going through IMT2000's 2G and reaching 3G. This technological advancement led to a decrease in technology costs, giving birth to affordable data plans. The versatility of the OS, which allowed application installation, led from the emergence of WIPI and Brew to Windows CE and Symbian, ultimately resulting in the appearance of iOS. The concept of a smartphone began very early, but it was the iPhone that sparked an explosive reaction from the public, and the iPhone can be called the first ancestor of modern smartphones.

**2. So, what about the tablet's ancestors and direction?**

According to the above (my self-made) assumption, tablets can also be said to be in an evolutionary stage. From MS's Origami Project to the current iPad, Android tablets, and Windows tablet PCs, various products are appearing, and their continuous development shows several patterns.

The obvious ones are network speed, performance, light weight, and battery life. The next is the combination of high resolution and pen input. So, when will tablets reach a threshold like 3G and iOS for smartphones, and what are they heading towards?

It is precisely the tablet's initial fundamental attributes: books and paper. No one would think that smartphones were created to replace books and paper. But what about tablets? I can clearly state that the ultimate replacement for advanced tablets is paper and pen. Therefore, by considering the attributes of paper and pen, we can envision the form of the 'modern human' tablet.

The attributes of paper are outdoor visibility, excellent resolution, lightness, and affordability. These were the attributes that revolutionized printed materials. Therefore, tablets are gradually evolving towards higher resolutions. It's obvious, but while the iPhone 4 has a pixel density of 326 ppi, the 10.1-inch new iPad has 264 ppi. If it were to be raised to the iPhone 4's level, it means the new iPad's resolution still needs to increase further. This implies that even Full HD (1920*1080) resolution is still insufficient for tablet displays, and ultimately, UHD-class resolution of around 4000 pixels horizontally and 2000 pixels vertically (over 500 ppi) for a 10.1-inch class device can be considered the final goal. Smartphones are already being sold with products in the 400 ppi range.

And this phenomenon means that existing LCD monitors used in work environments will have resolutions far exceeding UHD class.

In the case of pens, Wacom pens can be said to be at the forefront of their evolutionary stage. Actual Wacom drawing pads adopt a crisp, paper-like texture rather than the smooth feel of an LCD screen. Ultimately, if the goal is to resemble the properties of pen and paper, then Wacom pen attributes will be adopted. A surface with a texture similar to paper, and angle detection (found in Intuos-class devices) beyond just pressure sensitivity... Perhaps at that point, pen nibs might even be interchangeable for various textures. Reflecting this, Wacom's Cintiq has already started to appear in 13-inch class models.

**3. Why aren't tablets useful yet, you ask?**

If you ask why tablets aren't useful yet, I can answer simply: The iPhone of the tablet world hasn't appeared yet. Tablets are still evolving and expanding their reach. And someday, just as feature phones were relegated to drawers, a day will come when PCs disappear, and tablets will largely replace the roles of paper and books.
