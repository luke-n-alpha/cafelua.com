I wrote the title grandly, but this post is simply a summary of the PAG meeting I attended yesterday. I'd appreciate it if you could refer to it. Of course, there was a valuable second gathering after the main meeting. I didn't bring any business cards, so I couldn't exchange them, and I wish I had at least taken a photo.

**1. Event Title: Google Glass and AR Platform**
2. Date: May 15, 2013 (Wed) 7:00 PM - 9:30 PM
3. Venue: ROA Consulting Next Box, Yangjae Station

4. Related URL: [http://platformadvisory.kr/archives/2709](http://platformadvisory.kr/archives/2709)
**--- Content ---------------------------------**
**1. Introduction to Augmented Reality (AR) and the Potential of Google Glass**
- Presenter: Yongsoo Kim, Senior Managing Director, Intel Korea (formerly of Olaworks, acquired by Intel)
**1) Definition of Augmented Reality: Displaying meaningful related information from the virtual world added to the real world.**
**2) Technology Classification**
- Location-based AR: AR using GPS and compass (e.g., ScanSearch)
- Image recognition-based AR: AR using image recognition of markers, etc.
**3) Application Areas**
- Marketing: e.g., Marketing that obtains additional information or coupons by scanning magazines or signboards
- Games: e.g., Games that track and match the user's face
- Education: e.g., Books that show additional virtual information by scanning offline books
- Sports (Broadcasting): e.g., Broadcasting that overlays player information or draws the trajectory of the ball during a sports game broadcast
- Industry: e.g., Displaying additional work information or guiding where to insert parts
- Military: e.g., HUD that displays additional information about enemies on screen
**4) Use Cases**
- Scenario showing advice for chess or billiards
- Scenario showing information about a region or person
- Scenario where answers and explanations appear when scanning an exam paper
- Scenario where furniture from an online shopping mall can be virtually placed in one's home
**5) Problems of Existing AR**
- Difficult for users to perceive
- Cumbersome steps from perception to action.
- Revenue model, ROI not verified.
- Inaccurate sensors and frequent recognition errors.
- High battery consumption due to camera and computing.
- No standardized, universal devices.
- Always requires holding a smart device by hand.
- Lack of valuable content.

**6) WEARABLE COMPUTERS can overcome the problems of existing AR.**
**-> Google Glass could be the answer.**

**2. Discussion Content**
**Topic: Will Google Glass be commercially successful within 3 years?**
(Will it achieve a scale that can create an independent ecosystem by selling over 10 million devices?)
- Presenter: Professor Byung-sun Hwang

**1) Positive**
- Technology Aspect
: Mirror API will be a form factor that aims to connect everything based on web technology.
: As computing power increases, it will become a cloud edge.
- UX Aspect
: The mere fact that the display is in front of the eyes is very significant.
: It can serve as a second screen for TV, like a remote control.
- Service Aspect
: Replacing car navigation alone creates a significant market (it was a huge killer app in mobile in the past).
- Business Aspect
: If its purpose is limited and it's restricted to a showcase, it could spread.
: It could be successful enough if sold in specific categories (niche markets).
**2) Negative**
- Technology Aspect
: A battery that lasts only 2-3 hours is fatal.
: A seamless device is still difficult given the global network (infrastructure).
- Business Aspect
: Lack of killer apps.
: No partners like during the smartphone era, and it cannot control distribution like Apple. Looking at the Nexus case, Google lacks the ability to create and sell elegant devices.
: It's difficult for Google to establish SCM. Nexus was also outsourced.
: The device price of $1500 is too expensive to be a 'sweet price'.
: Difficult for telecommunication companies to sell with subsidies.
**3) Other Opinions & Insights**
- Watch vs Glass in Wearable Computing
: If you want to be in the newspaper, make glasses; if you want to make money, make watches.
: In the case of watches, active movements of participants are being observed.
: The manufacturing cost of watches is $100-200, and the consumer price of $300 is sufficient, so watches have an advantage in SCM.
- The purpose of Google Glass is a strategic move to collect user data for search.
: According to stories from a hackathon, it's a device for collecting user data for search, etc.
e.g.) Tracking user's gaze in a supermarket to extract products of interest to each user.
Inferring relationships between users who frequently meet offline, etc.
* Overall Opinion:
It is a very significant device for a paradigm shift, and success in a niche market's vertical ecosystem is certain, but selling 10 million units seems difficult. (The opinion is that a platform on the scale of smartphones might be difficult within 3 years.)
