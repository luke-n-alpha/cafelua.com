**1. 조기교육은 무조건 나쁘다? ****조기교육 아닙니다. 꼭 필요해요.**
네. 그동안 나빴습니다. 특히 국영수를 먼저 가르치기 위해서 내달리는 모습은 아주 꼴불견이었죠. 그런데 유치원 아이들에게 젓가락질을 가르치거나, 숟가락질을 가르치는건 괜찮은가요? 어쩌면 "소프트웨어 조기교육"이라는 용어자체가 처음부터 틀린지 모르겠습니다. 소프트웨어는 조기교육이 아니라, 젓가락질처럼 어린아이들에게 필요해서 가르치는 겁니다. 그동안 우리세대와 달랐던건 젓가락질이 어른들이나 하는 하이테크로 오해했던것 뿐이죠. 물론, 고급 소프트웨어 지식은 하이테크 맞습니다. 그러나, 아이들에게 가르치는건 그런 하이테크가 아니라, 레고를 조립하는 수준입니다. 한번도 소프트웨어교육이 무언지 본적 없는 이들이 C나 JAVA를 아이들에게 가르치는 줄 아는것 같습니다. 조기교육의 대표격인 수학을 들어봅시다. 미적분을 초등학생이나 중학생이 띄었다고 합시다. 뭐에 쓸까요? 시험잘보는데 쓰겠죠. 하지만, 초등학생이나 중학생이 C나 JAVA, Go나 Swift를 띄었다고 합시다. 뭐에 쓸까요?

미적을 배웠다고 34살 교수님과 논문을 쓰나요? 소프트웨어는 가능합니다. 어릴때 코딩했던 빌게이츠, 주커버그, 스티브잡스처럼 초 유명인 사례는 이제 귀에 딱지가 앉아서 따로 이야기하지 않겠습니다.
**2. 개발자 그거 하면 굶는다는데 ****안굶어요. 제 주변에 굶는 친구 못봤음,**
안 굶어요. 제가 아는 개발자 중 좀 한다하는 친구 중에는 굶는 친구 없습니다. 심지어는 한국 출신 구글 개발자 중에는 지방대 출신도 있습니다. 얼마전까지만해도 개발자가 의사만큼 받아야 된다면 펄쩍 뛰었죠. 그런데, 의사에 준하는 소프트웨어를 개발(IBM 왓슨, 딥마인드)하는 개발자들의 연봉이 그정도 안되면 이상한거 아닐까요? 영어 좀 되면 해외로 나갈 수도 있습니다. 가장 글로벌 장벽이 낮은 직종 중 하나입니다. 호주나 뉴질랜드로 가셔도 됩니다.
**3. 프로그래머도 없어질 직업이라 코딩 배울필요 없어?**** 전자계산기가 있으니 더하기 빼기를 배울필요 없다는 헛소리**
네 맞습니다. 틀린말은 아닙니다. 지금도 Getter, Setter정도는 IDE가 만들어주고, 요즘 코드는 극단적으로 짧아졌습니다. 단순히 요구사항 받아서 코딩하는 코더는 설자리가 점점 줄고 있어요. 그냥 마우스로 클릭, 클릭, 조기교육으로도 정말 가능합니다. 예전에는 전문 소프트웨어지식이 필요했던것이 단순히 클릭 몇번으로 끝나는 세상입니다. 하지만, 그 서비스를 운영하고, 기획, 설계하는데 소프트웨어 지식이 없다면 말이 안됩니다. 아키텍처로 올라가기 위한 가장 빠른길은 코딩을 배우고 해보는겁니다. 입만 터는 컨설턴트나 영업맨들도 존재하시는 것 같긴합니다만, 신뢰할 분들이 아닙니다. 대부분 그 분들도 한계를 느껴서 조금씩은 코딩이던 뭐던 해봅니다.  또한 거의 모든 영역에서 소프트웨어에 대한 이해가 필요합니다. 그 점이 가장 소프트웨어교육이 필요한 이유입니다. 지난 포스팅에서 말씀드렸지만, 알파고는 바둑을 연구하는데 최적의 수단입니다. 이세돌이 대국 몇번 두고 그정도 깨달음을 얻었다면, 알파고의 연산결과를 보고는 어떤 깨달음을 얻을 수 있을까요? 컴퓨터는 데이터 분석은 하지만, 데이터 인사이트를 만드는데는 미숙합니다. 하지만 데이터 인사이트를 만들려면 최소한 데이터 분석이 뭔줄은 알아야 합니다. 그 점이 코딩과 프로그래밍이 반드시 알아야 되는 이유입니다. 전자계산기가 범용화 되었지만, 여전히 덧셈뺄셈은 가르치잖아요?
**4. 그럼 우리아이에게 어떻게 가르칠까요?** C나 JAVA하지 말라고 말씀드렸습니다. 흥미를 떨어뜨리는 어려운거 시키지 마세요. 건축과 가겠다는 애들한테는 레고를 먼저 줘야지. 공학수학을 먼저 가르쳐서는 안됩니다.   우선 제 딸 아이에게 경험시킨 가장 좋은 도구는 Scratch Junior입니다. I'm writing this post because I was quite perplexed by a recent Chosun Ilbo article. It's an inconsistent article that talks about AlphaGo's destructive power and the importance of software, only to abruptly conclude with "However, early software education is bad." (Please understand that I'm writing this post feeling a bit agitated.)

First, let's talk again about the misunderstandings prevalent in the content of this article and its comments.
**1. Is early education always bad? It's not early education. It's essential.**
Yes. It has been bad so far. Especially the rush to teach Korean, English, and Math first was quite an eyesore. But is it okay to teach kindergarteners how to use chopsticks or spoons? Perhaps the term "early software education" itself is wrong from the start. Software isn't early education; it's something taught to young children because they need it, like using chopsticks. The only difference from our generation was that we misunderstood chopstick use as high-tech only for adults. Of course, advanced software knowledge is indeed high-tech. However, what we teach children is not that kind of high-tech, but rather at the level of assembling LEGOs. It seems that those who have never seen what software education is think we're teaching C or JAVA to children. Let's take math, a prime example of early education. Suppose an elementary or middle school student mastered calculus. What would they use it for? To do well on tests, presumably. But suppose an elementary or middle school student mastered C, JAVA, Go, or Swift. What would they use it for?

If you learned calculus, would you write a paper with a 34-year-old professor? With software, it's possible. I won't separately discuss super-famous examples like Bill Gates, Zuckerberg, and Steve Jobs, who coded when they were young, as those stories are now old news.
**2. They say if you become a developer, you'll starve. You won't starve. I haven't seen any friends starving around me.**
You won't starve. Among the developers I know who are somewhat capable, none are starving. There are even Google developers from Korea who graduated from regional universities. Not long ago, people would scoff at the idea of developers earning as much as doctors. But wouldn't it be strange if developers who create doctor-level software (IBM Watson, DeepMind) didn't earn that much? If you're decent at English, you can even go abroad. It's one of the professions with the lowest global barriers. You can go to Australia or New Zealand.
**3. Programmers are also a disappearing profession, so there's no need to learn coding? Nonsense, like saying there's no need to learn addition and subtraction because we have electronic calculators.**
Yes, that's correct. It's not entirely wrong. Even now, IDEs generate Getters and Setters, and modern code has become extremely concise. The role of a 'coder' who simply takes requirements and codes is diminishing. It's truly possible with just a few clicks, even with early education. What once required specialized software knowledge can now be done with a few clicks. However, it makes no sense to operate, plan, and design such services without software knowledge. The fastest way to move up to architecture is to learn and practice coding. While there seem to be consultants and salespeople who only talk, they are not to be trusted. Most of them also feel limitations and try their hand at coding or something similar. Furthermore, an understanding of software is needed in almost all areas. That is the most important reason why software education is necessary. As I mentioned in a previous post, AlphaGo is the optimal tool for studying Go. If Lee Sedol gained that much insight from playing a few games, what kind of insight could one gain from seeing AlphaGo's computational results? Computers can analyze data, but they are unskilled at creating data insights. However, to create data insights, one must at least know what data analysis is. That is why coding and programming are essential to learn. Even though electronic calculators are commonplace, we still teach addition and subtraction, don't we?
**4. So, how should we teach our children?**
I told you not to do C or JAVA. Don't make them do difficult things that kill their interest. For kids who want to go into architecture, you should give them LEGOs first, not teach them engineering mathematics. First, the best tool I had my daughter experience is Scratch Junior. [ScratchJr - Android Apps on Google Playplay.google.com](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.scratchjr.android) It's Android-based, and you don't need to know Korean or English. It's made up of simple icons and has an intuitive interface. My daughter's favorite thing to do is record the two words 'Daddy' and 'Fool' and loop them endlessly ㅜㅜ Or she just edits image resources. It's okay not to know algorithms. When I was young, I also spent all day playing with my MSX (SPC 1000-8bit), using the Screen9 command to draw dots on the screen with a cursor. That's enough.
If that's still not interesting enough, Lightbot is also good. It's just a game. [Lightbot : Code Hour - Android Apps on Google Playplay.google.com](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lightbot.lightbothoc) If they're elementary school students and want to try something more serious, Naver's Play Entry is also good. [Entry Education Research Institute Official WebsiteEntry is a non-profit software learning platform where anyone can easily and enjoyably learn and teach software.play-entry.org](http://play-entry.org/#!/) And if you want to increase the difficulty, you can try Arduino, App Inventor, etc. Since it's fun, fathers and mothers should try it together. Unlike other studies, software creates greater synergy when done together.
It's quite different from traditional education. That might be why those accustomed to traditional education seem to have an aversion to it. It's very bothersome. In the past, you just gave them books and answer sheets, and hit them according to the number of mistakes. But software education involves learning through mistakes and creating together. The paradigm itself is different from the start. Do our children only need early education in Korean, English, and Math? Don't joke. Children are already more familiar with digital technology than adults. Think carefully about whether you want to keep them as mere consumers or turn them into producers.
