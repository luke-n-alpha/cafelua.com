I picked up my junior at Seoksu Station, my cousin in Gunpo, and then we headed to Hoengseong via the Yeongdong Expressway.

On the way, it took some time due to a car accident. It seemed like a pretty major accident, as several ambulances rushed forward, and when we passed the scene, there was a long stretch of debris, indicating its scale. I didn't see it, but my junior said there was a considerable amount of bloodstains.

Although I felt like I had seen a rather unsettling scene, once we exited the highway and entered a quiet national road in Gangwon-do, starlight began to pour down, so much so that it felt like it would spill into the car.

Even on the way, we turned off the car lights several times to marvel at the magical sight, gasping in awe.

As we drove, searching for the Astronomical Village, we encountered many rough roads. Since it was a sedan, it was naturally weak on unpaved roads. Bumping and rattling, we carefully drove up the winding unpaved mountain for a long time, only to find an even steeper downhill road at the summit.

We decided to go further and started descending... It was incredibly rough. Every time I heard stones scraping against the car's undercarriage, my father's face came to mind. ㅜㅡ

After descending for quite a while... I thought, 'Wait, this isn't right.' I stopped the car, my junior went ahead to check, and I turned the car around.

Just then, a Galloper Wagon came down from the opposite direction (I envy 4WD ㅜㅡ). My junior returned with the news that we had passed the Astronomical Village a long time ago, and with dumbfounded expressions, we climbed the mountain again.

Suddenly, I heard something continuously hitting the car, so I got out to check... The right front tire was flat... --; And this was in the middle of the mountain.

After making a mess of the trunk, I finally took out the spare tire and tools and began changing the wheel.
{{IMG:1}}

Why, of all days, were the lug nuts so stubborn, making it so difficult for me? Every time I kicked it, the joint that tightens the nuts kept slipping off... In the end, my junior and I combined our strength and loosened them with the power of two people. (If we had just turned it together, it would have come off so easily without even using our feet... ㅜㅡ)

It took a lot of effort, and my hands got completely dirty.

Perhaps because we had driven up and down the mountain more than expected, I checked the fuel and found there wasn't much left. 'I should have filled up beforehand... And there are no gas stations here...' I thought, along with...

I had a premonition that tonight would be tough. --;

Convinced that we couldn't find the Astronomical Village, we parked the car at the summit of the mountain, where a villa-like building and two cars were parked, and took out our telescope and other belongings.

Stepping out of the car, the pouring starlight... it was a magnificent sight.

Just as we gasped in awe for a moment, and before the car's hood had completely cooled down, a person came down from the villa in front of us.

We apologized, thinking we had been too loud, but he said no and asked where we were from. When we said Seoul, he asked about our respective schools, and we answered. Then, he kindly explained about the stars: Sirius, Orion, M45, Cassiopeia, centered around the Milky Way...

And then he revealed a fact: where we had parked was in front of the Central Astronomical Observatory... (Gasp. No wonder he knew so much...)

He seemed to be a professor. The professor invited us into the observatory. We thought we were incredibly lucky, and when we went up to the observatory, a student, who appeared to be his disciple, was observing stars with a laptop connected. We then heard various stories from the professor about astronomy and cameras.

A memorable piece of advice was that it's good to carry a sturdy, pipe-like tripod. He said that famous astronomers of the past, like Kepler, achieved great things by observing stars with much cruder telescopes, so we shouldn't just blame our equipment.

With a 50x magnification telescope, we saw Saturn's rings and even Jupiter's moons. The Earth's rotation speed was so fast that objects disappeared from the telescope after just a short moment. Meteor showers truly fell frequently. Sometimes, the long, clear trails they left behind were like paintings.

However... it was incredibly cold. My hands were freezing... it felt like mid-winter. We offered everyone the tea we brought, but the hot water we had was insufficient, and we brought cup noodles to eat but forgot the chopsticks... --;

We took star photos like this and used my junior's PDA to find constellations...

Around 4 AM, it was so cold... and I had to drive tomorrow, so I went down to the car first, drank a can of beer, turned the heater on briefly then off (because I was low on gas --;), and curled up in the driver's seat to try and sleep.

Outside the car, starlight poured down...

My body was trembling from the cold, but I was immersed in a long, sad reverie. Outside the car window, the starlight seemed somehow melancholic... What a pathetic sight for a man, right? ^^ Haha. So, I grumbled and complained at my own ridiculousness, then tried to sleep.

I don't know when I fell asleep, but when I woke up, it was bright, and everyone else was sleeping in the car.

I started the car, even forgetting to say goodbye to the professor. My junior said it was okay because they had said their goodbyes, but after we left, it bothered me a little.

On the way, we saw the Astronomical Village in the middle. Since there were no lights at night, it wasn't strange that we couldn't find it. --;

With a very Korean feel and a landscape that seemed to stand between autumn and winter, I played some melancholic music and drove down the mountain. Scenery I hadn't seen at all during the night...

Seeing the beautiful mist over the flowing stream, my junior eventually asked me to stop and took a few photos.

{{IMG:4}}

Autumn leaves... and fields beautifully covered in frost. And the newly risen sunlight and mountain shadows. Clear, cold air that embraced us to the core. We left those behind and headed home.

After putting 40,000 won worth of gas in the car, we stopped at Anheung Steamed Bun Village on the way. While waiting for the steamed buns to cook, the grandmother at the bun shop told us to have some coffee from the water dispenser. Remembering the cup noodles we couldn't eat last night, we bought wooden chopsticks from the neighboring store, ate our cup noodles, and warmed ourselves by the cozy gas stove.

Then, we loaded a box of steamed buns each and ate a few on the way.

It really made me think, 'This is why people come all the way to Anheung for these steamed buns...' The taste was completely different from what I ate at home. Coincidentally, it was Anheung Steamed Bun Day, so instead of 20 buns for 5,000 won, we got 25 buns per box.

{{IMG:7}}

And on the way back. It was early morning, so the Yeongdong Expressway was clear, and I also liked the sunlight on the return journey. Everyone else was asleep, and I, listening to music alone, picked up a little speed. (Though I was a bit worried about the spare tire...) I felt like driving a bit faster. I drove a little quicker than usual, arriving in Seoul around 10 AM, dropped everyone off at their homes, and then returned to my own.

Then, fatigue washed over me, and I fell asleep as if collapsing. When I woke up again, it was already dark evening. An empty, dark house... I warmed up a few steamed buns and ate them, then cooked Jjapaghetti (I eat Jjapaghetti when I'm feeling down and alone. ^^), and now I'm sitting alone, writing this post.

Someday... I'd like to go back there again. Next time, I'll make sure to prepare a burner and plenty of fuel...

2003. 10. 27
