**1. 주목받는 창조경제의 소프트웨어 산업의 가치**현 정부가 내세운 창조경제의 핵심에는 늘 빠지지 않고 등장하는 예시들은 SW성공 스토리입니다. 기존의 Rule breaker가 되면서, 새로운 시장을 만들어내는 창조경제는 기존의 하드웨어와 섹션별 산업군 중심의 시각에서 벗어나 유연하고 다양한 관점으로의 산업과 비즈니스의 접근을 목표로 하고 있습니다. 이것이 가능한 이유 중 하나는 바로 한번 출시되면 고치기 힘든 하드웨어와 달리 소프트웨어는 유기적이고 변경이 용이하기 때문입니다. 따라서 소프트웨어에 맞는 비즈니스와 산업은 기존의 틀로 정의하기에 어려운 이유기도 하죠.**
****2. 그 가치에 비하여 암울한 소프트웨어 환경** 그런데, 정작 이 산업을 굴리고 있는 소프트웨어인재들, 곧 개발자들의 처우는 그렇게 장미빛은 아니었습니다. 한국이 IT강국이라고 홍보하고 있는 사이에도 지속적으로 컴퓨터공학과의 인기는 낮아졌고, 개발자들의 처우는 그렇게 좋아지지 않았습니다. 이제 막상 해외의 사례를 보고 "소프트웨어"의 중요성을 인식하고 보니, 정작 중요한 소프트웨어인재의 품질과 처우는 썩 좋지 않은 상황입니다. 이러한 상황중에 청년들에게 벤쳐 열풍으로 몰고가고 싶은 정부의 바램은, 인해전술을 쓰던 중공군 처럼 딱총한자루를 주고서는 적들이 무슨 원주민이라도 되는양 장미빛 낙관을 심어준채 전장으로 투입하는 것과 마찬가지입니다. 상대는 이미 머신건으로 무장하고 있는데 말이죠. 물량에는 장사가 없다고 그 와중에 좋은 결과가 일부 나올지는 모르겠지만, 사선에 내몰릴 청년들이 걱정되기도 합니다. 정부의 이러한 행동이 단순히 산술적인 취업율을 올리기 위한 퍼포먼스가 아니길 또 한편으로 바랍니다. 전세계적으로 소프트웨어에 대한 가치가 올라가는 중에, 대한민국은 이러한 상황이 된 이유는 물론 현업에 있는 선배들의 책임도 없다 안할 수 없습니다. 기술보다는 영업에 치중했고, 소프트웨어에대한 가치를 제대로 측정하지 않았습니다. 해외의 IT기업의 M&A의 목적 중 하나는 바로 중소기업의 소프트웨어 핵심인재의 영입에 있습니다. 그러나, 대한민국의 경우에는 대부분 회사, 곧 서비스 영향력, 곧 DB를 목적으로 하는 경우가 많죠. 이 이유는 파는쪽이나 사는 쪽이나 기술에 대한 평가가 박하기 때문입니다. 어차피 내부에서 개발해서 똑같이 만들어도 되는것, 이라는 인식이 강한거죠.  거기다 월화수목 금금금, 이라고 알려진 개발자들의 삶도 부정적인 인식을 강하게 만들고 있습니다. 더 우울한 사실은 주말에 수당도 쳐주지 않고, 그것이 일반화 된다는 사실이죠. 유지보수에 대한 비용을 쳐주지 않고, 사는 사람이나 파는 사람이나 유지보수에 대한 기대조차 안합니다. 따라서 품질 좋은 소프트웨어를 납품하지도 않고, 딱 원하는 만큼만 최저가에 해줍니다. 외주개발의 가장 큰 위험은 바로 이겁니다. 그리고 핵심 역량이 되는 소프트웨어는 외주 개발을 하지 말아야 하는 이유도 바로 이것이죠. 초기에 프로토타입 정도나 간을 보는 프로젝트가 아닌 핵심 비즈니스를 외주로 맞기는 짓은 미련한 짓입니다. 그러나 대한민국에서는 단기적인 성과만을 바라보다 보니, 싸게 외주 잘 물어오는 직원이 높게 평가를 받습니다. 이를 악순환으로 생각하는 사람 조차 적은 것 같으니 사실 걱정 스럽습니다. 경영자나 CTO급이 아닌 이상 사실 이를 손들 용감한 직원은 없겠죠. 어찌되었던 비용이 늘어나는 일을 손들 바보같은 직원은 없을테니까요. **3. 이제는 그 밥그릇을 되 찾을때** 아무튼, 소프트웨어 산업의 중요성 대비, 개발자들의 처우나 역량, 그리고 인식 자체가 별로 좋지 않다는 점입니다. 차라리 닷컴 버블때는 닷컴 버블이 꺼지기전 까지는 선순환을 만들었으나, 그 이후 오로지 악순환을 만든게 아닌가 싶습니다.  문제는 소프트웨어산업이 더! **1. The Value of the Software Industry in the Spotlight of the Creative Economy**
At the core of the creative economy championed by the current government, software success stories are always prominently featured. By becoming a rule-breaker and creating new markets, the creative economy aims to move away from the traditional hardware and sector-specific industry perspectives, adopting a flexible and diverse approach to industry and business. One of the reasons this is possible is that, unlike hardware which is difficult to fix once released, software is organic and easy to modify. Therefore, businesses and industries suited for software are also difficult to define within traditional frameworks.

**2. A Bleak Software Environment Compared to Its Value**
However, the treatment of the software talents, or developers, who are actually driving this industry, has not been so rosy. Even while Korea was promoting itself as an IT powerhouse, the popularity of computer science departments continuously declined, and the treatment of developers did not significantly improve. Now that the importance of "software" has been recognized after observing overseas examples, the quality and treatment of crucial software talents are actually not very good. In this situation, the government's desire to ignite a venture craze among young people is akin to the Chinese Communist Army's human wave tactics, giving them a single pistol and sending them into battle with rosy optimism, as if the enemies were mere natives. Meanwhile, the opponents are already armed with machine guns. While there's no business like volume, and some good results might emerge amidst it, I also worry about the young people being pushed to the front lines. I also hope that the government's actions are not merely a performance to statistically boost employment rates.

While the value of software is rising globally, it cannot be denied that the seniors in the field also bear some responsibility for Korea's current situation. They focused more on sales than technology and did not properly assess the value of software. One of the purposes of M&A by overseas IT companies is to acquire key software talents from small and medium-sized enterprises. However, in Korea, most cases aim for the company itself, meaning service influence, or databases. The reason for this is that both sellers and buyers undervalue technology. There's a strong perception that "it can be developed internally and made the same way anyway."

Furthermore, the developers' lives, known as "Mon-Tue-Wed-Thu-Fri-Fri-Fri" (working all weekend), also create a strong negative perception. The more depressing fact is that overtime pay is not given for weekends, and this has become normalized. Maintenance costs are not paid, and neither buyers nor sellers even expect maintenance. Consequently, high-quality software is not delivered; only what is minimally desired is provided at the lowest price. This is the biggest risk of outsourced development. And this is also why core competency software should not be outsourced. Outsourcing core business, rather than just initial prototypes or exploratory projects, is foolish. However, in South Korea, due to a focus on short-term results, employees who can secure cheap outsourcing deals are highly valued. It's concerning that few people even consider this a vicious cycle. Unless one is a CEO or CTO, there won't be a brave employee to raise this issue. After all, no foolish employee would raise their hand for something that increases costs.

**3. It's Time to Reclaim Our Livelihood**
In any case, compared to the importance of the software industry, the treatment, capabilities, and perception of developers themselves are not very good. During the dot-com bubble, it created a virtuous cycle until the bubble burst, but since then, I suspect it has only created a vicious cycle.

The problem is that the software industry has become even MORE! important. Therefore, the industry has begun to recognize that it's time to mend this broken bowl (livelihood) starting now.

**1) Developers, Let's Reclaim Our Livelihood Ourselves**
Thus, these movements are materializing, centered around OK JSP, operated by CEO Noh Sang-beom. Among them are the launch of a cooperative, the creation of standard contracts, and guidance for freelance developers.

Some people might have a negative preconception, associating "cooperative" with militant labor unions. Some unions are so powerful they could be called "aristocratic unions," contributing to the worsening working conditions even for non-union members. And no developer would want their services or businesses, built with code like their own children, to be ruined by the activities of such a cooperative.

However, I certainly agree that there should at least be a private organization to represent IT developers. And, it is crucial to make business owners who do not even provide minimal working conditions realize that this is wrong. (In fact, business owners who don't even adhere to those minimums are unlikely to succeed anyway. But many people will suffer in the process until they fail.) While it hasn't officially launched yet, such movements are already underway.

**2) The Government's Continuous Listening and Interest**
As the saying goes, "it takes two hands to clap," and in response to these movements by developers, the government and politicians have also begun to show interest. Examples include the "love calls" sent to the IT sector by Park Geun-hye and Moon Jae-in during the last presidential election, and more recently, Mayor Park Won-soon's 'Cheongchaek' debate (not a typo for 'policy'; 'Cheongchaek' is the correct term for a debate held for listening). (If at least Seoul City's SI environment improves, the ripple effect would be enormous.)

**3. The Software Industry is Refactoring to Drive the Creative Economy Forward Again**
Throughout its lifecycle, all code is not a one-time development but must be continuously maintained to adapt to the environment. In fact, once it's open, that's when it truly begins. However, it seems undeniable that our software industry has been steeped in inertia, doing things the way they've always been done.

Now is the time to refactor to suit the changed environment. I believe this is the way to pass on this industry, inherited from our predecessors, in a better form to our juniors. Isn't that the fundamental principle of development? Simply running and operating an inherited framework (code) is sufficient for operators.
