KT에서 키봇이라는 유아용 로봇이 나왔습니다. 인공지능 로봇이라면 저도 관심이 많은 분야라서 좀 뒤져봤습니다. 제 느낌은 이는 로봇이라기보다는 컨텐츠 플랫폼을 입은 유아용 장난감이라는 느낌입니다. 이 제품에 대한 이야기를 잠깐 해보겠습니다.{{IMG:1}}
**[이미지 출처 : [스마트 미디어 버즈](http://www.ebuzz.co.kr/content/buzz_view.html?ps_ccid=88270) ]**
** 1. 컨텐츠 플랫폼으로서의 키봇** 기계값만 50만원에 다달이 드는 7000원의 사용료, 그리고 500원의 유료 컨텐츠 사실, 싸지 않습니다. 과연 성공할 수 있을까요 ? 키봇이 노린 시장은 단순해 보입니다. 안드로이드탭이나 아이패드는 아이들에게 친숙하기에는 너무 어렵다라는 것이고, 이를 장난감의 탈을 씌워 더 어린아이들에게 팔아보자라는 속셈이죠. 컨텐츠플랫폼에 로봇까지의 확대. 개념은 신선하지만, 키봇의 형태를 보니 비관적이라고 봅니다. 키봇의 포지션은 잘못됬습니다.
** 1) 과연 키봇의 컨텐츠는 경쟁력이 있을것인가 ?** 스마트폰으로 부터 배운 컨텐츠 플랫폼의 경쟁력은 에코에서 나옵니다. 그러나 키봇의 컨텐츠는 철저히 KT에서 제작되며, 다양한 응용프로그램이 나오기 어려운 구조입니다. 차라리 여기에 안드로이드OS를 커스텀 하고, 올레마켓에 통합했으면 어땠을까 하는 생각이 드네요.  그리고 아이가 조금만 크면 키봇 컨텐츠보다는 아이패드와 갤럭시탭의 컨텐츠가 훨씬 좋다라는것을 알게 될겁니다. 그런점에서 키봇의 컨텐츠 플랫폼 전략은 틀렸다고 봅니다.
RFID로 낱말태그를 보여주는 기능은 스마트 패드에서도 전면카메라를 이용해 QR카드등을 만들 수 있는 아이디어로 활용 될 수 있겠네요. 아이디어는 돋보였으나 키봇이란 플랫폼 경쟁력은 글쎄올시다입니다. **
**** 2) 장난감에 얹어진 아이패드.** 낙하방지가 탑제된 이동형 장난감, 키봇이 가지고 있는 특징 중 하나입니다. 원숭이 인형이 움직이는데 아이들은 어떤 행동을 할까요 ? 낙하방지를 탑제한다고 해결될 일일까요 ? 아닙니다. 그에 맞는 내구성을 갖추는 편이 훨씬 현명한 전략이 아니었을까 합니다. 아이들이 정말 키봇을 6개월이상 작동가능하게 놓아둘지 의문입니다.
**2. 로봇으로서의 키봇**사실 가장 아쉬운것은 로봇으로서의 키봇입니다. 한국 최초의 유아용 로봇을 내세웠지만, 로봇이라기보다는 장난감입니다. **
**** 1) 자율성이 없는 키봇**로봇이라면 소니의 아이보처럼 자율행동이 가능해야 합니다. 애교 모드는 그저 버튼을 누르면 특별한 행동을 하는 패턴이니 그냥 역시 장난감입니다. 그에 반해 아이보같은 로봇은 자율행동을 하고 성장하고, 심지어는 프로그래밍까지 가능한 어른들의 장난감까지 성장합니다.

KT has released a robot for toddlers called Kibot. As I'm very interested in AI robots, I looked into it a bit. My feeling is that it's less of a robot and more of a toddler's toy dressed up as a content platform. Let's talk about this product for a moment.{{IMG:1}}
**[Image Source: [Smart Media Buzz](http://www.ebuzz.co.kr/content/buzz_view.html?ps_ccid=88270)]**
**1. Kibot as a Content Platform**
The device itself costs 500,000 won, plus a monthly usage fee of 7,000 won, and 500 won for paid content. Frankly, it's not cheap. Can it really succeed? The market Kibot is targeting seems simple. Android tabs and iPads are too difficult for children to be familiar with, and the idea is to dress it up as a toy to sell to younger children. Expanding from a content platform to a robot. The concept is fresh, but looking at Kibot's form, I'm pessimistic. Kibot's positioning is wrong.

**1) Will Kibot's content truly be competitive?**
The competitiveness of content platforms, as learned from smartphones, comes from the ecosystem. However, Kibot's content is strictly produced by KT, making it difficult for diverse applications to emerge. I wonder if it would have been better to customize Android OS and integrate it into the Olleh Market. And once a child grows a little, they will realize that the content on iPads and Galaxy Tabs is much better than Kibot's. In that sense, I believe Kibot's content platform strategy is flawed.
The function of showing word tags via RFID could be utilized as an idea for smart pads to create QR cards using the front camera. The idea was brilliant, but Kibot's platform competitiveness is questionable.

**2) An iPad mounted on a toy.**
A mobile toy equipped with fall prevention is one of Kibot's features. What will children do when a monkey doll moves? Will fall prevention solve the problem? No. I think it would have been a much wiser strategy to equip it with appropriate durability. I doubt whether children will keep Kibot operational for more than six months.

**2. Kibot as a Robot**
Frankly, what's most disappointing is Kibot as a robot. It's touted as Korea's first robot for toddlers, but it's more of a toy than a robot.

**1) Kibot Lacks Autonomy**
If it's a robot, it should be capable of autonomous actions, like Sony's Aibo. The "cute mode" is just a pattern of special actions triggered by pressing a button, so it's still just a toy. In contrast, robots like Aibo perform autonomous actions, grow, and can even be programmed, evolving into toys for adults.

The toys children need are not games on an LCD screen, but rather things they can move, touch, and hit. And sometimes, they need to be able to play pranks together. In this regard, Kibot fails.

**2) Kibot Lacks Communication**
Another disappointing aspect of Kibot is its inability to communicate with children. In Aibo's case, it could express anger or happiness, but these functions are absent. Even if children easily empathize, it's unclear if they will like Kibot's simple mini-car-like driving function.{{IMG:2}}
At a time when emotional communication in robots, like with recent social bots, is emphasized, Kibot's functions seem regrettably basic.

**3. Did Kibot, as an educational toy, aim too far beyond commercial success?**
I also wonder if it's unreasonable for me to expect from a 500,000 won Kibot what I expect from an Aibo costing over 1.5 million won. However, I sincerely hope that the direction of the first toddler robot made by a major Korean corporation is not just to put a device with performance inferior to a budget smartphone on a toy to sell content, but rather to be a machine that looks further into the distant future.
I hope the next Kibot doesn't just let children play Omok (Gobang) on its small LCD, but evolves into a social bot that can interact and play with children, like throwing and catching a ball together. The current Kibot seems to be nothing more or less than an iPad mounted on a toy. Unfortunately, the allure of electronic devices like iPads is immense for children, and while this children's market would sell like hotcakes even if it were just toilet paper with Pororo characters, if KT is a company that looks to the distant future of IT, I hope they aim higher in the robot business.
