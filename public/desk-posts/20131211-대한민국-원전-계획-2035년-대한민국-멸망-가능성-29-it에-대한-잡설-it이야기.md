2035년 대한민국의 원전비중이 29%로 늘어난다는 황당한 소식이 들려왔습니다.  얼마전 후쿠시마 참사를 바로 옆에서 구경했던 대한민국이라는 국가에서 결정한 내용이란 것이 믿을 수 없을 정도 입니다. 그리고, 이에대한 찬성론자는 두가지를 이야기합니다. **우리는 신뢰할 만한 기술을 가졌다. 대안이 없지 않느냐. **이 두가지에 대해 반박을 해볼까 합니다. ** 1. 우리는 신뢰할만한 기술을 가졌다.** **  진실은. 적어도 가용하는 동안에는 괜찮을거야.. 아마도..**  원자력은 잘 모르니, 제 전문 분야인 IT이야기를 잠깐 하죠. IT에서 가장 중요한 신뢰성은 24시간 서비스가 가용되야 합니다. 그리고 데이터는 절대로 날아가서는 안되죠. 이에 대한 해결책은 2중, 3중화와 분산이었습니다. 이토록 고신뢰성을 위한 IT의 전략은 사실상 모든 고신뢰성을 요구하는 기술에 해당되는 전략입니다. 그러나, 원전이 과연 이 방향에 맞는지? 의문입니다. 원자력은 분산과 거리가 먼 중앙집중식 발전 체제입니다. 값이 싸다는 이유 하나만으로 사실 중앙 집중식 기술을 채택합니다. 중앙에 집중된 기술은 아무리 날고 기는 방비를 하더라도 한순간에 무너지는 수가 있습니다. 천재지변이 될 수 있고, 인재가 될 수도 있습니다. 그 중 천재지변 이야기를 많이 이야기하는데요. IT분야에서는 천재지변에 대비한 방법은 원격지의 미러링입니다. 물리적으로 두개의 데이터를 먼거리에 보관하여 동기화 합니다. 그러나, 원자력은 IT기술과 달리 입지조건이 매우 제한적이다 보니 여러곳에 설치 또한 어렵습니다. 때문에 1기, 2기, 3기들이 다 붙어있죠. 1000년만에 오는 지진, 10000년만에 오는 지진을 대비하고 있지는 않습니다. 문제는 우리 살아있는 동안에 1000년만에 오는 지진이 올지 안올지는 사실상 검증하기 어렵다는 점입니다. 결국 파괴되는 것을 가정해야 하는데, 이에 대한 시나리오보다는 "파괴되지 않는다"라는 것만 강조하고 있습니다. (그 분들께 진격의 거인을 보여드리고 싶네요.) 소규모 피해만 입고, 파괴되어도 괜찮은 에너지 자원 정책이 고신뢰성이 있는 시스템이지, 철벽 무적의 벽을 두른 절대로 파괴되지 않는 에너지 자원 정책은 시한폭탄과 마찬가지입니다. IT기업이 성장하는 그림도 마찬가지인데, 초기 벤처 시절에는 시스템의 규모가 작거나 해킹에 대한 위험이 낮으므로 고신뢰성이 있는 시스템까지는 갖추지 않습니다. 이 때 중요한 것은 바로 속도죠. 시장에 빠른 대응이 중요합니다. 그러나 기업이 성장하고 고객이 많아지면, 한방에 훅 갈 수 있으므로 고 신뢰성이 중요해집니다. 한방에 기업 망하는건 순식간입니다. 페이스북이 글로벌 기업이 된데는 경쟁사의 장애도 결정적인 역할을 했죠. 대한민국이 벤처기업입니까? 누구를 위한 모험을 하는 것입니까? 서울에는 원전이 없어서 괜찮을까요? 원전이 안전하다고 우기는 기술자들을 붙들고 물어보세요. 대한민국의 지난 1000년간 있었던 가장 큰 규모의 자연재해를 견딜 수 있는지. 거기다 더 위험한 사실을 하나 말씀드리면, 대한민국은 분단국가입니다. 전쟁 소설 데프콘 한미전에서 미국을 소규모 병력으로 꼼짝 못하게했던 전술이 무엇인지 기억나시나요? 바로 한국 특수부대가 미국의 원전을 점령해버린 일입니다. 다시한번 묻죠. 북한의 특수부대나 이슬람의 테러단체가 원전을 점령하려 한다면 삼성의 계열사인 세콤으로 막을 수 있을까요? 초등학생의 사탕을 빼엇으러 오는 고등학생 양아치들과의 대결입니다. 안전한 기술은 뚫려도 되는 기술 뿐입니다. 그게 보안의 가장 기본 철학 이고, 그래서 2중화 3중화를 하는겁니다. **2. 대안이 없다.**** 진실은, 성장에 쏟아야 할 돈이 더 급하다. Absurd news has been heard: South Korea's nuclear power ratio will increase to 29% by 2035. It's unbelievable that this decision was made by South Korea, a nation that recently witnessed the Fukushima disaster right next door. And proponents of this decision cite two main points: **We have reliable technology. Isn't there no alternative?** I'd like to refute these two points.

**1. We have reliable technology.** **The truth is: It'll be fine... probably... at least while it's operational.**

Since I'm not an expert in nuclear power, let me briefly talk about IT, my area of expertise. In IT, the most crucial aspect of reliability is 24/7 service availability. And data must never be lost. The solutions for this were redundancy (2-fold, 3-fold) and distribution. This IT strategy for high reliability is virtually applicable to all technologies requiring high reliability. However, I question whether nuclear power truly aligns with this direction.

Nuclear power is a centralized generation system, far removed from distribution. It adopts centralized technology, essentially for the sole reason of being cheap. Centralized technology, no matter how robust the defenses, can collapse in an instant. This could be a natural disaster or a man-made one. Among these, natural disasters are often discussed. In IT, the method to prepare for natural disasters is remote mirroring. Two sets of data are physically stored at distant locations and synchronized. However, unlike IT technology, nuclear power has very limited site requirements, making it difficult to install in multiple locations. That's why units 1, 2, and 3 are all clustered together. They are not preparing for earthquakes that occur once every 1,000 years or 10,000 years. The problem is that it's virtually impossible to verify whether a 1,000-year earthquake will occur during our lifetime. Ultimately, we should assume destruction, but instead of scenarios for this, only "it won't be destroyed" is emphasized. (I'd like to show those people Attack on Titan.)

An energy resource policy that can sustain only minor damage and is acceptable even if destroyed is a high-reliability system, whereas an energy resource policy surrounded by an impregnable, indestructible wall is akin to a ticking time bomb.

The growth trajectory of IT companies is similar. In the early startup phase, because the system scale is small or the risk of hacking is low, they don't necessarily build highly reliable systems. What's important then is speed. Rapid market response is crucial. However, as a company grows and acquires more customers, high reliability becomes critical because it can be wiped out in an instant. A company can go bankrupt in a flash. Competitor outages also played a decisive role in Facebook becoming a global company.

Is South Korea a startup company? Whose adventure are we embarking on? Will it be okay just because there are no nuclear power plants in Seoul?

Ask the engineers who insist nuclear power plants are safe: Can they withstand the largest natural disaster that has occurred in South Korea over the past 1,000 years?

Furthermore, let me tell you an even more dangerous fact: South Korea is a divided nation. Do you remember the tactic that immobilized the U.S. with a small force in the war novel 'Defcon: US-Korea War'? It was when South Korean special forces occupied U.S. nuclear power plants. I ask again: If North Korean special forces or an Islamic terrorist group tried to occupy a nuclear power plant, could it be stopped by Secom, a Samsung affiliate? This is like a confrontation between elementary schoolers and high school bullies trying to snatch their candy.

The only truly safe technology is one that can afford to be breached. That is the most fundamental philosophy of security, which is why we implement 2-fold and 3-fold redundancy.

**2. There is no alternative.** **The truth is: Money for growth is more urgent. (Because there are no immediate results.)**

The argument that 'there is no alternative' was something I somewhat agreed with in the past. I'm not advocating for an immediate reduction in nuclear power plants right now. However, let me ask you one thing.

Imagine your child is terminally ill, and the doctor says they will die within a year if they don't have surgery. You have enough money for the surgery, though it's most of what you own. Then, you learn about an investment opportunity that could multiply your money by 100 times in 10 months. Would you dare to gamble your child's life for a 10-month venture?

Nuclear power plants are similar. Is there truly no energy? What's more surprising is that it's the wealthy, not the poor, who support this. Do you want to gain more, even by mortgaging the lives of those around you?

Does this mean that even if a nuclear accident occurs in South Korea, they can just take their earned money and go abroad?

**3. So, what about the US and Japan?**

**Google, a company with a market capitalization of $340 billion, is one of the world's largest electricity consumers, yet in recent years, it has spent over $1 billion (1 trillion KRW) on renewable energy projects like solar and wind power.**

**In contrast, what are South Korea, where the nuclear power promotion budget is 63 times that of renewable energy promotion (and will decrease by 43% from 37 billion KRW this year to 21 billion KRW next year), and Samsung Electronics, with a market capitalization of $180 billion, doing? The money Google spends is 27 times more than the South Korean government's budget. Is Google amazing? Or are we shameful?**

Personally, I don't consider IT companies like Google, Facebook, and even Korea's 'N' company to be eco-friendly. In fact, they are companies that consume an enormous amount of energy. However, they at least recognize that energy conservation and renewable energy are the answers, and they are focusing campaigns and technological investments around them. Even if nuclear power plants cannot be stopped immediately, we must move towards reducing our reliance on them.

**The will to secure renewable energy for the future is our future competitiveness, far more urgent than securing immediate energy through nuclear power.** Imagine one day the U.S. develops renewable energy that is far cheaper than nuclear power. We would have to pay enormous royalties to buy it, and the nuclear power plants we've already built would become nothing more than terrifying bombs. Of course, at least then we wouldn't be mortgaging the lives of our descendants, so that would be a small comfort.

If you're so keen on nuclear power, then develop a spaceship that can reach the sun. On the sun, an inexhaustible fusion power plant is vigorously operating, with enough fuel for tens of billions of years.

Fools who cannot learn from others' misfortunes only realize it when they experience it themselves, and endless greed is bound to lead to great disaster someday. I pray that South Korea does not play a foolish game of chance with the lives of its citizens. It must be nice for those with money. They can just escape...
