There are many articles discussing the reasons for Cyworld's failure due to crowdfunding, but I find it hard to agree with the argument that Cyworld was not a platform.

                                **The reason Cyworld Minihompy failed lies in the failure of its platform business strategy!** Cyworld Minihompy failed. Of course, Cyworld itself didn't fail. But Minihompy disappeared. Minihompy disappeared, and its content was transferred to a service called 'Cyhome'. Cyhome is a blog-style service. I once wrote that the blog craze would not surpass the Cyworld Minihompy craze, and ultimately, while blogs didn't surpass the Cyworld Minihompy craze, they survived longer, resulting in a draw. (Blog.. ggamnyang.com

http://ggamnyang.com/1483

In terms of user content distribution, Cyworld clearly had platform-like functionalities. While a two-way market can certainly be a very good factor for a platform's long-term existence, I believe that term refers to a market platform, not a platform in general. For reference, Naver Blog also doesn't provide 'skin' or 'content' marketplace features. Open APIs are not very useful, and RSS is not really used much these days... However, content distribution is achieved through blog advertising agencies and search. Anyway, building such an ecosystem would have been difficult for Nate to emulate given its smaller search market share compared to Naver, and personally, I think the number one reason for Cyworld's failure was the timing failure of its mobile strategy. SK Telecom was one of the culprits there, and since mobile doesn't support skin businesses, they should have quickly found other value. Considering that mobile led Facebook's recent growth, I think all of Cyworld's failures make sense.

http://news.naver.com/main/read.nhn?mode=LSD&mid=sec&oid=001&aid=0008150076&sid1=001
                                [**Facebook's Explosive Growth Thanks to Mobile... 80% of Revenue Relies on It** (Seoul=Yonhap News) Reporter Kim Yoon-gu = Facebook is showing remarkable growth, exceeding market expectations through mobile advertising and continuous user expansion. ... news.naver.com](http://news.naver.com/main/read.nhn?mode=LSD&mid=sec&oid=001&aid=0008150076&sid1=001)

The decisive blow to Cyworld wasn't Naver Blog, which took over other markets and gradually encroached on Cyworld's market, but Facebook and KakaoStory. Both companies are private SNS providers that executed their mobile strategies exceptionally well. This mobile platform strategy was also what I emphasized when I applied to SK Comms as a new recruit. (The problem was that I said such things while applying as a developer, which led to failure...) And, from the same perspective, the advice I'd like to give Cyworld is that we are already transitioning from mobile games to IoT games. We don't yet know what kind of SNS is suitable for IoT. There are talks that Foursquare and SeeOn aren't very good, and the best options are photo-based services like Instagram or KakaoStory, and video-based services. However, whether these are effective for IoT is still a question mark for me. All SNS and internet services are likely grappling with the same concerns. For now, it's right to focus on mobile as a fast follower, but if you want revival and growth, I hope you consider the next step. I believe platform strategy involves not only becoming a platform yourself but also maintaining sensitivity to how the platform environment surrounding your service is changing.
