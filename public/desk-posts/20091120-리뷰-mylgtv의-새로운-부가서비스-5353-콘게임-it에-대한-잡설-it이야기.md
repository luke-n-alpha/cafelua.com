Now I realize it was the opening screen of Gran Turismo, a racing game for PS3. I like games, but my wife dislikes them. ** 2. The Significance of KonGame**
** Value-added service with increased user accessibility through advertising and channel assignment
** Recently, [I mentioned that despite IPTV having excellent value-added services, the menu-based approach has low accessibility for channel-centric users. As an alternative, I suggested that value-added services, not just dramas, should also have repeated advertisements in an NVOD format.](http://blog.naver.com/fstory97/70072619831)
 But now I see they were actually doing it. And by assigning channels to value-added services, they cleanly solved the accessibility problem. I was surprised that there were an enormous number of channels in the thousands, and even more surprised that they were allocated to value-added services. In the future, if IPTV's additional channels increase, I wonder if channel guide magazines in the form of weeklies or monthlies will also be delivered. Internet portal TV channel guide pages will also become richer with IPTV.

**4. KonGame's Disappointment: The Inability to Connect to Actual Gameplay**
 Watching these contents inevitably makes one feel the urge to buy a video game console and software. KonGame has minor (?) bugs and, despite its great potential, still has shortcomings. This urge to buy soon creates a need to enjoy the games, but you cannot play games through IPTV. In a previous post, I talked about the [convergence of video game consoles and IPTV](http://blog.naver.com/fstory97/70073130001).
 There isn't even a feature to purchase game consoles or titles. It's a shame that shopping using IPTV hasn't been implemented yet. Such a service has the potential to lead to the sale of software, movies, music DVDs, or movie ticket reservations.
