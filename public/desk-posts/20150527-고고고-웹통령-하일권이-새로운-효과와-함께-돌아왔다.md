**2. A Webtoon with Animated GIFs? No. An Evolved Smarttoon**

[**Animated GIF - Enha Wiki Mirror** mirror.enha.kr](https://mirror.enha.kr/wiki/%EC%9B%80%EC%A7%A4)

I scrolled down a bit, noticed something unusual, and scrolled back up. Effects that move dynamically with the scroll. New layers appear as you scroll, layers move at different speeds depending on perspective, and in a mobile environment, certain actions trigger vibrations. In the comments, people were calling it a webtoon with animated GIFs. However, as someone with a development background, I quickly realized that these technologies were based on web technologies. The appropriate meeting of technology and content is probably what this is all about. In fact, previous Smarttoons had a few problems. The user experience was too different from scroll-based PC webtoons, requiring two rounds of editing, and the effects were limited, leading to opinions that more features should be provided. Also, from a user's perspective, the panels felt excessively narrow. In Rangtto's 'SM Player,' characters even complained that the panels were too narrow because it was a Smarttoon. This animated GIF webtoon? Smarttoon2? Or just Webtoon2? I don't know what to call it, but the information released to the press only mentions 'Webtoon Effect Editor' provision, so it seems there's no separate brand. For convenience, I'll call it Smarttoon2. The advantage of this Smarttoon2 is that it's a scroll-based webtoon where PC and mobile environments are not separately divided. Therefore, it has the advantage of maximizing effects with just a slight addition of effects to existing scroll webtoons. Technically, it involves separating the movement speed or effects of multiple layers based on the scroll position, a technology previously seen on web event pages.

**4. Is This the Mobile Content Pikicast and Naver Post Were Looking For?**
In fact, Naver seems to have pondered extensively over the answer to mobile content. Naver Post was one such attempt, but what I've recently felt is that Pikicast offered the closest solution. Flipping content suitable for mobile, pages with simple looping animations, and a social-based dissemination model. It was content typically optimized for mobile, and indeed, it's the most widely distributed content on Facebook. However, recent changes are visible in Naver Webtoon. The sharing to social networks, previously restricted due to copyright issues, has been eased, allowing active sharing of panels to social networks, and this effect-applied webtoon has provided an appropriate and powerful solution for scroll and mobile. It even made me wonder if this wasn't the answer Naver Post was looking for, rather than just a webtoon. Naver Webtoon is said to be operating independently as a CIC (Company-In-Company), a separate company within a company. I understand that it's currently striving globally alongside Lezhin Comics, and I hope for the day when Naver Webtoon's status catches up with Marvel worldwide.
