**1. Subway Test, First Encounter with WiBro Was Highly Satisfying**** 1) Industry-standard micro USB. Ensures compatibility with other smartphone cables.**
 On my way home after the product review, my phone battery died on the subway. I remembered Infomark's Egg on the subway and took it out. Its charging cable is a micro USB, identical to the Galaxy S2. When I plugged the cable into my laptop and connected it to the Galaxy S2, it charged and booted simultaneously. Anyway, I secured a way to contact my wife until I got home. The fact that the Egg uses a micro USB port isn't a major feature, but its compatibility with most devices is a big advantage. Thanks to this, I don't have to carry a separate cable for my Galaxy S2.
** 2) PC web surfing with excellent speed**
 Since I had a seat on the subway, I decided to activate the Egg. I booted the Egg, selected KT's 5GB free event for a month, and entered my personal information, and it was registered very easily. Then the familiar Naver homepage appeared. Previously, when I surfed the web on my laptop connected via Optimus Q USB direct, it felt a bit sluggish, but the laptop connected to WiBro felt comparable to using it at home. It was a moment of realizing that WiBro was not originally a technology solely for smartphones. After telling my wife to talk to me via Naver Talk for PC, I enjoyed web surfing. I enjoyed it so much that I ended up missing my stop by one station ㅜㅜ  ﻿
**3) Both a drawback and an advantage. The Egg, as a separate device, brings the blessing of WiBro to all devices. For pad and laptop users... long live the Egg!**
The clear drawback of the Egg is that you have to carry a separate device. However, the advantage is that the Egg works with laptops, pads, and smartphones without distinction. My first impression of WiBro was 100% satisfaction.
**2. In-car Test**
 The second test was during my commute. It was to check for dead zones when traveling on the national road from Anyang Hogye-dong to Jeongja-dong. **
1) Slight dead zones occur underground and indoors.**
When I first turned on WiBro in the underground parking lot on the second basement level, it didn't receive a signal. I figured it was because it was an underground parking lot where even 3G doesn't work well. As soon as I saw the exit on the first basement level, WiBro turned orange and started receiving a signal. It turned green immediately after exiting the first basement level. 
**2) Usable reception rate, better than SKT's 3G**
After that, on the way to Jeongja-dong, I played Naver Music and passed through a few short tunnels.
 The music never cut out, and only in the middle of tunnels did the light briefly turn orange, indicating a weaker signal. And after arriving at work, there was no signal inside the company. In the restaurant during lunchtime, the orange light came on, but it was usable. WiBro, which has some diffraction properties, is insufficient in deep indoor areas but was sufficient for use while on the move. In addition, I tested it on the Yeongdong Expressway between Buksuwon IC and Yangji IC, and the signal was continuously caught in long tunnels like Maseong Tunnel, suggesting WiBro base stations are installed there. If the number of users increases like 3G, I think the nationwide network will become denser, and indoor base stations will also increase. I should ask KT if it's possible to install a small base station (gap filler) if there's a WiBro dead zone at home. 
** 3. Charms Felt While Using the Egg** **1) Fast Speed**
** ** It's fast. The actual usage speed was around 3Mbit/sec for downloads, which is three times the speed of SKT's 3G. However, the network felt more stable.
