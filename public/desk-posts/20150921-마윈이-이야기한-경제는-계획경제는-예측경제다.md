Jack Ma stated that the planned economy would become superior in the era of big data, and it feels like proponents of free markets or democracy are reacting very sensitively, perceiving this as an ideological threat.

**1. This is not an ideological conflict, but a story of a new SW-centric paradigm****- Let's understand big data and e-commerce.**
However, I believe it's necessary to focus on 'big data' and 'Alibaba,' which he operates, rather than this ideological debate. Explaining big data first might be a bit difficult, so let's talk about Alibaba first. Alibaba is the world's largest B2B market. If B2B isn't clear, it might be easier to think of it as a wholesale market. There's nothing you can't find, and if you want to make something, you almost certainly have to visit here. If China is the world's factory, then Alibaba is the online shopping mall for those factories. And that shopping mall knows in real-time who bought what, when, and for how much. The sheer volume of this data is truly enormous, making it actual big data. It's not something that can be computed on a single computer; it requires distributed computing, and the data formats are often inconsistent, necessitating algorithms that can infer meaning from it. That's big data. Jack Ma and big data are naturally a perfect fit. One of the main applications of such big data is predictive marketing. The story of Amazon determining a customer's unknown pregnancy based on their purchase list and sending them brochures for pregnancy-related items is a well-known anecdote. Now, let's imagine something. Alibaba's data being used for China's economic policy. Of course, this might only be possible because it's a communist party, but it could reveal when a financial crisis might occur, who the vulnerable populations are, or even who might be making bombs to threaten the Chinese government. Based on this, warnings or actions could be taken in advance. The saying that whoever controls logistics and finance can control the world is likely not an empty one. This is probably also why Amazon and Coupang started their logistics operations.

**2. Big Data is not long-term planning, but real-time planning and prediction**
This is what a big data-based planned economy means. To be precise, new terms like real-time economy, predictive economy, or algorithmic economy might need to emerge, rather than just planned economy. It's not about annual plans and budget execution, but about preparing for and responding to events that might happen next week, or even three days from now. It's like how knowing whether it will rain today is a huge help, even if predicting the weather a week in advance is difficult. You'd know whether or not to carry an umbrella today. I find this statement very significant. Could Jack Ma have known about China's recent stock market crash beforehand? Perhaps the Chinese government also knew through Alibaba. Furthermore, as mentioned earlier, Alibaba is already involved in finance as a fintech company.

**3. Algorithmic Economy? Exploratory Economy? Evolutionary Economy? Predictive Economy?**
This is a planned economy entirely different from the 5-year plans of the Park Chung-hee era. From a software perspective, it would be a method of repeatedly attempting short plans alongside long-term plans and using feedback to find the correct answers. From a distance, it would look more like exploration and evolution than planning.

**4. More important is whether it can be executed.**
It could be called the power of software. However, it won't be as easy as it sounds. Even with such software capabilities, the organization and processes must be prepared for it. Is China building this capability? I cautiously wonder if there might come a day when Jack Ma takes on an important position within the Communist Party.
