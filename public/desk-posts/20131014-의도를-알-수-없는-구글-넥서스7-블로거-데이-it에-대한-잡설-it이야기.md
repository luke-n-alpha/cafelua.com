Last Wednesday, I attended the Nexus 7 blog day. As I'm not a full-time blogger, going out once a month is a big deal for me. Moreover, with a family trip scheduled for the very next day, I attended the event bracing myself for my wife's scolding. The reason I pushed myself so hard was simply because it was "Google."

In fact, I once predicted the domestic growth of tablets a long time ago, but compared to overseas markets, it's closer to a complete failure. I have a few thoughts on the reasons for this, but what does Google, the birthplace of Android tablets, think? What changes have occurred at Google, which is even running TV commercials in Korea for the first time in a while? I expected this event to answer these various questions.

**1. Product Introduction**

I won't talk about the buffet-style meal, which was close to fast food, or the T-shirt I received as a souvenir. Of course, the souvenir was much appreciated, but I didn't attend the event just for a souvenir and a meal.

**1) It's better. - That's it.**

Longer-lasting battery, Full HD sharp screen, light and simple design, powerful performance. {{IMG:2}} This was the kind of introduction that followed. Listening to it, I honestly felt like I was wasting my time. If it were a small or medium-sized company, I would applaud them for creating such a powerful device. However, this presentation was from Google. Moreover, as a new product, it's naturally expected to have better performance than its predecessor. In fact, tablets have always seen good devices released. The iPad did, and the Galaxy Tab and Galaxy Note did. But the domestic market hasn't changed. It seemed unlikely that the Nexus 7 would serve as a catalyst for the tablet market. I even thought that the Biscuit Tab, priced under 200,000 won, was a more groundbreaking and strategic device in that regard.

**2) Is it really cheap?****{{IMG:3}}**

Unfortunately, Google's Nexus 7 is not a very cheap tablet. Calling it a "value king" is only true overseas; when you factor in exchange rates and so on, it ends up being a device over 300,000 won, with the top-tier model exceeding 450,000 won. Compared to devices that tried to hit $199 and $299 overseas to be considered good value, the price in Korea seems to have been calculated without any thought beyond currency conversion. If they aimed for the same effect in Korea, the Nexus 7 should have been priced at 299,000 won.

**2. Start Your Day with Nexus 7 (Google)?**

**What kind of company is Google, and what can it possibly do?**

It even came up as a quiz question at the Google event: how many Google services are featured in the TV commercial? IT bloggers like me (and most attendees at the event) could easily answer this. But for the average user, how do the numerous Google services embedded in that commercial come across? Frankly, I give that Google commercial, which they ran for the first time in a while, a very low score. The reason is that Google's domestic service market share is still below 10%, and most users don't even know what Google's services are beyond 'Android, YouTube, and Search'.

While it might have been a successful ad overseas, I questioned what purpose it served for Google, a brand with weak recognition in Korea, to simply list a string of services. I think it would have been more important to first highlight the brand and its core services.

**3. The Nature of Nexus 7 as Seen in its Default Menu**

**- The Nexus 7 is a media device!****{{IMG:4}}**

Although I've been listing negative points, if we talk about Google's identity visible in the Nexus 7, YouTube, Google Play, Chrome, and Mail are prominently displayed. This signifies it as a communication tool and a media playback device. Also, beyond apps, Google Play's sales categories, which were the subject of two quiz questions, include movies/books, and the top-selling book is said to be "The Old Man and the Sea" for 1,000 won.

In other words, the Nexus 7's identity is not a device you cling to from the start of the day until the end, but rather one you use to watch movies, read books, or browse the internet while lounging on the sofa. And in fact, that's precisely the tablet's position. A device you carry all day long is the Galaxy S, or rather, a smartphone. If you carry the Nexus 7 all day, the Nexus 5's position becomes ambiguous.

**4. Google's Still Weak Brand and Domestic Content Services**

I believe Google's influence as a content service provider in Korea is negligible. The distribution of apps on Google Play, which Google touts as a strength, is already being shaken up by Kakao. Portals dominate the top spots.

As you know, music services are dominated by mobile carriers, IPTV reigns supreme for movie services, and books barely make a dent. During the quiz, there was a question: "How many copies of 'The Old Man and the Sea,' which is number one, have been sold?" The answer was positive, but the fact that "The Old Man and the Sea" is number one itself implies that it's not selling as much as one might think. If you look at domestic e-book sales sites, the top spots are occupied by RoFanMu (Romance, Fantasy, Martial Arts) and adult content. The reason is that the public wants light and titillating content. While recommended content and the low price of 1,000 won seem to have pushed "The Old Man and the Sea" to the top, it doesn't signify actual sustained engagement.

Where would you go on the Korean internet to find "expected job interview questions" as introduced in the ad? "Google Search?" Sorry, but students know that a cafe called **"Chwieop Ppogaegi"** (Job Breaking) dominates job information. This means Google is a minor player as a content business in Korea in many respects. And honestly, I thought this Nexus 7 announcement, like Amazon Kindle's, might signify an attempt to do something in connection with countermeasures. However, there was no such movement.

**5. If I Had to Pick Something Innovative, It's the Profile System**

Finally, like Windows, profiles are available for family members to set up their own environments. **{{IMG:5}}** **[ It's hard to see, but the blue button at the top is admin, the rest are family members ]** This was introduced as a main feature of the Nexus 7, but it seems highly likely to be adopted in standard Android OS later. I asked about this, but the assistant couldn't answer because they didn't know... ㅡㅡ; The problem, however, is that when the admin enables unknown file execution, all other family profiles also enable unknown file execution. Generally, security vulnerabilities arise from this feature, so more detailed control is needed, rather than just app exposure per profile.

**6. Google's Nexus 7, One of Them Among Domestic Android Tablets**

Summarizing the above, the position of Google's Nexus 7 in Korea can be roughly predicted. It will simply be one of many Android tablets, and while it's expected to gain some popularity due to "Nex-pride," considering the much faster release cycles of the Galaxy Tab and Note, as well as mobile carriers' antics and devices flooding the used market, the Nexus 7 is unlikely to dominate the market.

**7. Does Google Intend to Lead Change in the Korean Tablet Market?**

A few days ago, I visited Nami Island and was surprised to see many foreigners taking photos with tablets. Most of them had iPads and Galaxies. Koreans, on the other hand, were still taking photos with smartphones. I'm curious to see who will play the role of a catalyst for tablets in Korea. If I have time later, I might briefly discuss why the tablet market isn't thriving in Korea.

What's certain is that small and medium-sized businesses lack the capacity to solve this, and Samsung and LG are already selling Android phones and Windows tablet PCs well, so they have no particular reason to push Android tablets. I wonder how Google will tackle the domestic market, or if it even has the will to do so.
