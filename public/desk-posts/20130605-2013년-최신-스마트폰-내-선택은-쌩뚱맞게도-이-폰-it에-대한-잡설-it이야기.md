Even though it's been over a year and there are countless reviews, I'd like to add one more about the Galaxy S3.

**1. I am a hardcore user, excluding games.**
First, I used the Galaxy S2 and tend to use it hardcore for mobile web, reading, and viewing content. The only thing I don't do much is "gaming." Aside from games, I use my smartphone for SNS including Facebook, Google+, and KakaoStory, write posts, listen to music, watch paid videos, and read e-books. I primarily use the Naver app for searching and KakaoTalk and Line for messaging. I also use my smartphone for navigation, and it helps me with company work, schedules, and contacts. Besides that, I dabble a bit in app development, turning on developer mode to study. It's not that I don't play games, it's more like I can't. I don't have much time... What I want to emphasize is that my smartphone dependency is very high, and I am a hardcore user who uses it more than anyone else.

**2. What about the Galaxy S3 LTE's performance?**
First, I'll tell you the Quadrant scores for the Galaxy S3 LTE. This is compiled from various sources, but you can consider it roughly accurate.
Galaxy S4: 12,500
Optimus G Pro: 11,800
Vega Iron: 11,500
Optimus G: 7,600
**Galaxy S3 LTE: 5,200**
Galaxy S2: 3,600
Galaxy S1: 978

The Quadrant score is only half that of the latest models. **But, numbers are just numbers.** A user's perceived performance doesn't always match the CPU or GPU numbers.

**1) Mobile web performance: Network makes the biggest difference**
**- S3's LTE upgrade is significant.**
When opening a mobile web page, for example, the actual performance difference is mostly due to network speed. The difference between Galaxy S2 3G and Galaxy S3 LTE is greater due to the 3G vs. LTE difference than the S2 vs. S3 difference. The problem is that higher-spec phones like the S4, G Pro, and Iron are all on par with the S3 LTE. The only difference is that the S4's 802.11ac makes the biggest difference in Wi-Fi. It will probably take about a year for APs to become widespread, and 802.11n is perfectly fine for two years, so I'll skip that.

**2) Smoothness of screen scrolling**
**- From S2 onwards, it's all pretty good.**
The Galaxy S1 and S2 had a performance difference of almost three times. This difference was quite significant because it was the difference between unnatural and natural scrolling. The gap between S2 and S3 clearly feels less significant compared to S1 and S2. The difference between S3 and S4 feels even smaller.

**3) 2GB RAM significantly improves freezing**
**- From S3 onwards, it's all the same.**
From the S2, which became smoother, freezing was actually more of an issue, and freezing is difficult to interpret solely by those numbers. Freezing is heavily influenced by RAM size, and from the Galaxy S3 onwards, all have 2GB. There's no difference. Compared to the S2, the Galaxy S3 definitely feels like it has less freezing.
Initially, the biggest frustration with the S2 was that its application installation area was only the size of the S3's RAM. Only 2GB of apps could be installed on the internal memory... Coming to the S3, there's a vast space of over 20GB... Wow.

**4) Camera performance?**
**- Pixel count isn't what matters. Superior to G Pro, Iron.**
This was the decisive reason I gave up on the G Pro and Iron. I won't be printing photos taken with my smartphone on billboards, so I prioritize sharpness much more than pixel count. The Galaxy S3 seems to outperform other high-megapixel smartphone cameras, excluding its family line, the S4.

Based on my research, the camera performance ranking is **S4 > S3 > G Pro > Vega Iron**. Simply put, it's Samsung > LG > Pantech. Ugh.
**Since I take a lot of photos of my child and upload them to social networks, I couldn't compromise on camera performance.**

**5) AMOLED performance?**
**- Clear AMOLED with deep blacks.**
I said the G Pro was good, but after using it for a while, the S3's AMOLED is also vibrant and good. If the G Pro was dazzling, the S3 is vibrant, I'd say. Especially, the black color looks deep, making the screen appear clear.
IPS is bright, in a word. AMOLED is clear. That's the feeling. I don't really notice color distortion or anything like that. They say using "Natural Screen" mode makes it similar, but once you use AMOLED, the slightly exaggerated and clear feeling is so good that I don't feel like touching that mode. They say white looks yellowish, but I've been staring at Naver's mobile page all day, and it doesn't turn yellow.

**6) Excellent updates**
**- S3's upgrade will include almost all S4 features.**
Just before I replaced my Galaxy S2 (a few days ago), it received an OTA firmware update. The bug-ridden HTC Flyer only got one update after I bought it. The Optimus Q got two? Actually, I've been satisfied with the Galaxy S2 since ICS, but continuous upgrades made me feel like I was being supported.
The Galaxy S3 is also confirmed for a 4.2.2 update, and I'm waiting for it specifically for the lock screen widget feature, which is the only thing I want on the S3. After that, I'm not really interested in updates, but this continuous after-sales support gave me ongoing confidence in bug fixes.

**3. The Ice Age and Price that Made All Latest Smartphones Obsolete**
However, the most crucial factor was the price of the Galaxy S3 LTE.
Due to the current "Ice Age" (economic downturn/stagnation), smartphones are incredibly expensive. The "bus phones" (cheap phones) that are released have specs like the "Prada phone," which is on the level of the Galaxy S2. While it's difficult to disclose the installment principal, you can actually buy the S3 LTE almost like a bus phone with a little effort. **The installment principal is at least 200,000 to 500,000 won cheaper than other latest smartphones.** Since I needed two, including my wife's, the calculation showed a difference of 400,000 to 1,000,000 won.
That's the biggest reason I chose the S3. I wondered if it was worth spending more than 300,000 won on a smartphone I'd only use for two years. **300,000 won over two years is 12,500 won per month, and for two phones, it's 25,000 won. That's enough money to buy our children a toy every month.**

**So... I bought the Galaxy S3.**
