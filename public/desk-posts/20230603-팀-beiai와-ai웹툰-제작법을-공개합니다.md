Hello. I am Yang Byeong-seok, CEO of Believer, and I also oversee Team BEIAI, which produces AI webtoons for [LOOOL](https://loool.io/). Team BEIAI is an AI webtoon content production team that directly creates and edits webtoons using Stable Diffusion, rather than focusing on AI technology development like software development. Unlike me, who majored in software development and had experience as a webtoon PD, the reason our team, composed entirely of webtoon majors, is creating AI webtoons is not because they can't draw.

**Production Environment**
We are using a custom-built PC from Yongsan, priced in the early 3 million won range, equipped with an RTX 4090, and a Wacom One. The RTX 4090 graphics card used by our artists is expensive, accounting for most of the PC's cost. We wanted to provide Cintiqs, but since Believer had many Wacom Ones purchased for webtoon education, we are using those devices. We'll earn money and buy you larger Cintiqs.

In addition to these devices, Believer has numerous small PCs with RTX 3090s in the server room, which are used for Believer's AI education platform and also for generating AI images. Video memory is crucial for AI webtoon production, so we recommend RTX 3090 and RTX 4090. Those who need to generate high-resolution images or train their own models should definitely keep this in mind.

**Production Method**
We plan to disclose more details in the future, but here's a brief introduction to our webtoon production process. There's already a lot of scattered information in communities, and YouTube has excellent usage methods that even we haven't fully mastered. However, not all of this information is optimized for webtoon production. We hope that more people can easily approach AI webtoon production by referring to our method, so we're briefly sharing the methods we've organized so far, hoping more people will try AI webtoon creation.

First, our production process assumes the existence of a visual storyboard for the webtoon. Currently, we are working with a scriptwriter for the text storyboard, and internally, we are proceeding with visual storyboard creation and subsequent tasks.

**Creating a Base Model Suitable for the Webtoon's Intended Atmosphere**
The biggest advantage of Stable Diffusion is that various models are publicly available. [https://civitai.com/](https://civitai.com/) is a representative AI model sharing site, where you can select models most similar to the webtoon you plan to create. You can choose one or select multiple and mix them. In our case, we wanted to create a unique model, so we mixed several models. The model created at this stage will be used as the base model for generating backgrounds and characters, thus determining the overall atmosphere of the work.

**Character Concept Creation via Prompts**
We create prompts based on the character features discussed within the team to define the character's appearance. It's especially important to capture the work's atmosphere and clearly represent the character's features.
ex) Prompt for Berry, a character from <If I Drink This, I'm Going to Space>

**Character DreamBooth Model Creation**
Once prompts that achieve the desired atmosphere are finalized, we create character faces from various angles, crop each character's face, upscale them to 512x512 images, and prepare these images for DreamBooth training.

{{IMG:2}}
                                
Normalized images used for creating the character Berry model

After inputting the 512x512 normalized images and defining the character's keywords, we generate the DreamBooth model. Detailed DreamBooth training methods can be found by searching on YouTube. When creating the DreamBooth model, the base model created in the previous step is used to maintain consistency. The created model is tested with various keywords. If, when creating DreamBooth character images, the DreamBooth model feels standalone or overfitted, we mix in more of the base model to fix the character model. Since this model will be used continuously, it's important to create it well.

**Character and Background Creation using ControlNet based on Storyboards, Photos, and 3D Models**
Currently, we work based on hand-drawn storyboards and do not generate characters and backgrounds simultaneously. Although it involves the hassle of post-processing, it's advantageous for division of labor and aligns with our approach to traditional webtoon production processes. What we mainly use here is OpenPose, either by taking photos to create poses or by using 3D characters to select poses and compositions, and then generating characters based on these. For backgrounds, we use SketchUp to establish the composition and then create backgrounds with img2img. The Stable Diffusion feature used here is ControlNet, which you can also find good explanations for by searching on YouTube. The utility of ControlNet is continuously growing, so it's good to master it.

**Panel and Speech Bubble Placement and Post-processing**
Panel and speech bubble placement is the same as in a typical webtoon production process. We add shadows to the composited images. Although we didn't work on the background and characters at once, the density of AI images is very high, so appropriate shadows and lighting must be added for the background and characters to blend naturally. Specifically, we correct oddly extracted parts or relatively emphasize important areas by applying Gaussian blur or reducing saturation on less important parts.

{{IMG:3}}

A characteristic of AI webtoons is that the overall density is very high, so it's crucial to use post-processing to reduce the emphasis on less important areas. This is the biggest difference from AI illustrations.

**Fixing Clothing**
While clothing can be somewhat fixed through LoRa training, LORA requires a relatively large amount of training data, so we haven't created LORA models yet. Currently, we've gone as far as fixing clothing color output using the Cutoff plugin. We plan to review the technology, thinking it might be convenient to collect the generated uniforms from serialized episodes and then create a LORA model from them.

**Our Biggest Current Concerns**
- **Establishing a system to increase the probability of desired image output** - Currently, we generate many images to extract suitable characters and backgrounds. To increase this probability, quality control of our self-generated models and prompt management by the artists seem crucial. Especially for team projects, sharing prompts is important, so managing them is necessary, and building a system to manage and reuse well-generated cuts and data seems vital.
- **Advancing the character and background production process** - Currently, because we generate characters and backgrounds separately and then combine them, there's a lot of post-processing work, and the high quality and naturalness, which are advantages of AI, seem to be diminished. Therefore, we plan to further consider ways to switch to a method that generates characters and backgrounds simultaneously.
