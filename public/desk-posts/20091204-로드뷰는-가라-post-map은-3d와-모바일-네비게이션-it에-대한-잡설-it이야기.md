약속 장소를 미리보는 용도 정도로 쓰는 정도 였습니다.
궁극적인 맵은 3D라고 생각하고 있었는데.. 이미 하고 있네요.

개인 정보 문제도 있겠지만.. 정말로 궁극의 맵이란 생각까지 들게 합니다.

While people in Korea were still enthusiastic about Google's Street View, which Google had launched a long time ago, Google and MS were already moving towards 3D.
[MS's Bing Maps is counterattacking Google with its update.](http://www.internetmap.kr/entry/MS-%EB%B9%99%EB%A7%B5Bing-Maps-%EB%8C%80%ED%8F%AD-%EC%97%85%EA%B7%B8%EB%A0%88%EC%9D%B4%EB%93%9C-%EC%97%AD%EC%8B%9C-%EC%B5%9C%EA%B3%A0%EC%9D%98-%EA%B8%B0%EC%88%A0)
Frankly, I wondered if Street View was truly meaningful compared to its content maintenance costs.
 It was mostly used for previewing meeting locations.
I thought the ultimate map would be 3D... and they're already doing it.
 
Naver Map offers a CCTV service.
 An overseas company called AVE combines CCTV data and projects it onto Google Earth.
While there might be privacy issues... it truly makes one think it's the ultimate map.
[http://www.internetmap.kr/550](http://www.internetmap.kr/550)
 

 
The biggest issues for maps are:
 
**1. Real-time capability**
**2. Information**
**3. User convenience**
**4. Privacy protection**
 
I think.
How will maps evolve in the future? While domestic maps are also developing significantly, Google and MS are truly astounding in terms of scale and capability.
Given that domestic map web services always benchmark overseas maps, it seems they will soon adopt 3D technology.
Ultimately, should domestic portals partner with solutions like iNavi's K3?
