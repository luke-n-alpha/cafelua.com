I don't know if I'll have time to write about it soon, but the types of scammers who sell fake success are similar.

1.  **They state the scale of success upfront (topic-first style)** - This grabs people's attention. They often exaggerate.
2.  **They claim they weren't even specialists and discovered a secret that requires little effort** - This lowers the barrier to entry to expand their target audience of gullible customers.
3.  **They claim to know many famous people** - They prove their value through their network rather than their own skills and achievements.
4.  **The content lacks substance** - They spout plausible nonsense that makes experts wonder what they're talking about, and most of their specialized knowledge is inaccurate. If they had such content in the first place, they wouldn't release it this way. Government-standard consulting fees are 30 and lecture fees are 50. The content isn't open, and per audience member, the unit price usually exceeds 10,000 won, so releasing it online would be a loss.
5.  **They claim sole credit for success** - Truly successful people don't say others succeeded because of them or that they have a secret. That's because they've experienced that such success doesn't exist. They only talk about external networks, not about the employees and colleagues who worked hard with them. Did those friends succeed too? Is it an exit if only the captain escapes?

Even just looking at content titles and lists, they're identifiable, but surprisingly, gullible people who dislike effort fall for it, and their numbers are touted as if they represent truth and power. This is the exact opposite strategy of how I built my social power. Based on my major, I wrote insights that a small number of experts could gain, focusing on the field I was creating. Thanks to this, I was an IT power blogger with the lowest views, but those who sought me out were major corporations struggling with economic management research and technology strategy. In the content field, I even delved deeply into specific content and worked as a PD for the nation's top webtoon company. Without depth and time, it's nothing. Watching some video about 'manga promotion' or something... I scoffed. They can't even distinguish between manga and webtoons... It reminded me of when I was in charge of PPS ads for a certain N webtoon...
