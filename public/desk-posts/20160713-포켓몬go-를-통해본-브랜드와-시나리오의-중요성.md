What exactly made these games different from Pokémon Go? I think there's only one difference: brand. Two brands are hidden behind this success: Google and Pokémon. Pokémon already has high brand recognition, to the extent that Pokémon bread was a social issue in the past. Since it's already familiar through animation, no separate marketing costs are incurred for scenario or character learning. Furthermore, the scenario that stimulates nurturing and collecting desires is a perfect fit for this type of game. Moreover, Google's Pokémon-related Easter egg was revealed in 2014, and it even became a topic for April Fool's Day.

And Niantic Labs, which created Pokémon Go, is a company that originated from Google.

**Google Spins Off Niantic Labs, Specializing in Augmented Reality Game Development** Just two days after announcing its organizational restructuring, Google announced the separation of its augmented reality unit, Niantic Labs, into an independent company. This means Niantic Labs will not be included in the new parent company, Alphabet. Alphabet is scheduled to officially launch at the end of this year and will consist of Google and other business units. A Google spokesperson stated that the spin-off would accelerate Niantic's growth and allow it to work more closely with investors and partners in the entertainment sector. www.itworld.co.kr

http://www.itworld.co.kr/news/95003

Therefore, the success of Pokémon Go can be seen not as a coincidence, but as an achievement brought about by the convergence of strong brands. Some people in Korea might wonder why we can't create a game like Pokémon Go. Before that, what I want to tell them is that we never created Pokémon. That's why it's difficult. If we were to make such a game using Pororo, which targets toddlers, it would 100% fail. Recently, someone who filed a lawsuit against 'Hurejasik' mentioned 'Pororo' and 'Dooly,' and in Korea, where games and comics are often considered solely for children, I believe it's still too early for such a foundation to be built. While I hope for the global success of Korean cultural content, I think this is an opportunity to reflect on whether our perception is still stuck in arcades and comic book stores for delinquent teenagers.

Let's also cultivate world-class IPs.

ps. I think Pokémon Go's AR success is like Avatar for 3D TV. It's highly likely to be the beginning and end of smartphone-based AR games. Drawing a parallel from past experience, the Tamagotchi craze didn't spawn successful successors. Among the same genre after Pokémon, only Digimon was a successful model, but it couldn't surpass Pokémon. And crucially, gamers don't like moving around. They don't have that much free time either. Ultimately, it raises a question mark regarding its sustainability.
