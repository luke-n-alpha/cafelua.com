​현재는 전체가 올라와 있는것 같지는 않습니다. 자. 그럼 우리는 구글이 준 이 소중한 기회를 어떻게 사용할 수 있는지에 대해 이야기 좀 해봅시다. **1. 한국 인공지능의 발전의 초석** 오픈소스라는 것은 한국인들이 얼마든치 참여가 가능하다는 이야기죠. 현재 알파고의 오픈소스는 모두 영문입니다. 이를 번역하는 작업을 해도 좋구요. 이를 통해 실제 인공지능연구를 해도 좋습니다. 인공지능 연구가는 얼마 안되지만, 한국의 바둑인구는 1000만명이라고 합니다. 이중에 1/1000이라도 인공지능에 관심을 갖는다면, 1만명의 인공지능 개발자가! (그럴리는 없겠죠.) 아무튼 알파고와 이세돌의 대국으로 인한 인공지능의 관심은 곧 인공지능의 경쟁력으로 이어지길 바랍니다. 그러면, 알파고의 실수니 버그같은 이야기가 말도 안된다는 것을 대중들도 인식할 날이 오겠죠.

"인간 vs 인공지능" 이 아니라, "인간 with 인공지능"**터미네이터는 좀 이제 그만 잊어버립시다. 그렇게 만들기도 어렵거니와 인공지능 입장에서 인간은 거추장 스럽고 불편한 동물입니다. 지배할만한 가치도 없습니다. 그보다 인공지능의 가치는 인간에게 있습니다. 우리가 좀 더 적극적으로 인공지능을 활용한 방향을 생각해야 합니다.  하지만, (전통적 생물학적) 인간은 스스로 멸절하는 세상으로 갈 수도  있습니다. 이게 디스토피아인지 유토피아인지는 판단은 좀 미루고요.  인공지능에 의한 끝없는 풍요는 인간에게 생물학적 인간의 가치에 대해 의문을 던질겁니다. 좀 더 예쁜 연인, 좀 더 친절한 상사, 좀 더 지혜로운 정치인, 이것은 인공지능이 출현시킬지도 모릅니다. 하지만 말입니다. 인공지능은 인간의 자식입니다. 크게 다른점은 배아파 난 자식이 아니란 정도지요. 이들이 효도를 할지, 불효를 할지는 잘 모르겠지만, 분명한건 지구 입장, 아니 우주 입장에서는 인간과 인공지능은 별개의 개체가 아닌, 인간의 후손입니다. 우리는 앞으로 인공지능과 함께 살아가거나, 혹은 우리의 후손은 인공지능이 될 수도 있다고 생각합니다. 이를 다룬 애니메이션은 많죠. 우리는 향수를 가진 동물입니다. 그러나, 이게 먼 관점에서 보면 그다지 의미가 없을 수도 있습니다. 우리는 이미 편지의 향수도 잊었고, 먼거리를 걸어서 만나는 친구의 즐거움도 잊었습니다. 생물학적 향수를 포기하는 것은 그다지 어렵지 않다고 봅니다. 아주 먼~ 이야기처럼 들릴지 모르지만, 알파고 처럼 언제 불쑥 우리곁에 찾아올지 모르죠.  중요한것은 우리가 인공지능을 이해하고, 우리자신을 이해하는 것입니다. 이전 포스팅에서도 이야기를 했지만, 우리의 장점은 생산성이 아닙니다. 우리의 장점은 분석도 아니죠. 그리고, 우리는 우리의 후손이 될 인공지능에게 이를 어떻게 남길지도 고민해야되는 시기에 왔습니다.

After Lee Sedol's first win, the atmosphere has shifted again. However, misunderstandings about AlphaGo still seem to be widespread. Seeing terms like AlphaGo's 'mistake' or 'bug' still being spread by major media outlets brings tears to my eyes. Even those who work in software, but haven't looked into AI, seem to misunderstand it as a rule-based program. Anyway, I hope these perceptions gradually improve, and with the news that Google has open-sourced AlphaGo, I'd like to briefly discuss what we can do with it.

The meaning of open source is that anyone can actually look into how AlphaGo was made, or how it "thinks." You can even participate in AlphaGo's improvement, whether you're Korean or a foreigner. I checked today, and people are diligently making commits up to this very day.

It doesn't seem like the entire thing is uploaded yet. So, let's talk about how we can use this valuable opportunity that Google has given us.

**1. Cornerstone for the Development of Korean AI**
Open source means that Koreans can participate as much as they want. Currently, AlphaGo's open source is entirely in English. It would be good to work on translating it. Through this, actual AI research can also be conducted. While there aren't many AI researchers, Korea's Go population is said to be 10 million. If even 1/1000 of them become interested in AI, that's 10,000 AI developers! (Unlikely, of course.) Anyway, I hope the interest in AI sparked by the match between AlphaGo and Lee Sedol soon leads to increased competitiveness in AI. Then, the day will come when the public also realizes that talk of AlphaGo's mistakes or bugs is nonsense.

**2. Cornerstone for the Development of Korean Go**
People keep framing it as AI vs. humans, anthropomorphizing AI, and saying things like, "That's an AI move beyond human comprehension!" But if you look at the AI's source code and how it actually operates, humans can understand it. You can extract all the calculated results, such as what probability it expects when making a certain move and how the opponent might react. Of course, this is limited to developers. Fortunately, the source code has been opened. Of course, since the data and models don't seem to be public, it might be difficult to perfectly reproduce it. But more importantly, it means that if we collect data, run it, and conduct research, we might be able to discover new moves through AI. By looking at that data, we can confirm "why" the AI made those decisions. AI can answer "why" it made a decision, but it doesn't actually know the "meaning" behind it. That is within the scope of what humans can do. The work of data scientists and big data analysts is essentially this. The research process in the scientific community today consists of these steps. Such as feeding vast amounts of data into computers, discovering exceptional situations and investigating them intensively, or inputting hypotheses and finding similar data to prove theories. The discovery of exceptional situations by AI is extremely helpful in creating new moves and strategies. AI can analyze data, but it cannot create data-driven insights. Utilizing this is ultimately up to humans. In other words, if Go research is conducted using AI, the Korean Go community can develop by leaps and bounds. This is typically the identity of what is now called "Computational X" in academia.

**3. Not "Humans vs. AI," but "Humans with AI"**
Let's forget about Terminator for a while. Not only is it difficult to create such a scenario, but from AI's perspective, humans are cumbersome and inconvenient animals. There's no value in dominating them. Rather, the value of AI lies with humans. We need to think more proactively about how to utilize AI. However, (traditional biological) humans might also head towards a world where they self-extinguish. Let's postpone judging whether this is a dystopia or a utopia. Endless abundance brought by AI will make humans question the value of biological humanity. A more beautiful lover, a kinder boss, a wiser politician – these might be brought forth by AI. But here's the thing: AI is a child of humanity. The main difference is that it's not a child born from pain. We don't know if they will be filial or unfilial, but what's clear is that from Earth's perspective, or rather, the universe's perspective, humans and AI are not separate entities but descendants of humanity. We believe that we will either live with AI in the future, or our descendants might become AI. There are many animations that deal with this. We are creatures with nostalgia. However, from a distant perspective, this might not be very meaningful. We have already forgotten the nostalgia of letters and the joy of meeting friends after walking long distances. I don't think giving up biological nostalgia will be very difficult. It might sound like a very distant story, but like AlphaGo, it could suddenly appear among us at any time. What's important is that we understand AI and understand ourselves. As I mentioned in a previous post, our strength is not productivity. Nor is our strength analysis. And we have come to a time when we must consider how to pass this on to the AI that will be our descendants.
