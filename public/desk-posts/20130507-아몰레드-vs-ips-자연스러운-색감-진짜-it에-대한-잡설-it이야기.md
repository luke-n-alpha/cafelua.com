Following up on the previous discussion, due to the ongoing debate about natural color reproduction, I decided to compare them with my own eyes.
The test subjects are Optimus G Pro (IPS) vs. Galaxy POP (Super AMOLED).
{{IMG:1}}
Please understand if the image quality is poor, as it was taken with a Galaxy S2. I'm uploading them as is, fearing that color correction might alter the colors again.
The reference monitor is an Apple monitor, and the indoor lighting is daylight-colored with a slight yellowish tint.
You can check the URL of the image at [http://elevatorslife.wo.tc/90109567677](http://elevatorslife.wo.tc/90109567677).
This is said to be a screen adjustment color bar drawn by Dream High.
First, to state the conclusion, from my subjective perspective, bright colors appeared similar to the Apple monitor. However, the darker the color, the more similar AMOLED appeared. Especially for blue, IPS colors looked significantly different from the Apple monitor compared to AMOLED. It seems there was a reason why Galaxy pushed blue colors.
Also, interestingly, while IPS, which people always say has whiter whites, clearly has white whites, the Apple monitor also appeared yellowish due to the indoor lighting, and AMOLED's yellowish white looked more similar to the Apple monitor. This could be interpreted as the IPS implemented in LG being superior to Apple's monitor in terms of white, or it could be described as excessively bright. When the color of a light-emitting object is bright, it's generally easier for it to maintain its inherent color. Therefore, there's definitely an aspect where IPS can excel in outdoor visibility. But is it truly close to the primary colors? Which of those three colors is the primary color? I wish we could stop this meaningless debate about primary colors. For me, who only used AMOLED, IPS was rather dazzling. It could even be described as causing eye pain. In reality, eye fatigue is caused by afterimages, incorrect focus, and excessive light intensity. Among these, the eyes adjust to light intensity over time. That's why we look at things in both dark and bright environments. I think it's unreasonable to consider AMOLED's high saturation as a cause of fatigue. Will we really go crazy just by looking at white or yellow?

ps. Still, people will probably bring up the burn-in issue and attack. *Sigh*... If I had to choose one escape, I'd pick IPS for smartphones due to its good outdoor visibility. However, indoors, it does feel a bit excessively bright. And honestly, I hope color e-ink develops. The human eye was not originally made to look at glowing objects, whether AMOLED or IPS. In fact, modern people are severely overworking their eyes. Will we ever evolve to see glowing objects well?
