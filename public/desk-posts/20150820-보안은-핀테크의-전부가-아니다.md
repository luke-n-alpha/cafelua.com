[**[Cover Story] FinTech Challenges Where Biometrics Are Also Breached** me2.do](http://me2.do/F7OInlSR)

They say fear is spreading, yet mobile sales are skyrocketing, and the [#ecommerce](http://plain.kakao.com/h/e%EC%BB%A4%EB%A8%B8%EC%8A%A4) market is growing. Can it truly be objectively proven how serious that fear actually is? Furthermore, where did the idea that biometrics are safe come from? A good security measure isn't one that can't be breached, but one that's okay even if it is. In the case of biometrics, it should only ever be a supplementary measure. Biometrics, which cannot be altered (except through plastic surgery), are not suitable for use as a security measure. Because [#biometric](http://plain.kakao.com/h/%EC%83%9D%EC%B2%B4%EC%9D%B8%EC%8B%9D) information is widely spread, there's always the possibility of it becoming a second, unchangeable resident registration number. Therefore, biometric information should be stored carefully and used only as a temporary or supplementary measure.

I cannot understand why this fact, which would be common sense to anyone truly involved in security, is being misrepresented when discussing [#FinTech](http://plain.kakao.com/h/%ED%95%80%ED%85%8C%ED%81%AC). It only makes me think this article has an ulterior motive. Perhaps they are more interested in the [#securitymarket](http://plain.kakao.com/h/%EB%B3%B4%EC%95%88%EC%8B%9C%EC%9E%A5) limited to clients, rather than FinTech itself.

Security is important in FinTech, but it cannot be everything, nor is it the core. The core of FinTech is data and services, and security is merely a part of the service.
