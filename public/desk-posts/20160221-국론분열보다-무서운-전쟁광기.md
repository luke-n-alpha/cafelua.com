이에 대한 대안으로 들고나온게 사드인데, 이는 중국을 자극했습니다. 대통령의 전승절 참여는 정말 잘했다고 생각한 일이었습니다. 지나치게 빠른 성장으로 중국의 경제가 휘청거리기도 했으나, 중국은 세계의 공장이며 세계에서 가장 큰 시장입니다. 세계적인 경제위기에도 한국이 그나마 괜찮았던것이 바로 중국시장에서 잘했기 때문이기도 합니다. 중국관련거래주는 폭등했고, 대한민국의 숨통을 트였습니다. 그런데, 사드가 한반도에 배치시 중국을 자극하게 됩니다. 사드는 일단 한국 방어용이 아니라는 건 확실히 아셨으면 합니다. 한미일의 군사동맹을 곤고히 하는데는 효과가 있을지언정 한반도 방어에는 별로 도움이 되지 않습니다. 오히려 한반도가 중국의 표적이 되고, 지정학적 리스크는 더 커집니다. 중국과 싸운다는 생각은 안하는게 I have many IT-related posts to write, but I decided to write this one first. This is because it has gone beyond worry and is now somewhat frightening. The deterioration of relations with North Korea, following its rocket launch and the closure of Kaesong Industrial Complex, has intertwined with THAAD deployment, leading to worsening Korea-China relations and even calls for South Korea to possess nuclear weapons. While it could be dismissed as the "North Wind" that periodically blew before general elections, this time it feels more serious. In such situations, we must remain calm and protect the peace and freedom safeguarded by democracy, and the market economy fostered by capitalism. However, looking at news comments, I feel a serious threat that war hysteria could jeopardize all of this.

**1. The Closure of Kaesong Industrial Complex is a Tug-of-War with a Broken Rope**
I believe inter-Korean relations have already crossed an irreversible line with the closure of Kaesong Industrial Complex. What differentiates this from previous "North Winds" is that it has cut off any retreat. While the strong measure of closing Kaesong Industrial Complex was taken to sanction North Korea, it ultimately severely damaged the credibility of South Korea's economic cooperation measures with the North. Compensation for affected companies was discussed, but it seems unlikely that many South Korean SMEs will trust government policy and engage in inter-Korean economic cooperation in the future. After all, companies involved in projects from Mount Kumgang to Kaesong Industrial Complex did not fare well. Even if the situation improves in the future, restarting these projects would require an incomparable investment of costs to get companies to move. Given South Korea's economic scale, this would incur much higher costs compared to North Korea. Furthermore, it seems we've used up one of the only cards we had to control North Korea by increasing its economic dependence on the South. It wasn't nuclear weapons that brought down the Soviet Union and East Germany, but money. The closure of Kaesong Industrial Complex caused significant damage to businesses, highlighted South Korea's geopolitical risks, led to a KOSPI crash, and even prompted Moody's to mention a credit rating downgrade. It is clear that increasing war threats will negatively impact the South Korean economy, which is already struggling with its key industries like shipbuilding, steel, and electronics.

**2. THAAD Deployment and Worsening Relations with China are Absolutely Unacceptable**
Secondly, THAAD, proposed as an alternative, has provoked China. I thought the President's participation in the Victory Day parade was a really good move. Although China's economy has faltered due to excessively rapid growth, China is the world's factory and the largest market in the world. One reason South Korea fared relatively well during the global economic crisis was its success in the Chinese market. China-related stocks surged, giving South Korea breathing room. However, if THAAD is deployed on the Korean Peninsula, it will provoke China. I want to make it clear that THAAD is not primarily for South Korean defense. While it may be effective in solidifying the military alliance between South Korea, the US, and Japan, it offers little help for the defense of the Korean Peninsula.
