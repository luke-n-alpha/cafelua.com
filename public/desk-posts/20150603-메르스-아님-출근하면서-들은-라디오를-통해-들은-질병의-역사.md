**1. A story about the history of disease heard on the radio on the way to work.**
- It was one of the three major enemies that threatened humanity and nations.
- Disease prevention (quarantine) represents medieval and modern states: Before the Middle Ages, the existence of viruses was unknown, so they were interpreted as vengeful spirits or divine wrath, leading politicians to self-reflect or be disciplined. It was the realm of religion, not medicine. In the case of Buyeo, the king was killed. (This seems to be due to the characteristics of Buyeo, which was a tribal state with relatively weak central power and a theocratic system.)
- Korea's most recent epidemic history: around the 3.1 Movement in the 19th century, 7 million infected, 150,000 dead (estimated).
: This is only an estimate due to severe media censorship by Japan, which feared political unrest at the time.
- At one point, there was a rosy outlook that diseases could be conquered, but now the understanding has spread that they can only be controlled, not conquered. This has increased the importance of disease prevention.
- During the initial control process, severe human rights violations occurred due to vague fears.

**2. Thoughts/Reflections**
- We must learn from history.
- Disease prevention is very important; it indicates a nation's level of development.
- Perhaps because of the actual recognition of the existence of viruses, people seem to be more at ease, mistakenly believing they already know enough.
- Politicians, the Korean disease prevention system, and even the public often remain stuck in medieval thinking. Perhaps such stories are heard more often in churches...

**3. Conclusion**
- I hope the government, medical community, and religious community all stay vigilant.
