**Q**: 가족끼리 외식을 갔는데 다섯살 된 아이가 떼를 써 아내가 휴대폰 게임을 주며 달랬습니다. 그다음부터는 부부끼리 조용하게 외식을 즐길 수 있어 좋았는데 계속 이래도 괜찮을까요? **A**:  [요약] 아이에게 무관심은 큰 스트레스가 됩니다. 무관심하면 스트레스가 되어 3분정도 지나면 의자에서 발버둥을치고 울음을터뜨립니다. 관심을가지고 놀아주세요. 스마트폰은 사회성을 떨어뜨립니다.스티브잡스는 아이들 가르칠때 디지털 기기를 쓰지 않았고, 실리콘밸리의 명문학교에서는 디지털 제로 교육방침을 씁니다. -> 결론, 스마트폰 주지 마세요. 스마트폰(게임)은 마약이에요.

**1. 먼저 식당에서 아이에게 관심을 가지고 놀아주라? 애는 키워봤나?**저도 아이키우는 부모로서 이게 뭔 병신같은 소리인가 싶어 글을 작성합니다. 먼저, 식당에서 아이들이 우는게 무관심 때문이라고요? 애는 키워보셨는지.. 애들이 힘들어 하는 이유는 재미가 없어서 놀이를 할 수가 없어서 입니다. 3분안에 밥먹고 나머지시간을 아이들과 놀아주면서 밥을 먹으라는건가요? 이게 가능한지 한번 해보라고 먼저 말씀드리고 싶네요. 식당이 아닌 평상시에 밥먹을때 저희 아들딸에게 노래도 틀고 불러주고, 심할때는 장난감도 한두개씩 올려주기도 합니다. 일반적으로 그렇게 30분을 놀며 밥을 먹이고, 이제 3분안에 제 밥을 먹습니다. 그러나, 이게 식당에서가능한가요? 식당에서노래도 불러주고 장난감도 올려줄까요? 불판에 고기가 타는데? 더군다나 장난감도 가끔맘에 안들면 던집니다. 인내심을 가지고 다시 올려주고, 장난감을 올려서 같이 놀아주는 것도 스마트폰을 주는것과 마찬가지로 식사시간이 항상 노는 시간인줄알게 되므로 가능한 안해주려고 합니다. 맘에 드는 장난감을 안가져다 줬다고 땡깡이 늘거든요. 먼저 애를 안키워봤다는 생각이 강하게 듭니다. 도대체 한국 식당에서 어떻게 아이와 30분에서 한시간까지 놀아줄수 있는지 오히려 정신과 의사분께 묻고 싶군요. 그래서 저희 부부는 가능한 놀이방 있는 식당으로 가서 2교대로 밥먹습니다. 이런 경우에만 가능해요.

**4. 식당에서 아이들 스마트폰 보여줘도 괜찮아**자. 그럼 모든 부모들이 듣고 싶어하는 결론과 제 생각을 말씀드리겠습니다.** 식당에서 아이들 스마트폰 보여줘도 괜찮습니다. 단, 아이에게 스마트폰을 보여주는 명확한 이유를 인지시켜야 하며** 안그러면 식당에만 오면 스마트폰을 보는게 권리인줄 압니다.  적당한 수준의 당근으로 사용하면 매우 좋은 결과를 가져온다는 것입니다. 저희 아이들에게는 식당에서 밥 잘 먹으면, 음식 가리지 않고 먹으면 스마트폰을 보여준다고 가르칩니다. 사탕과 초컬릿이 무조건 나쁘지 않은것 처럼 활용하기에 따라 매우 좋은 수단이 됩니다. 글고, **사탕도 불량식품이 있듯이 아이에게 보여주는 적절한 콘텐츠에 대한 관심을 가져야 합니다. **제 딸은 주로 타요와 뽀로로를 좋아하는데 라바에 대한 선호도도 높지만, 아내는 라바를 보여주는데는 반대합니다. 라바는 주로 슬랩스틱 개그를 소재로하고 있기 때문에 아이에게는 아직 나쁜 영향을 줄 가능성이 높다고 여기는것이죠. 저도 여기에는 어느정도 찬성하는 입장입니다.  또한 TV프로그램도 가능한 교육적인 요소가 많은것을 보여주려고 하며, 코스모스와 같은 다큐멘터리도 정기적으로 함께 봅니다. (사실 이건 제가 더 좋아함)  **디지털을 이용한 교육**도 하고 있습니다. code.org에 소개된 프로그램들을 통해 아직 한글도 모르는 딸 아이에게 경험을 시켜주기도 했고, 태블릿에서는 lightbot을 깔아놓고, 절차적 프로그램을 통해 로봇을 움직이는 퍼즐 게임을 시켜주고 있습니다. 아직 너무 어려서 난이도가 높은것에서는 짜증을 내지만요. 그렇게 보면 단순히 스마트폰이라서 좋아하는 것은 아닌게 분명합니다. 스마트폰은 껍데기일 뿐 안에 담긴 SW와 콘텐츠가 더 중요합니다.
**5. 함께하는 디지털은 아이를 경쟁력있게 만든다.** 디지털은 사실 아이들 교육에 엄청난 축복입니다. 여태껏 저희 조상들은 이렇게 글로벌 수준의 교육을 어디서나 언제든 꺼내볼수 있는 수단이 없었습니다. 전세계 우수 학자들이 만들어낸 어린이 교육 콘텐츠들을 경험해볼 수 있는 시대가 있었나요? 그리고, 아이들이 스스로 인터넷 검색을 통해 자료를 수집하고, 경험해보고 무언가를 만들어볼수 있습니다. 그리고 그런 아이들이 세상을 아주 빠르게 변화시킵니다.

﻿**Q**: We went out to eat as a family, and our five-year-old threw a tantrum, so my wife gave him a mobile game to calm him down. After that, my husband and I could enjoy our meal quietly, which was nice, but is it okay to keep doing this? **A**: [Summary] Indifference to a child causes great stress. If ignored, a child becomes stressed and after about 3 minutes, will struggle in their chair and burst into tears. Pay attention and play with them. Smartphones reduce social skills. Steve Jobs did not use digital devices when teaching his children, and prestigious schools in Silicon Valley use a digital-zero education policy. -> Conclusion, do not give them smartphones. Smartphones (games) are drugs.

Reading this reminds me of the Ministry of Health and Welfare's 'drug-addled' campaign I saw recently.

**1. First, pay attention to the child and play with them in the restaurant? Have you ever raised a child?**
As a parent raising children myself, I'm writing this because I find this advice utterly ridiculous. First, you're saying children cry in restaurants because of indifference? Have you ever raised a child? The reason children struggle is because they're bored and can't play. Are you suggesting we eat our meal in 3 minutes and spend the rest of the time playing with our children while eating? I'd like to tell you to try that yourself first to see if it's even possible. Normally, when eating at home, not in a restaurant, I play songs and sing to my son and daughter, and sometimes even put one or two toys on the table. Typically, we spend 30 minutes playing and feeding them, and then I eat my own meal in 3 minutes. But is this possible in a restaurant? Should I sing songs and put toys on the table in a restaurant? While the meat is burning on the grill? Moreover, sometimes they throw toys if they don't like them. I try not to do this (putting toys on the table and playing with them) as much as possible, just like giving them a smartphone, because it makes them think mealtime is always playtime. They'll throw more tantrums if I don't bring the toys they like. I strongly feel that they haven't raised children. I'd rather ask the psychiatrist how on earth one can play with a child for 30 minutes to an hour in a Korean restaurant. That's why my wife and I try to go to restaurants with playrooms and eat in shifts. It's only possible in such cases.

**2. Steve Jobs and Silicon Valley are digital-zero? What nonsense...**

**3. Unscientific Claims Advocated by the Psychiatric Community** The Korean psychiatric community still spouts nonsense about the frontal lobe, left brain, and right brain. Today's neuroscience tells us that the brain is not such a simple machine. The brain produces results through more complex interactions. I once asked a strange venture that claimed to maximize learning effects using some kind of wave: "Is this the result of accumulating data on a massive scale and extracting statistically significant results?" They said they were talking about consistency with data from dozens of people. Doing something with dozens of samples in the field of pattern recognition? The attempt is meaningful, but the level is too unclear to be recognized as science. Most of the claims advocated by today's psychiatric community fall into this category. They are not results obtained from securing thousands of people and using various control groups. On the contrary, TED even presents various results showing that games are beneficial to the brain. In fact, high-resolution digital is indistinguishable from analog. Analog fundamentalists believe the human brain is analog, but neural networks are fundamentally digital. Human vision is composed of cells equivalent to digital elements of about 100 million pixels. Furthermore, the role of each part of the brain differs from person to person and also varies with brain development. People don't just play games, or just exercise, or just meet their parents throughout their lives. Therefore, whether digital is harmful to humans varies greatly depending on the comparison group and the situation. It's no different from comparing a child who played games for 12 hours out of 24 hours a day with a child who didn't play games at all, and then claiming that games are bad for children. Between a child who played games for 1 hour and exercised for 1 hour a day, and a child who only studied for 2 hours a day, which one would have more brain activity? I'll leave that to your imagination. Gaming is generally a more focused brain activity than studying, and it uses different parts of the brain than when exercising. Playing games is likely to involve brain activity similar to studying. Simply from a neuroscience perspective, the former child is likely to have better results. Of course, the child's academic performance might be poor.
**4. It's Okay to Show Kids Smartphones at Restaurants** Now, I'll tell you the conclusion and my thoughts that all parents want to hear. **It's okay to show kids smartphones at restaurants. However, you must make the child aware of the clear reason for showing them the smartphone,** otherwise, they will think it's their right to watch a smartphone whenever they come to a restaurant. Using it as a moderate "carrot" can bring very good results. We teach our children that if they eat well at the restaurant and don't pick at their food, we will show them the smartphone. Just as candy and chocolate aren't inherently bad, they can be a very good tool depending on how they are used. Also, **just as there are unhealthy candies, you need to pay attention to the appropriate content you show your child.** My daughter mainly likes Tayo and Pororo, and her preference for Larva is also high, but my wife is against showing her Larva. She believes that since Larva primarily uses slapstick comedy, it is likely to have a negative impact on a child at this age. I somewhat agree with this. We also try to show TV programs with as many educational elements as possible, and we regularly watch documentaries like Cosmos together (actually, I like these more). We are also doing **education using digital tools.** Through programs introduced on code.org, I've given my daughter, who doesn't even know Korean yet, some experience, and on the tablet, I've installed Lightbot and let her play a puzzle game where she moves a robot through procedural programming. She still gets frustrated with higher difficulty levels because she's so young. From that perspective, it's clear that she doesn't just like it because it's a smartphone. The smartphone is just a shell; the software and content inside are more important.
