#### [킬러들의 수다](http://movie.naver.com/movie/bi/mi/review.nhn?code=31606)

감독 장진
제작 2001 한국, 0분
**평점**

기막힌 사내들, 간첩 리철진의 장진감독의 새영화 " 킬러들의 수다 "를 보구
몇자 적어봅니다.

먼저의 기막힌사내들과 간첩리철진으로 저에게 정말 "좋은감독" 이란
평가를 얻었던.. ^^; (저한테 얻는다구 머 달라지는건 없지만..)
장진감독은 역시 저의 기대를 져버리지 않았습니다.

장진감독의 스타일을 그대로 유지한체 약간의 블랙버스터같은 특수효과와
멋진음악 그리고, 르와느적(거의 한국최초인듯)인 분위기..
볼거리까지 덤으로 얹어준 영화였습니다.
"고양이를 부탁해"를 아직 저는 보지 않았지만,
두 영화가 많이 비교되더군요.
어쨌든 대강 들리는 이야기에 의하면 "고양이를 부탁해"는 지극히 현실적
이라는 이야기였습니다. 그래서 작품성에서 더 많은 점수를 받고 있는듯
합니다. 나중에 그걸 보면 또 평가를 내리겠지만..

우선, 킬러들의 감독은 전혀! 현실적이지 않습니다.
그러나, 또한 아주! 현실적입니다.
무슨말이냐면.. 이 영화는 한편의 동화같습니다.
동화는 사실상 아이들의 문학으로 많이 취급되어왔지만,
당시의 사회상에 문제점을 느낀 많은 어른들에 의해 동화라는 형식을
빌어 많은 작품들이 쓰여져왔다고 합니다. (저도 줏어들은거지만..)

이 영화의 "킬러!"라는 키워드는
동화적 존재입니다. 배트맨,슈퍼맨,심지어는 시민쾌걸까지.. 이 수많은
맨류의 일환이라는것입니다.
존재하지 않지만, 많은 사람들의 사람들의 내면에 엄연히 존재하고 있고,
그러나 겉으로는 표출되지 못해 누군가가 대신 해주기 바라는
대리만족을 느끼기위해 창조된 허구적 존재라는사실입니다..

무언가 잘못된 세상, 가끔은 죽이고싶도록 미운..
때론 약자가 고통받는 것을 일거에 해결해줄수 있는 사람들.
거기에 약간의 현대적 현실감으로 더해 생긴것이 이 영화의 킬러입니다.

그리고, 거기에 존재하는 케릭터들은 엄연히 현실감있는 우리들의 군상
이고, 거기에 환상적인 존재라는 킬러가 등장함으로서 그들의 현실감
이 무너지고 그 존재에 의해 현실의 진실을 깨닫게 됩니다.

어떻게보면 약간 영웅적인.. 그런 케릭으로도 볼수 있습니다.
하지만, 그것이 영웅으로 끝난다면, 값싼...(아니 아주 비싼가..)..
헐리웃 영웅만들기에서 끝났을겁니다.

장진감독은 킬러라는 허구적 존재에 우리들의 현실적인 사람의 옷을
입혔습니다. 그들은 슈퍼마켓에서 물건을 사며, 똑같은 밥을 먹고,
뉴스를 보며, 웃고.. 때로는 괴로워 합니다.

초반에 4명을 살해하는 장면에서 갑자기 경찰들이 들이닥쳐서
1명을 끌고 갑니다. 초반의 킬러들의 살해방법이 아주 리드미컬하고
드라마틱했다면, 갑자기 들이닥쳐 차유리를 깨고 소리지르는 경찰들의
체포방법은 아주 단순무식하고 갑자기 즐거운 정신을 번쩍들게 해줍니다.
허구->현실로 넘어가는 장면이라고 맘대로 해석했습니다. ^^;(양해를..)

극 전체는 사실은 사회전체를 또는 삶에 관해 질문을 하고있습니다.
사회전체의 부조리, 삶의 허무함과 아름다움..
" 누군가를 죽이고 싶어하는 사람들이 있으므로 우리가 있다. "
라는 첫번째 대사는..
결국은
검사의
" 내가 너희들을 먼저 죽일거야. 굶겨서 죽일거라구. "
라고 말하는 것으로 인해 주제는 거의 확실해 집니다.
전체적 구도는      킬러:검사 (4:1입니다.)
그 다섯명의 연기는 어느한쪽으로 치우치지 않아서 극 전반적으로
잘 배분된것 같습니다. 예전 가장 최근본 국내 모영화에서는
그점에서 실패해버렸져..

어쨌든 검사는 4명을 합친거보다 더 많은 생각을 합니다.
다분히 "어른"에 가까운 케릭터이고, 킬러쪽은 "아이"에 가깝기
때문이라고 생각합니다.

킬러들의 입장은 오히려 단순해서 좋습니다.
싫거나 나쁘면, 죽인다.
지만..
경찰의 입장은 많은것을 고려해야 합니다. 이것이 현실과 비현실의
차이죠. 그러나 너무 많은 것을 고려한 나머지 본질을 잊어버린것에
대한 비판입니다.
검사의 대사가 이런 말이 있습니다.
" 경찰은 나쁜짓을 하지말라고 부탁은 안해요. 잡는거지.. "
그러나, 이말을 하면서 검사는 속으로 무슨생각을 했을까요.
경찰이란 직분은 이사회를 깨끗하게.. 약자편에 서기위해 있는것이
아니었던가.. 라는 생각을 했을겁니다.
그리고, 검사가 그사람에게 가서 결국은 부탁이란걸 해봅니다.
(결국은 무지막지하게 두들겨 버리지만..)
검사가 킬러를 채포하는데신 어깨에 총탄을 쏠때 생각했던 것이
무엇이었을까요. 아이를 보고 웃는 킬러의 순수성을 볼때.. 그는
자신의 순수성을 의심하지 않았을까요..
경찰이란 직업을 선택했던 이유.
하고싶었는데 자신이 하지 못하고 있는일을 해내고 있는 킬러를
보고선 화가 치밀었을지도 모릅니다. 그것은 결국 자신에 대한
화였겠죠.
" 사람 쏘는거 기분 묘하더라. "
사실은 그것이 검사도 원했던 것이라는것을 나타내는 겁니다.
그리고, 위에서 말한대사
굶어죽이겠다.라는 선언은 킬러들에 대한 경찰 본연의 도전장이라고
생각합니다.

지금 우리 경찰은 어떤지 모르겠군요. ^^;
어쨌든 장진 감독은 위의 세 영화에서 말하듯이..
공권력의 불신은 확실한것 같습니다. 그리고, 그 아이러니..

우리의 세상은 어쩌면 진짜 킬러가 필요한지도 모르겠습니다.

킬러가 의뢰인과 같이 짝은 기념사진들의 얼굴은 우리자신의 얼굴
입니다. 물론, 그 첫번째 네명과 어이없게 웃으며 죽은 기막힌
사내는.. ^^; (역시 기가막히군여..갑자기 운수좋은날이라는
소설이 떠오르는 이유는.. 장진은 우리나라에서 역설이라는것을

초반의 네명은 극의 진행을 위해, 그리고 마지막은 공권력을
대신한 응징과 권선징악적 요소를 보여준거겠죠. ^^
그 외의 의뢰들은 다분히 서민적입니다. 오혜영의 주문 역시

간첩리철진에서 두 이데올로기가 그렇듯 허무하게 정의되었듯이..

#### [Guns & Talks](http://movie.naver.com/movie/bi/mi/review.nhn?code=31606)

Director Jang Jin
Production 2001 South Korea, 0 min
**Rating**

After watching "Guns & Talks," the new film by director Jang Jin, who also directed "The Foul King" and "The Spy," I'm writing a few words.

Director Jang Jin, who earned the evaluation of being a "good director" from me with his previous works "The Foul King" and "The Spy"... ^^; (though it doesn't change anything for him to get it from me...) once again did not disappoint my expectations.

Maintaining director Jang Jin's style, the film also offered blockbuster-like special effects, wonderful music, and a noir-esque (perhaps a first for Korea) atmosphere... it was a movie that delivered extra visual entertainment.
I haven't seen "Take Care of My Cat" yet, but the two films are often compared. Anyway, from what I've heard, "Take Care of My Cat" is said to be extremely realistic. That's why it seems to be getting higher scores for its artistic merit. I'll make my own judgment once I see it later...

First, "Guns & Talks" is absolutely not! realistic. However, it is also very! realistic.
What I mean is... this movie is like a fairy tale. Fairy tales have largely been treated as children's literature, but I've heard that many works were written in the form of fairy tales by adults who felt problems with the social conditions of their time. (I just heard this, though...)

The keyword "killer!" in this movie is a fairy-tale-like existence. Batman, Superman, even Citizen Quake... it's part of this multitude of "men" types.
They don't exist, but they clearly exist within the inner selves of many people, and yet, unable to be expressed outwardly, they are fictional beings created to provide vicarious satisfaction, hoping someone else will act on their behalf...

In a world where something is wrong, where people are sometimes hateful enough to want to kill... sometimes, people who can instantly resolve the suffering of the weak. Add a touch of modern realism to that, and you get the killers in this movie.

And the characters that exist there are clearly realistic representations of us, and with the appearance of the fantastical killers, their sense of reality crumbles, and they come to realize the truth of reality through these beings.

In a way, they can be seen as somewhat heroic characters. But if it ended with them just being heroes, it would have been a cheap... (no, perhaps very expensive?)... Hollywood hero-making.

Director Jang Jin dressed these fictional beings, the killers, in the clothes of realistic people like us. They buy groceries at the supermarket, eat the same meals, watch the news, laugh... and sometimes suffer.

In the opening scene where four people are killed, the police suddenly burst in and and drag one person away. If the killers' methods in the beginning were very rhythmic and dramatic, the police's arrest method—suddenly bursting in, smashing car windows, and shouting—was very crude and abruptly jolted the joyful spirit. I arbitrarily interpreted this as a scene transitioning from fiction to reality. ^^; (Please understand...)

The entire play, in fact, questions society as a whole or life itself. The absurdities of society, the emptiness and beauty of life...
The first line, "We exist because there are people who want to kill someone,"...
ultimately, with the prosecutor's line, "I'll kill you first. I'll starve you to death," the theme becomes almost certain.
The overall structure is Killers:Prosecutor (4:1). The acting of those five individuals didn't lean too heavily on one side, so it seemed well-distributed throughout the play. A certain domestic film I saw most recently failed in that aspect...

Anyway, the prosecutor thinks more than all four killers combined. I believe this is because he is a character closer to an "adult," while the killers are closer to "children."

The killers' stance is rather simple and good: if they dislike something or it's bad, they kill. But... the police's stance requires considering many things. This is the difference between reality and unreality. However, it's a critique of forgetting the essence by considering too much.
The prosecutor has a line: "The police don't ask people not to do bad things. They catch them..." But what was the prosecutor thinking inwardly while saying this? He must have thought, "Isn't the role of the police to clean up society... to stand on the side of the weak?"

And then, the prosecutor goes to that person and eventually tries to make a request. (Though he ends up brutally beating him...) What was the prosecutor thinking when he shot the killer in the shoulder instead of arresting him? Seeing the killer's innocence as he smiled at a child... didn't he question his own innocence?
The reason he chose the police profession. He might have been furious seeing the killer doing what he wanted to do but couldn't. That anger was ultimately directed at himself.
"It feels strange to shoot someone." This actually indicates that the prosecutor also wanted to do it.
And the line mentioned above, the declaration "I'll starve you to death," I believe, is the police's inherent challenge to the killers.

I wonder what our police are like now. ^^; Anyway, as director Jang Jin implies in the three films mentioned above... distrust of public authority seems certain. And the irony of it...

Perhaps our world truly needs killers.

The faces in the commemorative photos the killers took with their clients are our own faces. Of course, the first four and the absurdly smiling, dead "foul king"... ^^; (Indeed, it's amazing... I suddenly recall the novel "A Lucky Day"... Jang Jin might be one of the few directors in Korea who truly knows how to use paradox in film, don't you think?) The initial four were for the plot's progression, and the last one showed retribution on behalf of public authority and an element of poetic justice. ^^
Other requests were largely mundane. Oh Hye-young's request, though it was said to make the country noisy, was not political but actually the request of a scorned woman.
Just as the two ideologies were so futilely defined in "The Spy"... why the left hand, specifically? ^^; Was it because the client's heart wasn't cruel enough to deliberately request the less-used hand...?

Overall, it's like a fairy tale... ^^ so everything is forgiven... Saying, "This is impossible! (reality)" and then... everything is resolved by a TV character (fiction) named "Oh Hye-young"...

It seems like a movie with one foot in reality and the other in fantasy.

The most impressive scene I saw was... the opera house scene... wonderful music... and integrated visuals... a performer's voice can be so captivating. To be or not to be... that is the question. ^^;
And experimental visuals... the scene where the prosecutor investigates the house, shown with a split screen.
And the wit... funny lines, the humor of their innocence, witty reinterpretations of scenes that could have been tense. Cartoon-like situations... Director Jang Jin's sensibility is truly outstanding...

I was hugely disappointed after watching "Musa," but Korean cinema definitely has a bright future. This film once again clearly showed that imitation isn't always okay.

What I'm most curious about is how they resolved the last request... "Kill me with lung cancer."

The killers' contact method hardly appears in the movie. How did they get that phone number...? The bag ended up being meaningless. ^^ Just severe forgetfulness.
Perhaps if you write XYZ on the Seoul Station bulletin board, they might appear...? If not in Korea, you could go to Japan and write XYZ on the Shinjuku Station bulletin board, and you'll be able to meet a man. However, be careful of beautiful women in the Japanese version.
It's a bit regrettable that the opera scene is exactly as seen in City Hunter. (I suspect City Hunter might not be the original either... I don't know where the original source is.)

Why are they all B-type...? --; I heard B-types are hot-blooded, have severe mood swings, hate losing to others, and dislike following common sense... I'm also a B-type... --; With slightly curly hair... Should I become a killer too?

Thank you for reading this far.
