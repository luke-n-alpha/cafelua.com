**1. In fact, AlphaGo's victory was already foreseen**
I am affiliated with the Software Policy Research Institute. The launch of the Software Policy Research Institute was based on the title of a "software-centric society." We have continuously emphasized that software is changing everything in society (an ongoing process) and that preparation for this is urgently needed. However, many people in society spoke of it as if it were a story of the distant future. Before the AlphaGo match, when I asked many AI experts if they thought AlphaGo would win, if I paraphrase slightly, many responded like this: " **AlphaGo will probably win the next match for sure, but Lee Sedol might win this one. No, more than that, we are not yet ready to accept the shock of AI defeating Lee Sedol. We wish Go could be left untouched.** " In other words, most AI experts had predicted AlphaGo's victory. However, these experts are also human, and they spoke with a mix of hope, not wishing for AlphaGo to win given the current sentiment in Korea.

**2. Are We Truly Prepared?**
Let's turn our attention back to ourselves, not AlphaGo. Are we ready to embrace such artificial intelligence? The government has hastily begun software education and announced investments in AI. It's late, but welcome news. However, the state of software, especially AI, in Korea is very barren. While the results created by software and AI are astonishing, these changes did not happen overnight. Just as it took time for Silicon Valley to emerge and its culture to take root in the US. The reason companies were passive in investing in AI is that, like basic science in the software field, there are no immediate returns compared to the investment. It's uncertain when competitiveness will be achieved, and it requires a high level of skilled personnel and significant equipment investment. Fortunately, leading companies like Google are open-sourcing a lot, and related papers and data are abundant. In fact, if we consider AlphaGo alone, there's no reason Korean software developers couldn't create something similar if given enough time and capital. However, the important thing is that creating the exact same thing is useless. The application of AI in each field is a fusion product that requires the combination of understanding that field and AI technology. Some of the engineers who developed AlphaGo were very knowledgeable about Go and initially organized and taught the patterns and techniques of Go. This would be impossible without an understanding of Go. What about healthcare, as Google and IBM talk about? Naturally, doctors must work together. Until now, we have watched the changes brought about by software as if they were a distant spectacle. However, we had not fully realized that software could bring about transformation in education, all industries, and right where we are now. But I believe the shock of AlphaGo made us feel that.

**3. The Path Forward**
I anticipate that there might be an overheating of software education and significant short-term investment in AI. Large investments in an unprepared situation will undoubtedly cause side effects. Even for software education alone, there is a shortage of people, resources, and awareness. What about AI? Companies that claimed to do AI have already been sold off or failed. The few remaining individuals seem to be moving to companies as data scientists, earning high salaries due to the recent rise of big data. Along with the advancement of software professionals, there is an urgent need for public awareness of software and innovative education. Rote learning aimed at getting better scores has revealed its limitations. Instead of competing with others using software, we must now learn how to live with software, and rather than expecting immediate results from short-term massive investment, we must foster a **"software culture"** together through long-term, consistent investment.
