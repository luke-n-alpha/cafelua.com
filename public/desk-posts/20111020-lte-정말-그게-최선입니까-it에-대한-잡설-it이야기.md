TV를 잘 보지는 않지만 며칠전에 개콘을 보기전에 TV에서 LTE단말 광고를 하더군요. 그리고 그 광고이후 또 다른 LTE단말. 연속해서 한 서너개의 광고가 LTE였습니다. (우왕~ 뭐냐 난 이미 갤s2 노예란 말이다.) 도대체 LTE가 보여주는 멋진 세상은 뭘까요? 그 기술의 가치가 무엇일까요? 그리고 LTE가 정말 경쟁력이 될까요? 먼저 LTE가 4g인가? 하는 이야기부터 하겠습니다. 사실 이몇세대를 규정하는 국제기구에서는 기술을 세대로 나눌때 그 기준이 있습니다. 그리고 그 기준은 어떤 목적 하에 세워지게 되죠. 그중 2g는 기존의 아날로그 방식에서 디지털방식으로 바뀌어 데이터 통신이 가능해지고, wap기반의 인터넷이 목적이었다면, 그 이후 3g는 웹처럼 이미지에서 동영상전송이 가능한 세대를 의미합니다. WAP에서 WEB으로 넘어가는 시대라고 보시면 됩니다. 그리고, 4g는? hd기반의 동영상스트리밍이 끊김 없이 돌아가고 QoS가 가능한 시스템을 규정합니다.  기본적으로 이 세대라는 것은 rich한 인터넷환경을 타겟으로 하고 있습니다. 그래서 4g규격은 본래 hd스트리밍이 가능한 속도, 곧 100mbps를 규정하고 있었습니다.

그런 중에 아이폰이 대박을 치고, 스마트폰의 확산이 된 시점에서, 갑자기 3.9세대 pre 4g라 불리던 기술인 lte가 2010년 12월 IMT에서 4g로 규정되버립니다. 사실 이 규정된 사실 조차 뜬금없다는 생각이 듭니다. 덕분에 이미 5년전인, 2007년에 10월에 3g 표준 기술로 체택이 된 wibro가 4g 대열에 들어갔습니다. 아무튼 앞뒤가 안맞지만 lte에 4g가 들어갔습니다. 아무튼 이 이상한 4g lte 에 대한 공격을 좀 해볼까 합니다.

**3) QoS(통제)가 가능한 기술 필요** 3g의 또하나의 문제는 데이터 폭증 으로 인해 음성통화까지 안되는 상황이 발생 한다는 겁니다.  이는 음성과 데이터를 분리할 수 없기 때문인데, 4g에서는 QoS라는 기술이 들어가 있어서 컨트롤이 가능합니다. 특정 유저나 데이터에게는 더 많이 할당할 수 있고, 우선순위를 줄 수 있습니다. 반대로 말하면 이동통신사에 이익이 안되면서 트래픽만 먹는 포털과 톡, voip의 품질을 컨트롤 할 수 있다는 의미죠. 기술적으로 컨트롤 할 수 있다지. 컨트롤 한다 라던가, 컨트롤 할거다라는 확신을 가지고 이야기를 한 것은 아님을 미리 밝힙니다.

I don't watch much TV, but a few days ago, before watching Gag Concert, I saw an LTE device commercial on TV. And after that commercial, another LTE device ad. Continuously, about three or four commercials were for LTE. (Wow~ What is this? I'm already a slave to the Galaxy S2!) What exactly is this wonderful world that LTE promises? What is the value of this technology? And will LTE truly be competitive? First, let's talk about whether LTE is 4G. In fact, international organizations that define these generations have criteria for classifying technologies into generations. And these criteria are established under a certain purpose. Among them, 2G changed from the existing analog method to a digital method, enabling data communication, and its purpose was WAP-based internet. Then, 3G refers to a generation where image and video transmission, like the web, became possible. You can think of it as the era transitioning from WAP to WEB. And 4G? It defines a system where HD-based video streaming runs without interruption and QoS is possible. Basically, this "generation" targets a rich internet environment. So, the 4G standard originally stipulated a speed capable of HD streaming, which was 100Mbps.

Meanwhile, as the iPhone became a huge hit and smartphones spread, LTE, a technology previously called 3.9G pre-4G, was suddenly defined as 4G by IMT in December 2010. Honestly, even this classification feels out of the blue. Thanks to this, WiBro, which was adopted as a 3G standard technology back in October 2007, five years prior, also entered the 4G lineup. Anyway, it doesn't quite make sense, but LTE got the 4G label. I'd like to launch a bit of an attack on this strange 4G LTE.

**1. Is 4G really made to be used as intended?**

So, let's go back to the beginning. I mentioned that 4G is a system aimed at multimedia use. However, unlike 3G, unlimited data plans have been abolished, and instead, to put a brake on excessive users, there are restrictions on using video and streaming with certain "safety plans." In other words, 4G is 4G, but it explicitly reveals that it's made for web surfing. (Which was originally 3G's purpose.)

**2. Have prices really gotten cheaper?**

Although unlimited data plans were abolished, they claimed speeds increased and prices became cheaper, so I looked into the plans. Although I'm busy these days and can't use much data, I typically use about 2GB for streaming Naver Music and other occasional uses. In conclusion, moving to LTE means I'd have to switch to a 62,000 won plan. With the Galaxy Note and Galaxy Nexus having larger WXGA-grade screens, data traffic usage is likely to increase. Resolution implies width, and width correlates with traffic. Ultimately, the 62,000 won plan becomes the standard, not the 55,000 won plan. In fact, it's a 7,000 won increase.

**3. Can you really use fast LTE?**

I'm currently using a Galaxy S2, which supports HSPA+. And the Galaxy S2 has become the most popular phone after the Galaxy S. However, I've never connected to HSPA+. I don't understand why they advertised HSPA+. What about LTE? For reference, even WiBro, which has nationwide coverage, receives many complaints due to shadow areas.

**4. LTE, reflecting the mobile carriers' concerns**

Anyway, I'd like to offer a half-certain speculation about why LTE emerged. In fact, all the causes stem from the explosion of 3G data and the dwindling SMS and voice call revenues.

**1) A practical price increase to cover investment costs**
Despite the explosion of 3G data, traditional revenue sources like SMS and voice calls are decreasing due to talk apps and VoIP. Carriers can't operate at a loss, and network investment costs are skyrocketing, so a new alternative is needed. That's where 4G comes in, as a practical way to increase prices.

**2) Abolition of the "chicken rib" unlimited data plan**
The unlimited data plan was created due to the rivalry between SKT and KT. I was actually against the 3G unlimited data plan because I anticipated that mobile carriers would face this situation. Ultimately, from the carriers' perspective, it's a means to reverse their policy mistake of creating unlimited data plans.

**3) Need for technology capable of QoS (control)**
Another problem with 3G is that data surges can lead to voice calls not working. This is because voice and data cannot be separated. In 4G, QoS technology is incorporated, allowing for control. More bandwidth can be allocated to specific users or data, and priority can be given. Conversely, it means carriers can control the quality of portals, talk apps, and VoIP, which consume traffic without generating profit for them. I'm stating that it's *technically possible* to control, not that they *will* control or *are certain* to control.

**5. Will LTE really solve the carriers' problems?**

The question is, will LTE solve these problems? I'm rather pessimistic. LTE is clearly several times faster than HSPA and is a technology capable of QoS. However, the capacity for simultaneous connections to a single base station is only double. What is the biggest problem with current 3G mobile communication? It's simultaneous connections. Ultimately, the biggest complaint from current users isn't that 3G is slow, but that it's unstable. The biggest issue here isn't the overall volume of traffic, but the number of sessions. A certain domestic company predicted that traffic would increase by nearly 1000 times by 2015. If this prediction is true, LTE can only cover 1/10 of that. Even if it's not 1000 times, recent signals from various sources suggest that a several-fold increase within two years is a reality. And the struggling LTE certainly won't be able to cover that range.

**6. Instead, advanced 3G technologies, WiBro, 3W, are gaining attention again**

**1) WiBro is more realistic**
Here, KT has brought up WiBro, which, as I mentioned earlier, somehow became 4G, as an alternative again. I find this option more realistic. WiBro is fundamentally an extended wireless LAN technology. While it may not completely solve the issue of simultaneous connections, it is practically usable everywhere more than LTE, and its price is much lower than LTE. 3W, encompassing WiBro, wireless-LAN, 3G, and 4G, also offers more leeway in actual capacity handling than 4G-only. KT is also expanding its use through dongles rather than requiring additional device purchases, so users of other mobile carriers can also use it. It's also more suitable for N-screen devices like pads, not just mobile terminals. The generous 30GB capacity provided by KT also implies that there is ample room.

**2) Could it be an opportunity to attract users from other carriers who feel deprived by LTE?**
If KT were to charge users from other mobile carriers to subscribe to WiBro, what would be a reasonable price? Personally, I think they could charge at least 7,000 won, which is the standard LTE plan of 62,000 won minus the 3G unlimited plan of 55,000 won. This would mean that SKT 3G unlimited users could use 4G from KT for 62,000 won. If KT manages this situation well, it could be a pretty good opportunity for them. Many Galaxy S2 users might feel frustrated by the launch of LTE. I think LTE might actually be providing an opportunity for its competitor, WiBro.
