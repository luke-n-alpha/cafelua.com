Samsung Electronics' biggest news seems to be the Galaxy Note 8-inch. As a Wacom enthusiast, I welcome the appearance of the Galaxy Note, but from a service and product perspective, the Galaxy Note 8-inch is a very disappointing product at this point. ** 1. Galaxy Note 8 hasn't moved an inch beyond the Flyer**

[HTC-P515E (Flyer)](http://shopping.naver.com/detail/detail.nhn?nv_mid=5732015281)

Brand HTC
1.5GHz, 1GB, 7-inch wide, 32GB, USB x 1, Android 2.3

[View Review](http://shopping.naver.com/detail/detail.nhn?nv_mid=5732015281&tc=6)

** I am using a 7-inch HTC Flyer. Although its specs are slightly above Galaxy 1st gen, making it slow and frustrating, it is almost identical to the Galaxy Note in terms of size and features. Instead of Wacom, it uses a pen called Scribe, and the 7-inch size isn't much different from 8 inches.**
** 2. Responding to iPad Mini? The weakness is Google's ecosystem lacking tablet apps**
Ultimately, it strongly feels like a product designed merely to match the iPad Mini. However, the problem is that the Android ecosystem currently lacks decent tablet apps. It's a situation where a virtuous cycle cannot be created, either due to a lack of hardware adoption or a lack of apps. The reason the iPad Mini was significant is that the iPad already had a considerably independent app ecosystem, which is a very different situation.

If Samsung Electronics' goal is to counter the iPad Mini, then I think Google or Samsung itself needs to have a tablet app strategy. My prediction is that if a response initiated with hardware is not ultimately concluded with software, countering the iPad Mini will not be easy.

Although it was the first to enter the 7-inch market, I suspect that the absence of software allowed the iPad Mini to easily capture the 7-inch market. In other words, what Samsung Apps needs to do is increase the number of apps in this market. (Even if it costs money...)
** 3. Differentiation as a productivity tool requires services, not just a pen.**
The biggest feature of the Galaxy Note 8 is, of course, its pen. Especially with a larger screen, the advantages of the pen can be fully utilized. But can this market be dominated by the pen alone? Ultimately, I believe differentiation can only be achieved if it's a complete package, including productivity services. Representative cases would be something like Evernote or Google Docs. Are the built-in notes or Polaris Office on the Galaxy at that level? Evernote does not offer differentiation from the iPad.

Looking at Naver Works service recently, it's evolving into a personal work tool by bundling mail, calendar, and address book. What Samsung needs to have, I believe, is this kind of business service leadership. Currently, the S Pen seems to have little meaning beyond a client-side notepad. If Samsung's Galaxy Note series is positioned as a productivity tool, it will inevitably clash with Windows tablets. What advantages or distinguishing features should Samsung's tablet have then? The lack of consideration for this makes it a disappointing product.
ps. I also briefly considered buying it because it's a portable Wacom tablet... but I skipped it primarily because of the camera. The camera was too weak to use as a phone replacement. Ultimately, it can only be a productivity tool or a secondary device, and considering devices like the Surface, it seems to have too few advantages. Ultimately, differentiation seems to be determined by the services one can experience, not just the device itself.
