1. 한국  vs 해외 메타버스의 차이

그러면서 새삼 느낀 건데, Hubs의 해외 메타버스와 국내의 메타버스 사용 유형이 같으면서 참 달랐다는 생각을 했습니다. ​** 1) 공통점. 펜데믹의 행사용의 활용** 먼저 눈에 띈건 WebXR Conference 같은 행사에서 적극적으로 활용되었거나 대학들이 제작한 공간들이 많았습니다. 아무래도 팬데믹 기간에 실질적으로 온라인 행사에 적극적으로 활용되었던것 같습니다. 빌리버 역시 현대백화점 그룹, 전남대학교의 행사를 치루며 이 프로젝트가 시작되었습니다.​

** 2) 다른점.  3D웹 메타버스의 미진한 활용과 정부주도의 3D게임 메타버스 활용**

다음은 한국은 3D기반의 웹메타버스 활용이 미진했다는 점입니다. 물론 여기에는 저희 같은 스타트업이 제때 기술도 보여주고 마켓팅도 해주고 했어야 했는데, 저희 잘못도 매우 크다고 생각합니다. (잘해야 잘 봐줬겠죠) 한국의 경우 메타버스를 쓴다고 많이들 말했지만, 대규모 행사에서는 Zoom, 게더타운. 그리고 2D웹 기반인 Zap이 사용되었고, 제페토나 이프랜드가 좀 곁들여지는 정도였습니다. 오히려 자본이 집중 되었던 게임 엔진기반 메타버스가 제대로된 대규모 행사를 치룬걸 본적은 없는 것 같습니다.  그리고 자료를 정리하면서 가장 크게 느껴졌던 부분은 Hubs에는 개인과 팀 아티스트들이 많아 3D공간 자체를 가상 예술 콘텐츠로 만든 공간이 많았다는 점입니다. 아무래도 WebXR이다보니 VR HMD를 쓰고 경험하게 되는 몰입감이 달랐기 때문인것 같습니다. 이는 한국에는 거의 없는 콘텐츠 부류입니다. 한국의 미디어 아티스트들이 메타버스를 활용한 경우가 거의 없었고, 펜대믹 기간에도 정부의 지원이 주로 기업을 중심으로 이루어졌기 때문에 현실을 보조하는 콘텐츠로서 현실을 3D게임엔진기반으로 옮겨 놓으려고 했던 관광메타버스 같은 것들이었던것 같습니다.​

** 3) 결론. 수요주도의 메타버스가 필요하며, 한국 아티스트도 보고 싶다.**

그런데 한국이던 해외던 여기서 제가 하고 싶은 말은 딱 하나입니다. 수요기반의 메타버스가 필요하다는 것입니다. 가장 제가 눈여겨 본건 해외의 아티스트들은 스스로가 수요였던것 같고, 앞으로도 이 분야는 가능성이 있습니다.  한국의 메타버스 정책도 기업이 아닌 아티스트들에 대한 지원이 있었으면 어떨까 싶습니다. 경기콘텐츠진흥원에서 진행하는 VR아트행사들도 보면 대부분 해외 작품으로 한국 작품은 거의 전멸입니다. 그 중 프로젝트를 정리하던  중 멋지게 본 독일의 프로젝트를 공유합니다. ​**2. Regarding the Hubs data, it was something I had an intern work on for about a week, and I hadn't looked at the data in detail until now. I'd like to share some thoughts I've gathered from reviewing it item by item.

1. Difference between Korean vs. Overseas Metaverse

As I did so, I was struck by how similar yet distinctly different the usage patterns of Hubs' overseas metaverse and domestic metaverse were. ** 1) Commonality: Use for Pandemic Events** What first caught my eye was the prevalence of spaces actively used in events like WebXR Conferences or created by universities. It seems they were actively utilized for online events during the pandemic. Believer also started this project while hosting events for Hyundai Department Store Group and Chonnam National University.

** 2) Difference: Underutilization of 3D Web Metaverse and Government-led 3D Game Metaverse Utilization**

Next, in Korea, the utilization of 3D-based web metaverses was lackluster. Of course, a large part of the blame lies with us, startups like ours, for not showcasing our technology and marketing it effectively in a timely manner. (They would have paid more attention if we had done better, right?) In Korea, many talked about using the metaverse, but for large-scale events, Zoom, Gather.town, and 2D web-based Zap were used, with Zepeto or Ifland only occasionally added. In fact, I haven't seen game engine-based metaverses, where capital was concentrated, successfully host proper large-scale events. And what struck me most while organizing the data was that Hubs had many individual and team artists who created 3D spaces as virtual art content. This is likely because, as WebXR, the immersion experienced with a VR HMD was different. This type of content is almost non-existent in Korea. Korean media artists rarely utilized the metaverse, and during the pandemic, government support was primarily focused on corporations, leading to content like 'tourism metaverses' that attempted to transfer reality into 3D game engine-based environments as supplementary content.

** 3) Conclusion: Demand-driven Metaverse is needed, and I want to see Korean artists.**

However, whether in Korea or overseas, there's one thing I want to say here: a demand-driven metaverse is needed. What I observed most closely was that overseas artists seemed to be the demand themselves, and this field continues to have potential. I wonder what it would be like if Korea's metaverse policy also supported artists, not just corporations. Looking at the VR art events hosted by the Gyeonggi Content Agency, most are international works, with Korean works almost completely absent.                             **Das HOUSE 2.0**

Introduction to Das HOUSE 2.0 at Kepler's Gardens. After two years of continuous shutdowns, public life has come to a halt. Cafes are closed, and mysterious avatars hide around every corner. They might tell you stories if you approach them carefully. Only the theater seems to be bustling with activity. It's filled with live music, an opening night atmosphere, and peculiar characters, and rumors circulate that someone has been recreating an exact replica of Notre Dame on stage for years. What's happening in this digital intermediate world? What is real, and what is virtual? There are also rumors that it's connected to a lost house and the story of its vanished inhabitants. Is it time to unravel its secrets? Das HOUSE 0.2 presents records, notes, and memories of M., one of the former residents of the house. M. disappeared after entering the house. His fiancée, A., tries to find meaning in this archive of loss, searching for clues or hints to M.'s whereabouts. minuseins' Das HOUSE is an invitation to a mysterious and atmospheric intermediate world. This project will begin with an experiential teaser and public test runs, followed by a dramatic VR series. The core question of this project is: How do online communities function? How is digital co-existence possible? What is lost in the process? Aura, presence, linear narratives? What new things emerge instead? The possibility of impossible spatial combinations, the abolition of death, a theater that transcends natural laws – and can we move beyond this eternal and tedious causality? Finally, how do modern stories work on the World Wide Web? In addition to planning and preparing for Das HOUSE 0.2, the team is responsible for the artistic design of approximately 30 Ars Electronica VR spaces and the technical setup of Mozilla Hubs.
