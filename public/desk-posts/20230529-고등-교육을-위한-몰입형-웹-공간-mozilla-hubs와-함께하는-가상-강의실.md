Hello, everyone! Today, I'd like to introduce a project that is revolutionizing higher education through amazing virtual worlds. This project is a virtual classroom utilizing Mozilla Hubs, led by Professor Gary Burnett, an engineering professor at the University of Nottingham.
**Education in Virtual Worlds**
Professor Gary provides his students with the opportunity to immerse themselves in computer-generated worlds. This allows students to actually experience and understand human-centered design issues in VR and AR. This virtual classroom, 'Nottopia,' has been visited by approximately 300 undergraduate and graduate students over the past three years, who have learned through interactive lectures, tutorials, and seminars in this space.
**Utilizing Mozilla Hubs**
Nottopia runs on Mozilla Hubs and has been used as a central virtual world for interaction with students. Professor Gary recorded and provided lectures to students in Nottopia, and also explored ways to lecture students in real-time as avatars. Furthermore, he leveraged the social power of Mozilla Hubs' VR platform to engage students in new and appealing ways through various activities such as treasure hunts, discussions with experts, design and empathy workshops, and virtual field trips.
**Educational Environment Built with Hubs**
Over the past three years, Professor Gary has built an educational environment in Hubs that can serve various roles. Among them, three features he is particularly proud of are:
- Wheelchair Avatar: Created an avatar that can be used on desktop or in VR, allowing users to be positioned close to the ground.
- Nottopia: Created doors within the main virtual world that lead to 360-degree images/videos. This helps contextualize learning.
- Tutor Tent/Secret Cylinder: Provided a virtual space where students can find professors anywhere. In this space, sound does not travel in or out of the tent, allowing students to have private conversations.
**Experience with Hubs**
When Professor Gary first started using Hubs, he was largely unaware of how virtual world design elements affected performance. However, by watching Hubs videos and reading posts on the Discord channel, he gained a better understanding of the importance of scene optimization, considering polygon count, texture size, file size, and more.
**Features Desired in Hubs**
Professor Gary states that the following features, in particular, would help support students even better if added to Hubs:
- Improved room capacity: Many university courses have over 100 students, and splitting them into multiple sessions or live-streaming to a lobby is not ideal.
- Improved/magical whiteboard app: Would be really useful for many sessions when used with sticky notes that can enter the world!
- Ability to access the keyboard without leaving the room in VR: Typing is a very useful and natural activity for students, but not in a headset.
- Avatar facial tracking: Communication is certainly more efficient between headset users and desktop users, but there are still missing cues that can limit student learning.
- Integrated avatar creation: It would be much more helpful to integrate avatar creation into the settings rather than asking students to go to a separate 3rd party app/website.
**Data and Research**
Professor Gary treats this project as a research project and has collected a significant amount of data from students. This data provides important insights into students' experiences, and academic papers continue to be published based on it.
**In Closing**
Professor Gary's innovative efforts demonstrate new possibilities for the use of VR in higher education. His work helps students gain a deep understanding of how to design, build, immerse, and interact in virtual environments. This will be crucial for students' future careers.
This journey with Mozilla Hubs is providing students with new learning experiences and playing an important role in expanding the possibilities of education. It is very exciting to watch how education is changing through these innovative methods.
Professor Gary's efforts go beyond simply creating virtual worlds; they are revolutionizing the very way education is delivered. His virtual classroom allows students to experience technologies that would be difficult to access in the real world, which helps them understand and solve complex problems in the real world.
Furthermore, Professor Gary is not only providing students with new learning experiences through Hubs. He is exploring various ways to engage them and stimulate their creativity. This shows that virtual worlds can be active spaces that foster learning and creativity, not just tools for delivering information.
Finally, Professor Gary's work provides us with insight into how virtual worlds can shape the future of education. His research offers a deep understanding of how VR can be utilized in higher education and what impact this can have on students' learning experiences and future careers.
All of this has been made possible through Professor Gary's efforts and the power of Mozilla Hubs. It is very exciting to watch how education is changing through these innovative methods. We hope that such innovations continue, and that we all take the lead in actively embracing these changes and exploring new ways of learning.
Professor Gary continues to explore and research in this area.
