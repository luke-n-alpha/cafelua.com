**그림. 가트너 글로벌 스마트폰 마켓 점유율** [http://www.tech-thoughts.net/2012/07/global-smartphone-market-share-trends.html#.U2pPVPl_vwo](http://www.tech-thoughts.net/2012/07/global-smartphone-market-share-trends.html#.U2pPVPl_vwo)  이후, 구글이 오픈 소스 기반의 안드로이드 OS를 출시하고, 제조사와 이동통신사와의 연합군 구도를 만들어 애플의 아이폰을 추월하는데 성공하여 모바일 OS에서는 안드로이드와 아이폰의 iOS의 양강 구도가 형성 되었다. 그러나, 기존의 데스크탑 OS의 강자였던 마이크로소프트의 윈도우8은 여전히 모바일 시장에서 뚜렷한 성공을 보여주고 있지 못하고 있다. **II. 데스크탑에서 다시 펼쳐지는 OS대전**앞서 이야기한 10.1인치의 아이패드는 스티브잡스의 예언대로 큰 성장을 하였고, 노트북과 데스크탑 PC시장은 크게 위축되었다.여기에 모바일 OS인 안드로이드 역시 태블릿들이 출시되면서, 전통적인 핸드폰의 역할이었던 통화의 욕구가 낮은 7인치 이상의 큰 화면의 모바일 기기가 큰 폭으로 성장하였다.그림. 가트너 2014년 기기별 판매 예측 {{IMG:2}} Gartner estimates that 2.5 billion devices will be sold in 2014, with Android devices accounting for 42 percent of sales. [http://www.idownloadblog.com/2013/07/17/apple-samsung-duopoly-to-continue/](http://www.idownloadblog.com/2013/07/17/apple-samsung-duopoly-to-continue/) 태블릿은 데스크탑의 영역이었던 집 안에서의 게임, 영화, 음악 등의 엔터테인먼트 영역에서 환영을 받았으며, 의료, 교육 분야에서도 점차 널리 사용되고 있다.애플의 아이패드 대비 늦게 시작한 안드로이드 태블릿의 경우, 활용 가능한 앱들의 규모가 크지 않아 큰 확산은 이루지 못하였으나, 오픈 소스로서 무료라는 장점과 낮은 수준의 컨트롤이 가능한 장점으로 인하여, 임베디드 윈도우가 사용되던 특수 목적이나 기업용 솔루션으로도 주목 받고 있다. 최근은 이를 넘어 전통적인 PC 처럼 키보드를 갖춘 노트북 형태의 기기가 출시되거나, MS오피스나 한글용 파일이 편집까지 가능한 오피스 앱들이 출시되어, 모바일 OS를 탑제한 기기들이 엔터테인먼트 도구를 넘어 점차 생산성 도구로서의 PC의 역할까지 침범하기 시작하였고, 모바일 OS의 성장은 데스크탑 OS의 역할 마저 넘보고 있다. **III.  변화된 OS 패러다임**기존의 데스크탑 OS시장은 경쟁이 완료되어 안정화된 시장이었으나, 새로 열린 모바일 OS 시장은 새로운 기술과 비즈니스모델의 경쟁을 통해 진일보 한 면이 있다. 그 중 가장 큰 특징은 OS를 한번 팔면 끝나는 유료 패키지 상품에서 고객과의 접점을 지속적으로 확보하여 서비스 이용의 대가를 받는 형태의 서비스의 일부분으로 OS의 가치의 변화 이다. 데스크탑에서는 OS 자체로도 어플리케이션을 구동하는 환경이 되는 작은 플랫폼이었으나, 모바일 OS에서는 OS벤더에 의해 서비스, 하드웨어, 개발 플랫폼까지 포함한 수직통합으로 만들어진 더 큰 플랫폼에서 제공하는 일부의 서비스 상품으로 바뀌었기 때문이다. 아래 표는 필자가 보다 상세하게 OS의 패러다임 변화를 비교한 내용 이다. **표. 변화된 모바일 OS시장의 패러다임**

위에서 언급한 OS의 가치 외에 각각의 상세 구분을 살펴보자면, 설치 방법은 기기에 포함되어 하드웨어와 라이프사이클을 같이 하게 되어 이로서 하드웨어 플랫폼과 함께 수직통합을 이루었고, 최종 사용자에게 OS도 만질 수 있는 어떤 것이라는 느낌이 강해졌다소프트웨어 비용은 제조사 입장에서는 일부 유료인 경우가 있으나, 사용자가 체감하는 가격은 무료이다. 이는 OS의 가치를 OS의 상품성보다는 플랫폼 경쟁력 차원에서 OS를 바라보고 있기 때문이다. 구글의 경우 오픈소스로, 마이크로소프트의 경우, 9인치 이하 무료OS를 내세워 제조사에게도 무료로 제공하고 있다. 업데이트 비용이 무료로 바뀐 것은 OS가 패키지에서 서비스상품의 성격이 강해짐에 따라 지속적인 업데이트를 통하여 고객접점의 지속적인 확보가 주요 화두로 떠올랐기 때문이다. 프리로드 애플리케이션의 경우, 플랫폼 경쟁시 사용자에게 위해 과거에 유료 소프트웨어에 준하는 소프트웨어를 무료로 프리로드 하는 사례가 생기고 있고, 플랫폼 벤더에 의하여 특정 분야의 소프트웨어 시장이 파괴되는 끼워팔기 논란의 여지는 데스크탑 시절과 마찬가지로 남아있다. 데스크탑에서 어플리케이션을 개발하기 위해서는 다양한 개발 플랫폼의 이용이 가능했으나, 모바일에서는 OS별로 주요 언어, 개발 플랫폼 의존적(iOS는 Object C, 안드로이드는 JAVA)으로 되어 있어, 개발자 인력 시장에도 영향을 주고 있는데 앱의 공급자 측면의 시장을 염두해 둔 플랫폼 전략이다..비즈니스모델이 소프트웨어 유통 수수료로 바뀌면서 iTunes나 구글 플레이 같은 소프트웨어 마켓이 OS와 플랫폼의 핵심기능이 되었다. 또한 이러한 응용 소프트웨어(앱)를 운용하고 개발하기 위한 각종 자원을 클라우드로 제공하고, 개발사에게 유료 과금을 하게 되었다. 이는 하드웨어의 시장보다 소프트웨어의 시장에서 비즈니스모델을 만들어내고 있는 것으로, 기기와 OS는 소비자의 접점의 역할을 하면서 실제 비즈니스는 소프트웨어에서 일어나고 있음을 의미한다. 일례로 아마존의 경우, 온라인 쇼핑의 유통 수수료를 비즈니스모델로 하면서, 쇼핑을 위한 기기와 소프트웨어를 매우 낮은 가격 혹은 무상으로 제공 하고 있다.소프트웨어를 유통하며 얻는 비즈니스모델을 취하고 있으므로, 소프트웨어 유통의 경우 소프트웨어 유통은 주요 비즈니스 모델로 떠오름에 따라 OS벤더의 통제에 두려는 경향이 강해졌으나, 과거 닫힌 마켓 이라고 이야기 되었던 이동통신사의 마켓과는 달리 검색, 추천 등에 관여하지 않음 으로서 플랫폼의 중립성을 지키면서, 이로 인해 높은 보안성과 일관된 사용성을 함께 획득하게 되었다. **IV. 4. 시사점****1) OS의 경쟁력은 플랫폼 경쟁력으로 이관.** OS는 좁은 범위의 기술 플랫폼이다. 그러나 기존 데스크탑 OS 시장에서의 OS는 최종 소비자와 생산자를 이어주는 역할을 적극적으로 있지는 않았기에 양면시장의 플랫폼이라 말하기에는 어려운 면이 있었다.그러나 모바일 시대 이후, 구글, 애플과 같은 OS벤더들은 각종 서비스와 정책, 하드웨어, 개발 플랫폼(개발자), 사용자까지 포함한 넓은 범위의 수직 플랫폼으로 만들었으며, 그 중 가장 핵심 서비스로서 OS를 선택하고 있어, 본격적인 플랫폼으로서의 OS상품을 만들어내고 있다. 따라서 OS시장을 바라 보는 데는 OS자체로서의 상품성과 성능 보다는 밀접하게 결합된 다른 서비스들을 포함한 플랫폼 시장으로서 보는 관점과 전략이 더 중요하게 되었다. 이미 대중화에 실패한 OS들은 OS자체의 결함이나 완성도의 문제가 아니었던 점이 이를 증명하고 있으며, 데스크탑 OS의 강자인 마이크로소프트의 윈도우8의 전략 역시 모바일 OS의 특징들을 따라 변화 하고 있다. **2) 한국의 OS전략들도 플랫폼 관점의 전략이 중요하다.**OS시장을 보는 관점은 이제 플랫폼의 경쟁력에 대한 관점이 매우 중요하게 되었으며, 새로운 OS를 만든다는 것은 새로운 플랫폼, 곧 자체적인 생태계를 구축한다는 것을 의미하게 되었다. 한국에서 직접 만드는 것으로 논의 중인 삼성의 타이젠과 정부의 한국형OS도 이러한 관점으로 살펴볼 필요가 있다.**

**I. Current Status of Desktop and Mobile OS Markets**
When thinking of the OS battle of the century, one recalls the clash between Apple and Microsoft: MAC vs. Windows. Apple, which opened the personal computer market with Apple computers, ceded dominance to Microsoft in the OS market battle. However, in the subsequent mobile OS battle, Apple's iPhone achieved great success, overwhelming existing smartphone OSs like Symbian and Windows Mobile. Furthermore, Apple released the 9.7-inch iPad, predicting the end of the traditional PC era, and once again made statements conscious of Microsoft's desktop OS, Windows.

Subsequently, Google launched the open-source Android OS and succeeded in surpassing Apple's iPhone by forming an alliance with manufacturers and mobile carriers, establishing a duopoly of Android and Apple's iOS in the mobile OS market. However, Microsoft's Windows 8, a strong contender in the traditional desktop OS market, has yet to show significant success in the mobile market.

**II. The OS War Unfolds Again on Desktops**
The 10.1-inch iPad, as mentioned earlier, grew significantly as Steve Jobs predicted, and the laptop and desktop PC markets greatly contracted. With the release of Android tablets, mobile devices with large screens of 7 inches or more, where the traditional phone function of making calls is less desired, also grew substantially.

Gartner estimates that 2.5 billion devices will be sold in 2014, with Android devices accounting for 42 percent of sales. [http://www.idownloadblog.com/2013/07/17/apple-samsung-duopoly-to-continue/](http://www.idownloadblog.com/2013/07/17/apple-samsung-duopoly-to-continue/)

Tablets were welcomed in entertainment areas such as games, movies, and music within the home, which were traditionally desktop domains, and are gradually becoming widely used in medical and educational fields. Android tablets, which started later than Apple's iPad, did not achieve widespread adoption due to the limited number of available apps. However, thanks to their advantages as open-source and free, and the ability for low-level control, they are gaining attention for special purposes or enterprise solutions where embedded Windows was previously used.

More recently, devices in laptop form factors with keyboards, like traditional PCs, have been released, and office apps capable of editing MS Office or Hangeul files have emerged. As a result, devices equipped with mobile OSs have begun to encroach upon the role of PCs as productivity tools, moving beyond mere entertainment devices, and the growth of mobile OSs is even eyeing the role of desktop OSs.

**III. Changed OS Paradigm**
The traditional desktop OS market was a stable market where competition had concluded, but the newly opened mobile OS market has advanced through competition in new technologies and business models. The most significant feature among these is the change in the value of the OS: from a paid packaged product that was sold once and then done, to a part of a service that continuously maintains customer contact and receives payment for service usage. On desktops, the OS itself was a small platform that provided an environment for running applications, but in mobile OSs, it has transformed into a partial service product offered within a larger, vertically integrated platform created by the OS vendor, encompassing services, hardware, and development platforms.

The table below provides a more detailed comparison of the OS paradigm shift by the author.

**Table. Changed Paradigm of the Mobile OS Market**

| **Category** | **Old Desktop OS Market** | **New Mobile OS Market** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Value of OS** | Independent software product for running a computer | One of the core services for using a platform-equipped device |
| **Installation Method** | Separate software installation on hardware | Included with the device |
| **Software Cost** | Paid | Free |
| **Update Cost** | Paid | Free |
| **Representative OS** | Windows, OSX, Linux | Android, iOS, Windows |
| **Preloaded Applications** | Fair trade, bundling controversy e.g., IE | Bundling, enhancing platform product value e.g., Safari, YouTube, MS Office |
| **Development Platform** | Multiple development platforms | Reliance on a single development platform defined by the vendor |
| **Business Model** | Sale of paid packaged software | Software distribution fees, cloud service usage fees |
| **Application Distribution** | Fully open | OS vendor controlled |
| **Security** | Relatively high risk | Relatively low risk |

In addition to the value of the OS mentioned above, let's look at the details of each distinction. The installation method is included in the device, sharing its lifecycle with the hardware, thereby achieving vertical integration with the hardware platform. This has strengthened the feeling among end-users that the OS is something tangible. While software costs may be partially paid by manufacturers, the price perceived by users is free. This is because the value of the OS is viewed in terms of platform competitiveness rather than its marketability as a product. Google offers it as open source, and Microsoft provides free OS for devices under 9 inches to manufacturers. The shift to free update costs is due to the OS evolving from a packaged product to a service, making continuous customer engagement through ongoing updates a key priority. In the case of preloaded applications, to gain platform competitiveness, software equivalent to what was previously paid is now being preloaded for free, leading to controversies over "tying" and the destruction of specific software markets by platform vendors, similar to the desktop era. While various development platforms were available for application development on desktops, mobile development is OS-dependent (iOS uses Objective-C, Android uses Java), impacting the developer talent market. This is a platform strategy that considers the app supplier side of the market. As the business model shifted to software distribution fees, software marketplaces like iTunes and Google Play became core features of the OS and platform. Furthermore, various resources for operating and developing these applications (apps) are provided via the cloud, with developers being charged fees. This indicates that business models are being created in the software market rather than the hardware market, meaning devices and OS act as consumer touchpoints while actual business occurs in software. For example, Amazon's business model is based on online shopping distribution fees, and it provides devices and software for shopping at very low prices or for free. As the business model involves software distribution, software distribution has emerged as a major business model, leading to a strong tendency for OS vendors to control it. However, unlike the "closed markets" of mobile carriers in the past, platform neutrality is maintained by not interfering with search or recommendations, thereby achieving both high security and consistent usability.

**IV. 4. Implications**
**1) OS competitiveness shifts to platform competitiveness.**
The OS is a narrow technical platform. However, in the existing desktop OS market, the OS did not actively connect end-consumers and producers, making it difficult to call it a two-sided market platform.
However, since the mobile era, OS vendors like Google and Apple have created broad vertical platforms that include various services, policies, hardware, development platforms (developers), and users. They have chosen the OS as the most core service, thereby creating OS products that function as full-fledged platforms. Therefore, when viewing the OS market, a perspective and strategy that considers the platform market, including other closely integrated services, has become more important than the OS's inherent product quality and performance. The fact that OSs that failed to gain widespread adoption did so not due to flaws or incompleteness of the OS itself proves this, and even Microsoft's Windows 8 strategy, from a desktop OS powerhouse, is evolving to follow the characteristics of mobile OSs.

**2) Korean OS strategies also need a platform perspective.**
The perspective on the OS market has now made platform competitiveness extremely important, and creating a new OS has come to mean creating a new platform, that is, building its own ecosystem. Samsung's Tizen, which is being discussed for direct development in Korea, and the government's Korean OS also need to be examined from this perspective.

**- Samsung's Tizen needs to be more active in ecosystem creation.**
Samsung's Tizen, recently adopted as the platform for the Galaxy Gear, differentiated itself from existing mobile OSs by choosing HTML5, a highly compatible and standard platform, as its technical platform, which was an appropriate choice from a developer environment perspective. However, looking at Tizen from this platform perspective, regardless of the OS's inherent performance or advantages, its ecosystem (device market size, quantity and quality of apps, developer ecosystem) has not yet achieved economies of scale, meaning the ecosystem cannot be said to be functioning smoothly. Currently, it seems more likely that Samsung is using Tizen as a counter-Android measure rather than making a full-fledged push into the platform market, and for Samsung, which currently maintains a good relationship with Google, this seems like an appropriate level of response. However, to pursue innovation as a hardware company and grow into a software company, which will become increasingly important, active investment and a decisive push will be inevitable.

**- Risks of government's Korean OS: lack of economies of scale and service platform.**
The recently discussed government's Korean OS should be viewed from the perspective of creating a new platform ecosystem, rather than merely choosing Linux as an alternative to Windows. From this perspective, two major concerns arise.
The first concern is the scale of the platform ecosystem. A market size of hundreds of thousands for government agencies is very large when viewed as a standalone software supply market, but it is too small for a standalone platform ecosystem. While there is a movement in the Chinese government to adopt Linux as a government standard, the situation in Korea is different from China, where the government agency market alone is comparable to the national scale. Amazon's strategy of modifying the already large Android to create its own platform, rather than developing a purely proprietary OS, is also a platform strategy aimed at minimizing the costs of building and operating an ecosystem and quickly securing competitiveness.
Therefore, for the Korean OS to function as a platform ecosystem, openness and compatibility with other already scaled platforms are the most crucial factors. Linux has a disconnect with the general user market that the Korean OS aims for, meaning very large additional costs would be required to scale this market, or the risk of failure is high.
The second factor of concern for the Korean OS is that the main competitiveness of a platform comes from the completeness and differentiation of its service platform. Tizen already has several service platforms, including Samsung Apps, but the Korean OS currently only has discussions about creating an OS, with no discussion about what services will be included. Furthermore, the costs for operating and improving a service platform are much larger and more important than the launch costs, but in the case of a one-off government-led project that only delivers an OS, the success probability of a service platform is very low.
Therefore, to summarize, the government's Korean OS must not only possess the completeness and competitiveness of the OS itself but also build scale to function as a platform and achieve competitiveness in its service platform. To do this, it must secure platform compatibility that aligns with the landscape of the private OS platform market, and simultaneously consider and pursue development and operation plans to achieve service competitiveness to activate the platform.
