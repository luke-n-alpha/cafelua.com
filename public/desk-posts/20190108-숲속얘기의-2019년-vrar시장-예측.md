**1. The Decline of Space Businesses, the Rise of Large-Scale Operations, and the Growing Importance of Narrative**
VR space operators believe that individual spaces are meaningless without creating a narrative for them. Therefore, large-scale and story-driven approaches are crucial for space businesses. For this reason, I've even suggested VR webtoons as pre-experience scenarios for each [space]... (but it hasn't happened yet). Investment in IP for animated comics for individual content is necessary. The experience is doubled simply by how well the story is laid out beforehand. Tokyo VR, transplanted from Hyundai, does this well because the Japanese public has an empathy for stories. We're not targeting otaku, so naturally, this doesn't appeal to Koreans, especially the younger generation, who aren't familiar with these stories. ㅜㅜ If each space doesn't have its own story, there's no reason to go there. Looking for a date spot isn't about killing time; it's about an experience that can only be felt there. It's even better if it can deepen a romantic relationship or allow children to play safely, and what we should benchmark isn't Tokyo's VR, but Carrie and Friends Cafe (캐카). The experience without wearing an HMD is more important, we need to create a justification for wearing an HMD, and when an HMD is worn, the relationships of those who went together should improve.

**2. Small and Medium-Sized Space Businesses Need Standalone Devices, More Solo Content, and Environmental Improvements**
For small and medium-sized VR space operators, space efficiency is more important, much like PC bangs and cafes. Therefore, wired models like HTC Vive or Odyssey, which take up space and are difficult to maintain, face challenges. Wireless standalone VR will become the mainstream for such spaces. And the content in these spaces will increasingly focus on 'solo-viewing' content, and as safety becomes more important, there's a high likelihood of demand for spaces with 'independence and safety'. Perhaps it will change more towards partitioned spaces like Japan's net cafes. The problem is that this is illegal under domestic law. The form of the space is similar to accommodation businesses... so legal amendments might be necessary.

**3. The Full-Fledged Start of Social AR/VR**
**1) Offline: Video-Passthrough AR Social Experiences**
The demand for both privacy and connection is expected to increase. This is a social element that comes with the expansion of devices, and in certain spaces, AR content where friends are visible through video-passthrough seems to be key. Especially for space operators, collaborative content using video-passthrough will be important. Glass-type AR is still too far off to create proper content.... Even HTC Vive is difficult to operate, so HoloLens or Magic Leap are impossible to operate in commercial spaces. I'd like to dissuade anyone planning to do business with Magic Leap or HoloLens. I'd bet that those who write proposals with these have never actually used the devices. (Better to just watch 'Memories of the Alhambra'.)

**2) Online: The Social VR Platform War**
In online spaces, social VR, like solutions from Oksusu or Salin, is expected to become mainstream. The entity with the most ambition to turn this into a platform is Facebook (Oculus Home). In fact, if this is lost, platform leadership can be considered lost... it will be a game of scale. And HW dissemination is very important, and what will drive this won't be interactive content, but high-quality lean-back content (video/animation/webtoon). Currently, there are no noticeable players in social VR that can threaten or defend against Oculus, and in video, YouTube, where mobile and PC power is directly transferred, is dominant. Most domestic telecom companies seem only interested in space businesses and stores, and if there's any hope, it's that we still have a chance in video, and high-resolution video is definitely worth pursuing based on 5G. (For reference, in mobile, HW providers centered around store platforms, and all other significant platforms were social platforms.)

These are my thoughts.
Whatever the case, the growth of VR platforms will continue to increase the importance of experiences and stories that can only be felt in VR. ComicsV's position focuses on stories and visuals that can only be experienced in VR. Simply porting PC/mobile IP and stories doesn't satisfy the expectations for the IP, so there's a desire for optimized or new stories that can be experienced in VR, and this experience will encourage continuous visits. That's ComicsV's position, and I hope this year is better...
