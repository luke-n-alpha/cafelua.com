**2. The burden of proof for product defect lies with the seller.** Therefore, the key issue here is the proof of defect. In fact, according to the Act on Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, etc. (abbreviated: Electronic Commerce Act), even a simple change of mind must be refunded within 7 business days. However, according to Article 18, Paragraphs 9 and 10, the shipping fee for a simple change of mind is the buyer's responsibility, while in the case of a defect, it is the seller's responsibility.http://www.law.go.kr/lsInfoP.do?lsiSeq=140566&efYd=20131129#0000[National Law Information CenterReliable Ministry of Government Legislation National Law Information Centerwww.law.go.kr](http://www.law.go.kr/lsInfoP.do?lsiSeq=140566&efYd=20131129#0000){{IMG:3}} In the case of a defect, or more precisely, if the product differs from its description, according to Article 15, Paragraph 3, if the product differs from the content posted online, the subscription can be withdrawn within 3 months from the date of supply, or within 30 days from the date the fact was known. And, in the event of a dispute between the seller and the buyer, the burden of proof lies with the seller, according to Article 15, Paragraph 5. Thus, the problem narrows down to whether there is truly a "defect," and the burden of proof for that lies with the "seller." Article 18 has a system called "late payment compensation" (지연배상금).
A mail-order seller (including a person who has received payment for goods, etc., from a consumer or has entered into a contract for mail-order sales with a consumer; hereinafter the same applies to paragraphs (2) through (10)) **shall refund the already received payment for goods, etc., within 3 business days from the date of receiving the returned goods, etc.** In this case, if the mail-order seller delays the refund of the payment for goods, etc., to the consumer, **they shall pay late interest (hereinafter referred to as "late payment compensation") calculated by multiplying the delay period by an interest rate determined by Presidential Decree, considering economic circumstances such as the overdue interest rate applied by banks under the [Banking Act](http://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSc.do?p_OC=DRF&p_history=C&p_lawname=%EC%A0%84%EC%9E%90%EC%83%81%EA%B1%B0%EB%9E%98+%EB%93%B1%EC%97%90%EC%84%9C%EC%9D%98+%EC%86%8C%EB%B9%84%EC%9E%90%EB%B3%B4%ED%98%B8%EC%97%90+%EA%B4%80%ED%95%9C+%EB%B2%95%EB%A5%A0&p_kword=&p_joname=&p_spubno=&p_epubno=&p_spubdt=&p_epubdt=&p_srchgb=L&p_jogb=&p_jono=#AJAX), within the range of 40/100 per annum.**
