**1. Innovation isn't about specs; it's about solving problems and changing our lives.**
Talking about innovation again, it's getting a bit tiresome now. After discussing whether Samsung and Apple are innovative or not, this time I want to talk about a South Korean startup that I believe embodies innovation. While iPhones and Galaxies are machines that cost nearly 1 million won, the device I'm about to introduce costs only 5 dollars, approximately 5,000 won. However, innovation is not determined by price. And personally, I don't think it's determined by sales volume or profit either. Innovation lies in solving our lives' problems and the challenges we face. The reason I thought the iPhone was innovative was because it allowed me to know where I was on the street, send URLs to friends from my hand, and immediately look up and solve problems when I encountered something I didn't know while out. Since the iPhone 4 and Galaxy 2, it's been a competition of specs and completeness, so I believe innovation has long since ended.

**2. We will put an end to the missing child problem. Reversal, a Korean startup challenging the world.**
First, let me show you a table.
{{IMG:1}}
375 undiscovered in 2013. What does this mean? The data above is statistics on missing persons from the Korean National Police Agency. 255 normal children, over 500 parents. How many families are suffering and grieving? I also have two children who are the apple of my eye. If they're out of sight for even a moment, my heart sinks – that's how our children are. I also worry if they'll be okay when they go out to daycare. They say the pain of a parent with a missing child is akin to death, so it's a truly horrific thought.

A South Korean startup has emerged, drawing attention for its potential to revolutionize this problem. The company name is Reversal, and the product name is Lineable. This product, seemingly a portmanteau of 'line' and 'wearable,' costing only 5 dollars, or about 5,000 won, is the most talked-about wearable device. (Although 'line' is in the name, it has no relation to Naver.)

A world without missing children, a dream technology that returns children to their grieving parents – doesn't that sound wonderful just thinking about it? A Korean company is attempting such technology.

**3. A technology unlike any other, can Lineable eliminate missing children?**
What anyone can easily imagine with technology is real-time tracking of a child's location, allowing parents to follow them no matter how far. However, such technology has only existed in movies or comics and has been difficult in reality. I also looked into various options for a while, but they were excessively large, batteries didn't last long, and there were limitations in location positioning. First, what hinders the creation of such a device is the battery and GPS. GPS error rates are over tens of meters, and Wifi or 3G/LTE consume an enormous amount of battery. SKT's recently launched smartwatches for children could be a solution to some extent, but the cost of maintenance is burdensome, and the idea of only my child wearing such a device among many children might make other children see them strangely, or the game-like features could create a sense of disparity. Above all, my wife's reaction to giving a smartwatch to a child, when even smartphones are viewed negatively, was that it's still far too early.

**1) Lineable's remarkable specs**
Let's watch the concept video of this "14g, fully waterproof and dustproof" bracelet.
First, in terms of hardware, it has a form that can be worn by a child without burden.

**2) It alerts you if they go beyond a certain distance.**
The technological background is beacon, a Bluetooth-based real-time location tracking technology. Services that prevent loss by using Bluetooth's characteristic of only being able to connect over tens of meters, causing the bracelet or phone to vibrate if it moves beyond a certain distance, already existed.
Actually, up to this point, it's not particularly unique.
{{IMG:2}}
In fact, even this much is a pretty good technology, as it alerts you when a child goes out of sight. The problem is that if they move more than tens of meters into a crowd, it becomes difficult to find them. However, Lineable solved the problem in a different way.

**3) You can know the location anywhere near a Lineable user.**
{{IMG:3}}
Do you understand the image above? As someone with a technical background, I understood the diagram at once. Simply put, another smartphone using Lineable tells my smartphone my child's location via a server. Let's imagine simply. It means that countless parents at Everland would help locate my child, and I, in turn, would help locate other children who have lost their parents.

Of course, there's a flaw here. If there are fewer Lineable users, the probability of location tracking also decreases. However, if it were me, I would gladly install the client app even if I wasn't a Lineable user. If I can use a little more battery to return even one more child to their parents, I would gladly sacrifice a bit of battery.

The point that moved me about this service is this: That we protect our children together. It can be said that it utilizes the fundamental hope inherent in internet technology.

**4. IoT, wearables? That stuff doesn't matter. Just as the iPhone was the iPhone, Lineable is Lineable!**
Although I'm an IT enthusiast, I don't care about IoT, wearables, or any of that. What matters is that an innovative technology has finally emerged that can help us protect our children together. In fact, we might have already possessed many technologies to solve the world's problems. What's important is to change our thinking, connect, and execute. That is the power of Reversal's Lineable.
