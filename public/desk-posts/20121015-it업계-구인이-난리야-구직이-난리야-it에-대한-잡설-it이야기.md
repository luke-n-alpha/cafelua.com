##### The following is a conversation I had with friends on my alma mater's undergraduate Facebook group timeline.
Do you think the problem is with hiring? Or with job seeking?
The conclusion is a money problem... Candidate Moon Jae-in said he would make IT professionals the best grooms. I wonder if that era will ever return... It once was like that, back when I was in the military. OTL.
--- Below is the timeline; friends' real names are not disclosed. For reference, I am from Soongsil University's Computer Science department, which mass-produces developers for the IT industry. (A so-called 'mass-produced developer'?) ----
Friend 1 ‎Friend 2!! Did you find a developer??? Is this country really struggling with hiring? Or with job seeking?
Friend 2 It's a situation where both a hiring shortage and a job-seeking shortage are happening simultaneously... There aren't enough people... but there are no *suitable* people... Most want more than their skill level and aim for higher-tier companies... That gap is hard to bridge... I'm not saying I don't understand, but... I just wish they'd apply with a realistic understanding of their own level....
Friend 1 That last point resonates.
Friend 2 To add a bit more... While I wonder how important academic background and credentials are for a developer... what feels truly excessive is...
We've hired new graduates and are now looking for experienced professionals.
My desired specs: A 4-year computer science degree from a university in Seoul (or, with some compromise, the Seoul metropolitan area) (or, with some compromise, just an engineering degree). Applicants' specs: Graduates of an unknown vocational college... or an unknown regional 4-year university, plus 6-month academy graduates + 2-3 years of experience at an unknown company....
If we close our eyes and just conduct interviews, their knowledge is truly superficial... yet they demand a salary on par with large corporations... If we force salary negotiations and hire them... they don't show up on the first day.
Conclusion: We couldn't hire a single person.
Friend 3. Friend 2, our company seems to be in a similar situation these days.
Friend 2. Overall, it seems true that the quality (?) of developers has declined since the aversion to STEM fields... However, I think the demand for mobile has surged, leading to a temporarily strange situation. The industry believes that most small and medium-sized companies claiming to specialize in mobile will be consolidated by 2013... Of course, whether 'Cheol-su hyung' (Ahn Cheol-soo) becomes president will be a crucial variable.
Friend 3 Hmm. I don't know much about mobile apps... but I hear a lot of talk about our company (and SDS) being a black hole. Still, we're desperate for people right now... 'ㅅ';
Friend 2 Friend 3, yeah... you guys should stop taking all the people.. lol. If it's like that there, imagine how it is here.....
Friend 3 So I just cleanly work with an overseas research lab haha.
Friend 4 Many people are leaving Samsung too... I wonder where they're going...
Friend 4 Seeing Friend 3's comment, I guess I need to think about how to survive as a domestic developer. Though this isn't a new problem...
Forest Story Personally... I think it's true that companies are paying developers too little. The reason why mashups aren't active in Korea is because companies think it's cheaper to make things by 'squeezing' their internal developers. There needs to be an understanding that development incurs significant costs for companies to buy and use others' solutions. The current situation, where developers are squeezed dry, makes no sense. Small and medium-sized businesses complain about not having money... but if they don't have that much money, they shouldn't start a business in the first place.
Forest Story If we were high-quality and cheap in our time, and our juniors are the same, it will just be passed down. We need to act expensive. Otherwise, the vicious cycle will continue (Ah... I don't develop anymore...). I don't think it's a hiring crisis yet.
Friend 2 I'm constantly conducting interviews right now... The problem is... they're low quality and expensive... From a supply/demand perspective, there are simply not enough people... Not to bring up academic background, but even mediocre vocational college graduates want a salary one rank higher... so we can't hire them...
Forest Story Friend 2, 'expensive for the quality' is from our perspective... The inability to hire means the market price has formed above that. And isn't this an opportunity for our own value to increase? Also, all companies want experienced professionals... but I wonder if there was ever a company that truly built a career path for junior developers to grow into mid-level developers. In the end, it was every man for himself, and they were just consumed. If you want cheap developers, you should hire high school graduates with good character and train them, or use Chinese or Vietnamese developers.
Forest Story I predict it would be better to lower your expectations. This phenomenon will likely accelerate...
