There were talks that Google I/O was unremarkable, but I recently heard surprising news from Google. The fact is, Google's photo storage service has become free. Actually, I compared cloud storage capacities recently, and at the time, I chose Microsoft's OneDrive, which offers 1 terabyte of space along with Office 365. Besides that, Amazon was already providing near-free cloud space, but I didn't choose it because Amazon boasted terrible speeds in Korea.

** 1. Why I chose Google Photos instead of OneDrive: Free and Fast!**
However, OneDrive has been uploading 450GB for two weeks now. The speed is truly terrible. But recently, Google introduced free photo space. I tested it, and it boasts a much more comfortable speed than OneDrive. Of course, there's one limitation: it's restricted to 16-megapixel photos and Full HD videos. First, for Full HD, most smartphones and cameras have a maximum resolution around that level, so it won't be a big problem. However, while 16-megapixel cameras are the resolution for the latest smartphones, it's a bit lacking for DSLR users or users like me with a Galaxy Zoom 2. Still, if you really think about it, once you reach around 16 megapixels, other factors become more important than pixels, so I think it's acceptable.

** 2. Smartphone Settings: Photos taken go straight to the cloud.**
You can download the app below.
https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.photos[포토 - Google Play의 Android 앱play.google.com](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.photos){{IMG:1}}
If your family wants to use the same photo space, you can create a separate Google ID. On my wife's smartphone, only the Google Photos app is logged in with my ID. However, you must be careful at this point: if two accounts are set up on an Android smartphone, a disaster can occur where all schedules and phone numbers download and get mixed up, so please turn off all synchronization when initially setting up the secondary account. Synchronization is set by default to only occur when using Wi-Fi, and if you set it to high quality, it seems to automatically resize and upload based on 16 megapixels.

While previous services that boasted cloud and free photo space merely offered cloud storage, Google seems to have created the ultimate solution in the photo field, offering seamless integration between mobile and PC, easy photo management, and even automation technology for photo sharing. Actually, the Google Stories feature has been around for quite some time, but as word-of-mouth about the free photo space spreads, this excellent technology seems to be gaining attention once again.

**4. Crisis for Domestic Photo Services**
N Drive has started charging for storage, while Daum Cloud has shut down its cloud business. The situation is evolving into a battle that demands powerful infrastructure and high technological standards. Personal photo services are very important. Images contain more information than text, and by interpreting them, much more can be done. I believe Google's 'home turf' attack on South Korea has begun. First, the most nervous will likely be direct competitors like N Drive and indirect competitors like KakaoStory. Having shut down Daum Cloud, how will Daum Kakao defend KakaoStory? Kakao Album seems to be already in a somewhat bad state, so it would be difficult to compete with that. I have a slight feeling that it's highly likely they'll move towards an integrated service of KakaoStory and Kakao Album, and in Naver's case, they are aggressively starting with Polarr, but it seems difficult given the sluggish social graph situation. So, will they adopt an unlimited photo policy like Google? I think we'll have to wait and see. Anyway... I think this is a time when Google, having laid the groundwork with Android, is trying to reap the harvest. I plan to write additional posts on this topic separately.
