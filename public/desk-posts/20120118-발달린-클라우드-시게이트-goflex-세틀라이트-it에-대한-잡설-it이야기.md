****1. 이동식하드는 왜쓸까?**이동식 하드는 USB에 부피는 크지만 대용량 데이터를 이동가능하다는 점에서 사랑받던 제품입니다. 문서파일이나 작업파일의 경우 수기가바이트 이내라 usb파일로 이동가능합니다. 하드디스크로 이동할 대용량 데이터는 때문에 대부분 프로젝트나 혹은 고화질 동영상이죠. 회사에서 프로젝트를 통째로 들고나가거나 엄마몰래 볼 동영상 파일 은닉에 적합합니다. 매우 삐뚤어진 목적으로 사용가능하군요. ㅋ
**2. 사용니즈가 낮아지고 있는 이동식 하드**그러나 이동식 하드의 입장은 점점 더 좁아지고 있습니다. 저렴해지고 용량은 늘리고 있는 usb나 sd카드, 무제한에 가까운 용량이라고 할 수 있는 인터넷에 항상 연결되는 스마트기기들, 거기다 이를 정면으로 타겟팅하고 있는 클라우드까지. 클라우드가 주목받는 기술인 만큼 언젠가는 이동식 하드도 플로피 디스크의 운명을 맞을지도 모르죠.
**3. 아직은 끝나지않은 이동식 하드**그러나 아직 이동식 하드시장이 끝난것은 아닙니다. 무제한을 제공하는 3g망은 아직 seamless와는 거리가 좀 있고, 대용량 파일 전송에는 속도가 부족하고, 4g망의 경우에는 그런 목적으로 사용했다가는 요금폭탄 맞기 좋습니다. 스미트기기의 용량은 현재 최대 64기가바이트로 부족한감이 있죠. 때문에 대용량 파일, 빠른속도의 이동을 원하는 유저들은 여전히 이동식 하드를 찾기 마련입니다.

**1. Why use a portable hard drive?**
Portable hard drives were beloved products because they allowed for the transfer of large amounts of data, despite being bulkier than USB drives. Document files or work files are usually within a few gigabytes, so they can be moved with a USB drive. Therefore, the large data transferred to a hard drive is mostly projects or high-definition videos. It's suitable for taking entire projects out of the office or for secretly hiding video files from your mom. It can be used for very twisted purposes. lol

**2. Portable hard drives with decreasing usage needs**
However, the position of portable hard drives is becoming increasingly narrow. There are cheaper USB drives and SD cards with increasing capacities, smart devices that are always connected to the internet with almost unlimited storage, and even cloud services directly targeting them. As cloud technology gains prominence, portable hard drives might eventually meet the same fate as floppy disks.

**3. Portable hard drives are not over yet**
However, the portable hard drive market is not over yet. The 3G network, which offers unlimited data, is still a bit far from seamless, and its speed is insufficient for large file transfers. Using a 4G network for such purposes could easily lead to a bill shock. The capacity of smart devices is currently limited to a maximum of 64GB, which feels insufficient. Therefore, users who want to transfer large files at high speeds still look for portable hard drives.

**4. Satellite differentiated as a media device for smart devices**
And Satellite added one more thing to differentiate itself: it turned the hard drive into a media device. Simply put, this thing is an HDD-type DivX player. And, as a portable device, it uses Wi-Fi instead of an HDMI cable for connection. The inconvenient part is that you have to install a separate app, but for smart device users, app installation is the most familiar action, so it's not difficult.

It's almost identical to Naver's N Drive app. The only difference is that the storage is a portable hard drive, and it connects via Wi-Fi only between the two devices instead of the internet. You can think of it as a portable cloud. For Seagate, whose main business is hard drives, this seems like an appropriate point of differentiation.

**5. I wish it would do this better.**
**1) WPS connection not supported**
Recently, I was using a wireless HP printer and an N6004M router, and they connected just by clicking buttons on both devices without password setup. I checked and found that the Galaxy S2 also supports WPS, but Satellite does not. The previous reviewer had set a password, so I struggled to connect until I found a way to reset it. After resetting, it became an open AP. Since it's an open AP when first used, be careful not to expose sensitive data by mistake. My WiBro Compact Egg had a different initial password written on each product, so I felt a bit disappointed by the lack of attention to security for a portable hard drive.
{{IMG:8}}
**[ Huh, Satellite just appears as an open AP ]**
**2) SD Card Reader**
I wish it had an SD card reader function. My friends who went on long overseas trips brought laptops. They wanted to view photos directly on their smartphones from the camera while traveling, but smartphones don't have SD card readers. Since this device could also be primarily for photo storage, an SD card reader function would be great.
**3) Support for various formats**
It supported far fewer formats compared to N Drive. Both for videos and documents. It seems more diverse format support is needed.
**6. Essential points to know when using a portable hard drive, backup is a must!**
Despite the convenience of portable hard drives, hard drives are fundamentally vulnerable to shock. Even fixed hard drives will eventually fail after repeated reading and writing. They are essentially consumables. Therefore, don't forget to back up. DVDs are not eternal either. **It is absolutely forbidden for those who store all their baby photos on a portable hard drive.** You might wonder why I'm dissing a portable hard drive in a review, but it's because I've seen people regret doing this (like my sibling...). Currently, the safest method is to store them on a cloud service like N Drive or Picasa. Although these services also record data on hard drives, they usually employ triple redundancy. This means data can be recovered even if one of the three fails. Broken hard drives are immediately replaced. However, the current drawbacks of these cloud services are their small capacity (about 17 times less than N Drive) and slow speed (about 48 times slower than ultra-high-speed internet). This might be woefully insufficient for today's high-resolution photos and videos. That's why I use a home NAS with mirroring. I'll talk about this in a future post when I have time. **Portable hard drives are convenient, but there's a crucial fact to remember when storing important data: the more important the file, the higher the probability of it breaking, according to the laws of IT. Regular backups are the only way to survive.**
