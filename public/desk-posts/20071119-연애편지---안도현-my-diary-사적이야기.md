Around twenty, everyone wrote a love letter at least once.

A time of longing that couldn't be fully expressed in words,
and loneliness that wanted to show something.

Reading it over countless times to check for errors,
pressing the pen firmly onto the blank page, we wrote.

As we wrote and erased endless desires,
one day, the dawn light would illuminate our forehead,
and the body and mind, which had been overflowing with the emotion of love until then,
would collapse with the sound of crumpling paper.

But we dreamed even in our sleep.

That for love,
it was okay to lose everything.

But friend, think about it.
Having lost those nights of writing love letters,

graduating from school and learning compromise,
finding comfort in marriage, learning subservience for promotion,
would you say you truly loved life?

People, politics, unification are tiresome;
stories of stocks, real estate, and cars are preferred.

Thinking that for my own sake,
this world can rot,

friend, would you say you lived cleaner than anyone else?
The love letters written around twenty were not like that.

They wrote that love
is knowing how to sacrifice oneself for others.

They wrote that love
is fighting off a thief if one breaks into your home.

They wrote that love
is not eating dirty food, even if you have nothing.

Love is not waiting,
but setting out to find it, one step at a time.

It is still written in those passionate love letters.
 
- Ahn Do-hyun -
 
ps. Today, on my way to work, KDMB radio introduced this poem.
When I looked up this poem online, I found that many people who like to talk about politics had widely shared it.
Political talk? Yes, it might be a poem suitable for political discourse.
 
However, to me, it seems to be a story about a lost dream and a lost heart.
Just thinking about living day by day, not listening to any dreams, ideals, or even God's calling,
isn't it a picture of someone scrambling to live a mayfly-like existence, just for a single day?
 
Everyone is fighting and competing to earn money and secure good positions,
but what am I doing now for the childhood dream I had, where I resolved not to live that way?
 
I think I've experienced heartbreaking love,
but I easily get annoyed with my wife, and though I had tough times getting a job,
now that I have a good job, am I truly living a diligent life?
 
Political talk, yes, it might be.
But the grand talk of who becomes president is not an issue I can decide at my level.
It's simply about how honest I've been with myself,
or how great a father I am in the eyes of my future children.
 
I remembered something a sister from my company's Christian fellowship said.

"Before, I prayed for a strong and smart child to be born,
but as my due date approached, I realized that wasn't God's will.
I am praying to become a good parent."
 
Political talk is actually simple. If there are "good citizens," "good leaders" will emerge.
In a climate where the prevailing thought is that it's okay if someone is capable, even if they compromise, are dishonest, or overlook injustice,
hasn't it become a ridiculous world to expect morality from leaders?
 
God seems to be asking me, full of worldly complaints:
 
"How cleanly and purely are you living?
You don't listen to My words, and you've extinguished the fire in your heart,
and isn't it I who am complaining about the world like a child?"
