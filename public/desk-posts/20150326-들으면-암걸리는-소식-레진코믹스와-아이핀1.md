by Soopsookyaegi​Today, I heard two pieces of news through my timeline. The news is that Lezhin Comics, a promising domestic venture in the webtoon industry, had its service blocked as harmful content by the Korea Communications Standards Commission (KCSC), and that after the public i-PIN hacking incident, the cause of the hacking was admitted to be a lack of maintenance costs. Let me explain why this news is so infuriating. The response level of each department seems to completely misunderstand the gravity of the situation, and it even feels like organizations lacking 'qualifications' compared to their 'responsibilities' are in charge.

**1. First, the infuriating news.  The blocking of Lezhin Comics by Warning.kr.**
Lezhin Comics was suddenly blocked without prior notice, according to the standards of the Korea Communications Standards Commission. Lezhin Comics is an adult webtoon service that has made significant contributions to popularizing the paid business model for webtoons and mainstreaming subculture. It was selected as a Global K-Startup in 2013 by the Ministry of Science, ICT and Future Planning and is a promising venture leading the Hallyu (Korean Wave) of webtoons alongside Naver Webtoon.

However, as mentioned, since it dealt with adult content, there was always a possibility of receiving a warning. But the suddenness without prior consultation, and the subsequent explanation, are utterly absurd. There were rumors that the reason for the block was a specific gag webtoon that caricatured genitalia, but this was not an official announcement, and there were many adult webtoons of that level, including those by famous webtoon artist Yang*soon, so it's unreasonable to pinpoint that as the specific cause. Therefore, quoting the official response from Microsoftware:

[https://www.imaso.co.kr/news/article_view.php?article_idx=20150325155604](https://www.imaso.co.kr/news/article_view.php?article_idx=20150325155604)
**1) Japanese manga is the problem.** "It appears that Japanese manga was serviced domestically with only translation and no illustration modification, and there were quite a few such manga," they explained. "We deliberated on Lezhin Comics and the realm of manga expression, but by providing numerous Japanese manga that do not align with Korean sentiment, without youth protection measures, and even offering samples, did Lezhin Comics fulfill its social responsibility?"

 The reason I got so angry reading this is:
** - "It appears that Japanese manga was serviced domestically with only translation and no illustration modification."** **Illustration modification is censorship from an artist's perspective.** Moreover, **it's absurd to even request illustration modification for adult content.** In the past, public broadcasters committed many ridiculous acts, like showing Sailor Moon wearing pants. I'll let that slide. Because it's P-U-B-L-I-C B-R-O-A-D-C-A-S-T-I-N-G. Lezhin Comics targets mobile platforms, which are personalized devices, and is an adult service that requires adult verification. This is utterly shameless.
**- "Japanese manga that do not align with Korean sentiment."** What exactly is "Korean sentiment"? The outrageous storylines seen in morning dramas? Korean-style fantasy dramas that evolved from Japanese dramas? Should all foreign films that use informal "you, you" speech, which doesn't fit Korean sentiment, only be released as dubbed versions? In this global era, what crazy country blocks distribution simply because it doesn't align with its national sentiment? Should our country also ban songs and make women cover their faces like Islamic nations? Before that, in this beautiful Confucian nation, there are countless "unfilial" religions like Christianity and Catholicism that refuse to honor ancestors, and they even dominate TV with things like Christmas. Why aren't they slapped with a Warning label? Empathy is crucial for media, including comics. If it doesn't fit "Korean sentiment," it won't be popular. For example, when watching American foreign films, family stories or school life can feel out of place, which is because they don't align with Korean sentiment. However, frankly speaking, Japanese subcultures fit Korean sentiment excessively well, to a fault. They fit much better than Western ones. The reason Korean services find it easy to enter Japan is precisely because their sentiments are similar.
Personally, I can think of works on Lezhin Comics that might have caused issues. Certainly, if they were published in print like in the past, they wouldn't have passed strict scrutiny. However, times have changed. Searching for doujinshi on Google is not difficult, and global culture is already quite open and pursues diversity. I'm not saying that's right. It's a matter of diversity. Personally, I dislike Ilbe, but I don't think blocking Ilbe is right. Rather, content that could potentially harm those who haven't yet established their values, like Ilbe, should indeed be an adult service requiring adult verification. Both Ilbe and Oyu are necessary as part of diversity. However, adult verification is a different issue. And Lezhin *does* have adult verification. I'd like to say, "**The KCSC Youth Protection Team fails to read the changes in Korean sentiment.**"
