[**Ignorant Korea... "Stop being serious and put away your books"** [Money Today Reporters Yoo Byung-ryul, Lee Chang-myung, Lee Jae-won, Park Da-hae, Gong Jun-seo | Edited by Kim Ju-dong, Lee Eun-jung] [Editor's Note] [New Korea Report] aims to understand the current state of South Korea and its problems... news.naver.com](http://news.naver.com/main/read.nhn?mode=LSD&mid=sec&oid=008&aid=0003303415&sid1=001)

As a digital advocate, I find it difficult to agree with this article. Light content is merely escaping books, and I find it hard to agree that a decrease in book consumption is reducing the public's intellectual consumption.
1. (Disagree) Reading a lot of books doesn't necessarily improve writing skills. - Writing improves by writing a lot. It doesn't matter if it's digital or with a pencil.
2. (Disagree) Light novels are also books. - Whether children's text consumption has decreased needs to be measured separately.
3. (Disagree) Books are not the only way to convey knowledge and works. Therefore, using book sales as a KPI to measure the public's knowledge consumption level is flawed. - There is no evidence that YouTube or webtoons are less beneficial than books. - Is the consumption of Wikipedia or Naver Knowledge iN not beneficial?
4. (Agree) Digital production and consumption channels for serious content are still few, and for now, it's still books. - Serious content is still consumed, and a suitable medium beyond books has not yet been found. - The closest equivalent was blogs, and many internet companies are currently contemplating the solution for this on mobile.
