​아주 멀리 우리를 지배하거나 혹은 천국으로 이끌어줄 어떤 위대한 인공지능이 아니라 지금 당장의 이러한 수준의 인공지능만해도 우리는 받아들일 준비가 되었는지 다시 생각해볼 필요가 있습니다. 이러한 인공지능이 가져올 단기적인 미래에 대해 몇가지만 말씀드리겠습니다.**1. 고용없는 성장, 극단적인 생산성 증가의 사회**​컴퓨터는 사람과 비교할 수 없을 정도로 빠릅니다. 이러한 인공지능이 대체 하는 특정 직업은 극단적인 생산성 증가가 발생할겁니다. 결과적으로 돈을 많이 벌 것이고, 노동자로 인한 많은 비효율은 제거 될겁니다. 다른 말로 하면 해당 기술을 가진 소수 기업은 글로벌 단위의 성공을 거둘 것이고, 기술을 가지지 못한 기업들은 도태될 겁니다. 이 와중에 해당 분야에 종사하던 화이트칼라 근로자는 일자리를 잃게됩니다. 영국 옥스퍼드대 연구팀에 의하면 미국의 702개 직업중 47%가 사라질것이라고 합니다.​해당 문제는 극단적인 실업이 발생할 것이며, 인공지능 기술을 가진 구글 등의 특정 SW기업에 돈이 집중 될 것을 의미합니다. 이를 사회관점에서 본다면 인공지능이 만드는 사회변화입니다. 마치 산업사회에서 블루칼라의 단순 노동자들이 기계로 대체되었던 일이 재현됩니다. 현재 그 결과로 선진국일 수록 블루칼라라고 해도 아주 지나친 단순 노동직은 남아있지 않습니다. 인형에 눈을 달거나 봉투를 붙히는 일이 없어진것과 마찬가지죠. 결국은 이 문제는 실업 문제를 어떻게 해결할 것인지와 함께, 기술의 도입으로 큰 돈을 벌어들인 기업의 부의 쏠림을 자본주의의 자연스러운 결과로 볼 것이냐 하는 문제가 있습니다.​현재 이에 대한 해답은 명확하지는 않습니다. 아마존의 경우에는 기술로 벌어들인 돈을 다시 기술에 재투자함으로서 고급 인력들의 일자리를 늘리는데 기여하고 있습니다. 또한 사회적인 시스템, 곧 세금을 통한 부의 재분배 방법도 있을것입니다. 돈을 버는 것이 기업의 존재의 이유기는 하지만, 천문학적인 금액이 한 기업에 몰리는 것은 해당 기업으로서도 그 막대한 돈을 어떻게 써야 할지 고민스러울 겁니다. 기업의 근본적 가치는 은행처럼 돈을 쌓아두는데 있는 것이 아니라 영속적으로 돈이 유통되고 성장하는데 있기 때문입니다. 현재 단기적으로 미국의 경우 M&A와 기술투자의 선순환이 이루어지고 있는 것으로 보입니다. 그러나 대한민국의 경우에는 M&A가 익숙하지 않고, 미국수준의 인공지능 기술을 확보가 가능하다는 보장이 없어 인공지능 기술이 국가에 이익을 가져올지 오히려 막대한 국부유출을 가져올지는 알 수 없는 일입니다. 해외 기술을 비싸게 도입해 사용하는 소수의 기업만이 이익이 증가되고, 실업만 가중으로 이어진다면 더 끔찍한 일이 될 수도 있겠죠. 기술의 발달은 부의 재분배의 논의와 함께 사회적 안전망에 대한 논의가 같이 이루어져야 합니다. 그렇지 않다면, 인공지능은 대중에 여가를 제공한 친구가 아니라 대중의 일자리만 빼앗는 악마가 될 수도 있습니다.**2. 인공지능의 결정을 따를 수 있는가**​전통적인 기계에서의 컴퓨터의 역할은 대체적으로 제어에 대한 일을 많이 했습니다. 필연적이고 명확한 부분에서는 많은 부분이 자동화되어왔죠. 그러나 명확하지 않고, 책임이 발생할 수 있는 부분들은 대체적으로 사람이 하는 일이었습니다. 예를 들어 암을 진단하고 수술을 결정하는 일은 의사의 역할입니다.​그럼에도 불구하고 사실은 많은 의료사고가 발생하고 있었으며, 이에 대한 대안으로는 법적인 부분과 문화적인 부분이 함께 뒷받침되어왔습니다. 그러나, 만약 이러한 부분까지 인공지능에 의해 결정이 된다면 어떤 일이 벌어질까요? 이미 IBM의 왓슨은 전문 의사의 평균수준의 진단의 정확도를 보이고 있으며, 이를 기반으로 한 사업을 진행중에 있습니다. 평균이라니 아직은 책임있는 사람의 의사가 더 좋다고 생각하시는 분들이 대체적일겁니다. 하지만, 99%의 정확도를 보여, 인간의 평균이나 최고의 전문의 이상의 정확도를 보여준다면 어떻게 하실건가요? 아마도, 왓슨의 말을 신뢰하고 수술을 감행하는 것이 논리적이고 평이한 생각일겁니다. 그러나, 1%에 재수없게 걸려서 사고가 발생했습니다. 누가 책임을 져야 할까요? 아니 그 사고를 받아들일 수 있을까요? 유사한 문제로 무인 자동차가 있습니다. 무인자동차가 사람이 운전하는 자동차보다 훨씬 안전하다고 해도, 사람들은 그 기계가 일으키는 사고에 대해 받아들을 준비가 아직 되지 않았습니다. 사람들의 평균적인 인식과 제도적인 부분 모두 다 아직 이러한 인공지능을 받아들일 준비가 되어있지 않죠.​

Just a few years ago, artificial intelligence was a vague entity to be realized in the distant future, an imaginary being seen only in sci-fi novels. A few years ago, I asked a professor specializing in pattern recognition, a sub-field of AI, this question:

"Will AI, as natural as those in cartoons and at a level where human rights must be discussed, emerge during our lifetime?"

The answer to this question was negative. Some scholars discussing AI predicted that human-level AI would appear within a decade or so, citing the complexity of the brain, but many scholars probably still responded with "Well, I don't know."

However, the technological changes that have poured out in recent years, and the stories in various films reflecting them, almost certainly indicate that AI is no longer an imaginary being, and that a significant level of AI will emerge at least during our lifetime. And, considering the pace of technological development, there is also the frightening prediction that AI, once it reaches our level, will easily surpass us in a very short time. Tesla's Elon Musk even said that AI could bring about a dystopia, while Google's Larry Page and Ray Kurzweil, conversely, were optimistic about the future.

Even without talking about such distant AI, computers are already recognizing cats, learning Chinese, writing articles, classifying photos, and diagnosing cancer at a level similar to humans.

**The Wonderful and Terrifying Potential of Computers That Can Learn** What happens when we teach computers to learn? Technology expert Jeremy Howard shares surprising new developments in the rapidly advancing field of deep learning. Deep learning gives computers the ability to learn Chinese, recognize objects in photos, or assist with medical diagnoses. (A deep learning tool that taught itself the concept of "cat" after watching YouTube for a few hours.) Let's explore a field that will completely transform the computers around you much faster than you think. www.ted.com

It's not about some great AI that will dominate us or lead us to heaven in the distant future, but rather, we need to reconsider whether we are ready to accept even the current level of AI. Let me tell you a few things about the short-term future that this AI will bring.

**1. Jobless Growth, a Society of Extreme Productivity Increase**

Computers are incomparably fast compared to humans. Specific jobs replaced by such AI will experience an extreme increase in productivity. As a result, a lot of money will be earned, and many inefficiencies caused by human labor will be eliminated. In other words, a few companies with this technology will achieve global success, while companies without the technology will be left behind. In the process, white-collar workers in those fields will lose their jobs. According to a research team at Oxford University in the UK, 47% of 702 jobs in the US are expected to disappear.

This problem implies extreme unemployment and the concentration of wealth in specific software companies like Google that possess AI technology. From a societal perspective, this is a social change brought about by AI. It's like the re-enactment of simple blue-collar laborers being replaced by machines in the industrial society. As a result, in developed countries, even blue-collar jobs that involve excessively simple labor no longer exist. It's like the disappearance of jobs like putting eyes on dolls or sealing envelopes. Ultimately, this issue involves how to solve the unemployment problem, along with the question of whether the concentration of wealth in companies that have earned large sums through the introduction of technology should be seen as a natural outcome of capitalism.

Currently, the answer to this is not clear. In Amazon's case, by reinvesting the money earned through technology back into technology, it contributes to increasing jobs for highly skilled workers. There could also be social systems, namely wealth redistribution through taxes. While making money is the reason for a company's existence, an astronomical amount of money concentrated in one company would make that company ponder how to spend such vast sums. The fundamental value of a company is not in hoarding money like a bank, but in the continuous circulation and growth of money. In the short term, in the US, a virtuous cycle of M&A and technology investment seems to be taking place. However, in South Korea, M&A is not common, and there is no guarantee that AI technology at the US level can be secured, so it is unclear whether AI technology will bring benefits to the nation or lead to massive capital outflow. If only a few companies that expensively adopt and use foreign technology see increased profits, and unemployment only worsens, it could become an even more terrible situation. The development of technology must be accompanied by discussions on wealth redistribution and social safety nets. Otherwise, AI might not be a friend that provides leisure to the public, but a devil that only takes away their jobs.

**2. Can We Follow AI's Decisions?**

In traditional machinery, the role of computers was largely about control. Many parts were automated in inevitable and clear-cut areas. However, tasks that were unclear and could incur responsibility were generally performed by humans. For example, diagnosing cancer and deciding on surgery is the role of a doctor.

Nevertheless, many medical accidents did occur, and legal and cultural frameworks have supported alternatives to address them. But what would happen if even these areas were decided by AI? IBM's Watson already shows an average diagnostic accuracy comparable to that of professional doctors and is pursuing business based on this. "Average," you might think, so most people would still prefer a responsible human doctor. But what if it showed 99% accuracy, surpassing the average human or even the best specialists? It would probably be logical and common sense to trust Watson's word and proceed with surgery. However, what if, by bad luck, an accident occurred in that 1%? Who should be held responsible? Or could that accident even be accepted? Autonomous cars present a similar problem. Even if autonomous cars are much safer than human-driven cars, people are not yet ready to accept accidents caused by machines. Both the average public perception and institutional frameworks are not yet prepared to accept such AI.

However, what is clear is that in a capitalist society, companies will eventually yield to these very clear numbers and seize great opportunities. For instance, the company providing the AI might offer insurance for accidents caused by AI. While AI cannot be held responsible, the company that profits from AI can be held accountable. And companies doing business with AI will crunch the numbers and progressively enter areas of business where they can bear the responsibility. Ultimately, it will be an era where AI's decisions are naturally accepted in many areas.

**3. AI Human Rights, and Society**

We are probably not yet at the stage for such discussions. However, it might happen someday. It's unclear which will come first: software created by humans discussing human rights, or an electronic brain that has directly scanned and modeled a human brain discussing human rights. In the former case, machines evolve into humans; in the latter, humans evolve into machines. This could even be called a new stage of evolution. Recently, movies and animations with such themes have been increasing, likely due to the visible achievements AI is demonstrating.

Technology is changing society. Saying we are in a software-centric society is, in fact, an early discussion of these very topics. We need to consider whether we are prepared for this and what we should discuss. It won't be solved by prematurely concluding that it will bring bad results and rejecting it. This is because it is already at an inevitable level, just as the anti-mechanization movements during the Industrial Revolution failed. AI is predicted to reach a singularity that surpasses humans. Perhaps that point in time will be the singularity of our human society, of humanity itself.
