Recent metaverse platforms, often based on game engines or developed by game companies, have emerged, prompting me to write this post to share my thoughts despite my busy schedule. As the developer of XRCLOUD, a web-based B2B cloud service, I've highlighted the advantages of web XRCLOUD by pointing out the limitations of game engine-based metaverses in terms of accessibility, speed, scalability, cost, and flexibility. However, the metaverse market currently seems overshadowed by AI, with some even claiming it has failed. I want to address this. First, the metaverse has not failed. In fact, it's thriving. It's just not thriving with Korean companies. Fortnite and Roblox are still doing well. Sandbox games are clearly on the rise. Of course, the growth of the mobile game market is a different story, but sandbox games are definitely successful. Amidst this, a Korean version of The Sims, "Inzoi," has been released, and "DokeV" is keeping people on edge, but I believe Korea's strength as an MMO powerhouse remains. (Korean gamers will probably turn K-Sims into K-GTA, right?)

Around the same time, a metaverse from a non-game company recently emerged. However, I won't disclose the specific service name because I have a negative assessment of it. It demands the following specifications, yet claims not to be a game, talking about hyper-realistic Seoul, commerce, and real estate.

{{IMG:1}}

They claim the metaverse will be a second Earth, where you can invest, everything will be possible, hyper-realism, and unlimited concurrent users... But these platforms never mention content. They talk as if content will grow and an ecosystem will thrive just by users joining. I believe it will fail. As an internet service planner, a developer building technology, a content planner, or a gamer, there are simply too many reasons why it won't work. This is an answer only those who haven't seriously considered internet services, don't understand technology, or don don't usually enjoy content could come up with. First, as a planner, it simply has no value. I don't understand what value they intend to provide to users. Even the advertisements for such metaverse platforms lack a value proposition. Immersive? Then why isn't it developed for VR? Next, as a developer, it's a truly ambitious challenge. But it's an impossible goal to develop. You might ask if it's possible in games, but those are all tricks for content, not user-generated content.

Game development companies do this because their target is the global game market, and those game development companies, though it might hurt your pride, are much larger than the company commissioning the project. It's like asking how much it costs to build an E-Mart from a supermarket. E-Mart, with its nationwide chain and private label products, is not just a big supermarket. Invest more and bring in the absolute best experts. This isn't something you can do by hiring Unity or Unreal academy graduates. Even if the economy is bad, skilled professionals have never been cheap. Next, as a content planner... it seems like content costs haven't been budgeted at all, so I have nothing to say. There was a ridiculous notion circulating that the metaverse is a game without the fun, but then why build expensive game features? If you're talking about performances in metaverse content, you need to first discuss who will perform. Recruiting good content will probably cost as much as the platform development. That's not cheap either. To recoup all that investment, the platform needs to be big, right? Yes. That's why everyone does it on YouTube. The expected value is different there. At best, they'd try Roblox or Fortnite, and if you want exclusivity, you need to compensate for that content's revenue. You have tourism content? Wasn't the purpose of tourism or cultural heritage to promote them? This is marketing content planning, but it only works on PCs with expensive graphics cards? How much marketing budget have you allocated? No, have you allowed enough time for marketing? Finally, as a gamer... are you asking me to play a game that I might or might not play even with a coffee coupon? And you expect to get paid for this? And invest my time to create content? Do you not know that Korean gamers are a true gaming nation that consumes content at lightning speed, no matter what game comes out globally?

I expect the metaverse market to continue to grow. However, I believe super-app-like metaverses are doomed to fail. There's a saying in software development: a technology that can do everything does nothing well and is useless everywhere. If you think of typical super apps, they're probably Facebook or Naver. Consider if they were super apps from the beginning. They weren't. They developed into super apps by succeeding in one position and then expanding their user base. Oh, there are super apps from the start: game engines and operating systems. These middleware or SDK software are not aimed at general users but at developers (experts). So, we should discuss them at the level of experts' needs, right? I should have explained the basic level of demand for each role earlier, but if we go down to the expert level... I'd need to write a post for each specialized profession. And you probably wouldn't read them. One of those is our service, which targets web developers.

Finally, I'll summarize this post. Super-app-like metaverses are a mirage or a scam. If domain scope is important, you should first approach the technology market for experts. However, in this case, the target demographic shrinks. For example, we can access all domains but limit ourselves to web developers and web demand. Game metaverses, not super apps using game engines, will do well. But they must thoroughly target the game market. Korea has potential. I look forward to great games emerging. Metaverse demanders who find it difficult to create global games should choose one of two options: create content for global game platforms or use an accessible and affordable metaverse like ours. I'm looking at the demand for 1000 concurrent users, but it's unnecessary. There's no reason for avatars to sit, and game features are even less necessary. After all, what you want to show are videos and documents, created 3D spaces, and Zoom-like functions, right? In fact, doing this properly is incredibly difficult technology. Features that users don't absolutely need should NEVER be developed by planners or developers!!! There's no such thing as "nice to have." If there's code that's not used but left in, it's good? You should be hit. It's not like you're trying to screw over the person taking over the project.

The biggest reason for the metaverse's failure is that it focused on the imagination of non-experts rather than the essence of demand, creating a bubble and then exiting. I wonder if those who created that bubble and left are living well.

It's the weekend, but I'm off to check my partner's statistics API. I couldn't resist buying Hogwarts Legacy when it went on 70% sale on Steam. That's what I want from the metaverse. If you want to tell the metaverse what to do, I wish you'd at least try to make one crappy game map first.

These are thoughts I've had for a long time, but back then, money was pouring in, so I didn't want to be a spoilsport, and I had many people to consider, so I didn't speak up. Now that things are lighter, I'm writing these honest posts like a personal blogger.
