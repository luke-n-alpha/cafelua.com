**1. My predictions for Kakao's future. How accurate were they?**

Among the businesses Kakao operates, which of the following elements are they currently engaged in?

1) Launching free services for user lock-in: PWE (Personalization Tool) service
2) Offline coupon market: Expanding into the local coupon (social commerce) market
3) Digital content and game market: Kakao Content Store
4) E-commerce market: Kakao Social Shopping
5) Media market: Curation and news service
6) Community market: Kakao-version of Knowledge-in and Azit revamp
7) Advertising market: The ultimate revenue model, search advertising

In fact, it's no exaggeration to say that with the merger with Daum, they are doing almost all of these.

Why am I stating the obvious? These seven items are not from an article written this year, but a list of businesses I predicted Kakao would pursue around 2012. Didn't I get them quite right?

Looking at Daum Kakao's recent actions, it can be summarized in one phrase: a firm resolve to fight Naver with a mobile-first strategy. With this conviction, they are gradually streamlining old Daum services and focusing their efforts on mobile. Some may complain about the termination of good Daum services, but my perspective is different. Even for a company with vast resources, its core personnel are limited. Engaging in many businesses means widening the front, making it difficult to concentrate on a single venture. Moreover, what if the opponent is Naver? I was someone who had always asserted that Daum could never beat Naver. The biggest reason for this was Daum Communications' failure in selection and concentration. Naver, whether successful or not, showed a commitment to focusing on one thing. Me2day, which they pushed heavily at one point, demonstrated this, and years of consistent investment in Japan eventually led to the massive success of Line. Naver's greatest strength is its persistence and focus on businesses it believes it must win. In contrast, Daum tended to match Naver's lineup rather than concentrating on services with potential. **2. #Search and News can shake up the Korean search market.**

#Search, which will change the landscape of mobile search, currently sees Naver still as the number one in mobile search. However, it's also true that it shows a much weaker performance compared to PC, and the reason is Google. In South Korea, where Android accounts for over 90%, the default search engine is Google. And, the number one search term on Google is Naver. -o- This means users search for Naver on Google and then conduct their searches on Naver. But it's clear that users are now distinctly aware of Google's presence, and as a result, Google's search has gained a market share incomparable to its PC counterpart.

However, the undisputed mobile powerhouse in Korea is KakaoTalk. Would you believe it has more users than Google Play? It also secures the most user time. Now, a portal has been integrated into KakaoTalk's main menu. It's clear that this will have a more powerful influence than Google's pre-load.

Furthermore, looking at recent trends in news consumption, internet dependency has overwhelmingly increased. The reason is the desire for breaking news. Traditional TV and newspapers, with their morning and evening editions, provide news consumption twice a day, which is unsatisfactory for users glued to their smartphones 24/7. During the MERS outbreak, people wanted to know the number of confirmed cases with real-time visualized images and consumed breaking news. And this news spread through local cafes and friends.

Most of the shocking news I heard spread fastest via text messages, Facebook, or KakaoTalk. Things like, "Did you hear about ***?" Currently, perhaps taking a breather, Daum's popular search terms are not attached to KakaoTalk. One of the most influential things on mobile is this popular search term service. Of course, even without it, the influence of already curated content would be undeniable. The destructive power of Naver's main page and Daum's main page was like that of gods in the blogger sphere. Ultimately, content creators will eagerly rush to produce content that fits this. Where PVs gather, quality content gathers. When quality content gathers, search also improves.

Of course, current search quality is far superior on Naver. However, search is strongly driven by issues and habits, so one cannot be complacent. Moreover, what's scary about KakaoTalk is that most users are logged in. It also possesses social information. This means it has far more means than Naver to know what kind of search results a user with a particular taste would be more interested in.

**3. The future of mobile search: O2O search**

Another variable of Daum Kakao that I didn't predict in 2012 is O2O. At the time, I only thought of it as a local coupon service. It was a time when it was difficult to imagine local commerce as a profitable direction. But now I can clearly say that the answer lies in O2O companies that innovate services based on online connections, rather than simply moving offline services online. Representative services of this kind include companies like Uber, and domestically, services like Kakao Taxi. These models transplant all behavioral patterns of daily life online and innovate the service method itself based on information. Such models have the potential to emerge from everything we do and experience offline. From waking up in the morning until going to bed, even without looking at a smartphone due to IoT. And this will revolutionize existing PC-based search services. If PC search was query-centric, mobile search will be context-centric. Early attempts similar to this were at the level of Naver or Daum apps providing nearby bus stop information. Now, beacon-based systems will recommend optimal information of interest and induce searches. You'll start seeing advertisements everywhere saying, "Try typing #** in KakaoTalk," just as you used to see "Try typing ** in the Naver search bar" on TV. And it won't just end with search results; it will connect with various O2O services. Mobile search will be at the nexus of blurring the lines between offline and online. In the future, it will aim for AI agent models like Siri or Google Now, but currently, given the significant gap in technological levels, service-centric O2O search, driven by people and processes, seems to have a considerable advantage during this transitional period.

**4. A true showdown in mobile search**

This is what I believe to be KakaoTalk's future and potential that I hadn't considered. Naver began to rise when it attracted users by saying, "Ask ** on Knowledge-in." After that, businesses themselves solidified its dominance by saying, "Type ** in the Naver search bar." However, this model is not working well on mobile currently. KakaoTalk, I believe, is currently closest to a solution for this. Of course, Naver won't stand idly by. Its large advertising pool, expansion of offline commerce centered around Checkout, and the fintech battle are also as interesting as the Battle of Red Cliffs. However, Naver has a slight advantage in that even if it struggles a bit domestically, it can apply Kakao's proven model to Japan and capture a larger market than at home. It already had a similar experience with games once.

I feel that KakaoTalk is truly sharpening its sword recently. In contrast, I worry that most of Naver's top talent might be focused on Japan or overseas expansion. There is no eternal number one. I look forward to the upcoming competition even more.
