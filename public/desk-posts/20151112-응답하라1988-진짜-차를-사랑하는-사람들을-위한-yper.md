**1. Loss of Competitiveness and Consumer Distrust in Hyundai and Kia Motors**
Recently, the distrust of domestic consumers towards Hyundai and Kia Motors seems to be surfacing. While there has been distrust, Hyundai and Kia cars have always had a price advantage over foreign cars that incurred tariff penalties. However, with Samsung Motors and Daewoo Motors being acquired by foreign companies, they are now essentially competing with foreign companies on their home turf. To make matters worse, aggressive pricing strategies from foreign car brands and the steadily increasing prices of Hyundai and Kia cars have significantly eroded their advantages. The automotive industry, which was a prime example of protecting domestic companies, is rapidly losing ground to foreign brands. Furthermore, in terms of future technology, including Tesla cars, the situation is not easy.

**2. Only Marketing Tactics That Constantly Push for New, Expensive Things; No Love for Cars.**
In response, Hyundai and Kia's marketing efforts to alleviate distrust are gradually appearing in the market. However, it seems difficult to wash away the distrust that stems from the products themselves, not just the marketing. More than anything, the poor durability compared to what's visible and the options that make consumers desire "one more step, one more step" suggest that Hyundai and Kia Motors feel more like a superficial sales tactic than a love for the product itself – the car. This perception will be hard to change.

Fundamentally, Hyundai and Kia Motors' sales strategy is based on 'envy'. The car you drive always looks shabby compared to a new car, and even the same model is made to be compared with higher-end options. And the terribly diminished durability due to cost-cutting seems to urge you to abandon your old car and buy a new one quickly, rather than cherishing it.

My dream car, I confess, is not a Mercedes or BMW, but a 'Mini'. Recently, Tesla has also made it onto my wish list, but the image 'Mini' gives me is not something that was built overnight. I scoffed when I saw an advertisement comparing the 'Soul' to the 'Mini'. It's not that the 'Soul' is a bad car, but the 'Soul' cannot instantly surpass the history and image of the 'Mini'. The trust and image built over time are precisely the attachment to that brand and image. A similar company in smartphones would be Apple, and I believe what Hyundai and Kia should pursue is not money, but the hearts of their customers. And even when people buy Hyundai and Kia cars, they should be able to feel 'pride' – the creators of the products themselves must pour love into them. This 'pride' doesn't come from having more money than others and driving a luxury car. That's 'pride' in money, not 'pride' in the car itself. I would choose a Mini even if I had to choose between a Ferrari and a Mini. (Of course, there's the option of choosing a Ferrari and buying three Minis...)

**5. Cars Heading Towards the Future, But Still, Cars Are Memories and Affection.**
It's curious that Yper, a 21st-century on-demand car service, brings up these old cars. As it's themed around cars, it must be talking about affection for cars. There are discussions that our generation might be the last to own and drive cars. When I was young, I read Black Beauty and really wanted to raise a horse. Our children in the future might feel similar emotions about today's privately owned cars. What's clear is that a car is not just a product, but a culture and a machine that aged with me. I miss my first car, a Tico. Back then, I recklessly tried to do an 'Initial T' in the rain and totaled it. What's amazing is that neither the passengers nor I were hurt at all. It seems that just as I loved that little car, it protected me too. I hope you cherish your first car... and your possessions. Isn't that the message your wife, no, Yper, is trying to convey? I wish Hyundai and Kia would learn from this.
