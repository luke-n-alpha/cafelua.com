**1. SoftBank's Masayoshi Son Gave Us a UFO!**

The content that appeared on my timeline, "SoftBank's Masayoshi Son must have given us a UFO," made me dumbfounded when I clicked the link. Is the video I'm watching in real-time an April Fool's video? Did Masayoshi Son appear in an SF movie? They created that robot that recognizes human emotions based on the cloud and moves and reacts like a human, and they're going to commercialize it for 2 million won?

I felt the same shock I experienced when I first encountered the iPhone, when I saw the Safari browser working perfectly in my hand, after watching this video. As someone who once hoped for artificial intelligence and wanted to participate, even a little, in creating machines that resemble humans, I couldn't help but be excited by the level of technology in that monster-like robot shown in the video.

**2. Is This the Reality of Robot Technology?**

It is a clear fact that artificial intelligence, recognition technology, and human-like robots have made great strides. However, many parts were lacking to truly feel cool, and nothing was perfect. But the robot named Pepper in the video had smooth movements, as if a person were wearing a suit, and voice synthesis technology that even conveyed emotion. Although it's partly programmed based on scenarios, the fact that it has cloud-based emotion recognition made me feel as if the starting point of the robots I'd seen in cartoons, which were on a different level from utility robots used for special purposes, was unfolding before my eyes.

I was so resentful of Japan, which invaded our country, wondering how such an outstanding Korean genius ended up living as a Japanese person.

**3. The Part Where We Feel Humans Are Human: Robots That Mimic Emotions**

There is something called the Turing test to determine the humanness of artificial intelligence. It's a type of blind test where you determine whether the other party is human or machine through conversation. It generally approaches by recognizing logic or the flow of context, in the form of intelligence and reasoning. IBM's Watson falls into this category. However, even if it were perfect, people do not regard that perfect machine-human as human. You can imagine the Vulcans from Star Trek.

This is because we distinguish humanness through the realm of emotion and communication. This area is known to be particularly difficult. It's only to the extent of reading and distinguishing the other person's mood from their facial expressions.

But Pepper's feedback is implemented even more astonishingly. Its gestures, tone of voice, everything seemed to be in the realm of emotion, not logic.

**4. What Happens If Machines Gain Emotional Capabilities?**

If Pepper has sufficiently implemented mutual emotions with humans, very complex things will happen. This is a technology that can replace people performing so-called emotional labor. Recently, there was news that many telemarketers lost their jobs due to the suspension of operations of a credit card company. If these technologies become more mature, robot and software technologies will begin to penetrate even these areas. Automated answering machines will become smarter, and Apple's Siri will become more human-like. Ultimately, people might even fall in love with robots, just like in cartoons.

**5. Machines in Reality, Not Movies, and Our Homework**

Anyway, the amazing thing is that this incredible machine costs only 1.5 times more than Google Glass. Is it time for robot technology to explode, like the advent of the iPhone for smartphones, thanks to Pepper? I felt like I had been hit in the back of the head. Along with the thought that Japan is not dead yet, what will the children who were excited by Pepper create when they grow up? Isn't there a Bill Gates somewhere who met the Altair 8800?

We might not be watching an SF movie, but already living inside one. Masayoshi Son of SoftBank, a man who makes the impossible a reality. His boast that he would eliminate nuclear power in Japan also seems not to be an empty promise. Technologies that, though immature, explode once they reach a certain threshold, making the whole world their market – haven't we been neglecting them too much by only looking one step ahead? One of humanity's greatest abilities, different from animals, is to make dreams a reality. It seems that the dreamers ultimately win, and Masayoshi Son appears to be a person with truly outstanding ability in that regard.
