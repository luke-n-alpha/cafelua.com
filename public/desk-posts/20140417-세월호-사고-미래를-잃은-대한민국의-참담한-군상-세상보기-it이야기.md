I only saw the headlines of the Sewol news on Naver's main page. I actually learned the detailed content from TV news and social media feeds, with a flood of accident reports so scattered that it was hard to tell what was true and what was fact. I discovered mistakes by some trashy media outlets that I wouldn't have even noticed in the past, thanks to Facebook news feeds. Some issues were pointed out via social networks, and only later did the traditional media open their mouths. In the early 20th century, after the Titanic sank, a movie was made, and James Cameron later brought it to the big screen with excellent technology, leaving a lasting impression on many. At the time, I wasn't drawn to the love story of Leonardo DiCaprio and Kate Winslet; instead, the real human stories within it deeply resonated with me. And now, in 21st-century South Korea, I am seeing those scenes again through TV and news feeds.

It was truly very different. The captain of the Titanic stayed with the ship until the very end, but we could not. Unlike the Titanic, the accident occurred near the coast, and the first report of the accident was transmitted from a student's mobile phone, back home, and then to the police. KakaoTalk screenshots were captured and uploaded to the internet, and news reports featured high-resolution actual videos of the accident taken by individuals with smartphones, making James Cameron's technology and direction seem insignificant. The lower-class passengers in third class were replaced by children from Ansan, Gyeonggi Province, a region often described as relatively underdeveloped compared to Gangnam or Seoul. Ansan is one of the areas in Gyeonggi Province where ordinary people predominantly reside.

Superman from Hollywood didn't come to lift the ship, and the wailing families, the bewildered government agencies, and the trashy media outlets that shamelessly focused on "first reports" or "insurance money" for the deceased struck South Korea in the back of the head.

My mind is roughly numb. I woke up this morning, and my heart wouldn't settle. Seeing my beloved sons and daughters asleep, it didn't feel like someone else's story, and tears came to my eyes. In the face of death, humans reveal their true bottom. South Korea revealed its bottom. And in the end, many futures of South Korea, which had not even bloomed, were sacrificed. I can somewhat predict what will happen next, and what will be identified as the cause of the accident.

The complacency that "it was fine yesterday, so it will be fine today," the complacency that "it won't be my child," the complacency of living day by day without understanding the meaning of one's position, and our own images of sacrificing the future for immediate results overlap.

Amidst all this, the beautiful stories of the dead and the survivors will be painted even more beautifully. They probably want to masturbate like that, damn it. But those beautiful stories are about the survivors and the dead, and from the moment the accident occurred, it is no longer South Korea's story. The truth has already become difficult to conceal. There are rumors that the rescuers' phones were confiscated, but in a situation where individuals are sources of information, scattered pieces of truth would have already leaked out. Some of this information will be diluted by suspicions, doubts, false rumors, and attention-seekers, but I don't know how much ability and will there is to do so, but it will be the role of the government and the media to collect and properly announce it. The Galaxy S5 had limited water resistance. Google Glass is a device more suitable for real-time social networking. What kind of hell will we see on the internet if the same thing happens 10 years from now? It's different from the Titanic; there are countless information mediums, and countless records will be obtainable. The lessons learned from the Titanic saved countless lives afterward. Except for damn South Korea...

And the price for that is harshly upon us today.
Economy? National defense? For whom does the nation exist, and for whom are we living?
The truly important future is perishing so meaninglessly...
Are the foolish hyenas not tearing at their own flesh?
If we don't learn from the flowing river of time, there is no future.
How long will we continue to live in a country where this is allowed?

Christians all said they were praying. However, many misunderstand that prayer ends with repentance or asking, but let's not forget that proper prayer must always be followed by action. The same thing must not happen in this land again.

When God struck Egypt, He took away Egypt's future (the firstborn). It is time to reflect on what we are doing wrong.
It's a damn heartbreaking day, and I'm sorry.
