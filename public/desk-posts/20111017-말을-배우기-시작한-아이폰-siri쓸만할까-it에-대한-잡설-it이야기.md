**1. 마법에 도전하는 기술, 인공지능**
예전에 저는 스마트폰이 스마트하지 않다고 포스팅을 썼던 적이 있습니다.
그 이유는 스마트가 의미하는 intlligence와 스마트폰은 매우 큰 차이가 있어, 스마트폰이라기보다는 범용 os폰이라는것이 더 정확한의미 일거라는 점 때문이었습니다. 사실 pc도 스마트함과는 거리가 멀다고 보입니다. 그런데 이번에 siri가 ios5에 탑제되며 다시한번 주목받고 있습니다.
기술이 진보하면 그 기술은 마치 마법같다고 생각합니다. 그러나 이 마법에 도전을 목표로 하는 기술이 있는데 바로 인공지능이라는 학문입니다. 설화에 등장하는 피그마리온과 호문쿨루스, 골렘등 인간을 닮은 것을 만들고 싶어하는 것이 바로 인공지능이라는 학문입니다. 그런데 이 인간다움이란 무엇일까요? 바로 그 key는 언어에 있습니다. 보고, 듣고. 생각하고, 말하는것을 말하죠. 바로 siri라는 결과물도 이 인공지능의 결과물입니다. 바로 듣고, 생각하고, 말하는것이죠. 어린아이도 글보다는 말을 먼저 배우기 시작하는데 글을 익히는것보다 말에 대한 노출이 더 많고 비교적 명확하기 때문입니다. 그래서 이제 아이폰은 한글이름처럼 이제 막 말하기를 배우기시작했습니다.

**2. siri에 녹아든 세가지 기술, 음성인식, 구문해석, 상황인식**
제목을 세가지를 달았습니다. 사실 siri를 음성인식 기술 정도로 간단히 알고 있지만, 사실은 세가지 기술의 집합입니다.

**1) 음성인식**
먼저 음성인식은 소리를 듣고 해당 음성신호를 기계가 알아들을수 있는 문자로 바꾸는 작업입니다. 이때 가장 어려운점은 노이즈와 범용성의 문제입니다. 노이즈는 주변의 잡음에서 음성을 골라내는 문제이고, 범용성은 개인의 특성과 억양에도 영향을 덜 받는 특징을 골라내는 문제입니다. 음성을 보안에 사용할 수도 있는 이유는 개인별로 성문이 다르기 때문인데 음성인식기술에서는 오히려 이러한 개인별 특성이 인식에 어려움을 주기도 합니다. 사투리라도 쓴다면 그야말로 gg. (서울말을 이번기회에 배워봅시다. 서울말은 말끝만 높이면 된다면서~)

**2) 구문해석**
텍스트로 변환이 되었다해도 그 말의 뜻을 모르면 말짱황입니다. 영어책을 보고 읽을수는 있어도 독해가 안되는 것과 마찬가지 상황인 셈이죠. 이때 필요한것은 언어모델입니다. 제가 siri의 국내 출시에 회의적인 이유가 바로 이 언어모델은 지역별 특성이 매우 강하고 영어나 중국어와 달리 일본어와 한국어는 어순이 다르기 때문이죠. 때문에 한일간 번역보다 한영번역이 더 엉망인 이유도 이러한 언어모델이 매우 다르기 때문입니다. 국내에서 이러한 구문해석에 관한 기술이 가장 많이 쓰이는 분야는 바로 검색입니다. 자연어로 검색했을때 조사등을 제거하고 각 어사들의 구조를 분석하여 확률적결과를 도출합니다. 연관검색어 추천검색어 역시 이러한 확률기반의 추천이기도 하죠.

**3) 상황인식**
마지막으로 상황인식이란 유저가 처한 상황에 맞는 결과에 따라 검색결과를 다르게 보여주는 것입니다. 유저가 낮12시에 검색한 질의와 밤12시에 질의한 결과가 다르다면 상황인식에 들어갈수 있습니다. 컴퓨팅환경의 외부에 대한 상황을 인지해 그에맞는 결과를 도출하는것이죠. 사실 동영상에 발표된 siri의 경우는 상황인식보다는 대화형식의 질의로 상황을 알아내는 방식으로 보입니다. 이런 형식을 인공지능에서는 전문가시스템이라고 합니다만, 본 포스팅에서는 그다지 중요하지 않아보이니 패스하겠습니다.

**3. 인공지능인가? 인공저능인가? 정말 쓸만해 ?**
인공지능 시스템을 보면 대부분 사람들의 반응은 두가지로 나뉩니다.
** 신기해! 혹은 바보같아보여 쓸모없어보여!**

그리고 학습을 통해 지능을 갖추는 시스템이기에 모든 상황을 학습시키기에는 무리입니다. 아이폰의 역사라해봐야 몇년 되지 않았습니다. 사람이 언어를 학습하는데도 수년을 언어에 계속 노출되면서 학습합니다. 요즘은 영아다중학습이다 뭐다 언어를 가르치기위해서 엄마가 붙어서 투입되는 노력이 상당하던데 기계에도 그만한 노력이 들기 마련입니다. 사실 기계는 아직 인간의 지능을 훨씬 못따라갑니다.

**1. Technology Challenging Magic: Artificial Intelligence**
I once wrote a post saying that smartphones aren't smart.
The reason was that there's a huge difference between what 'smart' (intelligence) means and what a smartphone is, so it would be more accurate to call it a general-purpose OS phone rather than a smartphone. In fact, PCs also seem far from smart. However, with Siri's inclusion in iOS 5, it's once again drawing attention.
I believe that as technology advances, it seems like magic. But there's a field of technology that aims to challenge this magic: artificial intelligence. The desire to create human-like beings, such as Pygmalion, Homunculus, and Golem from folklore, is precisely what the field of artificial intelligence is about. But what exactly is this 'humanness'? The key lies in language. It refers to seeing, hearing, thinking, and speaking. Siri, as a product, is also a result of this artificial intelligence. It's about hearing, thinking, and speaking. Even young children start learning to speak before they learn to write, because they are exposed to speech more often and it's relatively clearer than written language. So, the iPhone, much like a child (아이, 'ai' in iPhone), has just begun to learn to speak.

**2. Three Technologies Integrated into Siri: Voice Recognition, Syntactic Analysis, Context Awareness**
I've listed three things in the title. While Siri is often simply understood as a voice recognition technology, it's actually a combination of three technologies.

**1) Voice Recognition**
First, voice recognition is the process of listening to sound and converting that audio signal into text that a machine can understand. The most difficult aspects here are noise and universality. Noise refers to the problem of extracting speech from ambient sounds, and universality is the problem of identifying features that are less affected by individual characteristics and accents. The reason voice can be used for security is that each person has a unique voiceprint, but in voice recognition technology, these individual characteristics can actually make recognition difficult. If you speak in a dialect, it's truly 'gg' (game over). (Let's learn Seoul dialect this time. They say you just need to raise the end of your sentences~)

**2) Syntactic Analysis**
Even if it's converted to text, it's useless if the meaning of the words isn't understood. It's like being able to read an English book but not understand its meaning. What's needed here is a language model. The reason I'm skeptical about Siri's domestic release is that these language models are highly region-specific, and unlike English or Chinese, Japanese and Korean have different word orders. This is also why Korean-English translation is often worse than Korean-Japanese translation, due to these vastly different language models. In Korea, the field where technology related to this syntactic analysis is most widely used is search. When searching with natural language, particles are removed, the structure of each word is analyzed, and probabilistic results are derived. Related searches and recommended searches are also based on this probabilistic recommendation.

**3) Context Awareness**
Finally, context awareness means displaying different search results depending on the user's situation. If a user's query at 12 PM yields different results than the same query at 12 AM, it can be considered context-aware. It involves recognizing external situations in the computing environment and deriving appropriate results. In fact, the Siri demonstrated in the video seems to ascertain the situation through conversational queries rather than direct context awareness. This format is called an expert system in AI, but it doesn't seem very important for this post, so I'll skip it.

**3. Artificial Intelligence or Artificial Stupidity? Is it Really Useful?**
When people see AI systems, their reactions usually fall into two categories:
**Amazing!** or **It looks stupid and useless!**
The former is when people are surprised by placing their expectations for AI on the machine, while the latter is when they are disappointed by placing their expectations for AI on humans. In fact, the starting point of AI as a field is to try and solve at least some difficult problems that machines cannot solve at all, by imitating human intelligence. It's a field that values solving problems rather than achieving a high accuracy rate. Therefore, satisfaction can be low, and it's a technology not often used where accuracy is critical.
And since it's a system that acquires intelligence through learning, it's impossible to train it for all situations. The iPhone's history is only a few years long. Humans also learn language by being continuously exposed to it for several years. These days, a significant amount of effort is put in by mothers for infant multi-learning and other methods to teach language, and machines naturally require a similar amount of effort. In reality, machines are still far from matching human intelligence.
**To put it in terms of a driver's license, this situation is like a timid woman, Mrs. Kim, who has only learned course driving, being asked to drive in the middle of Seoul where reckless drivers run rampant.** And as we saw earlier, Siri is a fusion of multiple disciplines, so if even one of its engines performs poorly, the results will always be poor. As a result, it's often treated as a useless field with very high difficulty and research costs compared to its output. **Anyway, what I want to say is, don't have high expectations for Siri, but value it highly.**

**4. A Product of Basic Science, Artificial Intelligence. What About Us?**
In the field of computer science, where applied science occupies a large portion, artificial intelligence is one of the few areas that can truly be called basic science. Recently, however, Google's voice recognition technology has been introduced, Facebook has facial recognition technology, and the iPhone has Siri. Many results from the field of artificial intelligence are emerging one by one. This is partly because faster processing and the internet have enabled the collection of large-scale data, making many AI research projects, which were previously only theoretically possible, now feasible. And, albeit limited, these results are appearing one by one. Compared to the attention poured into smartphone OS, there's still no deep consideration for these fundamental sciences of software. Of course, this is the reality faced by most basic sciences, not just software. Can we expect things to change a bit in the future? Honestly, I don't really expect it.
Most companies' R&D in Korea only has D (development); in research and development, there's only development, no research. Ultimately, the attitude is that any technology needed for research can just be bought. In this situation, China is pushing forward with cheap and high-level technology. Even the iPhone is actually 'Made in China'. Sigh... the conclusion turned out a bit strange. I hope that Korea will also show a little more interest in these technologies and make long-term investments.
