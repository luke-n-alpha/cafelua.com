**1. 모바일에서 가장 잘 맞았던 서비스. 소셜네트워크**
모바일에 들어서 가장 크게 성공한 앱은 뭘까요? tcg,  .지도,  로컬커머스 여러가지를 떠올릴것입니다. 그러나 가장 크게 성공한 앱은 뭐니뭐니해도 소셜네트워크입니다. 소셜네트워크는 모바일이 가진 커뮤니케이션 속성을 가장 잘 살리고 있으며 모바일은 언제 어디서나 연결되어 있어서 소셜네트워크의 트래픽을 폭발적으로 증가 시켰습니다. 카카오톡이나 라인의 경우도 메신저서비스로 분류할 수 있으나 근본적으로는 소셜 그래프를 내제한 소셜네트워크 앱입니다. 이를 기반으로 전통적인 소셜네트워크인 페이스북과 비슷한 타임라인으로 카카오페이지와 라인 홈을 가지고 있는것을 보면 잘 이해가 가실겁니다. pc기반의 소셜네트워크의 강자 였던 페이스북과 트위터 역시 모바일로 더 크게 성장했음도 이를 잘 나타내줍니다.

**2. 정체성을 정의한 버티컬 소셜네트워크**
그러나 사람들의 관계란 다양한 니즈로 관계를 맺습니다. 우리는 살면서 특정 조건의 관계들을 맺습니다. 애인관계, 동아리, 업무관계. 이 모든 관계들을 돈독히 하는데도 소셜네트워크는 유효합니다. 그러나 오프라인에서는 특정 모임에서는 특정 역할을 하게 되는데 하나의 소셜네트워크에서 이를 모두 표현하는 것은 보는 사람이나 쓰는 사람이나 혼란 스럽게 마련입니다. 따라서 실제 소셜네트워크에서는 사람들은 자신이 원하는 특정 아이덴티티로 활동하게 되는데 이는 기획자의 의도와 상관없이 소셜네트워크 서비스의 속성을 결정하기도 합니다. 육아판이 된 카카오페이지나 국내에 기크들이 모여버린 구글 플러스도 일종의 그러한 서비스 죠. 이는 앱이 가진 속성이나 시장의 위치에 따라 결정되기도 합니다.

**1. The Service Best Suited for Mobile: Social Networks**
What is the most successful app since the advent of mobile? You might think of TCGs, maps, or local commerce. However, the most successful app, without a doubt, is social networking. Social networks best leverage the communication properties of mobile, and mobile's always-on connectivity has explosively increased social network traffic. While KakaoTalk and Line can be classified as messenger services, they are fundamentally social networking apps with an inherent social graph. You can easily understand this by looking at KakaoPage and Line Home, which feature timelines similar to traditional social networks like Facebook. The fact that Facebook and Twitter, strong players in PC-based social networking, grew even larger with mobile also clearly demonstrates this.

**2. Vertical Social Networks Defining Identity**
However, human relationships are formed with diverse needs. Throughout our lives, we form relationships under specific conditions: romantic relationships, club memberships, work relationships. Social networks are effective in strengthening all these relationships. But offline, in certain gatherings, people play specific roles, and expressing all of these within a single social network tends to be confusing for both the viewer and the user. Therefore, in actual social networks, people operate with a specific identity they desire, which can determine the nature of the social network service regardless of the planner's intention. KakaoPage, which became a parenting forum, or Google Plus, where geeks gathered in Korea, are examples of such services. This is sometimes determined by the app's characteristics or its market position.
Conversely, there are cases where a specific role is defined from the planning stage to aim for success, and these are called vertical SNS. Here, what needs to be discussed is very clear. Between, an SNS for couples, or LinkedIn, for professional networking, fall into this category. And Peoplegate, which I will introduce today, is also one of them. {{IMG:1}} **[ Not just friends, but kind friends ]** {{IMG:2}}**[ Welcome these kinds of friends! ]**

**3. Peoplegate: A Vertical SNS with the Clear Purpose of Talent Donation**
To first praise Peoplegate, it is a social network with the very clear purpose of talent donation. It's the concept of sharing what you're good at to do something meaningful and fun together.
When you start the app, you can set your talents like tags and write a brief resume like LinkedIn.
Furthermore, as vertical social networks target clear niche markets, their market size is limited. To overcome this, it's good to go global. The ability to toggle an overseas option for this purpose was also an interesting approach.

**3. Disadvantage: Initial Entry Barrier is Too High**
However, what I felt while testing this app was that the initial entry barrier was too high. It's not easy to get users to install an app, but it's even harder to get them to stay after installing it. Attracting users and making them return determines the success of an app.
**1) Unable to find acquaintances based on phone numbers**
First, what stood out was the inability to find friends based on phone numbers. {{IMG:3}}**[ It's not because I'm an outcast that I don't have friends. ]**{{IMG:4}}**[ Playing alone ]** The scenario of "My friend is doing such a good deed? Should I try it too?" doesn't happen. Facebook pushes known faces from the initial signup via email, and KakaoTalk via phone number, but with Peoplegate, you end up talking to yourself at first. It's perfect for introverted people to just delete it.
Securing a social graph through all available means like Facebook, Line, Kakao, and phone numbers is an absolutely essential feature for a late-comer social network.
**2) Need for basic sticker provision**
Stickers also seem to need an initial allowance. It's not even sold for a fee, yet initial users can't use stickers, which also unintentionally raises the initial entry barrier. It's sad enough not to have friends, but not being able to use stickers too...

**4. Need for Feature Improvements for Talent Donation**
**1) Difficult to find friends with specific talents**
Perhaps because it's not active, even with a clear purpose, it's hard to find friends for a specific purpose initially. When searching for "app development," I could find people with that talent, but when I clicked on their chat rooms, their timelines only showed old posts, indicating no continuously active users. {{IMG:5}} **[ Searched for app development, but most accounts were inactive... ]**
It's difficult to know how much and in what way a user can truly donate their talent, and you can't just approach a stranger blindly.
Therefore, specific information settings for talent exchange seem necessary. At least information like available times and locations should be included. And of course, it should be searchable by that information. {{IMG:6}}**[ Setting a talent is one thing, but having the intention to provide it is another ]**
**2) Project Room Needed**
Talent donation often involves talents coming together to plan a single project, but it seems that if people want to collaborate through Peoplegate, they have to use other private SNS like Band. Therefore, a feature centered around groups (projects) rather than individuals seems necessary. And for a platform to succeed, creating success stories using the platform is paramount. It seems necessary to discover and highlight such success stories through these project rooms. {{IMG:7}} **[ Despite being a talent donation vertical, only usage patterns similar to Kakao Story are seen ]**
There are also services like Tumblbug that are entirely project-centric instead of social networks, which might be good to refer to.

**4. Hope for Peoplegate to Become a Global Korean Social Network Dreaming of a Warmer World**
Recently, Korean social networks like Kakao, Line, and Band are making strong global inroads. It's very encouraging that Korean services are succeeding globally, moving beyond just being an IT infrastructure powerhouse. In the case of Peoplegate, since it's a service that capitalizes on people's spare time, it seems like a truly challenging app in Korea, except for the student demographic. I even wonder if it might be better suited for Western countries where volunteer culture is common. I hope Peoplegate refines itself well and transforms into a global service, not just a Korean one. {{IMG:8}}**[ The problem is, even when I open it, finding overseas friends still seems difficult ]** I also have something I'd like to try together – should I find app developers through Peoplegate?
