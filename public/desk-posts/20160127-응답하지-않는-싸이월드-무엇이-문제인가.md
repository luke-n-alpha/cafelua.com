Cyworld recently appeared on a crowdfunding platform. The goal is 500 million KRW, with 63 investors and 30 days remaining. Approximately 5.24 million KRW has been raised. 1. I don't know what they're trying to build.

Crowdfunding needs something that makes you go 'Wow!', but I don't know what that is here. BGM, travel photos, food photos... the direction isn't wrong, but what's Cyworld's unique strength? Why specifically choose Cyworld? What's the reason it can't be KakaoStory?

2. Are they willing to communicate with the people who will respond? Social is about stories, not numbers.

Secondly, the people who will respond are likely current Cyworld customers. The question is how actively they are communicating with them.

[**- YouTube** Watch your favorite videos and music on YouTube, upload your own content, and share it with friends, family, and people around the world. youtu.be](http://www.youtube.com/embed/czdc136m5NE)

However, looking at Wadiz's page, it's full of messages like, 'We've come to our senses now. We'll be fine going forward...' And they're showing it with numbers. If it were aimed at people who know whether Cyworld is worth 5 billion KRW or not, they would already be pursuing VC or angel investments. I'm sorry, but the people who will respond don't look at numbers. If they wanted to appeal to recognition or tell a story, they should have gone with a proper story, an emotion-based approach. Crowdfunding is a marketing tool and a promotional channel. Even with a small amount, it's about actively letting people experience that there are many others who share their thoughts, achieving something together. However, the current situation has had the opposite effect. Try searching for "Cyworld" in an internet search engine and see what results come up. Crowdfunding should be thought of as a campaign, not an investment tool. Therefore, the use of social media, including blogs and SNS, should be more active simultaneously. Just because Cyworld itself is social media doesn't mean they shouldn't use existing social media channels, right? If that's the thinking, it raises questions about whether Cyworld still has a feel for social media. Social is about stories. Additionally, if crowdfunding is approached more as marketing and a campaign rather than 'investment,' the current bank transfer method is complicated. Wadiz should also consider making it simpler, perhaps a one-click process.
