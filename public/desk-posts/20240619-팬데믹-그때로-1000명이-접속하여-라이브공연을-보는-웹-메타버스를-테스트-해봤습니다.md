Do you remember 2020, four years ago? In February 2020, the COVID-19 infectious disease alert level was raised to 'Severe'. Everyone thought it would end quickly, but the 'Severe' alert remained in place until April 2022. During that period, social distancing and outdoor mask-wearing were mandatory, and many offline events were canceled. As you all know, what rapidly gained attention during that time was the metaverse. For meetings that couldn't happen offline, the metaverse demanded real-time synchronization for many online activities that had previously been asynchronous, like web bulletin boards, transforming them into something akin to online meeting solutions. Game-type metaverses, in particular, which promised 3D environment synchronization and mass access, garnered significant attention and saw their value increase. In April 2022, the 'Severe' alert was lifted, the pandemic ended, and now it's hard to find people wearing masks on the streets as daily life has returned to normal. So, what happened to the value of the metaverse left behind by the pandemic? While popular platforms like Roblox and Fortnite are still doing well, the rosy future we envisioned didn't materialize, and many metaverse companies announced the termination of their businesses. Does that mean the metaverse had no value? I don't think so. I believe the metaverse, having become complex, was simply too heavy to create sustained value.

It's often said that the value of IT technology lies in either providing new experiences or reducing costs. Among these, the metaverse spoke of new experiences, but in reality, it didn't significantly reduce costs. Especially because it emphasized high-quality graphics, the costs were ridiculously high. Furthermore, it ignored the basic engineering fact that a large number of concurrent users and lightweight software have an inverse relationship, talking as if thousands of people could be invited into a real-world-like virtual reality. Especially with the addition of Web3, capital-driven marketing intensified, burying serious engineering attempts.

Belivvr is still attempting the metaverse, striving for a cheaper and more accessible version. In fact, during the pandemic, there were very few companies or services with actual experience hosting large-scale events in the metaverse. However, Belivvr successfully hosted several large-scale events, including Hyundai Department Store Group's ComingM and Chonnam National University's entrance and graduation ceremonies. At the time, due to the rushed nature, quantitative measurements were insufficient, and we primarily relied on AWS infrastructure resources to simply get through the event period. Of course, this was possible because we built our WebRTC architecture directly on cloud infrastructure to provide the service.

One of the conditions for the government projects we are currently undertaking and for attracting investment is mass connectivity, so we are giving this aspect more thought. Also, as a small company (currently even a one-person operation), rather than focusing on marketing, we aim to share the evolving state of our service almost in real-time, conveying the true level and vision of our technology without exaggeration or omission.
