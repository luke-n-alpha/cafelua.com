Someone asked for my additional opinion on Yello Mobile via email, so I looked into it further. Previously, I expressed some concerns about aggressive growth through M&A, but after looking into recent developments, I felt there were a few more things worth evaluating, so I'm writing this post. I found the following content in an article.

Usually, when M&A occurs, people think two companies completely merge into one to create synergy. However, hearing the story above, it seems like a completely different approach. It seems to have more of a purpose in lowering operational risks by sharing infrastructure-related aspects and the 'blood' of the company, such as lack of experience, personnel issues, and funding problems, which are among the biggest risks for venture companies. It seems they are actually experimenting with the "mesh up" of corporate environmental infrastructure that I discussed in my old blog post about creating a Korean-style Silicon Valley. If we were to create a Silicon Valley in Korea, where and how would we do it? [http://fstory97.blog.me/70138711965](http://fstory97.blog.me/70138711965) Whether that can only be achieved through M&A is a bit questionable, but it feels like mixing blood to share some of the assets. It seems to act like an agency for writers or celebrities. Anyway, this also seems like an interesting experiment. However, it might not be closely related to the explosive growth created through M&A synergy that investors expect. Rather, **the synergy of M&A seems to be aimed at enabling individual companies to perform better in their respective fields.** If even one company secures a "national app" in Korea or makes a big splash globally, the other companies will be able to operate much more stably. Just like Naver thrives on search and Kakao on Talk. The survival methods of ventures seem to be evolving more and more. This also implies that it's not easy.
