This is something I've always wanted to talk about.
Previously, I used Cyworld, which was synonymous with 'pumjil' (copy-pasting), and currently, I use a blog.
Then as now, I engage in a bit of 'pumjil' and scrapbooking, and I've also written informational posts (because I'm a programmer) and drawn pictures and written articles due to my hobbies.
I believe this gives me some right to speak on the matter.
While searching for data on portals like Naver and Cyworld on other blogs, I want to discuss the arrogance towards portal users that is widespread among bloggers.

**1. Google is excellent. Self-hosted blogs are excellent.**

- As an engineer, the answer is yes.
Google's search engine performance is truly remarkable. However, if you ask whether it made significant contributions to the development of the internet in Korea? The answer is no.
What was Windows 95's contribution? Of course, from an engineer's perspective, it was an excellent OS even then. But, if you say it was a commercial adoption of existing excellent technology, that is also true.
However, Windows 95's even greater contribution was that it dramatically increased computer usage among light users. What was Mac, which was said to be easy to use, doing then? Didn't they remain stuck with the perception of being educational devices used by intellectuals and tools for work, or expensive devices for the privileged? That's why I actually consider Apple's greatest work to be Apple II, not OS-X. When Apple II came out, its versatility and lowering of the user barrier were comparable to Windows 95. However, being overtaken by IBM and MS afterward wasn't because they were inferior, but because they failed to quickly move to the next stage when they were doing well. They came out with tremendous programs even after that. Excellent. But they were too fast for the market, and marketing couldn't keep up. Users were still not ready to understand such systems. Why would you expect users to understand the system?
In fact, Windows wasn't that much easier a system compared to Mac. However, the big difference was that it implied even someone who knew nothing could use a computer, and it worked wonderfully. Just like doctors tricking patients into believing an ordinary medicine is excellent, leading to self-recovery.
Google is excellent. But it's not user-friendly. Because of this, Korean users, who were far less sophisticated than excellent users overseas, found it difficult to dive into the world of computers. Even now, when I need information not found on Naver, I go to Google. But for 90% of users, Naver alone is sufficient. (90% are not engineers, scholars, or students. Many have never even opened File Explorer.)
Self-hosted blogs are the same. If customization is 'Best,' then code it yourself. Why run someone else's source code? If you want the features of a self-hosted blog, use it. But isn't it true that portals can't put in 50% of the effort to satisfy 10% of the needs?
What actually changed Korea's internet market was... not Naver, but Daum. ㅡㅡ; (That's the historical order. And Google didn't even exist then.) And as you know, Korean light users, accustomed to portal services, nurtured the Korean internet. Furthermore, it's also true that web programmers were mass-produced to solve government employment issues, leading to Korea suffering more chronically from so-called ActiveX or non-standard issues.
However, to speak frankly...
It is a clear fact that due to the high growth achieved by Park Chung-hee in the 1960s, economic issues arose because they couldn't be fixed in time, leading to the IMF crisis. Korea's internet history has also experienced similar growing pains. IT powerhouse? In fact, it's clear that Korea's automobile/steel/electronics industries also failed to secure many core technologies due to rapid growth. But if we keep pointing fingers at that... what on earth did Korea live on? It's a question.
Moreover, when I occasionally hear that the government supports Google, it's truly an absurd statement. They should rather support Daum or SK Comms.

**2. Portals rampant with scrapbooking and 'pumjil' are low-quality and full of thieves.**

In Korea, 'pumjil' and scrapbooking are a culture.
Of course, that doesn't grant absolution for copyright infringement. However, there's no reason to criticize even the scrapbooking and 'pumjil' of documents that are not copyrighted. It's because no specific system-level mechanism for copyright protection readily comes to mind. Would portals really like to be embroiled in copyright disputes? Also, as an engineer, I have a desire to save good material from others so I can refer to it personally anytime. You could criticize if your hard drive contains no Word or Hangeul files written by others, or no MP3 files.
Of course, as mentioned earlier, copyright infringement is a crime, beyond being a matter of low quality. However, portals don't abet because they want to. Does P2P abet because it wants to? Not only is the P2P system itself designed that way, but fundamentally, the structure of digital content and the Internet itself is vulnerable in those areas. Therefore, it can only be resolved within the bounds of users' moral conscience, culture, and law.
And portals would also try to find a way to protect it if one existed.
Because it's justice? No.
It's because it will inevitably become profitable in the future.

**3. Netizens who upload food photos and happy photos are self-deceiving**

Perhaps. Couples take photos and upload them, people upload photos of cool places they've visited, and what do they upload to the internet to show off... 'doenjangnyeo' (a derogatory term for a materialistic woman).
That's right. It's an act of self-satisfaction.
But isn't there an element of self-satisfaction in bloggers' act of posting articles? Do so-called great artists have no acts of self-satisfaction? What about great democratic activists?
There are entertainment films like D-War or Transformers, and then there are more cultural films by directors like Im Kwon-taek. However, you can't say which of the two is superior. That's because film is a mirror that expresses society through its medium. You can't always just study, nor can you always just play.
Do those who make great posts and criticize low-quality posts not have albums with their families? Don't they drink with friends or crack jokes? Don't they take a single photo as proof of having visited a travel destination?
The internet is also a mirror of society. Various behavioral patterns are reflected on the internet, merely expressing one's desires. Humans are animals that express themselves and live by receiving reactions. It's just that their methods of expression are different from yours.
In fact, I also uploaded a lot of material of that nature on Cyworld. Strictly speaking, at that time, I was in a more emotionally difficult situation, so I had more emotional thoughts than rational ones. The more so, the stronger the desire to express oneself and to be seen by someone, and through that connection with people, one finds comfort and sometimes even helps others.
Rather than cool... long, calm, and sharp posts, let's remember that a short comment like "Cheer up." can save a person.

ps. This is not directed at all bloggers. Many people write posts honestly about their feelings. That is right. Because, as I said, the internet reflects society and individuals. However, if you held such thoughts, I wrote this article with the hope that you would understand others a little more.
