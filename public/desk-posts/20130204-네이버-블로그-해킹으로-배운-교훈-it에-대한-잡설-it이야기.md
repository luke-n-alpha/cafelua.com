**1. Oh no. My blog was hacked!**
Yesterday, I was startled by the push notification from the Naver app. I discovered that a large number of adult and gambling spam ads had been posted on my blog, and comments and guestbook entries suspected it was a hack. Since it was Sunday, I was about to put my child to bed and go to sleep early, but I quickly booted up my PC and tried to access Naver. Fortunately, the password for the hijacked account hadn't been changed. First, I urgently changed the password, deleted the spam posts, and reverted the changed profile. As a result, it seems that those who subscribe to my blog posts have suffered significant inconvenience. [http://dna.daum.net/lens](http://dna.daum.net/lens) must have been flooded... (I apologize {{IMG:1}})

**2. How was I hacked?**
Looking at the access logs, the red-marked parts below were the suspicious paths.

2013/02/03 (Sun) 20:37 59.14.90.50 PC (Web)
**2013/02/03 (Sun) 20:01 112.204.143.178 PC (Web)**
2013/02/03 (Sun) 19:44 175.223.2.10 Mobile (Blog App)
2013/02/03 (Sun) 18:34 175.223.2.10 Mobile (Blog App)
**2013/02/03 (Sun) 15:56 112.204.143.178 PC (Web)**
**2013/02/03 (Sun) 15:25 112.204.143.178 PC (Web)**

Seeing the access logs around 3 PM, it seems they only accessed it then and didn't do anything. (This makes me even more uneasy... What were they reading?) They started working around 8 PM, and I became aware of the problem via a push notification around 8:30 PM, changing my password around 8:40 PM. They wreaked havoc on my blog for about 40 minutes, and what's more frightening is that the account was hijacked for over 4 hours. There's no trace of mass spam emails being sent, but I'm a bit worried they might have taken data from N-drive.

I didn't have time to go to a PC bang, nor do I usually download strange software, and I use different passwords for my Naver account and other miscellaneous accounts, so I was relatively complacent. The suspected scenario is that my password was stolen from a site where I used the same password, and then they tried to access my portal account. I heard Twitter was hacked recently, but my Twitter password is different.

**3. The Importance of Portal Accounts**
However, this incident made me realize the importance of my Naver account. Even for a blog used to freely write and share thoughts rather than for high added value, I realized that if an account is hijacked, a major incident can occur in an instant. The same would be true for work. Fortunately, I didn't keep company data on N-drive. (What a close call...) Since a corporate cloud service for work was introduced a while ago, I've been managing work documents and personal documents separately.

**4. A Naver Account that Cannot Be Protected by Password Alone, Let's Protect it with OTP.**
Ultimately, I decided to use Naver OTP. I also felt that the security measures of a single device couldn't be trusted. Against attack methods like keylogging or phishing, no security technology can fundamentally protect passwords. My judgment is that a heterogeneous security device like OTP is still the safest. I thought it might be overkill when OTP was integrated into the Naver app, but it wasn't. ㅜㅜ It was a truly necessary measure.

Since Naver has launched **nWorks service, I believe it's even more crucial to pay attention to the security of Naver accounts. I especially recommend that those considering nWorks definitely use OTP.**

For those whose Naver accounts are truly important, I strongly recommend using OTP. Security-level login may not work properly on heterogeneous browsers, and if your PC is compromised, dangerous things can still happen. First, go to any app market on your smartphone and install the Naver app (it's not on T-store; due to T-store's terms and conditions, the Naver app couldn't pass their review).

**5. I Apologize.**
Finally, I apologize once again for the inconvenience. This incident seems to have taught me a great lesson. If my blog had been completely deleted, my blogging activities would probably have ended. ㅜㅜ I'm still worried that my personal address book and personal information might have been stolen, but I'm truly relieved that it ended here. I hope you use my case as a mirror for safe internet activity. I am truly grateful to the neighbors who sent me push notifications. I owe you a great debt.
