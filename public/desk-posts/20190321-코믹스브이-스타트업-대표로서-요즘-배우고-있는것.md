고용되는 직원들과 협력하는 많은 회사들 사이에는 정말로 "이상한"사람들이 꽤 많다는 점이었죠.

모든 만남이 가치가 있는것이 아니고, 모든 사람이 순수한 의도를 인정하지 않는다는 것을 깨달았고, 이러한 사람들 혹은 기업간의 관계는 꽤나 피곤하고 회사의 소중한 기회들을 해칠 수 있다는것을 알았습니다.

때문에 만남을 더 소중히 여기고, 신중해야 할것 같습니다.​ 두번째는 "관계"를 이어가는 방법입니다.

이 부분 역시 그저 좋은 아이템을 발굴하고 구현해내는 실력이 결정하지 않습니다.

큰 기업에 있었을때는 그 관계를 이어가고자하는 노력을 안합니다.

왜냐면 그 노력은 관계를 맺고자 하는 많은 조직/사람들이 대신 해주고 있었기 때문이죠.

When I founded ComicsV, I was quite confident because I had selected a good item, had some clumsy experience as a PM, knew well about creating services with various people, had a network, and because I came from a development background, I could even back up the implementation when needed, so I thought I knew most things. I thought all that was left was how to build and operate a company.

However, as I passed the two-year mark, I realized something new. The story from an angel investor during the initial founding was, "I've watched him for several years, and I'm investing because he's not a strange person." And recently, the answer was, "We're collaborating because we've seen that he's not a strange person." At first, I didn't understand why "not a strange person" rather than "competent" should be a condition for collaboration, but I only recently grasped its meaning. It was the fact that there are quite a lot of truly "strange" people among employees and many collaborating companies. This was a point I couldn't feel when I was in the "superior" position (as a client/larger company) in the past. And I think it might be difficult for student founders to realize this meaning as well. Now I understand the reason why I need to distinguish this "not strange person," or rather, "trustworthy person," and that I had never learned this skill before. I realized that not every encounter is valuable, and not everyone acknowledges pure intentions, and that relationships with such people or companies can be quite exhausting and harm the company's valuable opportunities. Therefore, I think I need to cherish encounters more and be more cautious.

The second is how to maintain "relationships." I feel like I'm just starting to learn this, but if you've discerned "not strange people or organizations," the next step is to build "lasting relationships." This part, too, is not simply determined by the skill of discovering and implementing good items. When I was at a large company, I didn't make an effort to maintain those relationships. This is because many organizations/people who wanted to establish relationships were doing that effort for me. Therefore, it's difficult to learn this skill. It seems that the reason why successful companies often have weaker sales organizations is similar.

I've learned that while "items" (products/services) and "technology" are important for a startup, ultimately "people and relationships" are what truly matter. I also feel that because no member can be perfect, we need to leverage each other's strengths and provide mutual backup. Anyway, I was lucky enough to have people who believed in me, which allowed me to start, and with team members who still believe in me, I persevere, learn, and grow.

And sometimes, when I find even very young founders who already understand and manage these things well, I feel a sense of respect. Even if one has a bit more experience, knowledge, or brilliant ideas, a startup is a company before it is an item, and it's a place where employees and people live. These areas cannot be represented by team members with impressive IR (Investor Relations) careers. I also have experience passing public recruitment exams for quite good companies, but I am confident that our employees, who have gone through a selection process and are enduring tough times with us, are friends who would perform above average among the successful candidates of those good companies. However, due to lower paper qualifications, inability to present themselves well, or a bit of bad luck, they couldn't go there, and we are unable to provide them with the same level of treatment.

Anyway, we started with luck, and we are proceeding with luck. Even if something happens to the company, I would probably say this experience was incredibly fortunate in my life. The best thing would be for this luck to lead to the luck of success.

I write this from the perspective of someone who has gone just a little ahead, for those who dream of a startup... or who dream of entrepreneurship with only a great item or great technology. And I express my respect to senior entrepreneurs who have already learned these things and gone ahead. I hope and expect to learn more in the future and to be with even better people.
