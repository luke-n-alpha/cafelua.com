유쾌한 멀티라이더님의 포스팅에서 닌텐도에서 안경없는 3d기술을 도입할거라는 포스팅([안경없는3D?](http://www.multiwriter.co.kr/709) 정말 기발한 닌텐도의 깜짝쇼! )을  보았습니다. 해서 한참 안경없는 모바일 3d기기에 관해 생각해봤는데 근 미래에 나올 모바일기기의 3d기술은 최근 아바타에서 보였던 3d기술과는 개념이 전혀 다를거라는 개인적인 생각이 들었습니다.
그 내용을 이야기하기 이전에 본 포스팅에서는 먼저 3d란 무언가에 관해 말씀드리겠습니다. 제가 이해하는 수준이니 너무 태클걸지 마시고 보완할 부분이나 틀린부분은 지적해주시기 바랍니다.

**1. 컴퓨터그래픽스의 3d? 2d?**
먼저 컴퓨터 그래픽스의 3d란 아바타에서 본 3d와는 개념이 다릅니다. 최근 온라인 게임이 3d게임이라고 말하는데 그것은 그래픽 소스가 xy비트맵방식이 아니라 xyz값이 있는 벡터소스임을 의미합니다. (벡터와 비트맵이 뭐냐고 물으면 곤란합니다. 너무 길어져요. 찾아보세요.)
{{IMG:1}}
**[ 3D 그래픽을 만드는 그래픽툴인 3D Max ]**
이 벡터 소스는 컴퓨터의 가상카메라를 기준으로 2차원이미지가 만들어지고, 이 이미지는 역시 2차원적인 디스플레이 장치인 lcd나 브라운관을 통해 우리가 보게 되는데 이때 최종적인 값은 역시 2차원 비트맵값으로 출력됩니다. 다만 마우스를 움직여서 카메라를 이동시킨다던지 할때 3d오브젝트들의 이미지가 변화되는 모습을 통해 3d임을 느끼게 하는 것이죠. 사실 아바타가 아닌 일반 영화도 컴퓨터그래픽스 관점에서 보면 이미 3d오브젝트를 스크린에 담는 다는 점에서는 이미 3d영화인것입니다. 그런점에서 2차원 그림 소스를 연속으로 보여주는 방식의 구셀에니메이션은 2d영화고 기존 영화와 구별되는 점이죠.
****
** 2. 체감형 3d, HCI 기술의 등장**
그럼 아바타의 차이는 무엇일까요 ? 아바타는 영상소스의 3d화가 아니라 최종 사용자에게 3d란 느낌을 전달한데 있습니다.그럼 이 3d란 느낌은 무엇일까요? 사실 사람의 눈은 2차원만 처리가 가능합니다. 망막은 2차원으로 배열되어 있기 때문이죠. 그러나 사람은 태어날때부터 그것을 아는 것이 아니라 여러 인지생활을 통해 3d곧 깊이란 개념을 학습하는 것입니다. 선천적 시각장애인이 갑자기 시력을 회복하는 경우 사진의 물체와 실제 물체를 구별하지 못하는데 이것은 눈으로 비치는 영상을 보지못했기 때문에 학습을 하지 못한것이죠.  HCI 란 Human Coupter Interface의 약자로서 인간 중심적으로 컴퓨터를 만들고자 하는 학문입니다. 기존의 키보드와 디스플레이는 어떤 process를 위한 장치였다면, 터치나 음성 인간 본연의 인지 기술들을 쓰거나 사람의 심리나 행동패턴등이 바로 이 범위에 들어갑니다.

I saw a post by the delightful Multiwriter about Nintendo introducing glasses-free 3D technology ([Glasses-free 3D?](http://www.multiwriter.co.kr/709) A truly ingenious surprise show from Nintendo!). This led me to think for a while about glasses-free mobile 3D devices, and I personally believe that the 3D technology in mobile devices coming out in the near future will be completely different in concept from the 3D technology seen recently in Avatar.

Before discussing that content, in this post, I will first talk about what 3D is. This is my level of understanding, so please don't nitpick too much, but feel free to point out any areas that need improvement or are incorrect.

**1. 3D? 2D? in Computer Graphics**
First, 3D in computer graphics is different in concept from the 3D seen in Avatar. When people say recent online games are 3D games, it means that the graphic source is not an XY bitmap method, but a vector source with XYZ values. (It's difficult if you ask what vectors and bitmaps are; it would take too long. Please look it up.)
{{IMG:1}}
**[ 3D Max, a graphic tool for creating 3D graphics ]**
This vector source creates a 2D image based on the computer's virtual camera, and this image is viewed by us through 2D display devices like LCDs or CRTs, where the final output is also a 2D bitmap value. However, when you move the mouse to move the camera, the changing appearance of the 3D objects' images makes you feel that it is 3D. In fact, even regular movies, not Avatar, are already 3D films from a computer graphics perspective, in that they capture 3D objects on screen. In that sense, traditional cel animation, which shows a series of 2D image sources, is a 2D film and is distinguished from existing films.

**2. Experiential 3D, the Emergence of HCI Technology**
So what's the difference with Avatar? Avatar isn't about making the video source 3D, but about conveying a 3D sensation to the end-user. So what is this 3D sensation? In fact, the human eye can only process 2D. This is because the retina is arranged in 2D. However, humans don't know this from birth; they learn the concept of 3D, or depth, through various cognitive experiences. When a congenitally blind person suddenly regains sight, they cannot distinguish between objects in a photograph and real objects because they haven't seen images with their eyes and thus haven't learned. HCI stands for Human-Computer Interface, a field that aims to create human-centered computers. While existing keyboards and displays were devices for a certain process, touch, voice, and other inherent human cognitive technologies, as well as human psychology and behavioral patterns, fall within this scope.

**3. What do humans perceive as 3D?**
Now, I'll briefly explain the representative elements that make humans perceive something as 3D.

**First. Change in object size when gaze shifts**
This is also felt in current 3D sources. When a character runs, distant objects change relatively slowly, while nearby objects change significantly. This is because distant objects appear smaller. Humans, being animals that constantly move, bump into things, and touch them from birth, come to understand this. If plants had eyes, perhaps they wouldn't know this sensation.
{{IMG:2}}
**[ Perspective in painting ]**

**Second. Differences in object color and saturation due to light source and air**
This is what's known as the 'fog effect' in 3D graphics. Air also absorbs and scatters light, which is why distant scenery appears to have different colors. The subtle changes learned from the distance between the light source and the reflecting object are what humans perceive as depth.
{{IMG:3}}
**[ Background of the game TERA, featuring atmospheric perspective: Source Thisgame.com]**

**Third. Focus**
This is what's called 'focus.' The iris of the human eye acts as an aperture, determining distance. When the aperture is a certain size, light from a specific distance becomes clear, while light from other distances scatters. This is the concept of focus in cameras. 'Bokeh,' used in portrait photography, is also a technique that utilizes this principle. Modern cameras' built-in autofocus uses this effect to move the aperture and find the point where the image at the target coordinates becomes clear. Therefore, when you look at the foreground, the background blurs, and when you look at the background, the foreground blurs. This technique is widely used in actual films, and in 3D graphics, distant or near views are blurred according to camera settings.
{{IMG:4}}
**[ Interesting photo using depth perception through focus ]**
**[Source: **[**Fun Photo Taking, Camera Shooting Tips to Add Depth**](http://www.stylezineblog.com/659)** ] **

**Fourth. Binocular image difference**
The first, second, and third points refer to traditional perspective in 2D images. This fourth point is the method seen recently in 3D TVs and Avatar, which analyzes the image difference caused by the distance between the two eyes. If you alternately close one eye, you can feel the difference between the two images. This is precisely the image difference due to movement, as shown in the first point.
{{IMG:5}}
**[ Odd-eyed cat: It has two eyes to perceive depth. **
** In the case of fish, since they don't look in the same direction, their eyes developed to prioritize field of vision rather than depth perception. ]**

**4. Development of 3D Devices**
And surprisingly, our brain processes all of the above in real-time. It might even mobilize smell, temperature, or even 'qi'? Bats use ultrasound. There are also individual differences; athletes, for example, might have more developed senses. The speed at which the human eye autofocuses is astonishing, and it can detect even subtle differences in light. A camera cannot see stars and the moon simultaneously, but the human eye can. This is why 3D technologies find it challenging to trick the human eye into seeing true 3D. However, devices have continuously evolved.

**a. Camera and Film**
I believe the initial revolution in 3D was the camera and film. Cameras resemble many aspects of the human eye. They could focus and record color light, thus achieving a lot. However, the limitation of cameras is that the final image is 2D.

[ Camera and Eye: Source [Crystal Eye Clinic](http://www.crystaleye.co.kr/) ]
**b. Display Devices**
The second revolution was display devices that enabled video. The camera, which was fixed while viewing pictures, finally began to move. As this transitioned into computer games, it became possible to move freely using devices like a mouse.

**c. Experiential Cognitive Technologies HCI**
Now, the time has come for the third revolution: experiential, cognitive technologies. These are the technologies used in Avatar 3D, AR, and so on, which I will discuss as a separate topic in the entire next post. I will also cover how they could be utilized in future NDSLs.
