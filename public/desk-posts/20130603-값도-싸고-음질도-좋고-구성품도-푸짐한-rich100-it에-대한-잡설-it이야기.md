This is my second earphone review. To be honest, I'm pretty much an 'average listener' (not an audiophile). I remember being surprised recently when I received a 200,000 won Bluetooth earphone and realized how much sound quality could differ. This time, I received a request to introduce an entry-level earphone released by T-PEOS, a company that makes Hi-Fi earphones. My first impression is this: {{IMG:1}}
**The price is 18,900 won including shipping... If you calculate the 2,500 won shipping fee, the product itself costs 16,400 won.** This is almost the same price as the lowest price for the QuadBeat, the bundled earphone for the Optimus G, which is recently known as the king of price-to-performance. In terms of design, I don't particularly like the LG logo on the QuadBeat, so I'd say this is a clear win over the QuadBeat. It also comes in red and black, so there's a wider range of choices. It's said to be nickel-plated, and the shiny metallic trim looks pretty.

I'm not a professional review blogger, so please bear with the photos. They look pretty in person. Ugh. I'll just add some official product photos.{{IMG:2}} The bundled earphones for my Galaxy S2 were too short, but these are long enough. {{IMG:3}}The length up to the split is a generous 83cm. Ample length. This should be long enough to put in a bag and pull out. If it's too long, you can use the included clip. In addition to the main unit, it comes with a pouch and foam tips to enhance ear fit.

{{IMG:4}} The accessories are really generous for the price. {{IMG:5}}And tangle-free flat-type earphones
The most important aspect of earphones, sound quality, is definitely incomparable to the Galaxy S2 bundled earphones. However, it feels a bit lacking compared to Hi-Fi earphones costing hundreds of thousands of won. But that said, it feels like a different class altogether compared to regular bundled earphones. I wanted to compare it with the QuadBeat, but I couldn't get one. According to a review by someone on SEEKO, they even said the QuadBeat felt bland in comparison. The earphones I did compare it with were the iPhone earphones, which are known for good sound quality, and they were quite similar in level. (Because I'm an average listener...) I think this is a good earphone for those who feel the sound quality of their bundled earphones is lacking, or for those who want an earphone to plug into their computer, without breaking the bank. I also replaced the earphones for my work laptop with these.

Of course, it supports hands-free functionality for smartphones. When listening to music, if you use the hands-free button, one press plays/pauses, and two presses move to the next song. The saying 'you get what you pay for' is now an old saying. For users like me who frequently break earphones, these affordable and good quality earphones are truly the best choice. (Of course, you shouldn't break them.)
