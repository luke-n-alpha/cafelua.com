Today, I heard two pieces of news through my timeline. The first was that the service of Lezhin Comics, a promising domestic venture in the webtoon industry, was blocked as harmful content by the Korea Communications Commission (KCC). The second was that after the public i-PIN hacking incident, the cause of the hacking was attributed to insufficient maintenance costs, admitting to poor management. Let me explain why these stories are so frustrating (literally, 'cancer-inducing'). It seems that the response level of each department completely fails to grasp the seriousness of the situation, and I even feel that organizations lacking 'qualifications' compared to their 'responsibilities' are in charge.

**1. First, the frustrating news: Lezhin Comics blocked by Warning.or.kr.**
Lezhin Comics was suddenly blocked without prior notice, according to the standards of the Korea Communications Standards Commission (KCSC). Lezhin Comics is an adult webtoon service that has made significant contributions to popularizing the paid business model for webtoons and mainstreaming subcultures. It was selected as a Global K-Startup in 2013 by the Ministry of Science, ICT and Future Planning, and is a promising venture leading the Hallyu (Korean Wave) of webtoons alongside Naver Webtoon. However, as I mentioned, since it dealt with adult content, there was always a possibility of receiving a warning. But the fact that it was excessively sudden without prior consultation, and seeing their response, I find it utterly absurd. There were rumors that the reason for the block was a specific gag webtoon that caricatured genitalia, but this was not an official announcement, and there were many adult webtoons of that nature, including those by famous webtoon artist Yang*Soon, so it's unreasonable to attribute it specifically to that cause. **1) Japanese manga is the problem.**
" "It appears that Japanese manga were serviced domestically with only translation and no illustration modifications, and there were quite a few such manga," they explained. "We deliberated on Lezhin Comics and also extensively on the realm of expression in manga, but did Lezhin Comics fulfill its social responsibility by providing numerous Japanese manga that do not align with Korean sentiment, entirely and without youth protection measures, and even offering samples?" The reason I got angry while reading this is:
**- "It appears that Japanese manga were serviced domestically with only translation and no illustration modifications."** **Modifying illustrations is like censorship from an artist's perspective.** Moreover, **it's absurd to even request illustration modifications for adult content.** In the past, public broadcasters committed many ridiculous acts, like having Sailor Moon wear pants. I'll let that slide. Because it was a *public* broadcast. Lezhin Comics targets mobile platforms, which are personalized devices, and is an adult service that requires age verification. This is utterly shameless.
**- "Japanese manga that do not align with Korean sentiment."** What exactly is "Korean sentiment"? The outrageous storylines seen in morning dramas?
