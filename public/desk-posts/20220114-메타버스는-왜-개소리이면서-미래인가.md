그리고 이러한 환경을 위해서는 안정된 네트워크인 5G등의 네트워크 기술이 필요로하고, 디바이스는 VR/AR과 같은 차세대 기기가 등장할 겁니다. 물론, 이러한 환경이 원할하게 펼쳐지는데는 당장은 아닐 수도 있습니다. 하지만 상당수는 이미 가능한 시점에 왔으며 의미있는 결과들을 만들고 있습니다. 그리고, 이 기술들이 대부분은 하늘에서 뚝 떨어진게 아니라 전통적인 SW와 인터넷 기술에서 발전한겁니다. 메타버스로 가고 싶은데 무얼공부해냐 하고 묻는경우가 있는데, 기존의 기술들을 공부하고 더 공부해야 하는게 사실 메타버스입니다. 때문에 기존의 SW와 IT의 역량 없이 메타버스만 갑자기 더 잘하겠다거나, 혹은 메타버스만 잘하면 경쟁력이나 내실이 있다고 생각하면 안됩니다. 다시 말씀드리지만 메타버스는 뜰 수 있겠지만, 메타버스를 잘하려면 기존의 기술은 원래 잘해야 합니다.  메타버스가 개소리다 라고 말하는 것은 대부분 기존 기술의 무시나 혹은 부실한 기술위에 허황된 미래를 이야기하기 때문이라고 생각합니다.​좀 장황하게 설명드렸는데, 요약하자면, 일부의 메타버스는 개소리 맞습니다. 온전한 메타버스가 아니거든요.하지만, 메타버스의 미래는 존재하며 생각보다 기술면에서 진지하게 고민해야 되며 놀랄 만큼 발전하고 있습니다. 빌리버도 더 재밌는 것을 보여드리기 위해 준비하고 있는건 비밀입니다.

Having been in this field for a long time, I've decided to share my observations after a long while. If you ask, "Is the metaverse bullshit?" the answer is partially yes. At least, if it's a metaverse with the following intentions.

To elaborate a bit more on the cases above: ** 1. The COVID-19 stopgap metaverse that isn't purpose-built for the metaverse, but rather to impress VIPs.** The first is when the metaverse is merely used as an alternative to offline events during the COVID-19 pandemic. In such cases, there's pressure to spend the budget, a feeling that "others are doing it, so we should too," and a desire to simply replicate offline events online. A characteristic of these events is that they are supply-driven. Online content should be meticulously designed and planned with the user in mind, but often these events are merely for show and performance. In these cases, the goal is to release a press release and upload a well-shot video to YouTube. And especially, the protocol for the attending VIPs is important... requests like putting their photos on their avatars and making them resemble their faces... pouring all resources into graphic quality. They demand a metaverse that only runs on gaming PCs. There's no content. Optimization is unknown. All the money was spent on graphics. But the most expensive (developer salaries these days are insane) and important part is software, and there's no money left for software development after spending it all on graphics? Honestly, even we, who work in the metaverse, have heard these demands a lot. As someone from a public institution background, I can somewhat understand, but for event attendees or consumers, it's bullshit. It's like expecting to enjoy a principal's lengthy speech online. The characteristic of online services is conciseness, and the intended content should be delivered briefly and powerfully. Also, participants should enjoy it, but since the original purpose was a photo op, it feels more like they were mobilized. I know... there are events that need to be like that. Let's move past this for now. In fact, similar offline events just moved online, so there's nothing new or surprising. If older people gather in spaces meant for young people like Zepeto or Roblox, the young people are unlikely to find that space or event enjoyable. So there will be nothing to see. Because that "thing to see" was originally intended for those who don't frequent the internet. Therefore, let's set aside metaverses that are not purpose-built for the metaverse.

** 2. Discrepancy with imagination, demanding unlimited users with real-time interaction in an unlimited space.** The second is the discrepancy with imagination. This might be the element that legendary developer John Carmack criticized... they demand things that are impossible with current technology. These are demands that would make any developer tear their hair out. Currently, the number of simultaneous users possible in a video conference is around 30 to a maximum of 70. Of course, this video isn't even Full HD quality. You can consider this the maximum for real-time voice communication. And while it seems easy, it physically involves receiving voice signals from 30 PCs or mobile devices and then sending that data down to another 30 PCs or mobile devices, all while synchronizing timing. You might think that open-world online games have a huge number of people, but such online games primarily communicate via chat, and game character movements only involve receiving coordinates for synchronization, so the data exchanged is very small. Therefore, most metaverses that allow audio communication have a smaller user limit than video conferences. Gather.town, for example, solves this problem by limiting the number of people in close proximity. Metaverses, at a minimum, involve voice, or in the case of newer metaverses like Meta, they synchronize the full immersive experience of VR HMDs, so there's bound to be a limit on the number of participants. In gaming terms, it's like creating a room and playing an FPS game with up to 32 people. This is why online FPS games actually have real-time voice chat. We sometimes get requests for hundreds of people for offline event replacements, but this is technically and theoretically impossible. In games, they might group by area or use many tricks to make it feel like everyone is in the same space, or they completely separate by channels. (Currently, we are considering solving this in a channel-based form, like games.) Unlimited space and unlimited interaction are indeed important elements of the metaverse. However, that doesn't mean interaction among all people in a single space. The "unlimited" here is closer to the meaning that an unlimited number of users can enjoy a single piece of content simultaneously. Also, you can enjoy it with anyone, regardless of distance. This meaning of "unlimited" is the same as the unlimited nature of the internet and online games. In fact, many attributes of the metaverse simply refer to the internet. If you think about it in real-life offline settings, the number of people you can interact with in one spot is at most around 10. If the goal is simply to show many people together, I think editing a good video is more cost-effective and efficient.

** 3. The metaverse is urgent. You must do it. It's closing soon.** I work in the metaverse, but I don't say it's urgent. The metaverse is important, that's true. However, I believe it's a long-term endeavor. Because the metaverse, as I see it, is part of digital transformation.

The metaverse should be viewed as part of the broader digital transformation of organizations and society. It's not something that happens in the short term, so you can't claim to have "done the metaverse" just by holding one event. Furthermore, it's not about systems, but about changes in organizations and people. I suspect those who talk about the 4th Industrial Revolution and metaverse certifications are charlatans selling "salvation certificates." I'm sorry, but there's no such thing as a salvation certificate in this domain. Just as one must live a good and faithful life to go to heaven in Christianity, there is no one-time salvation certificate. Digital transformation is change, and it is still ongoing. What's particularly interesting is that it's adults, not young students, who need to learn about the metaverse. We might even need to learn from them.

The metaverse refers to a social phenomenon where the digital world and the real world, previously considered separate, interact and merge, or to humanity's near-future society where they are fully integrated.

However, this is too abstract and distant, so let me give you a slightly more concrete explanation. I'll provide two additional keywords that we (Believer) consider essential for the metaverse.

*   New Analog Service - A service that has digitally transformed even what was considered an analog experience.
*   Next-generation Web (WebXR, WebRTC, Web3, 5G, VR/AR Device)

First, there's the New Analog service. The sensation of touching and feeling something was, in fact, a service of experience provided offline. Representative examples were travel and performances. Such services could only be experienced offline. However, with the development of virtual/augmented reality technology, the analog experiences previously offered offline can now be digitally reconfigured and transmitted. This will provide us with new services beyond time and space. Of course, if this isn't satisfactory, it will just be a stopgap service for COVID-19, as mentioned earlier.

Second, there's next-generation internet technology. This is a more technical discussion; if I defined the metaverse as the internet, then this refers to future internet technology. First, to talk a bit about the technology stack, for front-end visible technology, there's WebXR technology. This is a web standard technology for building 3D environments on the web. It's a technology that our company, Believer, is primarily pushing, such as Mozilla Hubs (Believer is creating new technologies based on this) or Virbela Frame. And for connecting users in such 3D environments, there's WebRTC technology, which spans both server and front-end, allowing multiple users to exchange synchronized voice/motion/video data in real-time. Zoom and Gather.town, which you use, are built with this technology. It's a technology that received the biggest boost from the COVID pandemic. WebSocket can also be included in RTC.

Next is distributed web technology, which allows for defining, storing, and exchanging assets in such virtual environments. Also known as Web3, representative services using this technology include coins, NFTs, DIDs, token wallets, and IPFS. We also created the art exhibition hall Vspace, which features NFTs from VR webtoons, using WebXR, but platforms utilizing this technology (oncyber.io) have already started emerging overseas.

And for such an environment, network technologies like stable 5G are needed, and next-generation devices like VR/AR will emerge. Of course, such an environment may not unfold smoothly right away. However, a significant portion is already feasible and producing meaningful results. Moreover, most of these technologies didn't just appear out of nowhere but evolved from traditional software and internet technologies. When people ask what they should study if they want to get into the metaverse, the truth is that the metaverse requires studying and further developing existing technologies. Therefore, one should not think that they can suddenly excel only in the metaverse without existing SW and IT capabilities, or that excelling only in the metaverse provides competitiveness or substance. Let me reiterate, the metaverse may rise, but to do well in the metaverse, you must already be proficient in existing technologies. I believe that saying the metaverse is nonsense is mostly due to ignoring existing technologies or talking about an illusory future built on shaky technology.

I've explained it at length, but to summarize, some parts of the metaverse are indeed nonsense. Because they are not a complete metaverse. However, the future of the metaverse exists, requires serious consideration from a technological standpoint, and is developing at a surprising pace. It's a secret that Believer is also preparing to show you something even more interesting.
