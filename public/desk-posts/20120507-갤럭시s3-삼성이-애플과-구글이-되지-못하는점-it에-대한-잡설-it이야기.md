** 1. 삼성이 그린 미래의 모습은 무엇인가?** 삼성의 아쉬움은 갤럭시s3가 나오기까지 어떤 방향을 향해 달려가고 있는지가 보이지 않습니다. 갤럭시s3는 멋진 스마트폰입다. 그뿐이죠. 애플로 잠깐 돌아오겠습니다. Siri는 사실 형편없었습니다. 그러나 siri에는 애플이 추구하고자하는 지향점이 보입니다. 사람과 대화형으로 풀어가는 컴퓨팅도구, 그리고 아이패드, tv까지 이어지는 클라우드와 이를 잇는 자연스러운 사용자경험. 안드로이드를 만든 구글은 무엇을 원하는 걸까요? 애플로 lock in되는 세상을 저지하고, 모든 플랫폼간의 개방성과 유연성을 가지는 아키텍쳐 개발, 그리고 이러한 아키텍쳐 확보를 통한 빅데이터 선점. 삼성은? 휴먼 스마트폰? 휴먼스마트폰이 사용자패턴 분석의 최적화 작업을 의미하는건가요? 저는 일부러? 아니면 어쩌다보니? 애플이나 삼성의 행사를 보지 않습니다. 오히려 행사 이후에 제품의 스펙과 다른이들의 평가로, 다시 그 제품을 평가하죠. 그러나 갤럭시s3의 제품을 통해 느낄수 있는 회사의 미래가 없었습니다.

There's a lot of buzz about the Galaxy S3. If someone were to ask, "Which smartphone should I buy this year?", I'd tell them to buy the Galaxy S3 in Korea. This is because I believe it receives the best support in the domestic environment and has a large user base, so there won't be any major issues. However, if you ask if Samsung Electronics, the maker of the Galaxy S3, is on the right track, I'd say, "Well..." In fact, even when compared to Microsoft, which earned the title of "failure" in smartphones, if you ask if Samsung Electronics is doing well, I'd say no.

**1. What is the future Samsung envisions?** Samsung's disappointment is that until the Galaxy S3 came out, it wasn't clear what direction they were heading in. The Galaxy S3 is a great smartphone. That's all it is. Let's briefly return to Apple. Siri was actually terrible. However, Siri showed the direction Apple was pursuing: a conversational computing tool for people, and a cloud that connects to the iPad and TV, along with a natural user experience. What does Google, the creator of Android, want? To prevent a world locked into Apple, to develop an architecture with openness and flexibility across all platforms, and to preempt big data through securing this architecture. What about Samsung? Human smartphone? Does "human smartphone" mean optimizing user pattern analysis? I intentionally? Or by chance? I don't watch Apple or Samsung events. Instead, after the event, I evaluate the product based on its specs and others' reviews. However, through the Galaxy S3 product, there was no sense of the company's future.

**2. Samsung Electronics is trapped in phones, but for Apple, phones were just along the way. What if Apple was a Lego company?** Another thought that comes to mind is that Samsung Electronics' major events feel like they are conducted separately from the Galaxy Tab or Smart TV. Apple's phone, pad, and TV move as if they are one product. And lately, I've been feeling that the performance of each device in its traditional domain isn't particularly good. Didn't Apple just add smartness to phones and TVs, but rather, due to necessity, extracted smart devices of various sizes, and the killer apps suitable for those sizes happened to be phones and TVs? If you think this way, the enormous resolution of the new iPad makes sense. Apple is not a company that tries to create what already exists. Apple is simply creating computing devices of various sizes based on computing technology, and by size, they just happen to compete with phones, TVs, and PCs. Although they seem separate, we can assume they were a single lineup from the beginning. Simply put, the iPhone and TV are actually different-sized Lego blocks, and just like Lego, you can use these parts to build a spaceship. In contrast, Samsung Electronics is focused on enhancing the capabilities of a single block, and Google is guiding the technical standards for creating such blocks. Therefore, users feel that the battle between Samsung Electronics' phones using this technology and Apple is like a proxy war between Google and Apple. The problem is that Samsung seems to be focusing only on this one block, limited to phones. For Google, which has no intention of making the blocks itself, Samsung's limitations might lead them to seek other partners. If Apple is a Lego company, Samsung is still a company that only makes one Lego block. Apple's true fear is not its external aspects like Apple Stores or Unpacked events, but its internal capabilities and strategic prowess. That's what leads to enormous profits.

**3. Samsung Electronics needs to move beyond phones to have a future.** This is the demand I want to make of Samsung Electronics right now: move beyond phones and envision a future for Samsung that moves like a single organism. (This might involve a major task of reorganizing business units first...) Galaxy Tab, Smart TV, cars, kiosks, Windows. And for all these devices, openness and a well-refined platform will also become essential. If they don't show such a vision, they might be picked off one by one from their weakest points. Then, phones could be surrounded by other industries and annihilated in a siege. That might be Apple's strategy, releasing the new iPad instead of the iPhone 5. After the new iPad was released, the price of the existing iPad was lowered, and sales of the Amazon Kindle, once called the iPad's killer, reportedly plummeted. Is Samsung Electronics really drawing a picture for this? They're not thinking, "Pads don't count in phone sales statistics, so it's fine," are they?
