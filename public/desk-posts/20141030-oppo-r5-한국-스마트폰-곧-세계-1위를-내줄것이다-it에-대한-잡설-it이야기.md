Oppo, a Chinese smartphone manufacturer, has unveiled its new model, the R5. Its thickness is an astonishing 4.85mm. Considering the iPhone 6 is 6.9mm and the Galaxy Alpha is 6.7mm, a thickness of 4.85mm suggests that the competition for thinness may no longer be meaningful. However, its extreme thinness has become a talking point because it lacks a 3.5mm headphone jack.

{{IMG:2}}
The specifications include a 5.2-inch FHD AMOLED display, a 64-bit 1.5Ghz Snapdragon octa-core processor, 2GB of RAM, 16GB of storage, an f2.0 lens, a 13-megapixel rear camera, and a 5-megapixel front camera, but no 3.5mm headphone jack. The price is $500, to be released in December, which translates to approximately 529,000 Korean Won. This is beyond the level where one can debate whether Samsung is cheap or expensive, and furthermore, the hardware specifications seem impressive.

The only drawback is its small 2000mAh battery. However, it comes with a fast charger that can charge up to 70% in just 30 minutes. Instead of a 3.5mm headphone jack, it provides a USB-to-3.5mm headphone adapter. It also seems that a device capable of taking photos and listening to music via a Bluetooth remote will be sold alongside it. (I'm not sure if it's included in the $500 price. It's in the feature image list, but not in the specs.)

And, it's made in China. Oppo is even an unfamiliar name among Chinese smartphone manufacturers.

Oppo started making video and audio equipment in 2004, began producing smartphones in 2008, and is currently sold in 17 countries worldwide, holding a global market share of 1%. Currently, Apple holds 19% (2nd place) and Samsung holds 30% (1st place), so Oppo's market share is only 1/30th of Samsung's.

{{IMG:4}}
However, it cannot be ignored. Looking at the companies in the TOP 10, excluding Samsung, Apple, Sony, and LG (4 companies), the remaining 6 are Chinese manufacturers. If all of these are combined, they account for a whopping 23%, surpassing Apple. And since Oppo would be included in "Others," it means that Chinese companies occupy at least 24% of the global smartphone market. Korean companies, Samsung and LG, are currently defending around the 34% mark. Pantech can be considered less than a decimal point in "Others."

Currently, the size of the Chinese smartphone market is 38.5% of the global market. However, it is projected to occupy 50% of the market by 2018, meaning that the global market share of Chinese companies will increase even if they do nothing.

{{IMG:5}}
Let's look at Oppo more closely. In 2012, it held 0.6% of the market in China, but by 2014, it rose to 3%, growing fivefold in just two years. That's a terrifying growth rate.

{{IMG:6}}{{IMG:7}}
Samsung, with a global share of 30%, holds only 18% in China, and LG is in "Others."

What does this signify?

I previously mentioned that Xiaomi, which calls itself an internet software company rather than a hardware company, has a better software strategy than Samsung. Looking at Oppo, I'm starting to fear how much the hardware gap with Samsung has narrowed.

**This suggests that China will soon seize Korea's #1 position in smartphones. I can assert this. Currently, the hardware technology gap is shrinking, software strategy is weak, and price competitiveness is also absent.**

What strategy can Korea adopt in the future? Can we invest more in hardware to widen the technology gap? If so, can we charge higher prices? What should Samsung do? I cannot easily answer, but one thing is certain: continuing with the current hardware competition strategy is not viable. It's only a matter of time, whether it's 2017 or 2018, before China catches up.

Interestingly, while Apple also seems to be falling behind China, it's not facing the same crisis as Samsung. This is because Apple is playing a different game with iOS. However, Samsung is currently playing the same game as Chinese companies within Google's Android ecosystem.

**Future survival belongs to those who play a different game. What Samsung needs to do is find and create the rules of a different game. Until now, Samsung has learned how to win by competing within existing rules, but now is the time to test its ability to create new rules.** I wish Samsung good fortune.

ps. In the short term, the R5 also gave us hints about how the upcoming Galaxy S6 and iPhone 7 might turn out. It seems likely that the 3.5mm headphone jack and micro USB charging port will disappear entirely. A smartphone with all external ports removed, connecting and charging wirelessly, seems to be the direction for the next generation of smartphones. This would align well with IoT and also help sales of future smartwatches like the iWatch and Galaxy Gear.
