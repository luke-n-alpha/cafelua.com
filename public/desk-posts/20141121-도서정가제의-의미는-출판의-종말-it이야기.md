요즘 도서정가제 이슈로 시끄럽네요. 과연 이 도서정가제가 동네서점을 혹은 출판사를 구할 수 있을까요? 저는 오히려  **"촛불은 꺼지기 전에 가장 밝다."**는 말처럼, 도서정가제가 출판계 혹은 기존의 도서시장의 종말을 예고하는 신호로 봅니다. 이미 천천히 저물어가고 있던 도서시장은 모바일 빅뱅으로 인해 더 빠르고 가파른 하락을 보여주고 있습니다. 그런 상황에서 과연 도서정가제가 출판도서를 구할 수 있을까요? **1. 도서정가제의 착각, 온라인서점과 같은 가격이면 서점에서 살거다?** 온라인 서점과 오프라인 서점이 동일한 가격이면 소비자들이 온라인 서점을 찾지 않을까요? 최근 O2O나 옴니채널이라는 이야기를 들어보셨는지요? 이미 커머스시장에서 오프라인의 역할은 쇼루밍으로 바뀌고 있습니다. 오프라인에서 물건을 확인한후 모바일로 바로 결제하는 형태로 이동하고 있는 것이죠. 책을 읽는 곳은 집의 소파지, 서점이 아닙니다. 즉시 소비할 것이 아닌데 뭐하러 귀찮게 그자리에서 구매해야 할까요? 더군다나 책은 그렇게 가벼운 물건도 아닙니다. 오프라인의 역할은 가격으로 승부할 수 없습니다. 가게새도 내야하고, 인건비도 들고, 같은 가격이라해도 밑지거든요. 더군다나 책은 옷처럼 입어봐야만 알 수 있는것도 아닙니다. 블로그에 리뷰들이 존재하고, 책의 질감이 주는 가치의 비중보다는 책은 콘텐츠의 가치가 가장 높기 때문에, 오프라인에 적합한 물건이 애시당초 아닙니다. 도서정가제는 동네서점을 구원할 수 없습니다.  **2. 도서정가제의 착각, e북을 안사면 서점에서 살거다?** 도서정가제는 e북까지 포함합니다. 출판에 대한 비용도 배송비도 안드는데, e북의 가격이 동일하다면, e북 사용율은 크게 떨어지겠죠. 실제로 도서정가제는 한국의 e북 시장의 확대를 막는 가장 큰 원인중에 하나입니다. 아마존의 킨들이 성공한 이유는 e북의 가격을 파격적으로 낮추는 마켓팅을 했기 때문이기도 합니다. e북의 판매를 막으면 과연 동네 서점이 살수 있을까요?  이 점도 부정적입니다. 사람들의 읽을 거리가 e북만 있지 않습니다. 네이버를 열면 읽을 거리가 넘치죠. 이제는 소셜네트워크까지 한몫하고 있습니다. 거기에 웹소설과 웹툰까지, 카카오페이지와 네이버 포스트 같은 경우에는 사실 e북이기를 거부한 e북형 콘텐츠입니다. 모바일향의 스낵컬쳐가 유행하면서 콘텐츠는 더 잘게 쪼개지고 있으며 빠르게 또 엄청나게 많이 소비되고 있습니다. 사람들이 책을 안읽는다고요? 아닙니다. 오히려 문자소비량은 이전과 비할수 없을정도로 늘었습니다. 오프라인에서 도서나 출판 영역에 있던 콘텐츠들은 오히려 온라인으로 넘어오면서 도서정가제 때문에 마켓팅등이 어려워 역차별을 받게 됩니다. 안그래도 책을 안읽던 독자들은 결과적으로 책을 더 안읽고, 짤막한 읽을거리로 이동하겠죠. 도서정가제는 동네 서점을 살리지 못할 뿐만 아니라, 오히려 도서영역에 있던 책들의 e북 진출마저 가로막고 있습니다.  **

The fixed book price system issue is making a lot of noise these days. Can this fixed book price system really save local bookstores or publishers? I, on the contrary, see the fixed book price system as a sign foreshadowing the end of the publishing industry or the existing book market, much like the saying, **"The candle is brightest just before it goes out."** The book market, which was already slowly declining, is showing an even faster and steeper drop due to the mobile revolution. In such a situation, can the fixed book price system really save published books?

**1. The Illusion of the Fixed Book Price System: 'If the price is the same as online bookstores, people will buy from physical stores?'**
If online and offline bookstores have the same prices, won't consumers still choose online bookstores? Have you heard of O2O or omnichannel recently? In the commerce market, the role of offline stores is already shifting to showrooming. People are moving towards checking products offline and then immediately paying for them on mobile. The place to read a book is on the sofa at home, not a bookstore. If you're not consuming it immediately, why bother buying it on the spot? Moreover, a book isn't a light item. Offline's role cannot compete on price. They have to pay rent, labor costs, and even at the same price, they lose money. Furthermore, a book isn't something you need to try on like clothes to understand. Reviews exist on blogs, and since the value of a book's content is higher than the value derived from its physical texture, it's not inherently suitable for offline sales. The fixed book price system cannot save local bookstores.

**2. The Illusion of the Fixed Book Price System: 'If people don't buy e-books, they'll buy from physical bookstores?'**
The fixed book price system includes e-books. If e-book prices are the same despite having no publishing or delivery costs, e-book usage will significantly drop. In fact, the fixed book price system is one of the biggest reasons preventing the expansion of Korea's e-book market. Amazon's Kindle succeeded partly because it adopted a marketing strategy of drastically lowering e-book prices. If e-book sales are suppressed, can local bookstores really survive?
This point is also negative. People don't just have e-books to read. Open Naver, and there's an abundance of reading material. Now, social networks are also playing a part. On top of that, web novels and webtoons – services like KakaoPage and Naver Post are essentially e-book-like content that rejects being called e-books. As mobile-oriented snack culture becomes popular, content is being fragmented even further and consumed rapidly and in vast quantities. Do people not read books? No. On the contrary, the amount of text consumed has increased to an incomparable degree compared to before. Content that was in the offline book or publishing domain is moving online, and due to the fixed book price system, it faces disadvantages in marketing and other areas. Readers who weren't reading books much anyway will end up reading even less and move to shorter reading materials. The fixed book price system not only fails to save local bookstores but also hinders the entry of books from the traditional book domain into the e-book market.

**3. Even Workbooks, the Last Bastion of Local Bookstores, Are Being Consumed by Digital.**
In fact, it's no exaggeration to say that local bookstores survive on workbooks. However, even schools are already talking about electronic textbooks. One venture company has developed a service that analyzes a student's answers as they solve problems and provides appropriate new problems. Digital problem services that are optimized for me and updated in real-time are generally much cheaper online. Then there are identical workbooks printed for millions, and they're expensive. Services like MOOCs allow you to listen to lectures from famous professors worldwide, anywhere with an internet connection. Education, indeed, is one of the fastest-digitizing markets. Of course, schools might be a bit slow, but parents will quickly catch on to these changes because their children can get better grades than the neighbor's kids.

**4. The Only Way for Local Bookstores to Survive: Provide Experiences That Online Cannot.**
Then you might ask, "What about local bookstores?" The answer is simple: they must provide value offline that online cannot. Like book cafes, which have become less popular, they need to allow on-the-spot consumption of books and create a cultural code, offering unique experiences that online cannot. They need to create reasons for consumers to visit offline bookstores, rather than simply displaying products as they are now and hoping customers will come. Times have already changed. Moreover, Koreans are "the people of delivery," and they don't have that much time.

**5. The Fixed Book Price System is Actually a Publisher's Problem, Not Local Bookstores'; Publishing is Dead.**
Honestly, I believe this fixed book price system issue, with its superficial aim of saving local bookstores, is a facade. In reality, it's a battle between the publishing industry and online books. Local bookstores are already almost extinct, and instead, the publishing industry is trying to check online books and e-book services by exerting influence over content prices. However, as I mentioned earlier, the tide has turned, and the wave of change has already begun. I think it's as reckless as trying to stop the incoming tide with a shovel.

Furthermore, for publishing, which is an intermediary distributor, providing experiences that online cannot is not as easy as it is for local bookstores. The increase in tablet PPI is already threatening the quality of paper.
Some might feel that this situation means losing the pride of publishing and damaging the status of literature. However, it's clear that when Korean novels first appeared, there was a time when they were treated much like today's comics or games.

Change has already begun. While I can't easily say it will become completely extinct, I don't expect a spring season for publishing again.
