**1. 주민등록번호 문제**** 1) 불필요한 주민등록번호 수집, 안해야 되는건 default** 불필요한 개인정보 수집의 금지, 사실 리바이벌할 필요도 없이 강화된 개인정보보호법에 의하면 이미 명시화하고 있습니다. 업체로서도 과거에 관례적으로 받았던 주민등록번호도 이제는 비용의 측면에서 봐야하는 사정에 이르렀죠. 과거 개인식별을 위해 받았던 주민등록번호도 지금은 주민등록번호를 기반으로 실명인지 확인만 해주는 서비스도 있습니다. 굳이 사이트에서 주민등록번호를 받을 필요는 없습니다. ** 1인 1아이디를 원칙으로 해야된다면 확인 과정뿐만 아니라 업체당 할당받은 키 값으로 주민등록번호를 난수화시킨후 이를 다시 다른 유니크 값으로 전달한다면 풀지는 못하지만 1인 1아이디 정책도 고수할 수 있는 시스템을 구축할 수 있으리라고 봅니다.  아직 안되고 있다면 해당 인증기관에서 유일성 여부도 확인해주는 과정이 있었으면 좋겠네요.**금융거래 또한 pg사가 있으니 주민등록번호를 받을 이유는 이제 없다고 봅니다.
**2) 아직도 주민등록번호 중요한 일에 써도 될까?  유출시 비번은 바꾸는데, 주민등록번호는?**주민등록번호가 문제가 되는 이유는 이를 가지고 휴대폰 개통, 통장 개설등이 가능하기 때문입니다. 그러나 이미 몇차례의 주민번호 유출 사건이 있었던 지라 인터넷을 사용하는 대다수의 국민들의 주민번호는 이제 개인정보로서의 가치가 있는지 의문입니다. 비밀번호가 유출되면 가장 먼저 하는 일은 무엇일까요? 바로 비밀번호 변경입니다. 그런데 우리는 주민등록번호가 유출되면 무엇을 했나요? 여전히 주민등록번호는 중요한 과정에서 쓰이고 있습니다. 저 역시 옥션, gs칼텍스, 이번 싸이월드까지 매번 털렸고, 리니지에도 제 가짜 계정이 몇개가 생겼었습니다. 정말 주민등록번호 이번에도 그냥 넘어가도 될까요? 비밀번호를 바꾸듯이 주민등록번호를 바꿀필요성은 없을까요? 물론 정부는 엄청난 돈이 드는 일이라고 싫어할지도 모르겠습니다. 더군다나 아직도 수기로 작성한 서류가 남아있으니 문제는 더 복잡합니다. 만약 비용이 든다면 누구에게 그 책임을 지게 해야할지는 뭐 말안해도 예상하실겁니다. 몇년간의 소송으로 sk컴즈에 돈 좀 받는것도 중요할지 모르겠으나 주민등록번호 유출로 인한 위험은 그 돈을 무마되는것이 아닙니다. ** 또한 정부는 이번 일이 이번한번으로 끝난다는 보장도 없고 주민등록번호에 대한 개인정보 문제를 다시 고민해봐야 합니다. 농협사태를 보듯 은행에서 이 문제가 터지지 말라는 보장이 없습니다. 유출이후에 유출정보는 어떻게 폐기 할지, 또한 비즈니스를 원하는 기업들이 어떻게 개인정보소유룰 최소화시킬 수 있는 기술을 지원할지. 정부에게도 책임이 존재합니다.**

It seems the blogosphere's interest regarding the recent Nate hacking incident is focusing on the collection of resident registration numbers as the root cause. I'd like to briefly discuss the issue of collecting resident registration numbers, and since that alone doesn't truly resolve the hacking problem, I'll also talk about fundamental technical issues.

**1. The Resident Registration Number Problem**
**1) Unnecessary collection of resident registration numbers: Not doing so is the default.**
The prohibition of collecting unnecessary personal information, in fact, doesn't even need to be revived; it's already explicitly stated in the strengthened Personal Information Protection Act. For companies, even the resident registration numbers they customarily received in the past now need to be viewed from a cost perspective. Even for resident registration numbers received in the past for personal identification, there are now services that merely confirm real names based on these numbers. There's no need for websites to directly receive resident registration numbers. **If a "one person, one ID" policy is to be maintained, it would be possible to build a system where, in addition to the verification process, the resident registration number is randomized with a key value assigned to each company, and then this is transmitted as another unique value. This way, it cannot be decrypted, but the "one person, one ID" policy can still be upheld. If this isn't yet in place, I hope the relevant authentication agencies would also provide a uniqueness verification process.** For financial transactions, too, with PG (Payment Gateway) companies, I believe there's no longer a reason to receive resident registration numbers.

**2) Should resident registration numbers still be used for important matters? If a password is leaked, you change it, but what about a resident registration number?**
The problem with resident registration numbers is that they can be used to open mobile phone accounts, bank accounts, and so on. However, given several past incidents of resident registration number leaks, it's questionable whether the resident registration numbers of the majority of internet users still hold value as personal information. What's the first thing you do if your password is leaked? You change your password. But what did we do when our resident registration numbers were leaked? Resident registration numbers are still used in important processes. I myself have been compromised repeatedly, from Auction to GS Caltex, and now Cyworld, and several fake accounts were created for me on Lineage. Should we really just let the resident registration number issue slide this time too? Isn't there a need to change resident registration numbers just as we change passwords? Of course, the government might dislike this, as it would be an incredibly expensive undertaking. Furthermore, with paper documents still in existence, the problem is even more complex. If there are costs involved, you can probably guess who should bear the responsibility without me saying it. While it might be important to receive some money from SK Comms through years of lawsuits, the risk from resident registration number leaks is not mitigated by that money. **Furthermore, the government needs to reconsider the personal information issues surrounding resident registration numbers, as there's no guarantee that this incident will be the last. As seen with the Nonghyup incident, there's no guarantee that this problem won't occur in banks. The government also has a responsibility to determine how leaked information will be disposed of after a leak, and how to support businesses that want to minimize personal information ownership through technology.**

**2. The Local PC Environment, Always a Problem, Now Needs to Change**
The recent Nate hacking incident also involved the theft of an administrator's PC account. Similar to the Nonghyup incident, PCs connected to internal networks are consistently problematic.

**[ Source: Hankyung ]**

Looking at recent trends, the installation of unauthorized software on computing terminals that can access critical information seems to be the biggest security vulnerability. This system problem is not unique to Windows. It applies to all systems where software found on the web or internet can be installed indiscriminately, including Windows, MAC, and Android smartphones. No matter how excellent the security solutions from Symantec or AhnLab, if a hacking tool with an unfamiliar pattern appears under such a system, there's a very high probability of being defenseless. A system that allows unauthorized software installation is, in itself, potentially fatal. One of the myths that MAC is relatively safe is because there are fewer unauthorized MAC applications. However, in the case of Windows, it's often used as a gaming machine, and with pirated software circulating on P2P, this probability increases dramatically. Moreover, it's common for public officials to install illegal software, making this problem very serious. **The first virus (1985, Brain) was created out of anger due to damage from illegal software... The curse from that time is still valid. Therefore, the fundamental solution to this problem is to not install unauthorized software.**

**1) Is Mobile Safe?**
Let's briefly talk about Android, which was mentioned earlier. Recently, banking and Naver apps encourage direct downloads. This is because downloads via the Google Market take time and management is difficult, leading to an increase in direct download apps for various reasons. When installing these, a warning about unauthorized software is displayed. Of course, Naver or banks are unlikely to cause problems. However, users download and install apps without a second thought, much like ActiveX. Black markets are emerging, and apps are distributed through cafes. This is essentially a re-enactment of the PC environment. Of course, this isn't to say that the Google Market should become a monopolistic distribution platform like the App Store. Also, the Google Market itself has had malware uploaded several times, indicating that it's not perfectly managed. **How about allowing distribution only through companies capable of some level of software verification, such as portals, mobile carriers, and financial affiliates? The government, not Google, would certify distribution rights, and in case of an incident, each company would be held clearly responsible. And the reason this is now possible is that Android mobile carriers and device manufacturers are domestic companies. Furthermore, channels for illegal software like P2P and cafes should now be definitively blocked.**

**2) Hence, the Cloud**
In fact, when using PCs, a major reason for using a lot of illegal software was that it was difficult to do anything without it. The costs were also very high. However, with the shift to mobile, software prices have generally decreased significantly. Many good software options are now supplied at low costs, and one of them is cloud-based services. As discussed with Chrome OS, these software solutions are safe from security threats. The security threat here refers less to the possibility of a server being hacked and more to the lower possibility of a PC being compromised. This is because nothing is installed on the PC. Critical data is not on the PC, and even the software itself is not installed on the PC. Therefore, the possibility of malicious programs running through installation is eliminated. Neither Nonghyup nor Nate were breached by direct server attacks. Fundamentally, remote control of the server was done via a PC, and when the PC was compromised, it essentially paved a highway to the server. Many people are actually more concerned about cloud security threats. And they are right. If a server is breached, the security incident is indeed larger. Just looking at the recent Nate incident, 35 million data records were leaked, which is incomparable to a single PC being compromised. That's why several things are needed. Cloud services must further strengthen server security. Data must also be encrypted, and even if breached, data should be separated to minimize damage. If Cyworld's system had been installed on a single PC, even friend information and private posts might have been stolen. Cloud services thus have the advantage of differentiating security, and more detection and security technologies can be incorporated without users knowing. Of course, if the operating company is foolish, it can indeed be more dangerous. Even so, it's a clear fact that they maintain a much higher level of security than general PCs or small and medium-sized businesses. This is because the providers of cloud services are the ones who have to rack their brains over these security concerns.

**3) However, cloud services are not yet ready to be satisfactory. ^^; But, preparation is needed.**
While several solutions like Google Docs and Naver Word are appearing in the market, it's still hard to find ones that are clean enough to replace offline applications. Chrome OS seems to have the highest potential, but it still falls short of user expectations. However, the market can flip in an instant. The smartphone was not first introduced by the iPhone. It had been maturing and preparing for quite a long time with Palm and Windows Mobile. Then it was transformed by the iPhone in one go. And the cloud might go through the same process.

I once predicted that Windows 8 would be a tablet OS. So, let me make another prediction: Windows 9 will be a cloud OS. **At that time, many fundamental problems regarding illegal software will disappear, and the concept of security will likely change significantly. (Pakistani programmer, you won!) And I hope Korean IT is prepared for that.**
