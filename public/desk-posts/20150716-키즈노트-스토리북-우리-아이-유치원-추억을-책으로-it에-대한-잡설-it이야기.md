Do you know Kids Note, where memories from 20,000 Korean daycare centers and kindergartens accumulate? It's a service that was recently acquired by Daum Kakao, famous for KakaoTalk. It's reportedly used in a whopping 40% of daycare centers and kindergartens nationwide. As accidents in daycare centers and kindergartens have been continuously reported recently, parents are often anxious. However, for centers using Kids Note, activity logs are regularly sent with photos or videos, and comments can be added to these posts, making it easy to communicate with the center and its teachers, or check medication requests and meal plans. Since my son's daycare also uses Kids Note, I receive daily push notifications. Sometimes, while working, I get a push notification and feel happy seeing that my son is doing well.
{{IMG:1}}I don't know why he thought the toilet was his dad's ㅜㅜ
Anyway, Kids Note has launched a service called Storybook, which I'd like to introduce. It's impossible to continuously offer such a great service for free, right? You can't run a business by digging holes in the ground... Kids Note Storybook is a service that turns the photos accumulated in Kids Note into an album. It's a real album, not an online one. I was lucky enough to be selected for the experience group, so I'm leaving this review.

The most important thing: price! Around 300 pages costs 93,000 won... Based on my experience, I got 160 pages for about 3.5 months. If I had paid the actual price, it would have been around 60,000 won. Calculating at about 45 pages per month, that's 540 pages for a year. Approximately 167,000 won... It's a bit more expensive than I thought.
{{IMG:17}} I wonder if it wouldn't be better to offer discounts for monthly or quarterly subscriptions, or even better, allow daycare centers and kindergartens to customize them, adding more of their unique character, and then sell them directly. If daycare centers could customize them for 15,000 to 20,000 won per month and sell them mixed in with tuition fees, I think it would be hard for parents not to buy them.
