네이버의 미투데이 서비스의 의의

**1. 개인화 서비스의 의의**
- 최근, N디스크, 메일, 포토앨범, 칼랜더, 가계부, 통합계좌 등의 PIMS부터 시작한 개인화 서비스를 속속 출시되고 있습니다.

네이버의 서비스는 그동안 검색을 통한 접근도는 높았으나, 싸이월드의 미니홈피, 다음의 카페에 비해 UV는 썩 높다고 보기는 어려웠죠. 그러나, 본격적으로 개인화 서비스를 시작하기 시작했습니다. 이런 개인화 서비스에 한번 발이 묶이면 더더욱 다른 서비스를 이용하기 어려워지죠. 블로그등의 링크를 내부로 돌리는 것과는 차원이 다른 이야기입니다. 한번 여기에 데이터를 쌓으면 절대 빠져나가기 어렵습니다. 싸이월드가 여전히 먹고 사는 이유이기도 하며, 검색플랫폼이 메인인 구글 역시 강화하고 있는 서비스입니다. 플랫폼으로서 웹을 표방한 네이버로서는 이를 놓칠수는 없겠죠.

**3. 검색의 질을 향상시키기 위한 폭소노미 기반 메타 데이터**
제가 생각하는 가장 큰 미투데이의 장점은 바로, 폭소노미 기반의 메타데이터로서의 미투데이입니다. 미투데이에는 정보가 없다고 말하는 분들도 있지만 그렇지 않습니다. 미투데이 서비스를 찬찬히 보십쇼. 기존의 블로그와 가장 다른 점이 하나 존재하는데, 바로 외부 정보의 링크와 추천 시스템입니다. 검색 시스템의 기본이란 모든 필요한 데이터는 인터넷에 있다는것을 가정합니다. 과거에 많이 사용되었던 방법 중 하나가 링크의 수를 세서 페이지의 중요성을 판단하는것이었습니다. 하지만, 애석하게도 한국은 그게 잘안됩니다. 펌질이 발달한 이유도 존재하지만, 천성적으로 한국인들은 링크를 걸지 않습니다. 심지어는 블로거들 조차 글 하나를 쓸 때, 평균적으로 외부링크를 거는 것이 몇개나 될까요 ? 그나마 최근에 외부 블로거들은 트랙백이 일반화되어 그나마 링크소스가 많이늘어났습니다. 하지만, 여전히 많은 사람들이 링크를 거는것을 어려워하고, 라이트유저들의 경우에는 1년에 몇개의 링크를 걸까 모르겠습니다.
하지만, 미투데이는 다릅니다. 기사에 링크를 걸어서 자신의 의견을 피력하고, 사람들은 그것에 대해 추천을 하고, 또 다시 핑백을 쓰고, 넓디넓은 블로그의 텍스트에디터를 채워넣는것을 주저하던 유저들도 미투데이에서는 150자가 짧다고 불평할 정도입니다.

These are my thoughts after reading an article that questioned whether a service, which isn't particularly informative, could be meaningful for NHN, a company aiming to be a search platform.

The Significance of Naver's Me2day Service

**1. The Significance of Personalized Services**
- Recently, personalized services, starting with PIMS like N-Disk, Mail, Photo Album, Calendar, Household Ledger, and Integrated Accounts, are being released one after another.
{{IMG:1}}
Naver's services have historically had high accessibility through search, but their Unique Visitors (UV) were not particularly high compared to Cyworld's mini-hompy or Daum's cafes. However, they have now truly begun to launch personalized services. Once users are tied to these personalized services, it becomes even harder to use other services. This is on a completely different level from simply directing blog links internally. Once data is accumulated here, it's almost impossible to leave. This is also why Cyworld continues to thrive, and it's a service that Google, whose main focus is a search platform, is also strengthening. As Naver positions itself as a web platform, it certainly cannot miss out on this.

**2. Life log**
****

While social links like SNS are meaningful, if you carefully examine the Me2day service, it's a system that records every single action of an individual, including real-time updates via SMS, mobility via MMS, and location information through features like location tagging. It's not a system where you take time to write, like a blog. Such personal log services become a record of one's life and a social link for individuals, and
**The corporate value of such life log services lies in the log itself.** Today, many companies are curious about where and what individuals buy, and this market research data, through operations like data mining, helps discover new markets and acts as a competitive advantage for businesses. Fundamentally, this information is essential for a company to become the one that knows its users best. Of course, due to personal information protection laws, there are issues with using it carelessly, but such simple log information is often not subject to legal protection. If it were included as a protected subject, most internet services would practically become impossible. Some people might choose not to use Me2day because they fear being monitored by Naver, a monopoly. However, the same applies to using a supermarket or swiping a credit card.

**3. Folksonomy-based Metadata to Improve Search Quality**
The biggest advantage of Me2day, in my opinion, is Me2day as folksonomy-based metadata. Some people say there's no information on Me2day, but that's not true. Take a close look at the Me2day service. There's one key difference from traditional blogs: the linking of external information and a recommendation system. The fundamental premise of a search system is that all necessary data exists on the internet. One method widely used in the past was to count the number of links to determine a page's importance. Unfortunately, this doesn't work well in Korea. While the prevalence of "pumping" (copy-pasting content) is one reason, Koreans inherently don't link. Even bloggers, on average, how many external links do they include when writing a single post? At least recently, trackbacks have become common among external bloggers, increasing the number of link sources. However, many people still find it difficult to link, and I wonder how many links light users create in a year.
But Me2day is different. Users who hesitated to fill the vast text editor of a blog now link to articles to express their opinions, people recommend them, and then write pingbacks, to the point where they complain that 150 characters on Me2day is too short.

Below is a structural diagram of ISO/IEC 13250's TOPIC MAP. It's one of the representation methods for the Semantic Web, which might become Web 3.0 (?).
{{IMG:3}}
Doesn't something come to mind when you look at the structure above? **When considering the multiple Resources labeled "Web" below as the general Web and blogs, the Topics above could be Me2day or data analyzed from Me2day based on meaning. Links already exist, and major TOPICs are categorized based on recommendations.**
If we mine Me2day data to understand what information the public prefers, what evaluations and "Me2s" are attached to which pages (information), and their significance, and then mechanically judge it, a **folksonomy-based tagging system** that is robust against abuse and where the public defines web resources will emerge.
For this to happen, sufficient user participation is necessary, making it an essential reason for Naver to foster Me2day.

*** Of course, there's no way to know if Naver considered all this when acquiring Me2day; these are just my thoughts.**

**4. It might simply be that they wanted to preempt the market for SNS services optimized for mobile environments.** Just like Daum enthusiastically jumped into and preempted the UCC market in the past but ultimately didn't see much success, this could also become a service that doesn't gain traction. Currently, Me2day is experiencing excessive growth due to celebrity promotions, and it might even be overwhelming just to manage that. They might have simply wanted to create a Cyworld-like model within the Naver camp by selling tokens instead of acorns.

However, I believe Me2day is a meaningful service in several ways. As a life log service in a mobile environment... as an SNS... or as metadata for search... How this service will evolve is not yet determined, and no one knows if it will succeed. However, what's certain is that the significance of Me2day lies in the fact that light users have started linking, and an environment has been created where people know about and can enjoy applications using Open APIs, which I consider a very big deal. While the App Store seems amazing, if you ask college female students or people over 40 on the street, or those not proficient with computers, they still don't know what it is. For them, familiar services are still limited to mail and cafes, and the most familiar IT device for them is not a computer but a mobile phone.

**In fact, I believe Naver's explosive growth has reached its limit.** Existing IT users are not increasing rapidly, and neither the advertising market nor the e-commerce market is experiencing explosive growth as before. Through Open Cast, they wanted to draw in the power of external bloggers and external content to elicit user reactions, but light users, on the contrary, rejected it. The market outside Naver was not as attractive, and consequently, the synergy did not materialize as much as expected. Users wanted something new, but external communication or blogs did not create as much buzz or change web usage patterns as Cyworld did.

I imagine Naver also has many concerns. But what about this Me2day? I believe the path for Naver to break through now is overseas expansion, such as Japanese search, along with changes in the domestic web environment. This is the answer. **For now, as a user, I am very satisfied and find it interesting. But unfortunately, since I'm an IT professional, I shouldn't use myself as the standard. Users are always like children and want something new.**
