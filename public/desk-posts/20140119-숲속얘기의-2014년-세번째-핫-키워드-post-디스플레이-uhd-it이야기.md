My third hot keyword is UHD. To be precise, I believe that high-resolution displays exceeding FHD, rather than just UHD, will be the hot topic this year.

**1. Beyond FHD to UHD. This will be led by tablets and laptops, not TVs**
Already, 1920*1080 FHD displays have become the standard resolution for everything from palm-sized mobile devices to large TVs. It's practically safe to consider it the standard resolution.
However, high-resolution TVs called UHD have recently appeared. Of course, considering that there isn't much content available to utilize such high resolutions, one might ask, "Do we really need UHD?"
However, there are areas where UHD-level resolution is even more necessary: laptop monitors and tablet screens. Tablets and laptops are used at a closer distance than TVs, leading users to desire better image quality and more information. I'm not sure if smartphones exceeding FHD will appear in the mobile sector. However, what's clear is that tablets larger than 7 inches need to be UHD-level to achieve the same density as FHD smartphones, so there is definitely a need for this. The iPad is already at the forefront of this battle, and high-resolution content on tablets will also influence TVs.

**2. Curved displays for areas where curves are needed, like watches**
Adopting curved displays for smartphones or TVs is a method pushed by manufacturers, regardless of user needs. To apply a curve to a TV and create a more immersive display, it would need to have a distance and width similar to the curvature of the human eye, like an IMAX cinema. However, few consumers buy a TV and then adjust their sofa distance to match it. Therefore, in these areas, the demand for flat-panel displays remains higher.
However, the story is different for watches, which I mentioned first. A person's wrist is curved. Curved displays will find their place in areas that match such curves. For example, they could be used in small displays on the curved surfaces of air conditioners or refrigerators, designed for aesthetic purposes.

**3. The Crisis of E-Ink**
E-Ink might be meaningful in low-power devices like watches, but with the rapid drop in color display costs, it's questionable whether it can maintain market competitiveness. Until color E-Ink emerges, it seems destined to struggle against displays like IPS and AMOLED. Even its advantage in readability isn't as strong when faced with displays that have advanced to UHD and FHD levels.
Perhaps 2014 will be E-Ink's biggest challenge.
