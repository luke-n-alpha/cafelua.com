This presidential election was the first where mobile exerted greater influence than PCs. Although it was a presidential election marred by negativity, I believe there were many meaningful aspects and changes in various ways. So, I'd like to talk about those aspects.

**1. Information Gap Accelerated by Mobile**
First, for users influenced by the internet, internet broadcasts (Naggomsu), real-time trending search terms, and social networks had a significant impact. Especially, users who could access the internet via mobile showed faster information consumption patterns.

This phenomenon is also observed in media consumption, similar to how the staying power of #1 songs on music charts is getting shorter and single albums are gaining prominence. Presidential election issues were so dynamic on mobile that it was impossible to predict what would happen the next day.

However, this significantly widened the gap between users who had access to the internet and those who did not. Even my parents, who lean slightly left, don't know about Naggomsu.

The reporting speed of old media like TV and newspapers is much slower than the internet, and information is filtered and refined before being disseminated. Consequently, emotions were largely removed, or the subjective views of each media outlet clearly influenced the reporting.

While many expand this discussion to generational gaps, I see this phenomenon as an information gap, not a generational one. Therefore, I personally believe that this information gap is the biggest challenge left by the IT environment of this presidential election.

**2. The Reality of Social Network Abusing**
It was widely known through various cases that real-time trending search terms are exposed to the risk of abuse. As the influence of specific media affects internet search terms, simultaneous input of search terms causes rankings to rise significantly. This is similar to how webtoon artists' topics frequently appear in search rankings.

However, this time, including the "Sipaldan" incident, it was revealed that social networks can also be exposed to abuse. This might be because the size of the social network population in South Korea is relatively small, meaning that organized abuse doesn't require as much cost compared to overseas.

Such attempts at abuse also contribute to diminishing the value of social network big data. Therefore, for the sake of big data, which has recently garnered attention, serious consideration of abuse seems necessary.

**3. Emergence of Political Content on N-Screen**
The strength of n-screen is that even without large theaters like CGV, screens can be secured via the internet. "Namyeong-dong 1985" and "Memories of MB" tended to be linked at the top of n-store, because content could be watched immediately by searching in the search bar. "Namyeong-dong 1985" was exposed several times in real-time trending searches. Compared to UCC-centric political participation, this shows the possibility of much higher-quality political participation content emerging. It also clearly demonstrated that it could be an alternative for serious films like "Touch" or "Pieta" that struggled to find screens. Furthermore, internet content allows for various marketing methods such as viral marketing and search terms, thus becoming a path to encounter a much wider range of content.

And the increase in serious social participation content means that content influencing politics can emerge. It seems like it's now an era where documentaries are worth trying. This will likely accelerate social participation in Chungmuro.

**4. Accelerated Political Participation of Users with Diverse Perspectives via the Internet**
Both candidates promoted their pledges via the internet. Candidate Moon Jae-in even collected major pledges directly through the internet. And environmental or animal protection groups, as well as IT bloggers like myself, compared each candidate's pledges from their own perspectives.

This means that the means for users to participate in politics are increasingly expanding, not just during the election, but from before the election to the campaign period. I hope this trend continues even after President Park Geun-hye's election.

**5. Looking Forward to 5 Years from Now**
What changes will the IT environment bring in 5 years? Will paper newspapers still wield their current influence then? How will the broadcasting environment have changed? Will new means of political participation via the internet have emerged? The times are changing. Politics will also have no choice but to gradually adapt to the environment and change. I look forward to 5 years from now.
