**1. The Biscuit Tab: A Value-for-Money Gem to Awaken the Stagnant Tablet Market**
Since last year, there has been one market I've been paying attention to: the tablet market. The significance of tablets is that they were expected to replace PCs as information consumption devices in the living room. However, there hasn't been much market change in Korea. The iPad, which was a major player in the tablet boom overseas, didn't gain much traction in Korea due to the weakness of iOS. Samsung's Galaxy Tab, often seen as Apple's rival, failed to succeed because, unlike Galaxy phones, it didn't receive the power of mobile carrier subsidies, and its high price meant low value for money compared to its usability. Meanwhile, some low-cost Android devices did sell, but the marketing and technical support capabilities of small and medium-sized enterprises were limited, so they were only sold among a few enthusiasts.

However, the product I'm about to introduce is a device that can contribute a little to changing this domestic environment. Interpark, a KOSDAQ-listed company, has launched a tablet featuring a 1280*800 HD-grade IPS display and a quad-core CPU at an incredibly low price of 189,000 won. Considering that the Nexus 7, often called the king of value for money, costs around 300,000 won, this product might truly be the best in terms of value for money currently available.

You might wonder why such a product is sold at this price, or if it's too good to be true. However, Interpark's reason for releasing this product is a strategy to replicate Amazon's Kindle Fire in Korea. While Amazon might be unfamiliar to some in Korea, it's the company that created the Kindle Fire, which challenged the dominance of Apple's iPad.
{{IMG:2}} {{IMG:3}}
**[ Image Source : [http://me2.do/5Aa1zWej](http://me2.do/5Aa1zWej) ]**
Amazon adopted a strategy of supplying very affordable and usable tablets to the public, then pushing its own market app onto these devices to profit from content sales. This could be seen as a reverse strategy to Apple, famous for iTunes, which primarily earns profit from hardware sales. This was possible because Amazon had an overwhelming advantage in content competitiveness in online shopping and e-book sectors. In other words, Amazon, inherently a content company, entered the hardware market with tablets to increase content sales. Interpark in Korea is in a similar position, being a company that combines bookstores and shopping.

This product does not have GPS, so it is unsuitable for navigation. It can tag your current location via Wi-Fi, but it's best to assume it has no GPS. Given that most people already have smartphones, and mobile carriers offer free navigation apps, along with free navigation services like iNavi and Kim Gisa, the Biscuit Tab cleanly gave up on GPS. Rather than regretting the lack of GPS, I actually think it's a well-designed specification.

**- Launcher app that closely mimics Amazon, a slight regret regarding content strategy.**
I might review this home launcher in more detail later. But it's basically a content-centric launcher, like "My Library." Frankly, I'm negative about this Amazon-like launcher. Nowadays, users, especially on mobile, don't access content in single books or movie units. They prefer lighter, shorter content that's visually appealing at a glance and regularly grabs their interest. The strength of Naver Webtoons with their weekly serialized format, or Daum's recent service emphasizing "things to read," are examples of this trend. Therefore, a more advanced content push, suitable for mobile and digital, is needed rather than the traditional "My Library" approach.

**- Regret regarding the launcher's basic function of accommodating personalization needs.**
Another reason why using the default launcher might be burdensome is that the desire to personalize one's device is strongly reflected in launchers. This is evident in how recent launchers like Dodol Launcher, Buzz Launcher, and Kakao Home focus heavily on themes. However, the Biscuit Tab's launcher completely ignores this aspect. Therefore, users who dislike the bland design are highly likely to switch to Go Launcher at any time.

**ps. Is the Biscuit Tab a test version?**
There was a lot of talk in some reviews about the Biscuit Tab's firmware being a test version... But a release version or a test version is ultimately just a version name. Usually, a test version goes through QA, and if it meets certain standards, its version name is changed to "release" before launch. It seems like a developer's mistake led to the version name being "test." In any case, there was one firmware update immediately after I received the product, and it now appears to be labeled as the **August 7, 2013 release version**.
