I was searching for information on Gear VR and stumbled upon a truly absurd article, which I'd like to share. It's an article comparing Gear VR and Oculus. The content is very inaccurate, so I'll first meticulously debunk those points, and then explain the relationship between Gear VR and Oculus.

http://news.naver.com/main/read.nhn?mode=LSD&mid=sec&oid=092&aid=0002070154&sid1=001
**Five Reasons Why Gear VR is Better Than Oculus Rift** QHD-level ultra-high resolution screen… provides better realism (ZDNet Korea = Reporter Bong Seong-chang) For someone whose job involves experiencing new IT devices every day, nothing is less new than IT devices... news.naver.com

http://news.naver.com/main/read.nhn?mode=LSD&mid=sec&oid=092&aid=0002070154&sid1=001

The gist of the article is the nonsense that Gear VR is hardware-superior to Oculus. I will explain it properly.

■ **QHD-level ultra-high resolution screen… provides better realism**
"The display resolution used in DK2 is 1920x1080 pixels, which is Full HD. In contrast, the Galaxy Note 4, which acts as the display for Gear VR, has a QHD-level resolution of 2560x1440 pixels, making it much more detailed. In terms of realism, Gear VR is much more advantageous."

However, **the display for DK2 is 960x1080 per eye**. Where did they get 1920x1080 from? When the Galaxy Note 4 is inserted into the **Gear VR, its resolution is halved, becoming 1280x1440**. Of course, this is slightly higher than DK2. Are they saying it uses a Note 3 display in terms of specs? 960x1080?
**(Additional correction: I confirmed that the entire panel is used. Not the whole device, but just the panel. So, half of 1920x1080 is 960x1080, which is correct. I miscalculated. The reason for using the same panel is presumed to be the cost of AMOLED panels. I thought using individual displays would be cheaper and more efficient, but it seems they used the Note 4 as is due to mass production and HDMI. Ultimately, Cardboard, VR, and DK2 all use a single panel with a divider.)**

■ **20ms lower latency… no motion sickness felt**
???? The moment I saw this, I was breathless.
**DK2's latency is 2ms, 3ms**. It supports 60, 72, 75 Hz.
20ms would make it impossible to see properly.
"Experts believe that if screen latency drops below 20ms, most people will not experience motion sickness." Which experts did they ask?
**LCD response speed is not that slow. It's around 2ms, similar to AMOLED.**

**Update (2015.04.06): It has been confirmed as a fact that Gear VR has a faster latency of 20ms.** What confused me in the article was that it mentioned minimum latency due to AMOLED, making me think it was display latency. However, the latency referred to here is the total latency from when a movement occurs until it is reflected on the screen, which has nothing to do with the screen itself. Therefore, **while DK2 is ahead in rendering speed, the time taken for the screen to be transmitted to the head-mounted display via HDMI (screen latency = motion input time + rendering time + HDMI transmission time + VSync wait time) results in a total time of over 20ms.** However, the claim that AMOLED gives it an advantage, as stated in the article, is false. **Out of the tens of milliseconds of latency, the impact of AMOLED on the display time is, at best, a difference of less than 1ms.**

■ **Wireless freedom… enjoy VR anytime, anywhere**
This part is correct. Gear VR, or mobile VR as it's called, allows for a full 360-degree view and has the advantage of being wireless. However, Oculus has a reason for giving up 360 degrees. That is motion tracking.
**They said you don't get closer when you move your body, but with Oculus, you do.** That's why a camera is attached to the PC, Oculus requires more performance, and the viewing angle is limited compared to VR. **The PC camera performs motion sensing like Kinect. I can't understand how someone could write such an article without even trying it.**

■ **Reasonable price for enjoying virtual reality**
They calculated DK2 at 388,000 won and VR at 220,000 won... but they left out the price of the Note 4 for VR. Seriously; if you're going to do that, it would be more appropriate to compare it to a 20,000 won Cardboard.

■ **The relationship between Oculus and Gear VR… Gear is not actually Samsung technology.**
And finally, it seems they think Gear VR and Oculus are made by different companies... Gear VR is more of an Oculus product than a Samsung product. Oculus also distributes the SDK.
https://developer.oculus.com/downloads/#sdk=mobile
The relationship between Oculus and Gear VR is similar to the relationship between Google, who made Android, and Samsung. They are not competitors to begin with, and most of the technology belongs to Oculus. If this is the kind of comparison they're making, they might as well try to pit Note pen technology against Wacom. Seriously; (In the case of the Note, it's Wacom technology.)

■ **Oculus vs. Gear VR performance is PC vs. Smartphone**
And the hardware performance of the Note 4 is a drop in the bucket. This is because DK2 uses a PC's graphics card for rendering. Comparing PC gaming graphics cards with smartphone graphics performance is beyond a joke.

Even if you want to write an advertisement for Samsung, I hope you write it with accurate information. I recently bought a Cardboard and have been enjoying it, it's pretty good. I recommend trying Cardboard before Gear VR. In fact, there isn't a huge difference between Cardboard and VR. Also, there doesn't seem to be enough content yet to warrant buying Gear VR.

It didn't have the same impact as when I used Oculus. If you want the convenience of Gear VR, I recommend purchasing Pungpoong Magyeong (Storm Mirror).**
