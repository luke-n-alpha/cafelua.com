**1. This is not an ideological confrontation, but a story of a new SW-centric paradigm**
**- Let's understand big data and e-commerce.**
However, I believe it's necessary to focus on 'big data' and 'Alibaba,' which he operates, rather than this ideological debate. First, explaining big data might be a bit difficult, so let's talk about Alibaba first. Alibaba is the world's largest B2B market. If you don't quite understand B2B, it might be easier to think of it as a wholesale market. There's nothing you can't find, and if you want to make anything, you absolutely have to stop here. If China is the world's factory, then Alibaba is the online shopping mall for those factories. And that shopping mall knows in real-time who bought what, when, and for how much. And the volume of this data is truly enormous, making it actual big data. It's not something that can be calculated on a single computer; it requires distributed computing, and the data formats are often inconsistent, so algorithms are needed to infer meaning from it. This is big data. Jack Ma and big data are naturally a perfect fit. The main field where such big data is utilized is predictive marketing. The well-known anecdote is how Amazon, based on a list of purchased items, determined a customer's pregnancy status without her knowing and sent brochures related to pregnancy. Now, let's imagine something. Alibaba's data being used for China's economic policy. Of course, this might only be possible because it's a communist party, but it could reveal when a financial crisis might occur, who the vulnerable populations are, or even... who might create a bomb to threaten the Chinese government. Based on this, warnings or actions could be taken in advance. The saying that whoever controls logistics and finance can control the world is likely not an empty statement. Amazon and Coupang starting logistics operations is likely for the same reason.

**2. Big Data is About Real-time Planning and Prediction, Not Long-term Planning**
This is what a big data-based planned economy means. To be precise, new terms like real-time economy, predictive economy, or algorithmic economy might need to emerge rather than planned economy. It's not about annual plans and budget execution, but preparing for and responding to events that might happen next week, or even in three days. It's similar to how knowing whether it will rain today, even if predicting the weather a week out is difficult, is incredibly helpful. You get to know whether or not to use an umbrella today. I find his statement very significant. Could Jack Ma have known about China's recent stock market crash beforehand? Perhaps the Chinese government also knew through Alibaba. Furthermore, as mentioned earlier, Alibaba is already involved in finance as a fintech company.

**3. Algorithmic Economy? Exploratory Economy? Evolutionary Economy? Predictive Economy?**
This is a planned economy entirely different from the five-year plans of the Park Chung-hee era. From a software perspective, it would be a method of finding the right answers by repeatedly attempting short plans alongside long-term plans and incorporating feedback. From a distance, it would look more like exploration and evolution than planning.

**4. What's more important is whether it can be executed.**
It could be called the power of software. However, it won't be as easy as it sounds. Even with such software capabilities, the organization and processes must be prepared for it. Is China building this capability? I also cautiously wonder if there will come a day when Jack Ma takes an important position in the Communist Party.
