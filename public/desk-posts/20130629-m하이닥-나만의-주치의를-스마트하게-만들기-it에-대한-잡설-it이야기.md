**1. The Evolving Healthcare Environment**
As income levels rise and time passes, people's interest in health and the healthcare environment in South Korea are changing. In the past, due to inconvenient transportation and a limited number of local hospitals, the few doctors in the neighborhood served as the family doctors for everyone, and the residents had no other choice.

However, with the development of transportation and an increase in hospitals, the range of choices has widened, allowing people to select based on quality. Healthcare services have entered a competitive state, and consumers now desire medical services that are perfectly tailored to them. This is why developed countries, which entered this stage earlier, have personal physicians. In this post, I'd like to introduce an app based on the concept of a personal physician.

**2. "Personal Physician": A Concept Well-Suited for the Future Healthcare Environment and Mobile**
Just as every person's face is different, every person's body is also different, leading to the wish for a good doctor to exclusively manage one's health. However, in reality, having a personal physician is not easy for the average person to consider. That's why the app I'm about to introduce helps you create your own personal physician. It's not a game (don't imagine something like Nonomura!); it's an app where you meet real, existing doctors.

The reason this intention works well on mobile is that mobile devices are more personalized than PCs and are structured to be always online and suitable for forming relationships with others. This is why SNS exploded on mobile. **Applying "personalization and relationships," elements well-suited for mobile, to healthcare directly leads to a "personal physician system."** Seeing this app, my first thought was that this is a proper platform approach.

**3. However, the App's Biggest Misguided Approach Is to Try to Deliver Too Much Beyond Its Core Value in the Early Stages of Launch.**
First, the app's structure includes a left menu with "Consultation Room" for finding and booking appointments with a personal physician or consulting, "Health Information" for registering and receiving health information, and "Other" which includes settings. Additionally, at the top, there's "Health Index" (a service to be launched) and "View All" for notifications. The right menu consists of "Events/Announcements." Frankly, I wasn't very impressed with the app's menu structure when I first saw it.

{{IMG:1}}**{{IMG:2}}
[ This many left and right menus ]**

It's an app that just launched, but there are too many options and features. Moreover, this app, which uses the left, top, and right menus, already has as many menus as Facebook. I thought that first-time users might be intimidated. Mobile apps should be simple. When planning and developing, there's a desire to include many things, but including too much leads to difficulties in operation (cost) and ultimately prevents focus on the core. If I were to point out the biggest flaw of this app, it's that it tries to offer too much.

I was asked to introduce all the menus, but I will boldly skip introducing events, health information, health index, and pharmacy search in this post. **The core of this app is the "Consultation Room."** If these menus are intended for information push, considering the platform's revenue model, I'd like to say that it's more important to refine the user experience centered on the core function, the "Consultation Room," and expand the user base first to establish platform power. After that, it won't be too late to create information push tools. Representative apps that used this strategy include Facebook and Kakao.

**4. The Core of This App Is "My Meeting (Relationship) with a Doctor."**
The main screen of mHidoc shows your registered personal physician or recommends nearby physicians if you don't have one, encouraging you to find a new personal physician.

{{IMG:4}}**[ If you don't have a personal physician, register a recommended one or search for one ]**

Searching for a personal physician is easy, centered around location, medical department, theme, and disease.

Therefore, it has created a virtuous cycle of a social network where users gather, doctors join, and as more doctors join, users benefit. The doctor's profile page allows you to view the doctor's information and apply for them to be your personal physician. It shows location, facility information, areas of practice, and career history. Also, there's a "Thank You" concept, similar to recommendations, for when you're satisfied with a consultation, so if the user base grows, prominent doctors will naturally emerge.

**5. All Actions You Can Take with Your Personal Physician, and Their Medical Chart, Remain as a Timeline.**
What you can do with your personal physician includes consultation via chat, appointment booking, and phone calls. It can be said that all actions between a doctor and a patient are integrated.

**{{IMG:8}}****[ For doctors registered with mHidoc, detailed doctor information is available, and upon registering as a personal physician, consultation/booking/calls are possible ]**

And, if there's an injury or painful area, you can take a picture and show it directly to your personal physician, and all of this is recorded on a timeline, showing all medical actions chronologically and clearly.

**6. The Identity of mHidoc Is a Medical Professional Vertical Social Network Platform**
To describe the mHidoc app in one word, it is a "medical professional vertical social network platform" that connects doctors and patients. You might wonder why a medical app is a social network, but the core of this app lies in the virtuous cycle of relationships. First, the app's completeness is very high, and the mechanisms to expand the social network seem well-established. The success of this app seems to depend on expanding the initial user volume. As users expand, doctors must be able to experience the value of meeting more patients more conveniently by using this app. Securing a local doctor database might be a challenge, but I also thought the concept itself would be good to take overseas.
