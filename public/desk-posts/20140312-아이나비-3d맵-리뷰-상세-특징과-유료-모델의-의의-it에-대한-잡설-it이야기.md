최근 포스팅에 제가 안드로이드 기반 네비게이션인 아이나비 K11 Air를 사용하고 있다고 말씀드린적이 있습니다. 아이나비 K11은 A8프로세서를 이용하고 있고, 안드로이드 기반의 사실상 스마트폰과 동일한 스펙입니다. 최신 스마트폰들은 성능의 평가를 터치의 빠릿빠릿함으로만 평가함이 아니라, 3D나 가용램등의 요소등을 성능의 척도로 삼습니다. 마찬가지로 아이나비 K11 air는 그 성능을 십분 활용할 수 있는 3D맵을 제공합니다. 아이나비 K11의 주 특징 중 하나는 항공사진맵이었지만, 이번에는 3D맵에 대해 이야기해볼까 합니다.   ** 1. 고저차가 있는 진짜 3D** 아이나비 K11 air의 3D맵을 띄워보고 깜짝 놀랐던 것은 그동안의 평면 지도위에 3D건물만 세워두었던 3D가 아니라는 점이었습니다. {{IMG:1}}제 손이 나와서 좀 이상한데, 좌측 상단에 지면의 높이가 고저차가 있는것이 보입니다. 때문에, 실제 지표와 흡사하여 마치 헬리콥터에서 제 차를 찍고 있는 느낌 같이 느껴집니다. **2. 실제 건물과 흡사한 모델**{{IMG:2}}당연히 3D맵의 장점이자 경쟁력인, 건물의 모델들 수준도 훌륭합니다. 물론, 덕분에 년간 업데이트비용을 받겠죠. 이 데이터를 가지고 레이싱 게임을 만들어 보면 재밌지 않을까 ? 하는 생각도 했습니다. 예전에 시티레이서인가 서울의 실제 디자인을 가지고 레이싱게임을 만들었다고 화제가 되었었는데, 아이나비의 데이터를 그대로 이용해서 3D레이싱 게임을 만든다면 어떨까요? **3. 확대와 축소가 자유로운 3D**3D모델의 장점은 어느각도에서나 자유롭게 볼 수 있습니다. 기존의 2D지도의 경우, 평면을 버드뷰형태로 외곡해서 3D처럼 보이는 것이었다면 3D는 실제로 모델을 그 방향에서 그려주므로 자연스럽습니다. ​**[ ▼ 아래는 멀티터치에 의한 3D조작 동영상입니다. ]**

In a recent post, I mentioned that I'm using the Android-based navigation system, iNavi K11 Air. The iNavi K11 uses an A8 processor and has virtually the same specifications as an Android-based smartphone. Latest smartphones are not only evaluated by the responsiveness of their touch, but also by factors like 3D capabilities and available RAM as performance metrics. Similarly, the iNavi K11 Air provides a 3D map that fully utilizes its performance. One of the main features of iNavi K11 was its aerial photo map, but this time I'd like to talk about the 3D map.

**1. True 3D with Elevation Differences**
What surprised me when I launched the iNavi K11 Air's 3D map was that it wasn't just 3D buildings placed on a flat map, as had been the case before. {{IMG:1}}My hand is in the shot, which is a bit odd, but you can see the elevation differences in the terrain in the upper left corner. Therefore, it closely resembles the actual ground, making it feel as if a helicopter is filming my car.

**2. Models Resembling Real Buildings**
{{IMG:2}}Naturally, the level of detail in the building models, which is a strength and competitive edge of 3D maps, is excellent. Of course, this is why they charge an annual update fee. I even thought, wouldn't it be fun to create a racing game with this data? I remember a racing game called City Racer, or something similar, that became a hot topic for using actual Seoul designs. What if a 3D racing game were made using iNavi's data as is?

**3. Free Zooming In and Out in 3D**
The advantage of 3D models is that you can view them freely from any angle. While traditional 2D maps distorted a flat plane into a bird's-eye view to appear 3D, actual 3D renders the model from that direction, making it natural.

What surprised me while using the iNavi 3D map was its optimized dynamic view. It doesn't just follow and film like a helicopter; it provides an optimized view based on the user's location. **[ ▼ The screen below is a driving screen, not a game screen.]******

​**5. iNavi's Paid Model, Showing its Direction as a Service Company**
If I were to mention one drawback of iNavi's 3D map, it's that it's paid. Upon initial purchase, it comes with 1 year of free updates, and dealerships also support free updates. Update costs start from 20,000 won per year, with discounts available for purchasing 2 or 3 years at once. It's also possible to purchase per update: 6,000 won for 1 update, and 23,500 won for 5 updates, with differentiated pricing.{{IMG:3}} This approach seems like a very good direction for iNavi, which previously relied solely on package sales for revenue, to create a new revenue stream through software services. This business model has the advantage of maintaining continuous contact with customers and allowing for various promotions. To grow with this business model, it needs to transform from a product sales company into a service company. Based on my experience with the service, apart from partially adopting this model, there are hardly any special after-sales services or internet-connected services yet. Its aspect as a platform provider is also weak. However, the very act of attempting this makes me think positively that they are moving with the possibility of becoming a service company in mind. Someday, a situation will surely come where you can purchase a usage pass directly from the iNavi navigation system itself and update it immediately. Currently, iNavi's update method still involves wired, full-system updates, and it doesn't seem to properly support OTA-based incremental or frequent updates. For a model that supports Wi-Fi, this feels like a somewhat regrettable aspect.
