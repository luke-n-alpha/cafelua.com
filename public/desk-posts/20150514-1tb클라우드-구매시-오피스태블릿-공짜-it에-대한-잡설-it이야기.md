My wife and I use the Galaxy Zoom 2. While it's ridiculously thick compared to a regular smartphone, its photo quality is excellent, and since it's lighter than a mirrorless camera, we hardly use our Nex-5. As recent phones like the Galaxy S6 and G4 also highlight photography as a strength, the desire for high-quality photos taken with smartphones is increasing. And as we take more photos, photo storage has also become a very big issue. If you have a child like me, the value of photos is immense. Hard drives can fail at any time. Some people store them on portable external hard drives, but for children's photos, I strongly advise against it due to stability issues. For this reason, I was using a NAS with two hard drives that supported dual backup. However, it was an old NAS, so the speed was frustrating, and since I used it at home, I couldn't always leave it on. To view old photos again, I had to turn on the NAS. Also, viewing them on a smartphone wasn't very smooth. So, while considering cloud services as the best alternative, I'd like to share the know-how behind my choice.

**1. Cloud Comparison**
First, let me tell you my choice: I chose OneDrive. Previously, I was using NDrive for automatic smartphone backups. When considering moving my NAS to the cloud, the first thing that caught my eye was NDrive, which offered 1TB for 100,000 won, the lowest price. NDrive offers 1TB at the lowest price, and its mobile app is also very well-made. It even shows subtitles for videos uploaded to the cloud, making it optimal for the Korean market.

**3. OneDrive Tips and Disappointments**
**1) Convenient to manage on a PC with equivalent storage to OneDrive**
For usability, it's convenient to have a hard drive with the same capacity. If you set it up on that disk for Windows and edit files, they will automatically sync to OneDrive. I also first moved my NAS data to my PC. This way, you can manage it quickly from your local PC. If your laptop has insufficient storage, you'll have to use it as online-only. This is a bit inconvenient, more than you'd think.

**2) Disappointing Mobile App**
The OneDrive mobile app has the disadvantage that viewing photos is not as convenient compared to Dropbox or NDrive. It was a bit inconvenient that it doesn't automatically group and display photos by date. Moreover, after uploading 450GB from my NAS all at once, viewing by chronological order became meaningless. At least the NAS was managed by folders, so that was okay. MS has become very progressive since the CEO change, and I hope the Android app will be further improved.

**3) Disappointing Speed**
Until MS builds a cloud center in Korea, it will likely be slower than NDrive.

**4. Conclusion: MS seems to be a price bully.**
If you purchase 1TB of OneDrive for a year, you get an 8-inch tablet for free. It seems impossible to escape MS.
