**1. 소프트웨어 유통 플랫폼으로서의 Iptv 모델
1) 애플의 앱스토어가 인기를 끈 이유
** 기존의 윈도우즈 모바일 같은 자유로운 소프트웨어 플랫폼이 있었음에도 불구하고 애플의 앱스토어가 인기를 끌었던 이유는 무엇일까요?  이유는 단순합니다. 쉽다는 것이었죠. 판매하기도 쉽고 구매하고 사용하기에 쉽다라는 점이었습니다.
**  2) 쉬운 IPTV
**Iptv는 기존의 tv에 비해 복잡하다 어렵다라는 지적이 많지만 분명 pc보다는 쉬운환경입니다. 키고 채널을 선택하면 방송이 나오고 내가 파일과 폴더관리를 안해도 장르별로 vod컨탠츠들이 정리되어있으며 코덱 때문에 고민할 필요가 없습니다. 또한 인터페이스는 아주 직관적입니다.
** 3) 거실의 아이폰 IPTV
** 이를 소프트웨어에 적용한다면 어떨까요? 마찬가지로 검증된 소프트웨어를 손쉽게 접근할 수 있을 것입니다. 그리고 이를 만약 앱스토어 형식으로 개인이 판매가 가능한 구조와 수익공유를 한다면 꽤나 큰 시장이 될것입니다.  플랫폼은 java나 flash가 된다면 기존의 타 앱스토어 시장들과도 호환성을 지닐 수 있고, 소프트웨어를 판매자들을 쉽게 확보가 가능 하며 플랫폼의 검증에 대한 비용도 낮아질 것입니다. 방송도 보고 리모컨으로 조작가능한 벽에걸린 40인치 아이폰을 상상해 보십시오.

**1. IPTV Model as a Software Distribution Platform**

**1) Why Apple's App Store Became Popular**
Despite the existence of free software platforms like the old Windows Mobile, why did Apple's App Store gain such popularity? The reason is simple: it was easy. Easy to sell, easy to buy, and easy to use.

**2) Easy IPTV**
While IPTV is often criticized for being more complex and difficult than traditional TV, it is definitely an easier environment than a PC. You turn it on, select a channel, and a broadcast appears. You don't need to manage files and folders; VOD content is organized by genre, and you don't have to worry about codecs. Furthermore, the interface is very intuitive.

**3) IPTV: The iPhone in Your Living Room**
What if this were applied to software? Similarly, verified software would be easily accessible. And if this were structured like an App Store, allowing individuals to sell and share revenue, it would become quite a large market. If the platform were Java or Flash, it could maintain compatibility with other existing App Store markets, making it easier to secure software vendors and lowering the cost of platform verification. Imagine a 40-inch iPhone mounted on your wall, where you can watch broadcasts and control it with a remote.

**[Let's Mount an iPhone on the Wall]**
That is the future of IPTV.

**2. Family Games: The Content That Will Succeed on IPTV**
However, it will clearly be a market separate from mobile devices like the iPhone. It's illogical to assume that the software quality would be the same for a device under 5 inches supporting only stereo and a device over 40 inches supporting Dolby. Furthermore, mobile devices are typically one device per person, whereas a TV in the living room is a family-unit device. While children might try to monopolize it to watch animation channels, it's easy to predict that TV software will lean towards high-performance experiences shared with others, especially entertainment rather than information. And such a device already exists: Nintendo's Wii. Even its remote resembles a TV remote, and advertisements always show multiple people playing together.

This is the model IPTV should pursue as it evolves into a software market platform.

**3. IPTV Model as Software Itself**

**1) IPTV Sold as Software for Video Game Consoles**
Alternatively, if transitioning from an STB to a Wii isn't easy, one could consider implementing an IPTV STB as software on the Wii. Of course, in this scenario, IPTV would be piggybacking on Nintendo, but it could lower the cost of the STB and leverage the power of a strong gaming company like Nintendo. Naturally, the target could also be Sony's PS, Microsoft's Xbox, or the next-generation Natal, rather than just the Wii.

**2) Advantages of IPTV as Software**
When IPTV transforms from an STB business into a software industry and gains flexibility through this, conquering the global market will also become easier. If developed in this format, it would create a structure where it could immediately enter any country with the relevant video game console and high-speed internet, much like a web service. Furthermore, even if it is later ported to a mobile environment, if it has a software structure, it would only require porting, eliminating the need to implement separate specifications for mobile IPTV. At that point, we might even see an app called myLgTV in the App Store.

**4. IPTV's Crossroads**
{{IMG:5}}
Above, I've explained two main scenarios. It's possible to pursue both simultaneously, or perhaps just one. However, it's clear that neither path will be easy. Creating a software platform like WiPi required immense effort. IPTV would demand even more.
Moreover, these concepts lack specifications and standards, and they are quite outside the scope of current discussions.
Current broadcasting standards also feel overly constrained by existing frameworks. It even seems that operators, law enforcement, and the media are too fixated on "broadcasting" as a specific type of content, missing out on something much larger. The meaning of IPTV is no longer solely about broadcasting. Broadcasting is merely one method of delivering content to a large audience simultaneously.
More importantly, it's time to consider how content will be produced and distributed, how new content and business models will be created, and what models will be competitive in the future.
