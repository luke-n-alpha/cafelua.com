Sponsored by KT, my fellow bloggers have posted about 'filial piety phones' (phones for parents). I haven't received anything from KT, but I'd like to talk about filial piety phones. In fact, I'm going to be critical of existing filial piety phones, so perhaps it's only natural that I'm not receiving sponsorship.

**1. A Misconception Among Misconceptions About Seniors.** **Seniors will only make calls.**
The concept of filial piety phones is based on cheap plans, especially voice call plans, due to seniors' low economic dependence on their children. Thanks to this, the devices have specs that seem to have come from a time machine five years ago.

This is because there's a very big misunderstanding about seniors. It's assumed that seniors don't use complex smartphones and aren't a generation that feels the need for them.
**2. Reconsider Why Seniors Can't Use Smartphones**
However, I question this. The feature phones seniors used five years ago were, in the past, phones equipped with the latest technology. Seniors are accustomed to sending texts or asking their children to set their grandchildren's photos as their wallpaper. And through my wife's work as a welfare worker, I once participated in a computer learning program for seniors at a welfare center, and there were so many seniors who were financially comfortable and eager to learn computers that they lined up to wait.
While their learning ability was certainly lower than that of young people, their enthusiasm was full, and they enjoyed communicating with their children and grandchildren using information devices and creating communities just for themselves. And one more thing I realized was that there were no learning tools for seniors, and the programs weren't user-friendly. That's why I separately printed and created large learning materials.
Let's go back to smartphones. The Android on the latest smartphones is more user-friendly than Windows, but it's an OS created by Google, a prime example of a 'nerd' company. Also, while screens have gotten bigger, the resolution has also increased to boost ppi, making the text small. Is the situation different for filial piety phones? They just don't use Google's OS, but the screen is small to cut costs.
Is a keypad truly a fundamental need that seniors want? The preference for keypads is strong because it requires no separate learning, but a physical keyboard can be an advantage for all generations, not just seniors. More than that, I think the large numbers embedded on the keypad are also a factor that seniors would prefer.
**3. Next time, get your father a regular phone.**
This is my mother's recent request. She uses KakaoTalk, takes photos, and even makes video calls via Tango on my phone. Why would she make such a request to my father, who sometimes uses Kakao Story? It's because she felt it was dangerous for my father, who drives a lot.
**4. Conditions a Filial Piety Phone Should Meet**
**1) Consistent and simple UX**
My father likes listening to music, so I set up my ID on Daum Music for him. I explained, 'You press this icon, and then you press this here.' The advantage and disadvantage of Android is that each app has its own UX. Also, the Android style, consisting of a home screen and app drawer, is not a very good structure for seniors. In this regard, iPhones can be seen as more senior-friendly. In other words, manufacturers who want to create filial piety phones should consider this.
**2) Large screen with software-based low resolution**
Seniors need large font sizes. However, many apps do not support large fonts. Therefore, as phones themselves get larger, there's a need for software-based low-resolution implementation, not physical low resolution. The Galaxy Grand implemented physical low resolution, which I commend for its low price. However, I have to give it a low score for requiring the creation of two or more hardware lineups. Therefore, I believe software-based low-resolution processing technology is needed that can be used on devices like the Galaxy Note, Vega No.6, or Optimus G Pro.
**3) Home UI designed for seniors**
I once saw the simple mode of the Vega series, and it was a UI that came closest to this concept. While Samsung's Easy Mode was busy enlarging TouchWiz, Vega's efforts to maintain the existing feature phone style as much as possible while integrating key smartphone functions were notable.
**4) Provide easy access paths for essential apps**
Seniors' main needs are for a few top apps. Even creating a Google Play account for this is a huge task. Therefore, manufacturer or carrier markets and preloaded apps that don't require separate logins would be able to support this.
**5) Private SNS for seniors**
Seniors primarily use social media as a communication tool among family and friends, rather than public SNS like Twitter or Facebook. Therefore, proper support tools are needed for this, and currently, private SNS like Band seems most suitable. What if a Band launcher, similar to KakaoHome, were released and supported by manufacturers? While manufacturers could certainly create their own, considering their app quality and strategies so far, the chances of success don't seem very high.
**5. Let's See Some Smart Filial Piety Phones**
