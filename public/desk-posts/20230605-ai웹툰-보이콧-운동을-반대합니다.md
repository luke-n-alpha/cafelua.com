**Problem 5. Theft and distribution of a specific artist's art style using hypernetworks, DreamBooth, and Lora - A problem of the user, not AI.**

{{IMG:6}}

This is illegal, yes. The intent is clear, and it's illegal whether AI does it or a person learns it. This is because appropriating an artist's drawing is illegal. However, imitating an art style is, unfortunately, not illegal. A copied work is not illegal; it simply has less market value because it's not the original. While its value might be similar if it were a consumer product, this is not within the scope of copyright protection. I'd like to suggest registering a design patent, but currently, art styles are not protected. I don't know why that is, but perhaps it's because it's difficult to make a clear legal judgment.

**Problem 6. AI companies cannot draw hundreds of millions of images on their own. - False**

{{IMG:7}}

Yes, it's possible. There are many wealthy companies. One company has already done something similar. There have been projects to gather AI researchers to create such things, and in Korea, under the previous administration, there was a "Data Dam" project, so much more budget has already been executed than you might think, and it continues to be executed. The cost of image copyright is not that significant in comparison. However, before that, we've entered an era where so many images are no longer needed. That's why I say the opposite is meaningless. And with just a little more data added to a single model created this way, a model trainable on an RTX 3060 laptop with only 20 images can be produced. I wish this discussion could have happened when I advocated for an industry-academia-research collaboration system for artists in 2019.

**Problem 7. Individual artists cannot train their own images. -> False**

And so, individual artists can already train their own models. If you buy a used RTX 3090 PC, it will cost about 1.5 million won, and a week should be enough to study and actually try it out. The actual running time is one day.

**Problem 8. Portrait rights, sexual exploitation issues -> Again, not an AI problem**

Portrait rights infringement - Yes, those cases are visible. It's illegal, so it must be stopped. However, this wasn't something that only happened with AI. Sexual exploitation - Sexual exploitation targets people. Virtual characters are difficult to protect legally. However, it is certainly true that child pornography is subject to some implicit global regulation. This has happened whether it's AI or not. If the problem is that it's easier, then we should actually cut off the internet... **Regarding the mixing of ordinary people's photos in models** -

{{IMG:11}}

As you mentioned, such concerns exist. However, this problem is closer to an issue that arises during distribution. First, it's difficult to collect and reproduce ordinary people's faces exactly unless intended. Intending to do so is a violation of portrait rights, as mentioned earlier, and intentional sexual violence. Therefore, this is not a question of AI or not, but falls within the same scope as revenge porn. However, if generally run as is, it will always produce different faces. However, this is an operational issue, not a technological one.

**Problem 9. Will artists' income be protected by AI labeling? -> Impossible**

I find this argument even more regrettable. Because it's clear that artists' income will not be protected by AI labeling. This is a measure to distinguish AI, but the real threat of AI is not because it's indistinguishable, but because it's superior or more efficient than humans. Immediately, people will be able to assist with entertainment, and gradually, its scope and methods will expand. Clearly, such a stopgap policy proposal is not a fundamental solution. You labeled it as artists' "rights," but unfortunately, there are no codified "rights." Artists do not have the right to prevent individuals or companies from using AI in creation.

**Problem 10. Expressions that divide creators, what a real creator is**

{{IMG:13}}

Referring to "real creators" seems to imply that commercial artists are not real creators. There is no such thing. At least as someone who loved webtoons, whether they did webtoons to make money, or to tell the stories they really wanted to draw, whether they drew a single image in Challenge Comics, or were a top artist ranking #1 on Naver, they are all creators. They all know the pain of creation and loved webtoons and comics. There are also people who are trying to create better works using AI technology, and there is already much evidence that this method can be efficient. I see little difference from the past argument that doing webtoons didn't make one a cartoonist. Only artists themselves, not others, can define what a real creator is.

**Problem 11. The AI market is full of charlatans - Agreed, but difficult to see as an AI-specific problem**

{{IMG:14}}

I partially agree with this point. I don't think just anyone can do AI webtoons. And with the heated interest, it's a good time for crowdfunding and for charlatans to proliferate. However, this too shall pass. Many technology markets have been like this. However, in the case of commissions, it seems clear that a part of that market has already ended. But the commission market, where illegal secondary works accounted for a large proportion, was never a legitimate market with the most copyright disputes to begin with, and there are no legal issues unless someone distributed another's copyrighted work as their own. As I said before, art style is unfortunately not protected. I am of the opinion that it should be protected, but legally, it seems difficult. This is probably because judges are unlikely to know how to appreciate art. In this situation, the possibility of legislation is almost nil.

**Finally, I oppose the AI webtoon boycott movement.** This is not to defend webtoons or illustrations that violate copyright. AI is just a tool, and the moment this technology is banned, the Korean webtoon industry will clearly lose new opportunities. As I mentioned earlier, my proposal from 2019 has already expired. This is because technology has advanced while we looked away. An AI webtoon boycott would be making the same mistake again. I am concerned that the AI webtoon boycott, which received only 345 likes and 637 star ratings in Challenge Comics, is being heard as the representative voice of the webtoon industry. Naver Webtoon has about 10 million users.

**Additionally, I demand proper measures and policies for AI webtoons, not just simple regulations.** Just as the digital webtoon era connected traditional cartoonists and webtoon artists, I ask for education on AI webtoons and increased investment in technological research. Furthermore, I hope that the growing industry due to AI webtoons will participate in measures to protect the artistic and cultural aspects of comics that may diminish due to technological change, as well as fund-raising and policies for social change. Of course, there is a clear concern that such funds and budgets might again be used for political infighting or not properly delivered to charlatans, but I still believe it has its own meaning. I'm also a bit puzzled why only Naver Webtoon always gets hit. This is not about giving up on either technology or culture.

No other country is as sensitive and quick to respond to digital comics and webtoons as Korea. Korean webtoon artists' digital capabilities are excellent compared to global standards and worth fighting for. But if we extinguish the spark with simple AI webtoon regulations, how will we respond to the growth of the webtoon industry in other countries?

We plan to create an AI webtoon platform and continuously release AI webtoon creation technology after deep consideration. AI is not a technology only for large corporations but for everyone. Comics were originally accessible to everyone, a good tool for anyone to easily express their imagination. And AI will exist as a tool to make it even easier. Furthermore, not only individual creators but also companies will be able to build more efficient systems, and a webtoon creation ecosystem will be created where artists, as employees, receive more stable working conditions in such companies. Is this future really a bad future? Do I have proof? Game developers have already walked this path. Of course, they still complain that only profitable games are made, but indie games continue to be developed, and good companies offer quite decent treatment.
