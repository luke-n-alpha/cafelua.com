Professor Jung Ji-hoon of Myongji University, also well-known for his blog 'High Concept High Touch,' is hosting it. He vividly portrays anecdotes from Facebook with a realistic perspective. These stories are new and beneficial to me because they tell the startup stories of IT, my main interest, with very specific, empirical examples.

**- Facebook Stories That Are More Interesting Because They're Empirical -**
Rather than vague Silicon Valley fantasies or Mark Zuckerberg's greatness, this drama clearly shows how various factors contributed to Facebook's growth through numerous individual anecdotes before its rise. What I appreciate most about this drama, which has undergone more extensive historical research than the movie 'The Social Network,' is that it somewhat aligns with themes I've often discussed: it clearly shows through various anecdotes that global successes like Facebook weren't achieved solely by the creative ideas of people in their 20s. Particularly impressive was Sean Parker, the founder of Napster and a senior engineer in the IT field, who helped Facebook succeed as a company in its early days. I believe one of the biggest flaws in current domestic venture policies is that they ignore the existence of 'Sean Parker' figures. (Actually, I'm already too old to be Zuckerberg... Could I be Sean Parker? ^^)

Also, as I listen to this story, while Mark Zuckerberg is the protagonist, numerous colleagues appear who contributed to his success. It feels similar to the joy and adventure of Luffy, the protagonist of One Piece, as he meets friends and embarks on his journey. If you're considering a startup in the IT field, I highly recommend listening to this drama at least once.
**Of course, Facebook's success story is not the only answer. However, the DNA of success flowing within that story is found in many successful companies, and Facebook's crises were also problems that many failed companies couldn't overcome.**

Thinking about how to use my driving time more constructively and enjoyably, I started listening to 'Innovator's Challenger,' one of the podcasts I now look forward to every day. Since my posts are mainly about IT business, I thought it would be perfect for my visitors, so I'm recommending it.
