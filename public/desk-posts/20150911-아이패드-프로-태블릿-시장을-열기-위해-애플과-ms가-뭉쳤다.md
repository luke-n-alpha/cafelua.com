**1. iPad Pro's Pen and Steve Jobs**
Steve Jobs hated pens. This was a well-known fact. He said that a pen was a terrible interface on a small mobile phone and released the iPhone, an innovative touch device.

And after Jobs' death, Tim Cook released the iPad with a pen and keyboard.

Is it a betrayal of Steve Jobs? People seem busy mocking the deceased Steve Jobs. However, what Steve Jobs criticized was actually the resistive stylus of Windows Pocket PCs at the time. Indeed, the resistive stylus was a terrible interface. On devices that were too small, the stylus required writing tiny letters that made one's body and mind shrink, and the recognition rate was terrible on a shaky bus. Devices back then had very low resolution compared to today, so pen lines were very thick compared to today. And the screen was even smaller. In that respect, the iPhone's touch-based interface was revolutionary. And today, even Windows-based tablets with pens operate via touch before the pen is taken out and palm rejection activates, and interfaces combining touch and pen are also being considered. I don't think there's much reason for Steve Jobs to be mocked just because the iPad Pro came out with a pen. And he was someone who would withdraw his thoughts at any time depending on the situation. Of course, he also had a stubborn streak, never admitting he was wrong. Therefore, I believe that even if Steve Jobs were alive, the iPad Pro might have come with a pen. It didn't just come out; it even brought angle detection, which is usually only found on Intuos Pro, so this is quite excellent.

**2. Tim Cook is not Steve Jobs. Why with MS?**
However, what Tim Cook did that went a step further than Steve Jobs was to appear alongside Microsoft. Steve Jobs, due to his ill-fated relationship with Microsoft, would likely never have done such a show of partnership out of pride. So why did he appear with Microsoft?

**1) The iPad's potential as a productivity tool**
The iPad is already widely used in education and industrial settings. In these places, it's mainly used for viewing, looking up, or displaying something. And at such times, when asking 'How about this product?' or adding explanations, using a touch keyboard or drawing with touch is very inconvenient. Therefore, even a capacitive pen is sometimes used. At the point these demands emerged, the iPad already needed a pen.

As a productivity tool, the apps were still unremarkable. Meanwhile, Microsoft Office, the global standard for word processors, appeared on Android and iOS. At this point, it's a natural choice for the iPad to sell a keyboard. If there's user demand, why wouldn't they create it?

**2) MS and Apple's shared concern, a solution to "why aren't people using tablets?"**
MS released Windows 8, but people still insisted on desktop mode and demanded the Start button. For MS, which needed to secure the future of tablets, the existing user environment was becoming an obstacle. And they released Windows 10. They forcibly upgraded Windows and offered a solution that switched between desktop and tablet modes. However, tablet apps are still lacking. For the third-party market to open and become active, there ultimately needs to be user volume, or scale. Tablets didn't sell as much as expected, and their performance was poor compared to existing Macs and Windows PCs. Amidst this, Apple and MS joined hands and began creating similar forms of tablets. In other words, it means an environment where the same type of apps can operate has been created. The Windows and iPad markets have become a single market, and app developers now have more reasons to create tablet apps. In fact, the growth of Android benefited both iOS and Android. It seems they intend to reproduce the same phenomenon in the tablet market.

**3. Will MS sell Surface?**
The emergence of the iPad Pro has, in a way, proven MS's long-standing consideration of tablets as productivity tools to be correct. Of course, as an entertainment tool, the iPad was already doing well enough, and MS tried to imitate it. In any case, this announcement has led to more people re-evaluating the Surface Pro. But I don't think this will lead to increased hardware sales for MS. Recently, Chinese tablet PCs have become surprisingly good. Devices on par with Surface or iPad are already coming out at half the price. This means it's not the time to do business by selling hardware. Since MS's main business isn't hardware sales anyway, there's no harm.

**4. It seems the tablet era is finally opening. A challenge for Korea**
Two years ago, expectations for the tablet era were high, but it was less successful than anticipated. Windows 8 was also a flop, and with the change of leadership at MS and Apple, they sharpened their swords. And instead of drawing their swords against each other, they began to jointly pioneer the market. It seems the tablet era will gain more momentum. Amidst this, Korea's Active X seems to be in more trouble. Keyboard security doesn't work on tablet PCs without keyboards. iPad and MS Edge browsers do not support NAPI. Web technology is moving towards supporting only minimal standards, and apps are coming to the forefront. Shouldn't there be a discussion about how much to do with web technologies and what apps to create? And I'm using MS's Windows 10, and the service dominance of OS companies with preloaded software is overwhelmingly increasing compared to the PC era. This will likely be another concern for native Korean internet and mobile companies like Naver and Kakao.
