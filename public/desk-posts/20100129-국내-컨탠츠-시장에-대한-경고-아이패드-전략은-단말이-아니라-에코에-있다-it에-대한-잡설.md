**1. 다시한번 애플의 에코를 살펴 볼 때**
** - spec은 나왔으니 이번엔 에코를 봅시다.**
아이패드의 목표는 확실해졌습니다. 전자책 시장의 석권을 위한 플랫폼. 그렇다면 다시한번 애플의 단말이 아닌 에코에 집중해서 볼때입니다. 아이폰을 아이폰 웨이브까지 만들어낸 것은 단순히 잘만든 단말로 보기에는 어려운 면이있습니다. 애플의 아이폰이 이룬 가장 큰 혁신은 아이폰이 아니라 아이폰을 가지고 참여할 수 있는 에코, 곧 앱스토어에 있었습니다. 그렇다면 아이패드가 가지는 의미는 뭘까요? 일단 휴대용 게임기로는 해답이 아닌 것 같습니다. 버스에서 a4용지 만한 게임을 하고 있으면 딱 오덕으로 오인받을 겁니다. 그렇다고 넷북의 대체로 본다면 아이패드는 아이폰os가 아니라 mac os로 나왔어야 맞습니다. 결론은 책, 곧 ebook이란 의미입니다.
그러나 ebook에 적합한 특징은 오히려 킨들이 많이 가지고 있는 상황이고, 아마존의 출판물 유통 플랫폼은 견고합니다. 그렇다면 애플은 다시 무언가를 뛰어넘는것이 있어야 합니다. 그것은 바로 앱스토어를 이용한 출판 시장, 어플리케이션 외의 컨탠츠 시장입니다. 신문, 도서, 만화가 되지 않을까 싶습니다.

**2. 전자책 시장의 특징과 어려움 **
** - 신미디어와 구미디어의 충돌**
사실 이 시장은 우리나라의 음반시장만큼 지독히도 보수적인 시장입니다. 대형 언론사, 문학작가군, 인터넷보다도 많은   정보를 오프라인도서형태로 보유하고 있는 도서관, 학술지등 이러한 시장은 인터넷에 검색되지 않았고 종이도서가 온라인도서로 교체되면서 자신들이 가지고 있던 시스템과 권위가 무너지는 것을 두려워합니다. 온라인 도서 업체와 오프라인 도서업체의 마찰은 불과 얼마전까지만해도 뜨거운 감자였으며, 구글의 본문검색과 네이버의 본문검색 역시도 출판계의 반발이 있었습니다. 또한 신문 영역에서도 뉴스캐스트는 여전히 뜨거운 감자로 남아있습니다. 이렇듯 오늘날까지도 인터넷 사업에서 가장 어려운 이야기가 바로 이 분야입니다.
이는 신미디어와 구미디어와의 충돌이라고도 볼 수 있는데, 구미디어가 오래되었다고 가치가 없다는 것은 아닙니다. 오히려 여전히 종이로된 양질의 정보는 인터넷을 상회하고 있기에 네이버나 구글 같은 대형 포탈들이 목을 메는 것입니다. 반대로 신미디어는 신속성, 유연성, 낮은 유통마진, 개인화, 소비성의 장점을 가지고 있고 블로그의 등장, 오마이뉴스, 트위터등의 등장으로 폭발적으로 증가하고 있는 추세입니다.

**1. Re-examining Apple's Ecosystem**
** - Specs are out, now let's look at the ecosystem.**
The iPad's goal has become clear: a platform to dominate the e-book market. If so, it's time to focus once again not on Apple's device, but on its ecosystem. The iPhone's ability to create an 'iPhone wave' makes it difficult to view it as merely a well-made device. The biggest innovation achieved by Apple's iPhone was not the iPhone itself, but the ecosystem one could participate in with the iPhone – namely, the App Store. So, what is the significance of the iPad? Firstly, it doesn't seem to be the answer as a portable gaming device. If you're playing a game the size of an A4 sheet on the bus, you'll likely be mistaken for an otaku. And if it's seen as a netbook replacement, the iPad should have come out with macOS, not iOS. The conclusion is books, meaning e-books.
However, features suitable for e-books are largely possessed by Kindle, and Amazon's publishing distribution platform is robust. Therefore, Apple needs to transcend something once again. That is the publishing market using the App Store, a content market beyond applications. I suspect it will be newspapers, books, and comics.

**2. Characteristics and Challenges of the E-book Market**
** - The Clash Between New and Old Media**
In fact, this market is as stubbornly conservative as Korea's music market. Major media outlets, literary authors, libraries holding more information in offline book form than the internet, academic journals – these markets were not searchable online, and they fear the collapse of their systems and authority as paper books are replaced by online books. The friction between online and offline book publishers was a hot potato until recently, and Google's full-text search and Naver's full-text search also met with resistance from the publishing industry. Furthermore, in the newspaper sector, NewsCast remains a hot potato. Thus, even today, this field represents the most challenging aspect of internet business.
This can also be seen as a clash between new media and old media, but it doesn't mean old media is valueless just because it's old. On the contrary, high-quality information in paper form still surpasses the internet, which is why major portals like Naver and Google are so desperate for it. Conversely, new media offers advantages such as speed, flexibility, low distribution margins, personalization, and consumability, and is rapidly increasing with the emergence of blogs, OhmyNews, Twitter, and more.

**3. The Potential to Become a Giant Distribution Platform for New Media, Even Attracting Old Media**
** - The Emergence of a Second J.K. Rowling Through the App Store, A New Market for Bloggers**
Let's return to the iPad. My thought is that the iPad has the potential to emerge as a distribution platform for new media. Furthermore, its high popularity and vast ecosystem will make it an attractive market that even old media will have to participate in.
Specifically, if we're talking about Apple's e-books, content sensitive to the flow of time, like the OhmyNews app or Bloter News app, will likely emerge rather than just classic novels like 'The Little Prince'. If you think about it, comic apps and news sites already launched by Naver or Daum have such characteristics.

Apple has probably paid attention to this. Also, webtoons and online serialized novels, with their entertainment nature, could fall into this category. Currently, services like HappyCampus or Naver Expert Search operate on a structure where users set prices and are charged upon download.
Apple has realized the strengths of its App Store and understood what changes it could bring to the traditional media environment. Watching CNN is distributed not via the web, but as an Apple app, i.e., an e-book. **This would reduce the inconvenient traffic flow to Google, and the e-book App Store itself would become a portal.** This also strikes me as quite similar to Naver's Cast policy. The difference is that Naver Casts are entirely free and driven by advertising, whereas Apple allows publishers to receive money, and Apple can collect a commission from that. Apple's goal is to do platform business. This will clearly show a big difference from Amazon, which merely changed the platform of the traditional publishing system, and will allow Apple to reduce friction with Amazon and build its own independent e-book system. And individuals will also be the driving force behind it. Imagine this: if J.K. Rowling, the author of Harry Potter, had started serializing her work in the iPad e-book market, she would have made an enormous amount of money.
