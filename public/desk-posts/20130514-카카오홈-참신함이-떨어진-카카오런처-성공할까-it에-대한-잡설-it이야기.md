Facebook Home, Dodol Launcher from NHN's mobile forward base, Buzz Launcher in partnership with Daum Communications, and now Kakao, the name that first comes to mind when thinking of mobile in Korea, has also released a launcher. Kakao Launcher is a company based on social networks, like Facebook, and it seems to be a latecomer among domestic launchers. Can Kakao's launcher truly succeed?

**{{IMG:1}}****[ Kakao Home's main screen, nothing particularly different. ****The only difference is KakaoTalk being embedded at the bottom. ]**

**1. The Biggest Feature, Kakao Home Collection - Kakao Timeline**
First, after downloading and installing Kakao's launcher, I could describe it in one word.
The biggest feature of Kakao Launcher is "Kakao Home Collection." It appears when you swipe the home screen to the left.

**{{IMG:2}}****[ KakaoStory and KakaoTalk news are visible at once. ]**

**{{IMG:3}}
****[ You can turn it off in settings. There's no option yet to turn off individual 3rd-party apps. ]**

If we compare it to Naver, you can imagine Naver Me. This seems to mean archiving various pushes from the Kakao platform based on Kakao Home. While existing KakaoTalk pushes were received once and then volatile, Kakao Home's Collection will have a timeline, similar to Facebook or Naver Me.

**2. Leverage of Other Kakao Apps**
{{IMG:4}} It provides a Kakao folder, in a way already demonstrated by Dodol Launcher with its Naver bundle. The synergy with other Kakao apps seems to end with the Collection and this folder.

**3. No Content Leverage from KakaoPage**
What's surprising is the lack of leverage with KakaoPage. Unlike Amazon or Google Home, it didn't adopt the method of pushing books and movies as widgets. While Dodol Launcher is a separate entity from NHN and targeted globally, Kakao's target audience is mostly domestic users, which makes this an unexpected point.

**4. Competitive Edge Compared to Dodol Launcher?**
In my personal judgment, compared to Dodol Launcher, the outlook doesn't seem bright.

**1) Launchers are not suitable for Kakao's strength, virality.**
Dodol Launcher gains strength from NHN, or rather, Naver. It has the power to promote Dodol Launcher throughout Naver's services. Since Naver's services are broadcast-style, it's not particularly strange for advertisements to be inserted. However, it is expected to be very difficult to push Kakao Launcher into the personal domains of KakaoTalk and KakaoPage, which are the core of the Kakao platform. Therefore, the ability of the Kakao platform is expected to be very weak, while the ability of the Naver platform seems stronger.

**2) Latecomer in customization, the core need for launchers**
The core user need for launchers, as currently perceived by users, is customization. It means making my phone prettier. Since Dodol Launcher was the first to seriously target this area, it has established good relationships with leading phone theme shops in Korea and has already secured many themes, including celebrities and webtoons. While it might be possible to catch up by continuously securing third-party theme providers, the penalty of being a latecomer is clear. Furthermore, Naver also has powerful unique characters from Naver Webtoon. Although the copyrights for Naver Webtoon characters are not separately restricted, and they are sold on KakaoTalk, the fact that these characters and themes originate from Naver Webtoon will remain ingrained in users' minds.

**3) There's no reason to install Kakao Launcher.**
Finally, the most fatal reason is that there's no reason to install Kakao Launcher. The "Collection" feature, which appears to be Kakao Launcher's differentiating function as introduced above, is unnecessary for users who already have KakaoStory and KakaoTalk installed. In fact, it might even be more confusing due to duplication.

**5. Is Kakao's Surge Ending Here?**
Kakao Launcher actually offers nothing particularly new. Compared to Facebook's changes, it seems no different from existing launchers. While user expectations for Kakao are high, if Kakao Launcher fails to present any value that users can gain from using it, I fear it will simply be buried. It even feels like the initial user numbers for KakaoTalk-linked apps are weakening as new services are launched.
{{IMG:5}} **[ The more recently released a Kakao app is, the lower its initial user count.****Source: News Tomato  [http://me2.do/GRjpjacp](http://me2.do/GRjpjacp) ]**
Is Kakao's surge ending like this? In contrast, NHN's Line successfully entered the global market, Band reached 10 million users, and Dodol Launcher recorded 1 million downloads within a month of its release. It even makes me wonder if this is a repeat of Daum's initial flash with maps before collapsing against NHN. Of course, the assessment that Kakao had a very positive impact on the mobile platform strategies of old-fashioned portals like Naver is valid, and even giant companies like Google and Naver have countless services that failed, though users might not know about them. However, if this trend continues, it seems clear that Kakao's image of freshness, which users have come to expect, will take a hit.
