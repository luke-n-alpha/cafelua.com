Vibe Coding, ChatGPTs, Google AI Studio – it seems there's an abundance of AI tools that make creation incredibly easy. The hottest topics, in particular, seem to be MCP and AI agents. In reality, MCP and AI agents are not at the same level of technology. MCP is merely a protocol specification, while an AI agent is just a type of concretized AI service, like a web application. AI agents can also be used to easily create various tools. The resulting products, in terms of difficulty, are probably on par with Excel functions. However, the general public might find it hard to distinguish between simple applications made with Excel functions or Access, and commercialized applications. Some are even saying that these changes will eliminate developers or herald the emergence of "super individuals." I even saw a post on a timeline claiming that one person could do the work of 40 developers. Of course, the advent of Excel did enable one person to do the work of 40 people who used to draw tables and perform calculations. But in fact, these tasks are not the essence of work. The essence of work, in software, is divided into data and processes, and the most important thing is accountability. AI, in fact, takes no responsibility for anything. Of course, if reliability could be guaranteed to the extent that no responsibility is needed, then responsibility would disappear. But I heard that Vibe Coding is about "development that accepts probability instead of reliability." This isn't entirely wrong. Existing software also accepted probability. All software had availability and bugs. AI is no different. However, responsibility still follows, and there are standard values for probability. Even 15 years ago, during OCR development, it was the same. It was meaningless to say that a 96% recognition rate was good. People would say it was unusable because of the 4%. What about cars? Let's consider an AI car with 99% availability. Who would ride an AI car that malfunctions once every 100 rides? The acceptable tolerance varies by domain. Furthermore, AI was originally software intended to control such things. To now argue for allowing malfunctions is a somewhat incomprehensible claim.

And ultimately, controlling this tolerance incurs costs and involves surprisingly complex engineering. It might require using multiple models instead of just one, and real-time adjustments and feedback on the characteristics and output values of each model. Achieving performance in AI as a commercial service has always been like this. It's not just about hitting SOTA or writing papers, is it? Fortunately, engineering tools like these are now emerging, rather than just model competition, and it's starting to be proven that orchestration and chaining of smaller models can achieve effects comparable to larger models. From now on, AI is entering the realm of engineering, not just marketers, and seems to be developing into a software industry. And the culmination of this is, in fact, the AI agent. However, many people still seem to have a shallow distinction between charts made with Excel functions and commercialized applications, and rather seem to be using AI to reduce developers. It might be difficult to persuade them why such cost and effort need to be invested, and why it has value. "Excel is enough..." But you can't build Facebook and Naver with Excel. To concede a step further, the same goes for WordPress. (Of course, there are eccentric individuals who build DOOM and compilers with Excel and Minecraft. Let's just worship these god-level individuals and make an exception for them, as they are doing strange things as a hobby.)

For reference, AI is still an investment target, not a means to make a lot of money. Especially in Korea, it's just a recession, so nothing is easy. Also, while AI might be used as an excuse to reduce personnel, ultimately, companies that invest in AI technology will succeed when the AI market truly arrives. It feels like a very long tunnel. For individuals, and for the market.

Just random thoughts.
