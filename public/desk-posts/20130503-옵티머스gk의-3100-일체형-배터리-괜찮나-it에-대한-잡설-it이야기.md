LG에서 올라온 gk의 메뉴얼 중에는 이 정도 사양이라고 표기 되어있습니다.{{IMG:2}} 제 경우에는 인터넷 사용시간이 제일 중요한데, 연속 사용 시간이 LTE모드에서 350분 정도 기록하고 있네요.  소비자원의 "스마트폰 배터리 사용시간 측정 및 표기 방법" 대로 측정했다고 했는데 인터넷 상에서 이에 대한 기준 자료를 구할 수 있는 방법이 없네요. 소비자원에 문의를 해봐야 하나.. 웹서핑의 경우 사용행태에 따라 사용시간이 크게 달라집니다.
위의 정보를 토대로 말씀드리면, 거의 6시간정도의 인터넷 사용시간을 보장한다는 의미 같네요. 이 정도면 사실 충분한 시간이긴 합니다.
**2. 반드시 알아야 할 점, 약정 기간동안에 배터리용량은 줄어든다.**BUT. 문제는 하나 더 있죠. 사용횟수에 따른 배터리 수명 하락. 배터리 제조사는 500Cycle에 80%의 수명을 보증합니다. (최초 사용후 6개월, 6개월 이후에는 무상교환 안해준다는 얘기) 2년 약정기간동안 500회 넘게 충전한다는게 문제죠. 거기다 완전방전 시키고 완전 충전을 시키지 않으니 그 횟수는 더 잦습니다. 2년후에 최악의 경우 2000회 정도 충전한다고 가정하면

I was comparing the Galaxy S4, Vega Iron, and Optimus G Pro for my phone change next month, when I suddenly heard about a new model called Optimus GK, so I'm looking into it. I don't usually do reviews or smartphone analyses, but I'm sharing the information I've learned in hopes that it will be helpful. The phone I want is an affordable smartphone around 5 inches with a good camera. For 5 inches, the Galaxy S4 and Vega Iron were candidates. For a good camera, the Optimus G Pro and Galaxy S4. For an affordable phone, the Optimus G Pro and Vega Iron... It's as if they conspired to each get 2 points. In the midst of this, I heard news of a phone being released that might meet all the above conditions. The Optimus GK, which is said to be a scaled-down version of the Optimus G Pro. But there's one catch... the non-removable battery. I'm sensitive about battery life because I use the internet a lot. Newer phones generally perform well, and I wasn't worried since they had removable batteries... but why a non-removable one? Still, with information about its large 3100mAh battery, I decided to dig for more details.

**2. What You Must Know: Battery Capacity Decreases During the Contract Period.**
BUT. There's another problem. Battery life degradation due to usage frequency. Battery manufacturers guarantee 80% life after 500 cycles. (This means no free replacement after 6 months from initial use.) The problem is that it will be charged more than 500 times during a 2-year contract period. Furthermore, since it's not fully discharged and fully charged, the frequency is even higher. Assuming it's charged about 2000 times in the worst-case scenario after 2 years,

{{IMG:3}}
**[ Source: [http://www.lifelinebatteries.com/](http://www.lifelinebatteries.com/images/DOD-LIFECYCLES.jpg) ]**
**After 2 years, the battery will only fully charge to about 30%. (Typically, it's around 40-50% after 1000-1500 cycles.) In the worst case, after 2 years, the Optimus GK's battery capacity will be 1000mAh, allowing for just under 2 hours of internet use. (Usually, it's around 1500mAh, allowing for about 3 hours of use.)**
Actually, even this isn't bad, but the non-removable aspect definitely becomes a weakness. (Of course, removable batteries follow the same graph, but they can reduce the number of cycles due to frequent full charges and discharges, and have the advantage of being easy to use while charging.)
**3. Alternatives Can Cover It: Replacement and Portable Battery**
Of course, the battery can be replaced at an LG service center. For the Optimus G, the replacement cost was 37,000 won. (Which is said to be very cheap compared to Apple.) You can either purchase a portable battery or get the battery replaced at the service center. (It would be great if LG coolly offered one free replacement within 2 years.) Portable batteries can be purchased even cheaper. There are even batteries with 3 times the capacity of the Optimus GK. Wow. You could use that to get by, and then switch phones when your contract ends. Nothing seems to perfectly suit my taste. Oh well... For now, the Optimus GK is at the top of my wishlist. Since I'll be living with my wife next month, I'm researching as thoroughly as possible, because if I don't explain these weaknesses to her, I might get a lot of flak about the rapidly draining battery about a year from now. Should I just go for the Optimus G Pro...? My wife, who has small hands, probably wouldn't like a 5.5-inch phone.
