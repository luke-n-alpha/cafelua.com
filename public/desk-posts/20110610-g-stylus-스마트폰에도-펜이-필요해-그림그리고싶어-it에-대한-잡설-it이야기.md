**1. 갤럭시s2로도 그림그리고 싶다.**갤럭시s2로 바꾸고나니 넓은 화면이 눈에 들어오더군요. 어쩐지 예전처럼 다시 모바일에서 그림을 그려보고 싶은 마음이 생겼습니다. 감압식의 X1때는 제법 괜찮은 그림을 그렸습니다.{{IMG:1}} **[ X1 스타일러스 + 포켓아티스트 3.0 조합 ]** 그러나 정전식 터치를 쓰는 옵티머스큐때 적당한 툴과 펜을 찾지 못했습니다. 그때 사용했던 툴은 페인터나 포토샵에 비하면 형편없었고 앞에 스폰지같은게 달렸던 H10은 앞은 판판해서 그림그리기에 적당하지 않았습니다. 정확도를 높여볼까 칼로 깎았다가 심지어는 펜을 버리기도 했죠. **{{IMG:2}} 그러나 기기가 바뀐김에 다시 그림그릴 환경을 찾아보기 시작했습니다. 그래서 발견한것은 autodesk의 sbm(sketch book)앱입니다. autodesk는 일찍이 그래픽툴로 유명한 회사였고 pc용 그래픽툴과 비교안될정도로 싼 가격, 깔고 사용해보니 레이어와 다양한 브러기, 페인터와 비슷한 방식의 팔레트 제대로된 드로잉툴이더군요. 이제 펜만 찾으면 되게 됬습니다.

**1. I want to draw with the Galaxy S2 too.**
After switching to the Galaxy S2, the large screen caught my eye. Somehow, I felt like drawing on mobile again, just like before. With the pressure-sensitive X1, I drew quite well.
{{IMG:1}} **[X1 Stylus + Pocket Artist 3.0 Combination]**
However, when I used the Optimus Q with its capacitive touch, I couldn't find a suitable tool or pen. The tools I used back then were terrible compared to Painter or Photoshop, and the H10, which had a sponge-like tip, was flat at the front, making it unsuitable for drawing. To improve accuracy, I even tried carving it with a knife and ended up throwing the pen away. (Oh, what a waste of money~)

**2. SBM, such a cool app! From Autodesk!**
{{IMG:2}} However, since I changed devices, I started looking for a drawing environment again. What I found was Autodesk's SBM (Sketchbook) app. Autodesk was already a famous company for graphic tools, and this app was incredibly cheap compared to PC graphic tools. After installing and using it, I found it to be a proper drawing tool with layers, various brushes, and a palette similar to Painter. Now, all I needed was to find a pen.

**3. This time, let's find a pen! The world's first G-Stylus using conductive fiber.**
Then, hearing rumors that 3M was good, I was contemplating buying one when I personally received a request to try a new type of pen. It was exactly what I was looking for, so I accepted and tried it. They said it was a new pen that used conductive fiber instead of the usual rubber tip. I haven't used the 3M one, so I can't do a comparison post.

******[ Unboxing ]****
First, the exterior is luxurious and feels heavier than expected. The grip was satisfying, and it's also good for clipping the pen like this.
{{IMG:3}}

The pen tip is said to be made of fabric, woven like a net, and is a spherical shape with elasticity.

{{IMG:4}}** [Conductive Fiber Tip]**
It looks sturdy somehow. It's described as a conductive fiber material, but it looks almost like metal. I checked their website, and they even posted that it undergoes 20 types of reliability tests.
{{IMG:5}}
The most important thing is the user experience, which has definitely improved compared to the old H-10. Since the tip is spherical, the feeling of it pressing slightly as you draw gives a slight brush-like sensation. It also allows for much more precise touch than a finger. As advertised, it would certainly be useful even in cold winter. It also seems effective for those who use handwriting recognition instead of a touch keypad.

**3. Apple's patents reminiscent of Wacom's digitizers**
I'm not sure if it's an anecdote or a rumor that Steve Jobs excluded pens from the early iPhone, saying they weren't unique to humans, but Apple's recent patents seem to involve pens and sensing in the air, similar to Wacom.
{{IMG:6}}
{{IMG:7}}

**4. Although limitations exist due to the inherent problems of capacitive touch panels, G-Stylus showed great effort.**
However, to draw a final conclusion, it's not as satisfying as an electromagnetic induction pen. Could it be that the inherent properties of capacitive touch panels don't align with precise touch? Nintendo's new control device, the Wii U, features what appears to be a pressure-sensitive pen and demonstrates precise drawing.

{{IMG:8}}**[Nintendo Wii U]**
Because of this, laptops with hybrid touch panels combining capacitive and pressure-sensitive or electromagnetic induction methods are emerging. My TM2 is one such device. With the TM2, when an electromagnetic induction pen gets close to the screen, the capacitive touch panel automatically deactivates. So, it's fine to rest your hand on the screen while drawing. Because of this habit, I was flustered when using the G-Stylus, as other fingers unknowingly touched the screen, causing unexpected input. However, it's still difficult to find smartphones that use hybrid panels. For now, it seems I'll have to be content with this type of pen. Dot-level control is difficult, but a certain level of drawing is possible.
{{IMG:9}}
** [My first drawing using SBM + G-Stylus]**

I think better drawings will come once I get used to it. Ah... it's been so long since I drew, so it's a 'foot drawing' (a bad drawing). If you search for touch pens, many high-end ones are dominated by 3M and ELAGO brands, while general-purpose ones, called 'bullet pens,' are popular for their small size. In reality, the G-Stylus is a bit large for carrying on a strap; it's a standard pen size. If they made a smaller size like the bullet pen or an adjustable-length type, it would be better for mobile use. Of course, for pads, I think the current size is better, but... **[Correction] There are also small pens with an extension mechanism that can be attached to a strap.** Ultimately, the ideal is still a Wacom-style electromagnetic induction pen with both pressure and tilt recognition, but due to price, patents, and weight constraints, it doesn't seem to be easily adopted for mobile devices. Can we look forward to the day when Mr. Jobs (or Apple) bestows similar performance upon us? When I posted this on Facebook, my friend joked that Mr. Jobs dislikes trivial pens, so he'd tell us to cut off our fingers ㄷㄷㄷ

**5. The analog sensibility provided by a pen, used for thousands of years, cannot be replaced by a finger.**
For educational pad devices to replace paper, they cannot exclude the pen, which has been with humanity for thousands of years. Anyway, I am one person who believes that the analog sensibility provided by a pen is still valid.
