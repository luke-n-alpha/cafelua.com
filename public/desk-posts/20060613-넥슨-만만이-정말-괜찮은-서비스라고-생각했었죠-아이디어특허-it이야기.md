만화가들이라는 족속이 대체적으로 표현하는것을 즐겨하는 사람들이라

만화의 대중화를 이끌수 있다고 생각했었죠.

그후에 만만이와 붐이란 서비스를 보았고
굳이 프로가 아니어도 만화란 시장에 참여할수 있겠다 싶었죠.

무리해서라도 만화가들 몇명 섭외해서 해볼까도 생각해봤지만

대사가 이미지가 아닌 텍스트라면 다국어를 쉽게 지원할수 있겠다고 생각했습니다.

실력있는 한국의 만화가들을 일본에 직접 진출시킬수 있겠다고 생각한거죠.

So Naver is finally launching it on a large scale.
I thought Naver copied Nexon's service...
But it turns out it was Nexon's after all ㅡㅡ;

It was during the 2002 World Cup, when I had just returned to university as a junior.
[http://fstory.cafe24.com/manhwa](http://fstory.cafe24.com/manhwa)
I made something like this.

Back then, the concept of a blog wasn't properly established,

and Cyworld had just launched its Miniroom service,
showing its 2.0 version. Looking at how empty my Miniroom is in the example image...

Cartoonists are generally people who enjoy expressing themselves,
so I thought that if a professional platform could be created for them, connecting their work to revenue,
it could solve the problems of the existing comic market
and lead to the popularization of comics.

After that, I saw services like Manmani and Boom,
and thought that even non-professionals could participate in the comic market.
I even considered pushing to recruit a few cartoonists to try it out,
but it ultimately remained just a class project.

I thought it would be great to combine those old ideas with this new service.
First, a way for ordinary people to connect their template-based or self-drawn comics to revenue.
Second, a translation service.

I thought that if dialogue was text rather than images, it would be easy to support multiple languages.
Sometimes, leveraging the nature of the internet, animated GIFs could also be used.

If comic posts could be translated and published, like on EnjoyJapan,
it could bridge the gap between the Japanese and Korean comic markets,
and allow talented Korean cartoonists to directly enter the Japanese market. That's what I thought.
Indeed, only Naver could pull off a service like this.

Also, I had a hobby of mainly drawing on Cyworld's sketch board,
and I had the idea that an Oekaki board could be used for relay comics.

[http://tong.nate.com/fstory97/9632767](http://tong.nate.com/fstory97/9632767)
[http://minihp.cyworld.nate.com/pims/main/pims_gallery.asp?tid=16159007&urlstr=scra](http://minihp.cyworld.nate.com/pims/main/pims_gallery.asp?tid=16159007&urlstr=scra) - Motion Sickness Relay (Reference)

Anyway, why is it that in Korea,
unlike music,
comics can't be properly sold for money?
Although I draw as a hobby, I also pay 300 won at comic rental shops to read comics.
It's not a matter of whether 300 won is cheap or expensive,
but rather that the distribution channels are complex, and publishing incurs significant costs,
and customers can't buy or rent comics with a single click.

Well, even if we consider internet comics an exception,
internet comics themselves don't generate revenue directly;
the reality is that they aim for fame to lead to publication.
What a hassle...
It's ironic why digitally created content needs to be converted to analog to gain value.
Digital content, as digital, has infinite value.
Of course, there's also the charm of analog.

And a community for people who like the same comics can also be maintained...

These days, I also think it would be great to have a service that can foster Korean Flash animation artists.
Though not well-known by Koreans, foreigners were quite enthusiastic about it.
[http://www.sambakza.net/](http://www.sambakza.net/)
Like Amalok-nim... I also think it would be enjoyable if a platform were created where they could freely express themselves and generate income.

The reason I posted this here is
that there seem to be many people from the planning department, so please take note.
I'm a developer, after all.
And if I may ask, please don't overwork developers ^^;
While developers often have issues because they only know development,
conversely, if planners understood the development process, they would know how important their initial work is,
and how much cost, meaning developer overtime, is incurred when changes are made midway. I wish they would understand that.
Time and labor are also costs.

Lately, someone recommended the book 'Joel on Software' to me, and I've been reading it.
It really makes me think about a lot of things.
Sometimes, it's even quite unconventional.
Encouraging writing novels in specifications...
Anyway, I hope to see more wonderful services emerge in the internet world.
Monday is Monday. And the internet flows on.
