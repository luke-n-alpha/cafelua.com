**1. 누가 주의깊게 봐야 하나? - 새 휴대폰 구매고객, 약정이 끝나는 사용자**먼저 내용을 요약하자면 먼저 새 휴대폰을 구매하거나, 약정이 끝나시는 분들이 주의깊게 살피셔야 합니다. 저같이 약정이 아직 남아있는 경우에는 통신사에 전화해서 이리저리 계산해봤는데, 도친개친이더군요. 저는 비교적 따질거 따져 가입한 사용자라 오히려 조금 손해 났습니다. 그래서 현재 요금제를 유지하기로 했습니다.
** 1) 약정 할인을 기대했던 새 휴대폰 고객은 도친개친** 먼저 새 휴대폰 고객에게 오는 메리트는, 나쁜 소식을 말씀드리면 사실 별로 없습니다. 달라진 것이 있다면, 기존에 단말기 가격이다 뭐다 하며 요금제에 약정 깔던것이 할인이 없어졌고, 기본료가 낮아졌고, 무제한 음성통화가 지원된다는 점이죠. 요약하자면, 선택폭은 좁아졌으나 요금제가 명확해졌습니다. 선택폭이 넓다는게 무조건 좋은게 아닙니다. 그동안 이통사는 넓은 선택폭으로 더 많은 낚시를 했거든요. 사실 거의 도친개친입니다. 그러나, 그동안 머리 아파서 그냥 막 가입하셨던 호구분들에 대한 보호효과가 먼저 탁월할 것 같습니다. 그런 분들은 제 포스팅도 이미 안보겠죠. 그래서 정책일겁니다. 정보소외자들에 대한 혜택을 줘야 하는것이 나라가 하는 일이니까요. 이 경우에는 가장 큰 의의는 요금의 단순화입니다. 어떤분들은 도친개친이거나, 변경 후 혜택이 떨어진다고 말씀하시지만, 평균적으로는 단순할 수록 소비자가 결정하기 쉽습니다.

Since the Mobile Device Distribution Act (DanTongBeop), one piece of good news is that mobile plans are becoming simpler. Compared to the past when voice and data plans were mixed, offering a complex array of options, data-centric plans now mean you no longer have to worry about voice calls.

**1. Who should pay close attention? - New phone buyers, users whose contracts are ending**
To summarize, those who are buying a new phone or whose contracts are ending should pay close attention. In my case, with an existing contract, I called the carrier and did some calculations, but it turned out to be six of one, half a dozen of the other. Since I'm a user who signed up after carefully considering everything, I actually ended up losing a bit. So, I decided to stick with my current plan.
**1) New phone customers who expected contract discounts: Six of one, half a dozen of the other**
First, for new phone customers, to give you the bad news, there aren't really many benefits. What has changed is that the previous contract-based discounts tied to device prices and plans have disappeared, the basic monthly fee has decreased, and unlimited voice calls are now supported. In summary, while the range of choices has narrowed, the plans have become clearer. A wide range of choices isn't always good. Until now, mobile carriers used a wide range of choices to 'fish' for more customers (i.e., to trick them). In fact, it's pretty much six of one, half a dozen of the other. However, it seems there will be an excellent protective effect for those 'suckers' who used to sign up without thinking because it was too complicated. Such people probably aren't even reading my post. That's probably why it's a policy. Because it's the government's job to provide benefits to those who are information-disadvantaged. In this case, the most significant meaning is the simplification of fees. Some say it's six of one, half a dozen of the other, or that benefits decrease after the change, but on average, the simpler it is, the easier it is for consumers to decide.
**2) Used phone buyers and overseas direct purchasers: Must-read 20% plan discount**
Used phone buyers and those who purchase directly from overseas can receive a 20% plan discount. This provides a similar effect to buying a new phone with a contract discount. {{IMG:2}}

{{IMG:3}}
**2. Are there cheaper options?**
Recently, I went to E-Mart and saw the Galaxy S6 (not the edge) being sold for around 30,000 won, including the device. It was a moment where the power of MVNOs was felt. The current government seems to be simultaneously promoting MVNO activation, and it appears to be bearing fruit.
**3. Another tip**
There's a 'safety plan' (안심 요금제) which, after a certain amount of data usage, reduces the speed but allows for unlimited usage. For U+, the speed is around 400kbps, which is sufficient for web browsing and Facebook, and even YouTube works if viewed in low resolution. Of course, Kim Gisa (a navigation app) runs with plenty of speed, and listening to music on Bit (a music app) is fine, with only a slight loading time at the beginning. The conclusion is, whether it's LTE or whatever... in fact, even 3G, which provides speeds of 1.5Mbps or more, is sufficient for most user patterns. Therefore, don't obsess too much over whether it's LTE or the speed.
