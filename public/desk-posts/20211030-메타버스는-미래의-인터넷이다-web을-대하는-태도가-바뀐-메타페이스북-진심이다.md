이 글을 회사 블로그에 쓸까 하다가 약간 좀 쓸데 없이 흥분한데다가 좀 더 개인적인 글이 될 것 같아 오래간만에 네이버 블로그에 글을 씁니다. 메타로 사명이 바뀌었다는 소식과 함께 페이스북의 영상을 보았습니다.

사실 그동안 VR에서 일을 하고 있는 제게는 혁신에 가장 잘 어울리는 회사는 페이스북이었습니다. 애플이고, 삼성이고 낡았습니다. 엔지니어 입장에서는 새로운 도전이 1도 없습니다. 모바일 생태계는 고착화 되었고, 디스플레이 접던 카메라가 10만배 좋아지던 결국 스마트폰은 스마트폰일 뿐이었습니다.  당장 스마트폰만큼 시장이 커지길 바라는 사람들은 처음 오큘러스를 매우 큰 비용으로 인수할때는 잠깐 주목했다가 당장 돈이 안되고 생각했던만큼 좋지 않자 외면했습니다. 아마도 레딧플레이어원이 보여주었던 환상은 너무 컸던것이겠죠.  하지만 페이스북이 키우고 있던 오큘러스는  기어VR, 오큘러스고, 오큘러스퀘스트, 오큘러스퀘스트2 까지 매번 매우 큰 기술적 발전을 보여주었습니다. 제가 처음 어릴때 녹색스크린의 컴퓨터를 보고 느꼈던 흥분, 대학때 스마트폰 전신인 PDA를 보고 흥분, 그리고 세번째 가장 큰 흥분은 연구소에 있을때 카드보드를 보고 흥분했던 일입니다. 그것이 가진 컴퓨팅 세상의 가능성과 기술의 방향성을 보았기 때문이죠. 중간에 하드웨어를 보고 느꼈을때의 흥분말고 SW를 보고 느꼈던 흥분도 있었습니다. PDA에서 보았던 흥분은 휴대폰에 SW를 올릴수 있다는 것이었습니다. 2.5G망의 WIPI(한국 초기 무선인터넷 플랫폼) 2기 교육생으로 굳이 쫓아가서 배웠던것도 PDA에서 보았던 그 미래를 그렸기 때문입니다. 하지만, 그 미래는 3G망과 아이폰이 나오고 망이 깔리고 꽤 시간이 지나고 나서 터졌고, 4G가 될때즘은 거의 대중화 되었습니다.   영상을 보고 역시 페이스북은 제가 생각한 미래를 그리고 있구나 하는 생각을 했지만, 한편으로는 좀 걱정스러웠습니다. 이러한 멋진 미래를 끌고 나가고 있는 페이스북이 최근 많은 빅브라더의 의혹을 받고 있기 때문이었죠. 또한 오큘러스 스토어에 앱을 런칭해본 입장에서 여태껏 겪은 오큘러스는 빅브라더에 거의 가깝게 느꼈기 때문입니다. 신생 앱개발사가 마켓팅하기도 어렵고, 소위 간택된 앱만 유리한 위치에 있었고, 저희 처럼 게임이 아닌 서비스를 하는 경우 극히 무언가를 하기 어려운 구조였기 때문입니다. 심사가 없다고 했던 AppLab런칭에도 1달이 넘게 걸렸습니다.  또한 게임이아닌 웹툰, 교육 플랫폼 같은 서비스 중심인 저희의 경우 이벤트나 리퀘스트,리스폰스 방식의 앱/웹 개발방식과는 거리가 먼 프레임단위의 유니티 개발방식은 여러모로 잃는게 많았습니다. 거기다가 유니티의 하위호환성 버전문제는 머리가 지끈거리는 일이었죠.

I was going to write this post for the company blog, but it got a bit unnecessarily excited and felt more personal, so I'm writing it on my Naver blog after a long time. I watched Facebook's video along with the news that the company name changed to Meta.

Honestly, for me, working in VR, Facebook has always been the company that best embodies innovation. Apple and Samsung are old news. From an engineer's perspective, there's no new challenge whatsoever. The mobile ecosystem has become stagnant; whether displays fold or cameras improve 100,000 times, a smartphone is still just a smartphone. People who hoped the market would grow as big as smartphones initially paid attention when Oculus was acquired for a huge sum, but then turned away when it didn't immediately make money or wasn't as good as they expected. Perhaps the fantasy shown in Ready Player One was too grand. However, Oculus, nurtured by Facebook, showed significant technological advancements with every iteration: GearVR, Oculus Go, Oculus Quest, and Oculus Quest 2. The excitement I felt as a child seeing a computer with a green screen, the excitement in college seeing a PDA (the precursor to smartphones), and the third biggest excitement was seeing Cardboard while in the research lab. This was because I saw the possibilities of the computing world and the direction of technology it held. In between, there was also excitement I felt not from hardware, but from software. The excitement I saw in PDAs was the ability to put software on mobile phones. I even went out of my way to become a second-generation trainee for WIPI (Korea's early wireless internet platform) on the 2.5G network because I envisioned the future I saw in PDAs. However, that future only truly exploded much later, after the 3G network and iPhone emerged and the infrastructure was laid, becoming almost mainstream by the time 4G arrived. After watching the video, I thought, "Facebook is indeed envisioning the future I imagined," but at the same time, I was a bit worried. This was because Facebook, which is leading such a wonderful future, has recently been under suspicion of being a "Big Brother." Also, from the perspective of having launched an app on the Oculus Store, my experience with Oculus felt very close to Big Brother. It was difficult for new app developers to market their apps, only so-called "chosen" apps were in a favorable position, and for services like ours, which are not games, it was an extremely difficult structure to achieve anything. Even the AppLab launch, which was supposed to have no review process, took over a month. Furthermore, for our service-oriented apps like webtoons and educational platforms, the frame-by-frame Unity development method, which is far removed from event- or request/response-based app/web development, resulted in many losses. On top of that, Unity's backward compatibility version issues were a headache.

A Unity bug that popped up in "Yumi's Cells" Episode 4.

The devices we've developed and launched for include Oculus Go, Daydream, HTC Vive, FOVE, DPVR, Windows MR, and PICO. It was almost unbelievable how many there were. Because of this, we even considered putting a porting layer within Unity, similar to old mobile phone development, and looked extensively into UniRX, an event-based development method. However, juggling both web and client development just to keep the company afloat meant we didn't have the luxury to invest in refining Unity's development methods. So, almost in defiance, we were building WebXR-based technologies and services. This was because it allowed us to avoid dependence on the Oculus Store. However, there was still no way to expose users to the Oculus Store's home screen, and logging in was inconvenient. We explored various workarounds, such as ClassV using a PIN code method linked to its own login system. Seeing that video in such a situation made me sigh deeply. It's a wonderful future, but could it be a dictatorial one? Although they said they would open the SDK, it seemed like they would only provide very closed, game-engine-dependent features rather than standard-based ones. I wanted to ask Mark Zuckerberg, "What do you think of Tim Berners-Lee? Are you trying to monopolize the future of the internet?" However, today, after discovering this video and revisiting the Facebook Developer Center, my mind changed. When Facebook employees were recently asked about Mark Zuckerberg, the short answer was "He is not evil," and I could feel that Facebook is seriously contemplating the future of the internet. I could see how they are approaching 2D apps. 2D apps refer to apps that can provide various existing information services, not just 3D games. Specifically, they interpreted this as an "Infinite Screen," simply an extension of the browser, and finally opened the way to create WebApps. In short, it means Oculus has begun to embrace the open internet of the future. This video resolved many of our concerns. It finally opened the way for the web and Oculus to be organically connected, and for web-based apps to be developed. Facebook officially provided PWA and WebView, which we had planned to develop ourselves to overcome WebXR's limitations because Facebook hadn't opened them up. If things continue this way, Facebook seems poised to become a company that combines Google and Apple. A company with Apple's hardware and Google's openness. In short, the video above can be summarized as follows. Facebook is serious about building the future of the internet, especially if it's building out all these architectures. I felt the same excitement I experienced with Android Cupcake (1.0) once again. I see the future of technology not in videos, but in the developer center. You can feel much more in advance than from a well-made video. When I first saw the iPhone, I felt frustrated, but Android's developer documentation completely cleared that up. And the result is the market we have today. But now Facebook has taken both the excitement of the iPhone and the excitement of Android. My thoughts were right. We will strive to develop well. We will soon demonstrate the results of WebXR and immediately apply PWA. I was wondering how to implement Korean localization at the JavaScript level for the Infinity Office keyboard, but it seems like this approach will solve it quickly. We just need to trust Facebook's sincerity and focus. **Believer is an internet company of the metaverse, and as Zuckerberg said, the metaverse is not a 3D gaming environment, but the future of the internet itself. The meaning of "Meta" implies not just what appears in 3D, but more software architecture, like metadata. Meta is inherently invisible.**
