최근 3D TV 광고가 많이 나오고 새로운 신사업인양 이야기 되고 있습니다. 새로운 활로를 필요로 하는 산업계에는 찬물을 끼엊는 얘기겠지만,  제가 유저라면 그렇게 비싼 돈을 주고 굳이 3D TV를 사야 하나 싶습니다.

Recently, 3D TV advertisements have been appearing frequently, and it's being discussed as if it's a new growth industry. While this might be a cold shower for industries desperately seeking new avenues, if I were a user, I'd wonder if I really need to spend so much money on a 3D TV.
And will 3D TV truly provide a consistently enjoyable and new experience? As someone who is technology-oriented, I'd like to explain my reasons for being negative about 3D TV, one by one.

1.  The biggest reason: Lack of 3D TV sources
    Are there really many broadcasts currently available in 3D for 3D TVs? As I explained about 3D technology before, creating 3D sources requires the use of stereo (binocular) cameras.

    http://100.naver.com/100.nhn?docid=130986

    [Stereo Camera](http://100.naver.com/100.nhn?docid=130986)

    Science > Technology Science > Mechanical Engineering > Precision Machinery
    A special camera capable of capturing two images simultaneously. It uses a method of photographing the same object with two shooting lenses spaced at a certain interval...
    [Source: Doosan Encyclopedia EnCyber](http://www.encyber.com/search_w/ctdetail.php?flag=openapi&contentno=130986).

    The problem is that there aren't many sources created with such expensive and special equipment. Therefore, I believe it's better to buy a regular LCD TV that's even one inch larger than to buy an expensive 3D TV just to watch a few costly 3D broadcasts.

2.  So, the technology to watch non-3D broadcasts in 3D

    What has emerged recently is technology that reprocesses regular broadcasts into 3D sources in real-time. At first, I wondered how it could create images with binocular disparity that didn't exist in the first place. I roughly understood the principle through the images inserted in [Pennyway](http://pennyway.net/)'s review. The animated image below represents depth by creating binocular images as a GIF and animating them. Through this, I could understand how 3D TV reconstructs images.

    **ⓒ DreamWorks Pictures L.L.C./ Paramount Pictures. All rights reserved.**
    **Source: **[**http://pennyway.net/1415**](http://pennyway.net/1415)** **[**[Transformers Blu-ray on Samsung 3D LED TV]**](http://pennyway.net/1415)

    The principle of that technology is:

    First, it analyzes the image to create objects that will be reconstructed in 3D.
    This is the most crucial technology, analyzing the light and shadow, edges, and the degree of change (frequency) in the spatial coordinates of colors. Areas where the color gradually brightens are made to protrude slowly, while areas with abrupt changes are made to protrude significantly. For direction, darker areas are lowered, and brighter areas are raised. It seems to analyze larger areas (objects) first, then smaller areas (objects).

    Second, it covers these recognized virtual objects and distorts them accordingly. Filter effects like embedding a sphere in Photoshop also use this technology.

    {{IMG:1}}

    http://pomnago.com/click/?type=outlink&kind=goods&section=kids&pagen=&no=11504

    **[ Beads made with Photoshop ]**

    These filter effects are essentially a method of applying the image's texture to a virtual object. Filters show very fast performance for real-time processing compared to actual 3D rendering.

    At this point, the image created is not just one, but two binocular images, using two filters with different values instead of two actual cameras. The distortion is varied by changing the angle. (This is the same method as when you apply a shadow effect in Photoshop, where numbers change the direction and angle.)
    Third, by mechanically projecting each binocular image onto the screen, you see each image with your two eyes through glasses.

3.  Technical problems with watching 2D in 3D
    However, there's a problem: the possibility of creating incorrect 3D images.

    1) Incorrect virtual object creation due to color distortion
    Of course, much effort has been made to prevent this. However, in the image above, the background explosion appears reversed. Perhaps to emphasize the character, the background explosion in the character's area is darkened. This was probably done in post-production during CG work. Thanks to this, the originally round explosion cloud was perceived as being sunken behind the Autobot.

    Furthermore, in environments with diffuse reflection of light, or if mirrors, water, or luminous objects are present, the possibility of selecting incorrect objects is even higher if the content doesn't provide this information beforehand.     2) Incorrect information delivery due to distortion.
    Above all, the subtle incorrect information created by distortion is completely ignored. The images created by distortion are not real 3D information but fabricated images. As seen in movies, technology that converts low-resolution photos to high-resolution, or perfectly transforms 2D into 3D, cannot exist. This is because the original photo information does not contain the data needed to create such images. This technology merely forcibly enlarges or transforms into 3D, smoothly filling in missing parts. It may look plausible, but the information is either inaccurate or distorted.

3.  Distortion of content unintended by the producer
    Above all, such machine processing ultimately leads to content distortion. This distortion is unintended by the content producer and cannot be controlled. As seen in the Autobot attack scene above, movies are often reprocessed during post-production to achieve dramatic effects. While it might be possible to embed object information as metadata in the content, the amount of work would be enormous. In that case, it would be simpler to just shoot with a stereo camera from the beginning.

    + 3.1. Bonus. The government's industrial direction to invest in 3D content also doesn't look very good.
    Content is a story and a work of art. Investment in new technology is good and should be pursued, but it cannot be the entirety of the content business. Many low-budget films succeed brilliantly, and [the one-person animated film I reviewed yesterday](http://blog.naver.com/fstory97/70086226734) can also exist. If cultural industries are unconditionally subjected to industrial needs, the quality of culture could be undermined by market-driven logic.

4.  User's sense of incongruity
    1) {{IMG:4}} User's spatial confusion due to incorrect information delivery
    As I explained before in [my explanation of 3D technology](http://blog.naver.com/fstory97/70083034596), spatial perception is learned.

    [Experiential 3D HCI Technology is Rising (1) - What is 3D?](http://blog.naver.com/fstory97/70083034596)

    And this learning effect can inevitably cause subtle stress due to such minor incongruities. This is what makes people feel dizzy. And in the future, they might even learn that it's not real space. It's similar to how no one today is surprised to see small people inside a box on a TV screen. I wonder what effect this will have on young children who are still learning spatial perception. Though no parent would put 3D glasses on a baby.

    2) Disadvantage of not being able to properly experience content delivered by the creator
    As I mentioned above, unintended distortion leads to unintended experiences, and you cannot properly hear the story of content that uses color, focus, and lighting to tell a narrative. Even a slight change in color temperature can alter the feeling of a painting or photograph, so if this is distorted, you will get a completely different feeling and ultimately cannot fully grasp the artist's story.

    It's right to watch 3D content only in 3D. Watching a broadcast means listening to the storyteller's narrative, not filtering it. But transforming it just because there are few sources is not right.

    3) Do families have to wear glasses to watch TV in the living room?
    It's hard to imagine a family sitting around wearing glasses to watch TV, which is already a major cause of communication breakdown. It feels like watching TV, which used to be a casual activity like peeling an apple, is now becoming a chore.

5. Is Expensive 3D Really Necessary?
What's my conclusion? I don't think there's any reason for our household to switch to a 3D TV just because everyone else is. In fact, using mature technology is more beneficial for the user. If you feel that a unique experience is worth that cost, then purchasing it would be natural, but I'm actually negative about it. It's always good to have features as options. However, how many of the numerous features on your TV do you actually use? Despite this, the emphasis on 3D is probably the industry's desire to popularize the technology first. They know very well that they still lack content. Although interest in 3D has focused on Avatar, I wonder if other countries are as noisy about it as Korea is. I'm also not sure if broadcast standards have even been set.

Being ahead isn't always good. Let's live a little slower.
