My wife and I use the Galaxy Zoom 2. While it's ridiculously thick compared to a regular smartphone, its photo quality is excellent, and it's lighter than a mirrorless camera, so I barely use my Nex-5. With recent phones like the Galaxy S6 and G4 also touting their camera capabilities, the desire for high-quality photos taken with smartphones is growing. And as we take more photos, storing them has also become a huge task. If you have a child, like me, the value of photos is immense. Hard drives can fail at any time. Some people store them on portable external hard drives, but for photos of children, I strongly advise against it due to stability issues. Because of this, I was using a NAS with two hard drives that supported dual backup. However, it was an old NAS, so the speed was frustrating, and since I used it at home, I couldn't always keep it on. To view old photos, I had to turn on the NAS. Also, viewing them on a smartphone wasn't very smooth. So, as the best alternative, I was considering cloud services, and I'd like to share the know-how behind my choice.

**3. OneDrive Tips and Disappointments**
**1) Convenient to manage on a PC with equivalent storage to OneDrive**
For usability, it's convenient to have a hard drive of the same capacity on your PC. If you set it up on that disk for Windows, editing will automatically sync to OneDrive. I also moved my NAS to my PC first. This way, you can manage it quickly on your local PC. If your laptop has insufficient storage, you'll have to use it as online-only. This is a bit inconvenient.

**2) Disappointing mobile app**
The OneDrive mobile app has the disadvantage of not being as convenient for viewing photos compared to Dropbox or N Drive. The lack of automatic date-based grouping is a bit inconvenient. Moreover, since I uploaded 450GB from my NAS all at once, viewing by chronological order also became meaningless. At least the NAS was managed by folders, which was okay. MS has become very progressive since the CEO changed, and I hope the Android app will improve further.

**3) Disappointing speed**
Until MS builds a cloud center in Korea, it seems it will be slower than N Drive.

**4. Conclusion, MS seems to be a price bully.**
If you purchase 1TB of OneDrive for a year, you essentially get an 8-inch tablet for free. I can't escape MS.
