KakaoTalk recently launched its search business. I had predicted a long time ago that Kakao would inevitably enter the search business someday. However, mobile search is not as easy as PC search. This is because the mobile search ad market is not as optimistic compared to PC ads. It's an environment where users find it very inconvenient to input queries, and the screen is also very small, so search ads are not a particularly good market compared to PCs. However, what's clear is that the proportion of users' mobile usage time is increasing compared to PC usage, and even in the living room, more users are lightly acquiring information via tablets rather than sitting in front of a PC. Therefore, it's clear that the existing PC search market has reached its limits. So, how will KakaoTalk approach search in the future? Let's talk about that briefly.

**1. Shop Search Directly in Chat Window, Card View-Based Section Search**
Currently, KakaoTalk allows immediate search by pressing the '#' button in the chat window. What's also interesting is that it displays search results in a card view, allowing you to swipe to see results for each section. The third main menu item, 'Channel,' maintains the traditional portal-style scrolling method, but the '#' search shows a much cleaner format. Which one is the correct answer, I don't know yet. However, KakaoTalk can analyze data from these two types of search results to see which is more suitable for mobile users, or if it varies by usage pattern. The ability to share search results means that one person's search result can be disseminated to many. Therefore, it could also mean having a higher impact than typical search results. Moreover, KakaoTalk, with an reach rate of over 96%, can be said to be the search window that the entire nation spends the most time looking at on mobile.

So far, this is all common knowledge. However, let's talk about something a little different. This is about the O2O lineup that KakaoTalk is recently building. Of course, please note that the content below is a fictional scenario I've envisioned, not an official announcement from Daum Kakao.

**2. My Predicted Kakao O2O Search Model**
Daum Kakao recently acquired Kim Gisa for 62 billion won. They are also aggressively pushing Kakao Taxi, and Kakao Pay seems poised for a major showdown with Naver Pay in offline payments this year.

As for O2O examples, in the case of Lineable, a lost child prevention bracelet I introduced before, it uses crowd GPS based on installed apps. Also, if you install the CU convenience store app recently, a popup appears informing you of various benefits every time you enter a CU store. Recently, everything from laundry, car wash, to mobile phone battery charging... almost all aspects of daily life are gradually moving online based on apps, delivering services right where I am. The characteristic of these businesses is that they are based on location and apps. What all companies wishing to engage in O2O business desire most is for their app to be installed on every user's device. What does this mean? In the past PC era, when starting a business, companies would create a homepage and register their businesses with search engines like Naver, allowing users to access services through search. That was the very foundation of the search business. However, what about the mobile era? Do we have to register mobile web pages again? If we use mobile web, can we receive beacon services? It's impossible to solve this with just the web, which is the traditional search ad model.

This point means that the mobile search market could be different, and this is where KakaoTalk's search opportunity lies. KakaoTalk can become both a search platform and an app platform for all companies wishing to engage in O2O business.

For example, let's say I type 'hand car wash' from my current location. Currently, car washes offering hand car wash services are marked on the map. But what if Yper, which I introduced before, is available in the service area?

**3. So When Will Kakao Search Be Considered a Success?**
When did Naver enter its period of success? I believe it was when TV commercials started saying, 'Type OOO into the Naver search bar.' All advertisers placed ads on Naver and urged users to type something into that search bar. Kakao Search will be similar. However, its location won't be TV, but rather offline stores. After saying, 'Search for OO on KakaoTalk,' it will provide additional coupons or value to users who make payments or share it with friends through this. Thus, I believe the point when advertisers start advertising themselves is when search advertising will succeed. Will we see messages like 'Search for OO on KakaoTalk' or NFC tags encouraging Kakao search while walking around? Won't there be some resolution around this time next year?
