**1. The History of Disease, Heard on the Radio During My Commute.**
- It was one of the three major enemies that threatened humanity and nations.
- Quarantine represented medieval and modern states: Before the Middle Ages, the existence of viruses was unknown, so they were interpreted as vengeful spirits or divine wrath, leading politicians to self-reflect or be disciplined. It was a religious domain, not a medical one. In the case of Buyeo, the king was killed. (This seems to be due to Buyeo's characteristics as a tribal state with relatively weak centralized power and a theocratic system.)
- Korea's most recent epidemic history: around the 3.1 Movement in the 19th century, an estimated 7 million infected, 150,000 dead.
- This is only an estimate due to severe media censorship by Japan, which feared political unrest at the time.
- At one point, there was a rosy outlook that diseases could be conquered, but now there's a growing recognition that they can only be controlled, not conquered. This has increased the importance of quarantine.
- During the initial control process, severe human rights violations occurred due to vague fears.

**2. Reflections**
- We must learn from history.
- Quarantine is extremely important; it reflects a nation's level of development.
- Perhaps because of the perceived understanding of the virus's existence, people seem to be overly reassured by the illusion of already knowing.
- Politicians, the Korean quarantine system, and even the public often remain stuck in medieval thinking. Perhaps one would hear more such stories if they went to church...

**3. Conclusion**
- I hope the government, medical community, and religious community all stay vigilant.
