**1. Samsung's Crisis Began with Software.**

Recently, rumors of Samsung's crisis have been circulating due to its sluggish earnings announcement. While some even interpret Samsung's crisis as Korea's crisis, let's set aside such discussions and talk more dispassionately about the root causes of Samsung's crisis. I believe the cause of Samsung's crisis lies in software. The smartphone market created after the iPhone saw a paradigm shift from hardware to software, and while Google successfully countered the iPhone with the power of its software, Samsung, lacking a software platform thereafter, has entered a crisis situation – this is how I view the market.

More specifically and narrowly, this could refer to software distribution platforms like Google Play or messenger platforms like KakaoTalk, but these specific examples are not the entirety of the paradigm shift.

The shift to a software-centric society and what a software company and a pro-software culture mean is a much larger story. So, if I were to give one more example of what I'm currently thinking, I'd like to discuss 'what is software-centric?' with another example.

**2. Apple and Samsung, Tesla and Hyundai**

What companies come to mind when you think of companies representing South Korea? If there's Samsung for semiconductors and mobile phones, there's Hyundai Motor in the automotive industry. Many discussions revolve around threats to Hyundai Motor, such as low-cost cars from China and India, European brands, and technology that achieves high fuel efficiency. Additionally, Hyundai's corporate margins, which can be affected by rigid labor issues, are also pointed out as problems. However, I consider these issues to be rather secondary. Although not widely introduced in Korea, there is a figure shaking up the global automotive market: the genius Elon Musk, often called the real-life model for the movie Iron Man. Elon Musk is currently the CEO of Tesla Motors and is known in Korea as the person creating a sensation in the electric vehicle industry overseas.

Recently, when Elon Musk visited Japan, the headline published in Huffington Post Korea was [Elon Musk, "Japan is the Heart of Tesla"].

Currently, Korea's smartphone market is dominated by Android. And Samsung is a representative company producing Android smartphones. **3. Why Do These Differences Occur? Characteristics and Power of Software Companies**
*   It doesn't just mean the number of software personnel or proprietary technology.
As I mentioned earlier, I said software is more important, but this should not be misunderstood as simply having a large number of software personnel or exceptionally high technology. Of course, that is important and a necessary condition for the paradigm shift I'm talking about, but it is not a sufficient condition. Many Koreans work at Google, and Korean developers are highly sought after in Silicon Valley. And Samsung possesses even more excellent developers than that. Therefore, I believe it's difficult to find the cause based solely on this.

**4. The Difference Between Apple and Samsung, Tesla and Hyundai, as I See It**

The difference I perceive lies in a more comprehensive corporate culture and approach.

Apple is a company that creates things for users holding iPhones, while Samsung is a company that makes smartphones. Furthermore, Tesla is a company that creates things for people who drive Teslas, while Hyundai Motor is a company that makes cars. Do you see the difference?

Let me rephrase that. Apple is a company that creates things for mobile users connected to the internet via iPhones, and Tesla is a company that creates things for users who want to move from one place to another.

For Apple and Tesla, smartphones and electric vehicles are tools to solve user problems, and they are just one of the solutions they offer, not the entirety of the product. Rather, for them, the customer is everything. They are approaching more fundamental problems. As a result, they might not even need to sell iPhones and electric vehicles if necessary. In the case of Amazon, which wanted to deliver content to customers, they practically gave away Kindles for free.

The essence of a software company is to solve problems that people face. In fact, there's no need to debate whether one is first or second in a specific industry, or whether one is a fast follower or a first mover. While this might be meaningful to those who like to rank or categorize, it may not be very significant for those pursuing innovation. There isn't a specific industry, but rather specific problems, and various companies are racing to solve those problems.

As software organically involves processes of combining (grouping), dividing (categorizing), and connecting (networking), it becomes ambiguous even to define which industry category a software company belongs to. Is Apple, which makes OS, a software company or a hardware company? Is Tesla Motors, which plans to distribute electric charging stations, a car company or an energy company? And, surprisingly, the neurons in our brains operate this way. The power of software can be said to be consistent with the power of our thought.

**5. The Path Samsung and Hyundai Must Take: Becoming Software Companies Focused on Problems.**

The software company I envision is not just a company with many developers. A software company means that its organization and culture themselves embody software. While many software-related technologies will be needed to approach this, what's more important is breaking free from hardware-dependent ways of thinking.

A software mindset that approaches problems with a more fundamental problem-solving perspective is what can create a software company. On the contrary, large corporations like Samsung and Hyundai, with their robust organizations, processes, and cultures, might be the most challenging companies for software innovation. However, what is clear is that a hard rock can be broken by a soft wave like software. This is not a problem caused by the rock being less hard. It's because an irresistible tide is rising.
