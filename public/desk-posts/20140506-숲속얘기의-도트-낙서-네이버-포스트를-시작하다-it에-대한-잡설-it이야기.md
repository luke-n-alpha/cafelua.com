네이버 포스트를 시작했습니다. 네이버 포스트와 직접적으로 관계된 분에 의하면 포스트는 모바일에 최적화된 블로그보다 고 퀄리티의 콘텐츠를 타겟으로 한 곳이라는 이야기를 얼핏(?) 들었습니다. 공식적인 이야기는 아니니 태클 걸지는 말아주시구요. 다만 이 언질은 네이버 포스트가 어떠한 포지션에 위치하는가에 대한 이야기에 대한 해답이 될 수 있을것 같습니다. 블로그가 홍보의 대상이 되거나, 미니홈피적인 사적인 용도로 사용되고 있는데 반해, 좀 더 정제되고 콘텐츠로서 가치가 있는 타겟을 잡은것이 아닌가 싶은 생각이 듭니다. 마치 e북과 블로그의 중간 즈음에 위치하면서, 웹툰이나 웹소설처럼 완결된 콘텐츠가 아닌 연재를 통해 독자와 함께 숨쉬는 콘텐츠 정도의 느낌이라고나 할까요?  그래서 저도 시작해봤습니다.아래 이미지를 클릭하시면 제가 작성한 포스트로 이동합니다.{{IMG:1}}

I've started Naver Post. According to someone directly involved with Naver Post, I vaguely(?) heard that Post targets higher-quality content than blogs optimized for mobile. This isn't an official statement, so please don't nitpick. However, this hint might provide an answer to the question of Naver Post's positioning. While blogs are often used for promotional purposes or as personal mini-homepages, I get the feeling that Post aims for more refined, content-valuable targets. It's almost as if it's positioned somewhere between an e-book and a blog, offering content that breathes with the reader through serialization, rather than being a completed work like a webtoon or web novel. So, I decided to start it too.

[http://post.naver.com/series.nhn?seriesNo=12551](http://post.naver.com/series.nhn?seriesNo=12551) I initially thought about focusing on IT stories, which I cover on my blog, but as for unique content that only I have, I decided to flash back to the Cyworld Oekaki drawings I've accumulated since 2012, a period in my 20s when I wandered most intensely. It's also an experiment to see what thoughts come to mind when I look at those doodles from over 10 years ago.

What I felt while doing it was that having a table of contents for each installment was a bit inconvenient for a serialized work. And the table of contents couldn't be omitted... Aside from that, the ability to directly pull content from a blog seems to be a unique charm of Naver. I should consider republishing my blog posts in the future as well.

How will Naver Post evolve, I wonder? In fact, even in the mobile era, content creation tools beyond blogs don't seem to be emerging very well. Can it differentiate itself from blogs and establish itself as a major content creation tool?
