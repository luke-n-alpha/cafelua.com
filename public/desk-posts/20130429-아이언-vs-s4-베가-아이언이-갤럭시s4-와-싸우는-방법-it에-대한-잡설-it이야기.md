The launch date for the Vega Iron was set for April 26th. I wanted to see the actual Vega Iron phone, but it didn't seem to be as widely available as I thought. For some reason, there was even a booth set up in the company's break area for a comparison demo between the Vega Iron and the Galaxy S4. Also, many news articles portray the Vega Iron as a rival to the Galaxy S4. However, strictly speaking, the Vega Iron and Galaxy S4 are devices of different tiers.

**1. Different Tiers, Yet Compared?**

As you can see from the spec table above, except for price and design, all specifications are lower than the Galaxy S4. Considering that Vega's camera quality has consistently been lower than Galaxy's even with similar camera specs, it's difficult to say that the Vega Iron is superior to the Galaxy S4 in terms of device performance.

**2. A Viable Option for Rational Consumers**

Of course, the Vega Iron is a viable option for rational consumers.

First, smartphones have reached a high level of standardization, so the perceived performance difference isn't significant. The price difference, based on a 2-year contract, is about 10,000 won per month, totaling approximately 240,000 won, which is not a small amount. The merit of post-purchase support is also not very high, as many users have never even updated their OS, and the performance of Jelly Bean is already satisfactory.

**3. Vega's Gambit: "LG is not the rival to the Galaxy S4."**

Vega's significance can be found elsewhere: it's a strategy that disregards LG, the second-place player. While LG Electronics avoided a direct confrontation with the Galaxy S4, Vega seized the title of "Galaxy S4's rival." In reality, there's a huge difference in scale between Pantech and LG, which holds a 4% share of the global market. If you consider the worldwide mobile phone market share, Pantech falls into the "other" category.
{{IMG:2}}
Pantech's domestic market share is doing relatively well compared to LG. This is because Pantech is performing reasonably well in the low-end device market.

What I want to say is that Pantech's strategy this time threw a stone of direct confrontation with Samsung, effectively making LG "other." And what they brought to the table was the emotional aspect of "design." This strategy is actually similar to how Apple challenged Microsoft. Despite its low market share, it made the higher-share platform "other." Samsung Electronics also turned the Android vs. iOS war into an Apple vs. Samsung war, making other Android devices "other." During the patent war between Samsung Electronics and Apple, many believed that Samsung, with its lower brand, benefited. If users start comparing the Galaxy S4 and Vega, Pantech will passively gain some of Samsung's brand power and marketing budget.

**4. Galaxy's Weaknesses Pantech Targeted**

Pantech seems to have targeted the following weaknesses of the Galaxy:

**1) Overly high market share leading to a common and uninspired design**
With a market share exceeding 70%, users are already feeling fatigued by smartphones. Furthermore, since the Galaxy S3 and even the Note, Samsung Electronics has maintained a family look. As a result, the familiar rounded design of Samsung's smartphones can cause user fatigue. No matter how good a BMW is, if BMWs account for over 70% of cars on the road, would users still feel satisfied with the design? I touched the Galaxy S4, but I couldn't tell the difference from the Galaxy S2. It's familiar, but I don't know what's improved...

Therefore, I believe the Vega Iron focused on design as its key point, both in hardware and software. Pantech's launcher UI offers more diverse and fresh attempts compared to Samsung.

"All the pretty girls use that phone" - there's no better marketing than that. They are the magic women who can turn a paper cup of fish cake soup into a Starbucks coffee cup.

**2) User demands for lower specs**
As I mentioned above, user fatigue with specs has also increased, leading to lower user satisfaction with high-end smartphones. While an octa-core is innovative, users don't really feel it. The perceived difference between WXGA (1280*768) and WVGA (800*480) is very large, but the perceived difference between WXGA and FHD (1920*1080) is relatively small on a small smartphone screen. Therefore, by slightly lowering the specs, one can gain the advantage of better battery life. Every performance comes with a trade-off.

**3) Increased price competitiveness due to government subsidy regulations**
Government subsidy regulations hit more expensive devices harder. Naturally, the lower-priced Vega Iron can benefit a bit more.

**5. The Smartphone Market Returns to Red Ocean and Design...**
It feels like the feature phone market is being replayed. As feature phone performance improved significantly, there were many pretty and emotional features on the external LCD screen. I particularly remember the Ice Cream Phone that popped open when shaken. Since Google largely drives innovation within the LCD screen, the innovation that manufacturers can achieve will increasingly focus on design elements, and user demands will gradually shift in that direction.

Software will be the domain of service companies like Google, Naver, and Kakao.

Was the rectangular design with an LCD screen embedded in it Apple's trade dress? Attempts to overcome that limitation will continue. Seeing the LED on the top right of the Vega Iron now feels almost welcoming. I believe such attempts will become more widespread in the future.
