**3. 카메라와 스마트폰의 의존성 문제
**삼성의 카메라가 삼성의 스마트폰에만 의존적이어야 한다고 주장하는것은 아닙니다. 오히려 삼성카메라 제조업체의 경우에는 아이폰에도 연결되고 lg의 윈도우7에도 연결될수 있겠죠. 그리고 그 편이 갤럭시와 상관없이 삼성카메라의 발전을 꾀할 수 있을거라고 봅니다. 분명 구글이었다면 그렇게 했을겁니다. 그리고 산업표준을 만드는 것도 괜찮은 방법입니다.

그러나 만약 스티브잡스가 카메라를 만들었다면 어땠을까요? 그 사람 성격이라면 아마도 삼성카메라의 사진관리는 아이튠즈를 통해서 하도록 했을것이고 아이폰과만 연동이 되었겠죠.

어느쪽이 정답이라고 말하지는 못하겠습니다. 어쩌면 적절히 처음에는 갤럭시 s에만 해당기능을 열어주다가 시간텀을 두고 다른기기의 업데이트를 이후에 해주는 방식으로 양쪽을 다가져갈수 있겠죠. 구글의 레퍼런스폰처럼 말이죠.

**3. Camera and Smartphone Dependency Issues**
I'm not arguing that Samsung cameras should be dependent only on Samsung smartphones. Rather, a Samsung camera manufacturer could connect to iPhones and LG's Windows 7 devices. And that way, Samsung cameras could develop independently of the Galaxy. Google would certainly have done it that way. And creating an industry standard is also a good method.

But what if Steve Jobs had made a camera? Given his personality, he probably would have made Samsung camera photo management go through iTunes and only link with iPhones.

I can't say which is the right answer. Perhaps, initially, they could enable the feature only for the Galaxy S, and then, after a time interval, provide updates for other devices, thus having the best of both worlds. Like Google's reference phones.

**4. N-screen Requires a Broader Perspective**
It's the N-screen era, but I feel like everyone is too focused on just three types of devices: TV, mobile, and tablets. The targets could also be cameras and refrigerators.
Especially when you look at the proportion of photos in the vast amount of traffic distributed on the internet, it's a content that cannot be ignored.
What surprised me about the iPad 2 launch wasn't the fast performance, AP features, or many apps – those are old news – but that they reduced the weight by nearly 15%.
I hope Samsung's next camera will also reduce weight, have a swivel screen for selfies, and be a device that links with smartphones. Isn't competitiveness born from what one does well?
