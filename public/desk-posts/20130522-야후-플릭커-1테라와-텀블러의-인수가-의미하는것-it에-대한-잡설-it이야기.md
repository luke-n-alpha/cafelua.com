**3. 야후가 만들려는 것, 인스타그램과 트위터를 합친 소셜 네트워크 서비스**** " 사진 기반 소셜네트워크를 통해 세상을 보는 야후의 눈을 갖겠다. "** 결국 플릭커와 텀블러가 의미하는 것은 사진에 대한 주도권을 갖겠다는 의미입니다. 그리고 그 방법은 아마도 인스타그램과 트위터의 특징을 모두 갖춘 소셜 네트워크 서비스가 될 것 같습니다. 왜 이렇게 사진에 집중하는 것일까요? 바로 사진은 사용자의 기억이기 때문입니다. 사용자 한명 한명이 야후 서비스의 눈이 되고 기억을 쌓습니다.  기억은 무의식과 의식으로 나누어진다고 합니다. 무의식에 영역에는 우리가 미처 인식하지 못하는 모든 기억이 있다는 의미도 있죠. 플릭커의 원본 이미지는 이러한 의미로 해석될 수 있습니다. 저도 개인용 1테라짜리 NAS(네트워크 하드디스크)를 쓰면서 원본 이미지를 저장해둡니다. 이 때 대부분 이미지는 다시 찾아보지 못합니다. 혹시 보게 된다면, 예전의 오프라인의 사진에서 그러하듯 그 당시의 하이라이트 장면을 본다기 보다는 그당시의 장면들을 연속샷으로 보게 됩니다. 똑같은 장소에서 내 아이의 다양한 표정들을 보게 됩니다.  그에 반해, 텀블러, 현재 제게는 페이스북은 제 사진 중 하이라이트 장면만 올립니다. 그리고 거기에 정성스럽게 댓글도 붙히고 다른 이들과 대화를 하죠. 이 두가지가 이어진다면, 원본 데이터는 하이라이트 데이터와 연관데이터 라는 정보가 생기게 됩니다. 하이라이트 데이터는 다른 사용자와의 관계와 댓글을 통한 추가적인 context(숨겨진 정보)를 얻어 낼 수 있습니다.   이것이 향후에 이미지에서 사물이나 정보를 뽑아내는 인식기술과 대용량 데이터 처리 기술인 빅데이터 기술이 만나면 어떤 결과가 나오게 될까요? 야후는 사용자들을 통해 세상을 보는 눈을 가지게 됩니다.  페이스북에서 어떻게 이 친구들을 내게 추천해주었을까? 놀랐던 그 경험을 야후에서 하게 될지도 모릅니다. 내가 갔던 장소, 내가 즐겨 입는 옷, 내가 다른 이들과 있을때 어떤 표정을 짓는지까지 시각적인 정보를 통해 알아낼 수 있습니다. 이러한 예측은 제가 구글 프로젝트 글래스에서 했던 것과 같습니다. 결국 글로벌 인터넷 서비스들이 가고자 하는 목표는 같다는 의미입니다.

Amidst this, news broke that Flickr increased its free storage to 1 terabyte and Yahoo acquired Tumblr. While the Tumblr founder becoming a billionaire at a young age is a hot topic, I'd like to talk about what Yahoo is trying to achieve above all else.

**1. What Flickr's 1 Terabyte Means**
**" Store all photos as unlimited originals. "**
{{IMG:2}}
Flickr is not very well-known in Korea, but you only need to remember two things. First, it's a service that allows you to upload original-sized images from your digital camera and view them in various sizes as needed. It's most similar to the photo album feature of N Drive. Second, like Twitter, it allows access to or uploading of Flickr images via various Open APIs. And because of these two features, it's a service that allows you to directly upload images taken with a Samsung Galaxy Camera and view them in an LG Electronics digital album. However, there was one hurdle: paid subscription was required to remove storage limits and fully utilize all features. But now, this hurdle has largely been removed, and charging has been changed to apply only to customers who need more than 1 terabyte or wish to remove ads. This means, instead of 'pay if you want full features,' it's 'we'll only charge very rare heavy users.' Therefore, Flickr's increase in free storage is unlikely to significantly help Flickr's revenue.

But why did Yahoo, which was said to be struggling, take such a bold step, sacrificing revenue? The message to users is just one. It means to store all photos as originals, virtually unlimited, on Flickr. This suggests they saw it as more valuable than paid subscriptions. I think this was an excellent choice for Yahoo. Yahoo may have declined, but it still possesses vast amounts of cash. Therefore, a certain amount of revenue loss can be tolerated. However, what cannot be tolerated is an increase in future uncertainty, i.e., a decrease in membership and usage rates. This was an 'investment' precisely for the latter. Moreover, these investment costs gradually decrease, while the value gained from the investment actually increases. This is because hardware and network costs decrease over time, but the value of user data, which cannot be regained, continuously increases.

What then does the Tumblr acquisition mean? It means for users to attach additional information to the data uploaded on Flickr. Who is in the photo, what they think about this photo – all this data is generated through social network communication.

**3. What Yahoo Aims to Create: A Social Network Service Combining Instagram and Twitter**
**" To gain Yahoo's eyes to see the world through a photo-based social network. "**
Ultimately, Flickr and Tumblr signify Yahoo's intention to gain dominance over photos. And the method will likely be a social network service that combines the features of both Instagram and Twitter.

Why such a focus on photos? Because photos are users' memories. Each user becomes Yahoo's eyes and builds memories for the service.

Memory is said to be divided into unconscious and conscious. The realm of the unconscious also means that it contains all memories we don't consciously perceive. Flickr's original images can be interpreted in this sense. I also use a personal 1-terabyte NAS (Network Attached Storage) to store original images. Most of these images are never looked at again. If I do look at them, rather than seeing highlight moments as with old offline photos, I see continuous shots of scenes from that time. I see various expressions of my child in the same place.

In contrast, Tumblr, or for me currently, Facebook, only uploads highlight moments from my photos. And I carefully add comments and converse with others there. If these two are connected, the original data gains information as highlight data and associated data. Highlight data can extract additional context (hidden information) through relationships with other users and comments.

What results will emerge when this meets recognition technology that extracts objects or information from images, and big data technology for processing large volumes of data? Yahoo will gain eyes to see the world through its users.

The surprising experience of how Facebook recommended those friends to me? You might have that experience with Yahoo. It can identify visual information such as places I've visited, clothes I enjoy wearing, and even the expressions I make when I'm with others. This prediction is the same as what I made for Google Project Glass. Ultimately, it means that global internet services are heading towards the same goal.

In the short term, due to Yahoo Korea's withdrawal, it seems unlikely that general light users will easily access Yahoo Flickr. In conclusion, the impact on domestic users can be said to be minimal.
