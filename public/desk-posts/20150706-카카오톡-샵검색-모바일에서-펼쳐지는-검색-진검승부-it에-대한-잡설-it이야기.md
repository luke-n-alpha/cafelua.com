**1. Kakao's Future as I Predicted It. How Accurate Was I?**
Among the businesses Kakao engages in, which of the following elements is it currently pursuing?

1) Launching free services for user lock-in: PWE (Personalization Tool) service
2) Offline coupon market: Expansion into local coupon (social commerce) market
3) Digital content and game market: Kakao Content Store
4) E-commerce market: Kakao Social Shopping
5) Media market: Curation and news services
6) Community market: Kakao version of Knowledge-in, and Agit reorganization
7) Advertising market: The ultimate revenue model, search advertising

In fact, it's no exaggeration to say that due to the merger with Daum, Kakao is doing almost all of these things.

Why am I stating the obvious? These seven items are not from an article written this year, but a list of businesses I predicted Kakao would pursue around 2012. Didn't I get it quite right?

Looking at Daum Kakao's recent actions, they can be summarized in one phrase: a firm resolve to engage Naver in a mobile-first showdown. With this conviction, they are streamlining old Daum services one by one and focusing their efforts on mobile. While some may complain about the termination of good Daum services, my perspective is different. Even for a very wealthy company, its core personnel are limited. Launching many businesses means widening the front, making it difficult to concentrate on a single venture. Moreover, what if the opponent is Naver? I was someone who had always asserted that Daum could never beat Naver. My biggest reason was Daum Communications' failure in "selection and concentration." Naver, whether successful or not, consistently showed focus on one thing. Me2day, which was heavily pushed at one point, demonstrated this, and years of consistent investment in Japan eventually led to the massive success of Line. Naver's greatest strength is its persistence and focus on businesses it believes it must win. In contrast, Daum tended to match Naver's lineup rather than concentrating on services with high potential. However, it was clear that Daum was incomparable to Naver in terms of scale and capital. As a result, it struggled to win even one battle and was easily defeated by Naver in piecemeal fashion. By conceding mail and cafe services, Daum effectively became a perpetual second-place player.

However, after merging with Kakao, Daum has recently shown a distinctly different approach, centered around Chairman Kim Beom-soo. It's a consistent march towards a single direction. Of course, there may be failures along the way, but moving towards a single objective is clearly a different strategy. I'd like to discuss the objective I perceive and Daum Kakao's "Next" (Daum).

**2. #Search and News Can Shake Up the Korean Search Market.**
#Search, which will change the landscape of mobile search, currently sees Naver still holding the top spot in mobile search. However, it's also true that Naver shows a much weaker performance compared to PC, and the reason is Google. In South Korea, where Android accounts for over 90%, the default search engine is Google. And, the number one search term on Google is Naver. ㅡoㅡ This means users search for Naver on Google and then conduct their searches on Naver. But what's clear is that users have now distinctly started to feel Google's presence, and as a result, Google search has gained an incomparable market share compared to PC. However, the undisputed mobile powerhouse in Korea is KakaoTalk. Would you believe it has more users than Google Play? It also commands the most user time. Now, a portal has been integrated into KakaoTalk's main menu. It's clear that this will have a more powerful influence than Google's pre-load.

Furthermore, looking at recent trends in news consumption, internet dependency has overwhelmingly increased. The reason is the demand for breaking news. Traditional TV and newspapers, with their morning and evening editions, provide news consumption twice a day, which is unsatisfactory for users who are glued to their smartphones 24/7. News about MERS, for example, saw people wanting to know the number of confirmed cases in real-time with visualized images, and they consumed breaking news. And such news spread to local cafes and friends. Most of the shocking news I heard spread fastest via text messages, Facebook, or KakaoTalk. Things like, "OMG, did you hear about ***?" Currently, perhaps taking a breather, Daum's trending searches are not attached to KakaoTalk. One of the most influential services on mobile is likely this trending search service. Of course, even without it, the already curated content alone will have an undeniable impact. The destructive power of Naver's main page and Daum's main page was like that of gods in the blogosphere. Ultimately, content creators wanting to produce content suitable for this will eagerly jump in. Where PVs (page views) gather, quality content gathers. When quality content gathers, search also improves.

Of course, in terms of current search quality, Naver, which has dominated this ecosystem and developed technology for years, is far superior. However, we cannot rest easy, as search is strongly driven by issues and habits. Moreover, what's formidable about KakaoTalk is that most of its users are logged in. It also possesses social information. This means it has far more means than Naver to understand which search results a user with a particular taste would find more interesting.

**3. The Future of Mobile Search: O2O Search**
Another variable for Daum Kakao that I couldn't predict in 2012 is O2O. At the time, I only thought of it as a local coupon service. It was a time when it was difficult to envision local commerce as a particularly profitable direction. But now, I can clearly state that the answer lies in O2O companies that innovate services based on online connectivity, rather than simply moving offline services online. Representative services of this kind include companies like Uber, and domestically, services like Kakao Taxi. These models transplant all behavioral patterns of daily life online and innovate the service method itself based on information. Such models have the potential to emerge from everything we do and experience offline. From waking up in the morning until going to bed, even without looking at a smartphone due to IoT. And this will revolutionize existing PC-based search services. If PC search was query-centric, mobile search will be situation-centric. If early attempts were at the level of Naver or Daum apps providing information about nearby bus stops, now, beacon-based systems will recommend optimal information of interest and induce searches. You'll start seeing advertisements everywhere saying, "Try typing #** in KakaoTalk," just as you used to see "Try typing ** in Naver search bar" in TV ads. And it won't just end with search results; it will connect with various O2O services. Mobile search will be at the heart of breaking down the boundaries between offline and online. In the future, it will aim for AI agent models like Siri or Google Now, but currently, the technological gap is still significant, so for a transitional period, service-centric O2O search, solved by people and processes, seems to have a considerable advantage.

**4. A Real Search Showdown Unfolding on Mobile**
This is what I believe to be KakaoTalk's future and potential that I hadn't anticipated. Naver began to rise when it attracted users by saying, "Ask ** on Knowledge-in." After that, businesses themselves solidified the market by saying, "Try typing ** in the Naver search bar." However, this model is not working well on mobile currently. KakaoTalk is currently thought to be closest to a solution for this. Of course, Naver won't stand idly by. Its large advertising pool, the expansion of offline commerce centered on Checkout, and the fintech battle also appear to be as interesting a war as the Battle of Red Cliffs. However, Naver has a slight advantage in that even if it falls behind a bit domestically, it can apply Kakao's proven model to Japan and capture a larger market than at home. It already had a similar experience once with games.

I feel that KakaoTalk has truly sharpened its sword recently. In contrast, I worry that most of Naver's top talent might be focused on Japan or overseas expansion. There is no such thing as a permanent number one. I look forward to the upcoming competition even more.
