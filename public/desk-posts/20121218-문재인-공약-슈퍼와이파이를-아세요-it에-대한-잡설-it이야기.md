**** 1) 무료**  와이파이 기술은 매우 값이 저렴합니다. LG U+도 무료 와이파이를 서비스하고, 커피숍과 공공기관에서 흔히 보이는 와이파이는 무료입니다. 이 점은 그대로 가져갑니다. **2) 전국망**  와이브로 확산이 늦었던 이유가 무엇일까요? 전국망, 정확히 말하면, 커버리지의 문제였습니다. 음영지역 제로를 목표를 한다는 내용입니다. 이것이 가능한 이유는 슈퍼와이파이는 구 TV방송의 낮은대역의 주파수를 이용하기 때문입니다. 주파수 대역이 낮으면 회절성이 높아 장애물에 대한 투과율이 높고, 도달거리도 깁니다. **1. If the Blood of the Internet Industry, the Network, Hardens - Introduction: How Will You Reduce Communication Costs?**
In the internet industry, the network is an indispensable element. Just as stable and affordable electricity is needed for the home appliance industry, and stable fuel for the automotive industry, the network is the blood for the ICT industry. In that regard, we had the advantage of being an IT infrastructure powerhouse, but the household burden of wireless internet, triggered by LTE and other technologies in the wireless internet market, is even showing signs of becoming a stumbling block for the wireless internet industry.
Regarding this, both the Park camp and the Moon camp proposed measures to reduce communication fees, but there was no explanation of specific plans for these measures. I thought that if things continued this way, it would likely become a performative fee reduction, merely increasing pressure on mobile carriers to lower rates, just as it happened during the Lee Myung-bak administration. However, the plan revealed by the Moon camp this time is very specific and well-conceived. So, driven by the desire to quickly introduce it before the election, I hastily wrote this post.

**Candidate Moon Jae-in's Knowledge Content and Communication Network Grand Integration Strategy**
[http://www.moonjaein.com/digital.pdf](http://www.moonjaein.com/digital.pdf)

**2. Not Pressure on Mobile Carriers to Lower Fees, but a Technological Paradigm Shift - Pledge 1: Building a Super Wi-Fi National Network**
**Super Wi-Fi** is a term coined by the U.S. [Federal Communications Commission](http://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%97%B0%EB%B0%A9_%ED%86%B5%EC%8B%A0_%EC%9C%84%EC%9B%90%ED%9A%8C) (FCC), referring to a wireless internet service that utilizes white space, the unused radio spectrum of TV. [http://me2.do/FP9NjDN](http://me2.do/FP9NjDN)
You can refer to Wikipedia for a technical explanation, but I'll first tell you the points that consumers will feel.
**1) Free**
Wi-Fi technology is very inexpensive. LG U+ also offers free Wi-Fi, and Wi-Fi commonly seen in coffee shops and public institutions is free. This point remains the same.
**2) National Network**
What was the reason for the slow spread of WiBro? It was a national network, or more precisely, a coverage issue. The goal is zero dead zones. This is possible because Super Wi-Fi uses low-band frequencies from old TV broadcasts. Lower frequency bands have higher diffraction, meaning better penetration through obstacles and longer reach. That's why AM radio signals are strong. **Can you imagine a Wi-Fi base station with the reach of a mobile communication base station, combined with the advantages of inexpensive Wi-Fi?**
**3) Net Neutrality**
I mentioned net neutrality at the beginning of the Super Wi-Fi deployment. This appears to be to prevent new ICT businesses from being undermined by the hegemony of mobile carriers. If this aspect is considered from the initial deployment phase, I believe many of the current controversies surrounding net neutrality could be resolved.
**4) From a Frequency-Centric Vertical Structure to a Technology Layer-Centric Horizontal Structure**
{{IMG:1}}
This is about transforming the current mobile carrier-centric vertical network structure into a horizontal one. The most surprising aspect of this diagram is that it doesn't distinguish frequencies. I also speculate that concerns about the frequency auction system, which I previously pointed out, are integrated into this. This structure seems to directly reflect the recently popular cloud-based business environment.

**3. Republic of Korea Government Internet Platform Pledge 2: Building a Knowledge-Sharing Economy Foundation**
The document states 'Building a Knowledge-Sharing Economy Foundation,' but I chose a title that's easier to understand. Under the 'Digital Jiphyeonjeon' (Hall of Worthies) section, there is a part about integrating and opening national content. And, strengthening information disclosure, global standardization. It may seem complex, but you can understand it simply: **Open the nation's closed information to a platform level. And by engaging in international standardization activities for this e-government system, prevent the Republic of Korea government's internet platform from becoming a 'Galapagos' (isolated) system, and take leadership in technical standards.** The good thing consumers will feel is improved internet e-government services. You're already easily using many e-government systems built during the Roh Moo-hyun era, aren't you? ^^ Improvements to systems like cash receipts and year-end tax adjustments during the Lee Myung-bak administration can be seen as similar benefits.

**4. Combining Pledges 1 & 2 to Create 500,000 Good Jobs Pledge 3: Creating 500,000 Future-Oriented "Good" Jobs**
Although I thought 500,000 might be a pessimistic number, seeing these implementation plans makes me think it might not be an impossible figure. **First, large-scale ICT projects will be launched due to the Republic of Korea Government Internet Platform initiative.** Naturally, employment will increase, right? In fact, in some ways, it's not significantly different from the Lee Myung-bak administration's attempt to implement the Four Major Rivers Project. It's the same in that it involves pouring national funds into creating some infrastructure. However, while the Lee Myung-bak administration built physical infrastructure in riverbeds that may or may not be used, Candidate Moon Jae-in has decided to invest in a software platform called e-government. **Of course, if large corporate SI (System Integration) subcontracting structures jump in again, it will repeat past bad examples.** Therefore, **I suspect that the establishment of governance and the authority of the Fair Trade Commission are included in 3-2. SI subcontracting, structured in a multi-tiered 'gap-eul-byeong-jeong' (prime-sub-sub-sub) system, cannot be called good jobs, can it?** However, it's difficult to find specific implementation plans for this part in the detailed policy tasks, so I wonder if it would be better if it were strengthened a bit.

**5. Summary.**
**The core seems to be creating an environment for the wireless network software industry on top of an inexpensive wireless network infrastructure enabled by Super Wi-Fi, and injecting funds into the ICT industry through the e-government sector, with public institutions taking the lead and the government's software platform being advanced.**
{{IMG:2}}

**6. What do you think of Candidate Moon Jae-in's vision for the ICT industry?**
First, from a consumer's perspective, isn't the proposal for stable, free Wi-Fi appealing? While there will undoubtedly be a struggle for hegemony among mobile carriers, the leadership in Super Wi-Fi lies with global internet companies like Google and MS. There are certainly domestic companies that desire this, and if the government empowers them and learns from the mistakes made with WiBro, I believe a better future can definitely be envisioned.
If you download the policy platform, you'll find many more good things in the detailed policy tasks. The public release of educational archives from EBS and KBS, isn't that great too? While half-price tuition is important, such a paradigm shift is also very crucial.
Even if Candidate Moon Jae-in is not elected, I hope Candidate Park will definitely review these pledges. Please also look at the pledges, not just the negative campaigning, before you vote.
