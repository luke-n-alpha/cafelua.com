LTE 69요금제 기준, 200분 해봐야 93Mbyte입니다. 거기다 초과되면 말도 안되게 데이터 대비 비쌉니다. 원래부터 음성 통화는 그정도 안받아도 비용은 큰 문제가 없었던 것이죠. 덕분에 "데이터 중심" 요금제는 정말로 데이터만 기준으로 요금을 볼 수 있게 되었습니다. 다시말하면 데이터중심 요금제는 요금의 정상화에 가깝습니다. ​**3. 어떤 폰을 구매해도, 혹은 기존 폰도 보조금을 받을수 있다.****- 단말기 시장의 이통사 독립, 단말기 시장의 정상화** 또한 단통법에 의한 최근 최근정책 중 하나가 20% 요금 요금할인제란 것이 것이있습니다.있습니다 단말기에 뒤섞인 통신요금의 장난을 빼내는 것으로 보입니다. 통신사의 보조금에 의해 단말의 판매에 영향을 받는것이죠. 이 때문에 가성비가 좋은 스마트폰들이 빛을 못보니, 스마트폰 시장이 왜곡되고 있다는 것으로 봐야 합니다. 그래서 나온 것이 20%요금 할인입니다. 원래 이동통신사를 통해 구매한 신규 단말기에서만 받던 요금할인을 외부에서 구매하거나, 기존 단말을 쓰는 고객들도 받을 수 있게 하는 것입니다. Since the Mobile Device Distribution Act (DanTongBeop) was implemented, netizens are buzzing that the smartphones they want to buy have become more expensive. Or, they say it's wrong that the latest smartphones are being pushed aside even by cheaper older models.

**1. Solving the Problem of Information Asymmetry Caused by Marketing**
**- Price and Quality Competition Now Functioning Properly**
However, let's think about it again. Is it strange that cheap smartphones sell well? Samsung's sluggish performance isn't actually because Samsung can't make smartphones. Smartphones have already become standardized at a high level, and the market situation has shifted to price competition. But when the element of marketing is added, it clouds rational judgment. Excessive subsidies were precisely that. Subsidies made people mistakenly believe that the latest smartphones were cheap. Consequently, there was no price competition in the domestic market. And in a market where there is no competition, customers are bound to lose out. If there are 10 customers, one customer who skillfully uses marketing gains a significant profit, while the other nine suffer more than expected. Netizens who complain usually refer to this one customer. And, I frankly confess that I am also one of those people. I have never paid more than 150,000 won for a smartphone at its installment principal cost. This means I received subsidies exceeding the legally designated amount. However, this phenomenon, caused by information asymmetry in marketing, cannot be considered good for customers as a whole.

**2. Data-Centric Plans**
**- Abandoning Profiteering Voice Plans and Normalizing Rates**
I also consider the recently introduced data-centric plans to be good. Although I didn't switch because, after calculating, it didn't seem very beneficial for me, it's great news for those who make a lot of voice calls. Some might wonder why such excessive (?) penalties are being imposed on mobile carriers, but technically, voice and data are not separated at all, and the money received from customers for voice communication is overwhelmingly expensive compared to the cost incurred. Voice consumes 8 kbytes per second. The traffic for a 1-hour call is only 28 Mbytes. Based on the LTE 69 plan, 200 minutes only amounts to 93 Mbytes. Moreover, if exceeded, it becomes ridiculously expensive compared to data. Originally, voice calls didn't need to be charged that much, and costs weren't a big issue. Thanks to this, "data-centric" plans truly allow rates to be viewed based solely on data. In other words, data-centric plans are close to normalizing rates.

**3. Regardless of which phone you buy, or even with an existing phone, you can receive subsidies.**
**- Independence of the Device Market from Carriers, Normalization of the Device Market**
Also, one of the recent policies under the Mobile Device Distribution Act is the 20% rate discount system. This seems to be about removing the trickery of communication fees mixed into device prices. Device sales were influenced by carrier subsidies. Because of this, smartphones with good value for money couldn't shine, leading to a distorted smartphone market. That's why the 20% rate discount was introduced. This allows customers who purchase devices externally or use existing devices to receive the rate discount, which was previously only available for new devices purchased through mobile carriers.

It seems effective in preventing overconsumption and will greatly help normalize the device market. Various phones from overseas will be able to compete domestically. In fact, the excessive number of Galaxy phones in Korea made the market seem abnormal. This means that domestic smartphone companies, which had it easy thanks to carrier marketing, now have to engage in quality and price competition.

Fundamentally, I welcome the ideals of the Mobile Device Distribution Act. Although there may be minor and major side effects during its implementation, I highly regard its objective of fair market competition. And I believe its fruits are gradually emerging. However, the recent sales of the Galaxy S6 Edge and G4 seem lower than expected, which must be bitter for manufacturers like Samsung and LG. But I think this is what a normal market originally looks like. This is because I believe the market until now was an abnormal boom that occurred during the transition from feature phones to smartphones. Looking back, it seems the biggest factor influencing Galaxy series sales, beyond product quality, was the increasing number of smartphones. Now, we've moved on to the next stage. It's time to expect fundamentals with quality and excellent strategies.
