| I once played a game called TRPG in the past. Infinite freedom... imagination... After all, the story is decided by the participants, and the master handles it, so any absurd story could unfold depending on the master's discretion. The true fun of a game isn't in a pre-made system, but in how much I can participate and change the story. That's RPG. The original meaning of Role Playing. It's a bit disheartening to see everyone hunting monsters according to fixed rules these days. Mages hunt monsters. Rangers hunt monsters. Warriors hunt monsters. Even elementary schoolers hunt monsters. Everyone hunts monsters. ㅡㅡ; Games that emphasize flashy combat systems... Is there nothing else besides combat??? To think that the future game I imagined is just this... Anyway, because of this, I'm not very obsessed with games these days. While web surfing, I came across a cafe like 'Devil Raising Cafe'. Wondering what on earth this was, I looked it up... and found tons of 'raising' cafes everywhere. These cafes, which seem to be a spin-off of raising simulation games that started with Princess Maker, surprisingly create their bulletin boards as certain areas (e.g., Back Forest, Weapon Shop, etc.), where users freely write and unleash their imaginations. Perhaps users already know what they want? If there's a space where users can happily imagine and showcase things, even without necessarily providing flashy graphics or such, a simple text editor can become a great game. Moreover, the responsiveness of community interaction, utilizing the 'cafe' characteristic, seems to hold amazing potential. There's no reason to necessarily demand real-time interaction in online games. The imagination of users and the fact that someone they know is writing or progressing the story is exciting in itself. If this is utilized well, new web games could be created. By providing things like shops and dungeons, displayed as list-type posts or other posts in a cafe, the poster can operate it, and the master (operator, sysop) can advance the story and easily provide images. Even a system where monsters can be users. Children can create wonderful new homes even with just dirt and broken dishes. Don't game developers need such ideas? Where have some of the great web games I played in the past disappeared to? Even if it's a crude idea, I'm not sure whether to post this on my blog for public viewing or just share it with people inside NHN. Now that I'm actually an NHN person, I'm worrying about things like this... ㅡㅡ; Most of the posts I wrote in the past about the ideas or services of a certain S company have also been changed to private.
