Recently, with a change in work and the Chuseok holiday overlapping, I've had almost no time to write anything. Moreover, once I stopped writing, I couldn't bring myself to write a post. At some point, I even lost confidence in writing quality posts. Anyway, I decided to try and write something. It's a comparison and choice between the much-talked-about Galaxy Note and iPhone 5.
To start with the conclusion, I choose the Galaxy Note. As someone still under contract for a Galaxy S2, I can't even dream of buying it, but looking at the known specs, it's undeniably attractive. And unlike the Galaxy S2 vs. iPhone 4 comparison, the Galaxy Note vs. iPhone 5 comparison clearly highlights their distinct characteristics. ㅠ It's hard to talk about the shortcomings of either phone anyway, so I'll discuss the advantages of both and why I chose the Galaxy Note.

****
**1. iPhone 5**
First, it's the iPhone, which is still shrouded in mystery. Not much information has been revealed, so a direct comparison seems meaningless, but I'll speak based on what's known.

**1) The first advantage of the iPhone is its 'ganji' (style/coolness).**
The Galaxy Note, being in the 5-inch range, definitely falls short compared to the iPhone in terms of weight and thickness. The already beautiful iPhone seems to have reduced its bezel and thickness even further, making the screen appear even more expansive.

**2) Portability, but make sure to bring charging equipment**
While Galaxy boasts battery replacement as an advantage, and the Note has a larger battery, I still think iPhone's battery optimization is unmatched. Furthermore, it's better for one-handed grip than the Note, which has gone over 5 inches.

**3) Diverse Apps**
While Android already has everything one could need, the App Store remains powerful.

**4) Diverse Peripherals, even Samsung audio supports only iPod**
In terms of peripherals, the iPhone is still superior. Especially in the audio category, many devices feature iPod docks. It was also hard to find audio devices that support Bluetooth. I'm simply puzzled as to why Samsung doesn't make audio devices that support the Galaxy S. Thanks to this, I'm considering buying a Bluetooth receiver.

**5) If you're a 3GS user, iPhone 5**
3GS users, who once caused a sensation, won't forget the apps they've already bought and the satisfaction the iPhone brought them. The iPhone 5, released as their 2-year contracts are ending, is clearly in a more advantageous position. I think it might perform better than the iPhone 4.

****
**2. Galaxy Note**

**1) S Pen, the grandeur of Wacom**
The S Pen made me exclaim, 'Oh my, I must buy this!' If it were capacitive or resistive, I wouldn't have envied it. As someone who chose the tm2 laptop even if it meant an extra 600g, I believe electromagnetic resonance (EMR) pens are the truth. With the S Pen, it's not just for notes, but high-quality drawings are also possible. Touch devices that use EMR pens don't register touch when the pen is brought close. Therefore, you don't have to awkwardly lift your hand like when writing with a capacitive pen, and the tip is sharp, plus it even has pressure sensitivity. ㅜㅜ The reason Wacom enthusiasts exist, much like QWERTY enthusiasts, is because they can't forget this writing feel.

**2) 5-inch screen capable of full PC browsing**
I also used to think whether 5 inches would be a bane or a boon, but now I feel the hurdle for users has significantly lowered compared to before. One of the reasons I predicted the iPhone's failure in Korea back then was that it was too big and touch was inconvenient. However, many touch phones in Korea helped lower this hurdle for users. And users have also become accustomed to larger screens, like the 5-inch screen of the Galaxy S2 and others. While a large screen reduces portability, users who have experienced the refreshing charm of a large screen are now willing to sacrifice a bit of portability. Moreover, the Galaxy Note has XGA-level resolution, meaning it can handle regular PC browsing without issues. I think this is a very significant advantage.

**3) Galaxy Note with 4G**
As someone who came with high hopes for SKT's 3G network, my disappointment is immense. I even thought, 'I should have gone with KT and added WiBro...' Since actual user reviews for LTE haven't been posted yet, I don't know much, but I can have expectations that it will provide stable service. In fact, when devices like the Note come out, usage is bound to increase, and with increased usage, 4G will quickly become saturated, so I'm skeptical about how well it will be operated, but at least it's 4G... (Strictly speaking, LTE is 3G. True 4G starts with LTE Advanced, which exceeds 100Mbps. The term 4G is being overused as a marketing term, ignoring the 3GPP's definition of 4G standards... Is this also false advertising?)

****
**4) After-Sales Service**
This is clearly a big advantage for Samsung. Felt like I should mention it.

**3. What kind of new customers will switch to iPhone 5 and Galaxy Note?**
It's clear that the Galaxy Note has very attractive specs. However, a significant number of existing Galaxy users are tied to 2-year contracts. This is even more pronounced because the Galaxy S2 sold quite well. Moreover, it's iPhone 3GS users whose contracts are ending. They generally had strong anti-Samsung sentiments, and the Apple lock-in effect via the App Store was already in place for these users. Can they really win these users over to the Galaxy Note?
And for the remaining feature phone users, Samsung will likely have an advantage in terms of brand recognition. And the Galaxy Note's new features will also be fresh. Now, with exactly one cycle (2-year contract) of the smartphone effect having passed, I don't know how the market will change in this second round. I thought a new player like MS would enter in the second round, but they are really late. We'll only know once both the iPhone 5 and Galaxy Note are released, but they are certainly keeping many people waiting.
