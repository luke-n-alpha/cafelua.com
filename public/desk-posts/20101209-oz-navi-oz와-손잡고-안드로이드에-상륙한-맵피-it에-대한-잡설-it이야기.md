**1. 네비게이션 시장의 판도변화

**2. Oz와 손잡고 안드로이드에 상륙한 맵피**
그래서 맵피는 결국 이동통신사중 lg의 u플러스와 손을 잡고 이번앱을 내놓기에 이르렀습니다. 아마도 다른 이동통신사와도 물밑작업이 있었는지는 모르겠지만, 현재 가장 고전하고 있기는 하지만 개인적으로 사용자로서의 가격대 만족감은 가장 높은 u플러스에 맵피가 편승하면서 u플러스의 사용에 대한 이유가 더 생겼습니다.

**1) 안드로이드앱중 가장 큰 크기를 자랑하는 거대한앱**
처음 앱을 다운받으면 지도를 제외하고 다운받아서 그런지 그다지 크지않습니다. 그런데 업데이트를 시작하면 어마어마한양의 크기의 데이터를 다운받기 시작하는데 그 크기가 무려 2기가에 달합니다. 때문에 업데이트는 맵피홈페이지에서 다운받기를 권하고 있습니다. 맵피 홈페이지에 가서 통합업데이트프로그램(스마트업데이터)을 다운받아 실행하면 u플러스의 아이디와 비번을 입력받아 업데이트하는 부분이있습니다.

안드로이드의 가장 강력한 기능중 하나인 usb마운트기능을 이용해 업데이트를 시작합니다. 이 기능은 안드로이드단말의 sd카드를 외장usb로 사용하게 하는 기능으로 지도와 이미지등의 데이터를 메모리에 다운받습니다. 이 방법은 기존의 맵피의 업데이트방식이라 쉽고 빠르게 수행할 수 있습니다. 그런데 빠르다곤 해도 2기가라 만만치 않습니다. 덕분에 전 음악을 지워야만 했죠. 옵티머스큐를 살때 받았던 4기가 메모리의 절반을 할당했으니 어마어마한 크기입니다. 여태껏 설치한 앱중 가장 크다고 할 수 있었습니다. 8기가나 16기가 메모리 구입을 고려해봐야겠습니다. 안드로이드의 또하나의 강점은 메모리 업글이 쉽다는거죠.

**2) 역시 맵피의 훌륭한 UI**
설치후에 실행한 맵피의 기능은 역시 매우 만족스러웠습니다. 기존 맵피의 모든기능을 이용할 수 있습니다. 3d버드뷰, 교차로에서 보여주는 정확한 이미지, 초성검색, 특정 지역에 들어서면 해당 지역의 음성안내, 차선안내와 하이패스안내까지.

**3) 안드로이드에 얼마나 잘 안착했을까?**
맵피를 띄워두고 홈키를 눌러 다른작업을 하다가 다시 맵피로 돌아와도 멀티테스킹은 완벽히 잘 동작했습니다.
작아진 lcd대비인지 글씨는 큼지막 해졌고, 속도와 다음안내의 주요정보가 차지하는 비율이 기존의 7인치 네비보다 훨씬 커졌습니다. 때문에 버튼들도 커졌죠. 이러한 배려는 오랜기간 터치기반의 임베디드 어플을 개발한 맵피의 경험에서 나오는 산물이 아닐까 싶더군요.
근데 옵티머스큐의 쿼티는 지원하지 않았습니다. 그나마 터치 쿼티를 지원합니다만 쿼티처럼 편한 기능을 어째서 지원하지 않는지 ㅜㅜ

**4) 주행중 성능 : gps
**좌석버스에 타서 주행성능을 테스트해봤습니다. 확실히 기존의 속터지는 아이리버의 네비에 비해서 옵티머스큐는 빠른 속도로 현재위치를 잡아냅니다. 더군다나 버스의 안쪽 좌석임을 생각하면 정말 괜찮은거죠.

**5) 주행 성능 : 데이터와 배터리 소모
**출발전과 도착후의 캡쳐사진입니다. 구간은 서현역에서 안양 농수산물 구간입니다.

**1. The Changing Landscape of the Navigation Market**
When talking about navigation, the first makers that come to mind are Inavi and Mappy. They used to compete for the top 1st and 2nd spots in the industry and indeed offered the best usability. However, even amidst this, among mobile carriers, SKT's T-map was renowned for finding the most effective routes through real-time traffic information via its 3G network. I, too, previously used SKT's X1 and tried both Mappy and T-map, but T-map was so good at finding routes that it more than made up for its somewhat clunky usability. In fact, there were countless times I was frustrated listening to Mappy. Especially when I knew a road was congested, I'd trust the DMB traffic information built into my iRiver Mappy only to end up in trouble more than once. In fact, overseas, Google has virtually dominated the navigation service market with its powerful map platform, and the reason why domestic carriers and portals are eyeing this market is precisely because maps are essential for SNS and search. Therefore, the future difficulties for Inavi and Mappy were somewhat predictable.

**2. Mappy Lands on Android, Partnering with Oz**
So, Mappy eventually partnered with LG U+, one of the mobile carriers, to release this app. I don't know if there were any behind-the-scenes negotiations with other carriers, but Mappy's alignment with U+, which is currently struggling the most but personally offers the highest price-to-satisfaction ratio as a user, has given me more reason to use U+.

**1) A Giant App Boasting the Largest Size Among Android Apps**
When you first download the app, it's not that big, perhaps because it downloads without the maps. But when you start updating, it begins downloading an enormous amount of data, reaching a whopping 2 gigabytes. Therefore, it's recommended to download updates from the Mappy homepage. If you go to the Mappy homepage and download and run the integrated update program (Smart Updater), there's a section where you enter your U+ ID and password to update.

Updates begin using the USB mount function, one of Android's most powerful features. This function allows the Android device's SD card to be used as an external USB, downloading map and image data to the memory. This method is Mappy's traditional update method, making it quick and easy to perform. However, even if it's fast, 2GB is no small feat. Thanks to it, I had to delete my music. It allocated half of the 4GB memory I got when I bought the Optimus Q, which is an enormous size. It was arguably the largest app I've ever installed. I might need to consider buying an 8GB or 16GB memory card. Another strength of Android is that memory upgrades are easy.

**2) Mappy's Excellent UI, as Expected**
After installation, Mappy's functions were, as expected, very satisfying. All existing Mappy features are available: 3D bird's-eye view, accurate images shown at intersections, initial consonant search, voice guidance for specific areas, lane guidance, and even Hi-pass guidance.

**3) How Well Has It Settled on Android?**
Even when I minimized Mappy by pressing the home key to do other tasks and then returned to Mappy, multitasking worked perfectly.
Perhaps due to the smaller LCD, the text became larger, and the proportion of key information like speed and next guidance became much larger than on traditional 7-inch navigators. Consequently, the buttons also became larger. This consideration seems to be a product of Mappy's long experience in developing touch-based embedded applications.
However, it didn't support the Optimus Q's QWERTY keyboard. It does support a touch QWERTY, but why it doesn't support a convenient feature like the physical QWERTY is a pity ㅜㅜ

**4) Performance During Driving: GPS**
I tested the driving performance while riding a local bus. The Optimus Q definitely locks onto the current location much faster compared to the frustrating iRiver navigation I used before. Moreover, considering I was in an inner seat of the bus, it's quite impressive.

**5) Driving Performance: Data and Battery Consumption**
These are screenshots before departure and after arrival. The route was from Seohyeon Station to Anyang Agricultural and Marine Products Market.

You can check battery and data consumption. In fact, data consumption is not large as it only receives coordinates. Battery consumption is not an accurate measure because I changed various options while driving, but it seems fine for about 2 hours of use without charging. If you have a cradle and charger in your car, time won't matter.

**6) How Accurate is the Route Finding?**
In terms of route finding performance alone, according to an acquaintance who has used both the old T-map and the current KT map on iPhone, the old T-map was much better at finding routes. So, I also briefly tested OZNAVI.
**{{IMG:8}}**

However, it was difficult to know how accurate the prediction was. It was a short distance, and it started snowing on the way, causing traffic to suddenly slow down. No matter how fast the traffic information, it wouldn't be real-time to that extent. Still, the arrival error wasn't very large. Unlike the previous Mappy, the default search option is traffic mode, not recommended mode.
I'm curious where this traffic information is collected from. This data needs to be accurate to gain the highest trust. One possible clue is the Mappy terms and conditions during installation. There's a part where you agree to the use of your location, and since Android devices are connected via 3G, they can send the current location and speed to Mappy. In other words, Mappy's performance could improve as more users use it. Of course, I don't know if Mappy is currently developed that way, and I can't guarantee it. I'll have to reconfirm Oz navi's accuracy when traffic is really heavy or on long-distance trips.

**7) Detailed Data is the Biggest Strength**
It was quite impressive to see Mappy and Inavi even provide guidance for speed bumps. Also, updates for speed cameras are very fast thanks to user participation.

**4. Goodbye, iRiver NV Life**
I've been using iRiver's NV Life. The built-in Mappy has received continuous free updates, and the recent update was a major overhaul that made it much better, but the essence of a navigator is still route finding.
Hoping that the frustrating Mappy has been reborn, it seems the Optimus Q will now take its place. So, I bought a cradle.
