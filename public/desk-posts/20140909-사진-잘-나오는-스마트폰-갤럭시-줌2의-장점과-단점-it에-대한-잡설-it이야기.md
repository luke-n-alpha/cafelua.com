**1. Exterior - A thicker Galaxy S3 with a 10x optical zoom attached.**
The exterior simply looks like this.{{IMG:2}}To put it simply, this phone's exterior is a Galaxy S3 with a 10x optical zoom. The screen size is the same as the Galaxy S3, and I even suspect the display is the same. However, unfortunately, it is thicker and heavier than the Galaxy S3. When you actually hold it, it feels quite substantial, even a bit heavier than a Note. The Zoom 2 weighs 200g; considering the Note series was 183g, it's almost the heaviest smartphone currently available. Of course, it's not so heavy that you can't carry it. You get used to it after carrying it around.

**2. Performance - Most phones are decent these days. S4-level?**
The hardware performance is superior to the Galaxy S3. You can consider it to be around S4-level. For your reference, I'm also uploading the AnTuTu benchmark results.{{IMG:3}}It's higher than the Nexus 4, but below the Note 3. The Nexus 4 is also quite fast.

**3. The Best Feature, Forget Everything Else, The Camera - It's just a camera that even supports manual mode.**
There's only one reason to buy this device: the camera. Actually, I had been eyeing the Galaxy S4 Zoom before, but the S4 Zoom had a smaller screen than the S3 and performance around S2.5 level, which was simply unforgivable. The Zoom 2 is what happens when you maintain perfect camera performance while transforming the exterior into a smartphone, making it usable. Objectively, it features a 20-megapixel sensor and 10x optical zoom. Some people have an endless pride in the iPhone's camera, wondering if it's still better than an iPhone. I can definitively say it is "overwhelmingly better." What truly determines camera performance is the size of the sensor and lens, and the Galaxy Zoom series has at least that of a typical point-and-shoot camera. Furthermore, the zoom lens shows overwhelming performance when pulling in distant subjects. Its low-light performance, especially at night, is outstanding. Cons****​1) Heavy Weight, Weak Durability - Insurance is a Must**It's not without its drawbacks. As I mentioned earlier, its heavy weight, and the resulting weakness in durability, combined with the Galaxy S3's notoriously fragile "sugar screen," seem to increase the risk of breakage. My wife and I both experienced screen damage within a month of purchase. Since then, we've been handling them very carefully. When buying, it seems essential to get insurance. **2) Flashlight Unavailable - A Choice Due to Powerful Xenon Flash**Another minor drawback is that the flashlight function is unavailable. This is because it's equipped with a camera-style xenon flash, which emits an enormous amount of light instantaneously, making continuous illumination like a conventional LED flash impossible. Personally, I prefer a regular smartphone flash because I use the flashlight more often than the flash when taking photos, but in any case, by making it truly camera-like, it ended up with a drawback that isn't really a drawback. **3) One Battery, External Memory Absolutely Necessary, and an External Battery Wouldn't Hurt...**** **It comes with only one battery. This might not be strange for iPhone users, but at least the battery lasts longer than the Galaxy S3. Of course, if you zoom in a lot with the camera, the battery drains faster. However, it doesn't feel like it's used for a shorter time than a regular camera. I have a portable battery, but I haven't had to use it much. Still, external battery prices are reasonable these days, and having one makes things much more comfortable in many ways. Get a 5000mAh one. Since the internal memory is small and it takes high-quality photos, external memory is essential. When purchasing, buy a 32GB one along with it. **​****4) Carrier - LG's Service is Going Downhill,**The last weakness is LG Uplus, as it's a Uplus-exclusive phone. I used the Galaxy S3 LTE on KT, but somehow the LTE speed felt slower. Moreover, to get any service, you have to travel far and wide to find a direct store. Membership points are useless and less generous than KT's. With KT, it was great to watch movies on IPTV at half price, but the carrier was really a problem. Unfortunately, since it's released as a Uplus-exclusive device, if you want to switch to KT, it's only possible with a 3G SIM. I heard that switching to SKT allows LTE, but I haven't tried it yet. It feels like Uplus, which is increasingly being pushed out of the market, is actually regressing in its service. They should be doing better, not worse. The mandatory 3-month plan is 89,000 won ㅡoㅡ, while KT's was 69,000 won... **5. Conclusion** If you're looking for a smartphone that takes good photos, I highly recommend trying the Galaxy Zoom 2.
