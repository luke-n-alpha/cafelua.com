Our biggest long-cherished project this year, entering the Chinese market, is set to officially commence. This is being realized based on a close relationship with DPVR, a global VR HMD manufacturer. The most crucial factor in the VR market is still the distribution channel. While ComixV boasts overwhelming accessibility compared to other VR content due to its advantage of supporting both web and mobile, it's difficult to claim superiority in accessibility when compared to the competitiveness of mobile webtoons, which are snack culture.

However, ComixV is a startup targeting the upcoming VR/AR market. Therefore, securing the most important customer touchpoints in the current VR market is crucial. In that regard, VR platforms that directly connect with users, such as HMD manufacturers, mobile carriers, and LVEs, are inevitably very important channels.

**ComixV Targets China in Collaboration with Haotoon... To Offer Original Webtoons and Various Webtoon Chinese Services** ComixV, which offers global VR webtoon services, announced that it launched a platform service directly targeting the Chinese local market in collaboration with Haotoon on the 15th. It will be first unveiled to Chinese consumers starting on the 19th at the '2019 World VR Industry Conference' held in Nanchang, China. In addition to over 100 episodes of original VR webtoons directly planned and produced, original works from Korean webtoon companies such as Daum Webtoon, Manhwagajok, and Storysoop will be converted into VR webtoons and presented in Chinese. Just as the early webtoon market was content aimed at attracting users to portal platforms, VR platforms need VR webtoons. This entry into China also began with the requirement to properly support the infrastructure within China. The launch in the Chinese market was a big challenge for ComixV. We implemented a scalable system and synchronization between multiple regions (though it's not yet perfect). Starting with D'Live's requirements, we implemented an API configuration that supports multiple versions for content distribution across DPVR and various channels, and resolved chronic file issues by implementing the API, which was previously built with MS Azure Blob's file system, as a memory cache. Many people, when thinking of a platform, easily assume it's a content distribution platform that's done once developed. However, in reality, backend development issues account for a larger portion than the visible changes. For this, having an effective development structure is also the biggest concern for platforms like ComixV. The platform created through this opportunity also aimed to build a foundation to open up the VR webtoon upload system for artists in VR illustration to users. For this, we completed the development of a distributed processing structure for 360 images two months ago. While there are still many stabilization issues and remaining development tasks, we can say that we have at least laid the basic structure for user expansion.

In terms of VR webtoon content supply and distribution, we established a conversion system overseas, and in addition to direct contracts with artists, which we've been doing, we also signed secondary copyright agreements with agencies including Daum Webtoon. As there were no precedents, contract review took more time than expected. Later, we will need to consider how to create a standard contract by comparing all these agreements. I also believe that the value of the VR webtoon market will be determined by how the contracts are structured. It seems necessary to create a structure that clearly communicates that VR webtoons are a market that expands in scale without competing with the existing webtoon market.

The platform's launch is not the end, but the beginning of the battle. This is because sustainability is the most important thing. It would be a lie to say it's not difficult. However, hearing the stories of those who support us gives me strength and makes me want to push further. And no matter what anyone says, we will advance little by little at our own pace, mobilizing the resources we have. In these times when we hear so many stories about difficulties, I hope ComixV can leave a footprint that is at least somewhat helpful to the VR industry and the comic industry. The exhibition must be in full swing in Nanchang right now... I couldn't go due to work, but my heart is there.
