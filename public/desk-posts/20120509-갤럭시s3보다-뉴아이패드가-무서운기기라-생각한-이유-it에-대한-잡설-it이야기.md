** 2. 애플의 위협이던 저가형 안드로이드 태블릿 견제방법** 애플의 또다른 위협은 저가형 안드로이드 패드입니다. 바로 킨들파이어 같은 놈입니다. 저는 안드로이드의 가능성은 바로 이 시장에 있다고 봤습니다. 아직은 컨텐츠가 패드나 태블릿 모두 부족한 상황에서 컨텐츠가 풍부한 아마존같은 cp사들이 안드로이드를 택하고 하드웨어 성능빌달로 저가형 안드로이드를 빠른속도로 깔 수 있다고 말입니다. 분명 이 전략은 성공적이었습니다. 뉴아이패드가 나오면서 기존 아이패드의 가격을 낮추기전까지는 말입니다.
{{IMG:2}}
출처 : 광파리의 IT이야기 ([http://kwang82.blogspot.com/2012/05/blog-post_07.html](http://kwang82.blogspot.com/2012/05/blog-post_07.html))
물론 저가형 단말이라고 생각하기에는 가격을 낮춘 애플의 단말이 좀 비싼 편이긴 하지만, 그 성능에 주머니를 조금 더 열어볼 생각을 하게 마련입니다.
또한, 애플은 이미 깔린 구버전의 기기를 지원을 끝까지 해주기로 유명합니다. 이때문에 철지난 제품이라 하더라도 믿고 구매할수 있습니다. 그러나 현재까지 보여준 안드로이드 진영의 구단말 지원은 애플에 비하면 매우 나쁜편에 속합니다.  결과적으로 구 아이패드를 낮은가격에 파는것은 소비자로 하여금 떨이제품이란 느낌보다는 저가형 애플기기를 구매한다는 느낌이 들게 만듭니다. 거기다 애플이 저가형으로 라인업을 늘려가고 있는것 또한 사실입니다. 애플이 고가 단말만 만든다는 편견이 부서지는 순간이고, 저가형 안드로이드단말과 경쟁력이 생기게 됩니다.
** 3. 누가 애플좀 말려줘요.** 지금 같은 상황에서는 애플의 독주를 막을 회사가 그다지 없어보입니다. 스마트폰 시장에서 안드로이드 진영의 성장이 눈에 띄기는 하지만, 다른 시장에서의 진출 속도가 생각보나 너무 뒤쳐지고 있습니다. 그러한 가장 큰 원인 중에 하나는 삼성전자와 같은 강력한 엔트리급 단말을 만드는 회사가 없기 때문입니다. 오히려 삼성전자의 경우 자체 플랫폼을 생각하고 있는것 같은데.. 심지어는 바다OS도 아니며 3rd party까지 완벽한 생태계가 이루어지기에는 애시당초 의지조차 보이지 않아보입니다.  해외에서는 그나마 아마존이 애플의 대안이 아닐까 싶습니다만, 이번 아이패드의 회심의 반격으로 어떤 대응을 할지 궁금합니다. 아마존에서도 엔트리급 단말을 내놓을까요 ? 이미 10.1인치 킨들이나, color ink 킨들의 이야기도 있더군요. 컨텐츠 파워를 지닌 아마존의 전략은 아직도 유효하다고 봅니다. 국내에서는 SKT나 교보문고, Yes24, 그리고 네이버를 비롯한 포털이 이러한 전략을 구사할 수 있으리라고 봅니다. 이미 교보문고의 경우 ebook을 만들어 내놓았으나, color ink의 고가 ebook을 내놓아서 그 가능성은 멋지게 보여주었으나, 시장의 중심에서서 싸우기에는 무리인것 같습니다. 다음의 경우 스마트TV로 다음TV+로 멋지게 진출했으나, Boxee나 Roku이상의 무언가를 보여주고 있지는 않습니다. 아직은 갈 길이 멉니다. 플랫폼이라고 하면,iOS나 앱스토어만 떠올립니다. 하지만, 애플서비스 전체를 플랫폼으로 놓고 보았을때, 이를 효과적으로 대치하고 있는 진영은 어디일까요 ? 구글은 하는데까지는 해준 모습입니다. 워낙 전면에 나서기 싫어하는 Geek하고 서비스와는 거리가 먼 친구들이라..  HW와 SW의 통합으로 인해 각 산업군의 경계가 무너지고 있다는 생각이 드네요. 미래는 애플이 지배하게 될까요 ?  애플빼고 오픈플랫폼이라고는 웹밖에 남지 않게 될까요 ? 어떤 회사던 너무 잘 나가면 곧 내려가게 된다는데, 애플도 사이클상 슬슬 그럴때가 된것 같은데.. 언제즈음 내려가게 될까요 ?

Last time, I talked about Samsung failing to grasp the big picture, and Apple doing well from an N-screen perspective. Some might have instantly thought I was an Apple fan or a Samsung hater, but I'm actually closer to being an Apple hater. Of course, I'm not a Samsung fan either. Personally, I don't like either company. They are two companies where power is concentrated, and decisions are based on performance rather than individual creativity. I think global Samsung is Apple, and Apple's Korean version is Samsung. As a humble commoner, I feel that if such companies dominate, life will become tiring and lose its diversity and fun. Anyway, I'd like to talk about the new iPad, which only upgraded its CPU and resolution, compared to the Galaxy S3's various spec improvements.

No matter what anyone says, the tablet and pad market's reliance on Apple is very high. Even though I write posts and do many things with a Flyer, it's also true that the Android tablet market is just starting up and is lagging in terms of app count. And the more unfortunate news is that there's nothing stopping the iPad's entry-level devices, unlike the Galaxy S. The performance difference between the Galaxy Tab 10.1 and the iPad is clear. Furthermore, this new iPad has drastically increased its resolution, exceeding TV's Full HD 1080p, but there's a lack of content to properly enjoy it. But what if content that can fully utilize the iPad's ultra-high resolution emerges? What would happen to the market? As you know, the killer apps for pads are books, video, and educational services. The iPad already has an overwhelming number of educational apps for children. Books are dominated by Amazon, but what about video? The point to note here is that Apple is directly contacting content providers like Warner Bros. for its iTV. Ultimately, this means the emergence of ultra-high-resolution content that can only be enjoyed on the iPad is possible. And they want to stream this to a TV with a larger resolution via AirPlay, but there's no TV to support it. That's precisely when the need for an iTV with an integrated display arises. Thus, in an N-screen environment, the scenario is crucial. This is why I'm concerned that Samsung is currently only focusing on phones and TVs. Moving beyond Full HD specs is not an easy decision for broadcasters or home appliance manufacturers. This is because it would require breaking the specs that were established through agreements across various industries. Users who have experienced ultra-high resolution cannot go back to lower resolutions. Isn't this why Android smartphones gained an advantage over the 3GS, and why the Galaxy Note's screen is getting uncomfortably large?
As an aside, net neutrality issues could hinder Apple; network providers will surely object when ultra-high-resolution content is transmitted over their networks. The problem is that Apple, which even Samsung couldn't control, is now bossing around carriers, and they actually manage their relationships with network operators relatively well.
** 2. How Apple Counters the Threat of Low-Cost Android Tablets** Another threat to Apple is low-cost Android pads. Devices like the Kindle Fire, for instance. I saw Android's potential precisely in this market. In a situation where both pads and tablets still lack content, content providers like Amazon, rich in content, could choose Android and rapidly deploy low-cost Android devices through hardware performance development. This strategy was clearly successful. That is, until the new iPad came out and lowered the price of the existing iPad.
{{IMG:2}}
Source: Kwangpari's IT Story ([http://kwang82.blogspot.com/2012/05/blog-post_07.html](http://kwang82.blogspot.com/2012/05/blog-post_07.html))
Of course, Apple's price-reduced device is still a bit expensive to be considered a low-cost device, but its performance often makes people willing to open their wallets a little wider.
Furthermore, Apple is known for providing long-term support for its older devices already in use. Because of this, even outdated products can be purchased with confidence. However, the Android camp's support for older devices, as shown so far, is quite poor compared to Apple's. As a result, selling the old iPad at a lower price makes consumers feel like they are buying an affordable Apple device rather than a clearance item. Moreover, it's also true that Apple is expanding its lineup with more affordable options. This is the moment when the prejudice that Apple only makes high-end devices is broken, and it gains competitiveness against low-cost Android devices.
** 3. Someone Please Stop Apple.** In the current situation, there doesn't seem to be many companies that can stop Apple's dominance. While the growth of the Android camp in the smartphone market is noticeable, its expansion speed in other markets is much slower than expected. One of the biggest reasons for this is the lack of companies that produce powerful entry-level devices like Samsung Electronics. On the contrary, Samsung Electronics seems to be considering its own platform... but it's not even Bada OS, and there doesn't seem to be any initial will to create a complete ecosystem, including third-party support. Overseas, Amazon might be the only alternative to Apple, but I'm curious to see how they'll respond to the iPad's decisive counterattack. Will Amazon also release an entry-level device? There's already talk of a 10.1-inch Kindle or a color ink Kindle. I believe Amazon's strategy, with its content power, is still valid. Domestically, I believe SKT, Kyobo Bookstore, Yes24, and portals like Naver could employ such a strategy. Kyobo Bookstore has already released an e-book, and while its high-priced color ink e-book impressively demonstrated its potential, it seems difficult for them to compete at the center of the market. Daum, for instance, made a great entry into smart TVs with Daum TV+, but it hasn't shown anything beyond what Boxee or Roku offer. There's still a long way to go. When we talk about platforms, we only think of iOS or the App Store. However, when considering the entire Apple service as a platform, which camp is effectively replacing it? Google has done what it can. They are geeks who dislike being in the forefront and are far from service-oriented... I feel that the boundaries between industries are blurring due to the integration of hardware and software. Will Apple dominate the future? Will only the web remain as an open platform, besides Apple? They say any company that does too well will eventually decline, and it seems like Apple's cycle is slowly reaching that point... When will it start to decline?
