When I go out, I do bring my Sony NX-5, but using a mirrorless camera while carrying my second child in a baby carrier is quite cumbersome and burdensome. Because of this, I was quite interested in models like the Galaxy S4 Zoom in the past, and recently, upon hearing the news that its successor, the Galaxy K Zoom, was released, I did some Googling and am sharing my comparison.

First, comparing the camera performance, it reminds me of the Sony QX-10, which was a hot topic recently. In fact, it overwhelms the QX-10 in terms of megapixels. Most importantly, it's an all-in-one smartphone that isn't as thick as the previous Galaxy S4 Zoom. However, compared to the S5, it's thick. Haha, it's not 'fat' but more like 'chubby' – that's what it would probably argue. {{IMG:2}} Source: [http://www.trustedreviews.com/samsung-galaxy-k-zoom_Mobile-Phone_review](http://www.trustedreviews.com/samsung-galaxy-k-zoom_Mobile-Phone_review) Still, I was going to expect a lot from its camera performance, given that it's a proper lens-type camera... but then... In aspects like the non-application of ISO Cell and the F2.2 aperture, the Galaxy S5 seems to offer better performance for night photography. However, the K Zoom supports ISO up to 3200 and has a slightly larger sensor, which earns it good points. In conclusion, I think I'll need to see actual sample photos to confirm if the Galaxy K Zoom performs better than the Galaxy S5. This even makes me wonder if that's why it's called Galaxy K Zoom instead of Galaxy S5 Zoom. The Galaxy K Zoom seems to be about **Galaxy S4 body + Galaxy S3 display + QX10**. As someone who is satisfied with my Galaxy S3, this level of smartphone performance is actually sufficient for me, so my attention is drawn more to the design and camera performance.

While the QX-10 and Galaxy K Zoom are cameras with zoom, the Nokia Lumia 1020 boasts overwhelming camera performance and elegant design, despite being a camera without zoom. You could call it a smartphone with a prime lens, perhaps? The Lumia 1020 isn't sold domestically, and the fact that it runs Windows instead of Android is a concern, but purely in terms of camera performance, it's a very appealing product.
