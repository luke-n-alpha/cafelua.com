WebXR메타버스를 만드는 빌리버의 루크입니다. 한동안 소식을 안올린 것 같고, 저희 이야기를 안 풀어놓은것 같아, 오래간만에 포스팅 올려봅니다. 서비스를 오픈하고 올릴려고 했는데, 뜻처럼 잘 안되네요.저희 빌리버의 메타버스는 국내 다른 메타버스 기업들과 달리 꽤 다른 곳에서 출발했습니다. VR웹툰을 개발했던 코믹스브이가 이전 사명이었던 만큼 대다수의 개발자들은 웹 개발자였고, 그래서 메타버스의 시작도 웹기반의 글로벌 오픈 소스인 모질라허브를 통해 만들었습니다.​

This is Luke from Belivvr, creating WebXR metaverses. It feels like a while since I've posted an update or shared our story, so I'm putting up a post after a long time. I had planned to post after launching the service, but things haven't gone as smoothly as intended. Unlike other metaverse companies in Korea, Belivvr's metaverse started from a quite different place. As our previous company name was ComicsV, which developed VR webtoons, most of our developers were web developers. Therefore, our metaverse also began with Mozilla Hubs, a web-based global open-source platform.

{{IMG:1}}

**1. The biggest advantage of open-source WebXR metaverse: powerful core feature updates**

{{IMG:2}}

Mozilla Hubs provides an excellent and easy-to-use web editor for the metaverse, **Spoke** ([https://hubs.mozilla.com/spoke](https://hubs.mozilla.com/spoke)). It offers a variety of powerful features such as screen sharing, various file sharing options, and integration with global 3D stores, and is continuously updated.

{{IMG:3}}

The most recent update to Mozilla Hubs editor Spoke, the mirror feature, was contributed by a Belivvr developer.

{{IMG:4}}

The background for developing this component came from an idea during the graduation and entrance ceremonies at Chonnam National University: changing graduation attire. As some of you may know, you can change avatars in VRChat. A mirror component is needed to check this within the space. While Spoke doesn't yet offer an avatar component, avatars can already be swapped within Hubs, so I believe it wouldn't be difficult with a little development. Unfortunately, due to our limited resources and the process of adjusting customer requirements, this scenario was not adopted, and we only developed up to the mirror. As it's open source, anyone can easily extend its functionality, and it has the advantage of continuous updates even if we don't contribute.
**2. Belivvr is preparing XRCloud by extending Mozilla Hubs.**
Furthermore, through cloud infrastructure, it can be easily scaled without physical concerns and can be linked, authenticated, or combined with various legacy web infrastructures. This makes it a strong fit for the web infrastructure used by most companies and institutions. We are also preparing for a g-cloud transition with Naver Cloud. Belivvr is creating a PaaS platform called XRCloud to enable web developers, who constitute the largest portion of the developer ecosystem, to easily build metaverses and metaverse platforms, and its launch is imminent.

{{IMG:5}}

We started from a different place than other metaverse companies that received significant investment with the goal of selling goods and creating an economic ecosystem within the metaverse, which I believe leads to different products.
**3. Frankly, the reason Belivvr isn't pursuing a metaverse platform is a lack of conviction about market viability.**
To be honest, unlike other metaverse companies, I lack conviction in the space and avatar ecosystem of the metaverse. The metaverse will undoubtedly be a very large market, and if there are many users, spaces and avatars will be sold and consumed within it. Cyworld did, and it's a business model proven in many MMORPGs and online games. However, these successful platforms have prerequisites: content and user numbers. Before Cyworld's Miniroom, there was Mini Club. Before icons were sold on blogs, blogs already existed. Fortnite, Roblox, and Zepeto talk about the metaverse, but they are actually games. They are social gaming services where friends consume games together. Ultimately, space and avatars are not the core; rather, consumable killer digital content is the core in such a metaverse ecosystem. People gather to consume digital content in a 3D environment, so they care about their appearance and want to show off their space. From that perspective, the metaverse is not a platform. Although I once published posts and research suggesting that Pokémon Go could evolve into a platform, there was something I didn't know at the time: game development methods and game companies' business models mostly don't align with platforms. While there can be debate about what a platform is, I consider it a business software platform that is continuously and easily extensible. Simply put, Naver and Kakao are examples. Narrowing it down further, it could be a specific vertical domain, like Baedal Minjok or Coupang. The characteristics of these platform companies are that a significant number of their developers work on the web, their backend is enormous, and even when developing apps, they prioritize scalability and accessibility in their architecture, and they release updates in short cycles. My argument is that successful metaverse services that sell spaces and avatars are, to date, not platforms but rather community and social-based content services. Whether such services can evolve into platforms remains to be seen. And when I thought about the market for selling these spaces and avatars, it occurred to me that not only Meta but also Unity and Unreal could be considered close competitors. The easiest market for selling spaces and avatars when developing games is the asset stores of Unity and Unreal. **Unreal and Unity**, which view the metaverse as a future growth engine, effectively hold the keys to cloud services and asset services, positioning themselves with increasingly easier content creation tools and stores, essentially advocating for the metaverse and appearing to aim for platform status.
