이전에 모바일웹을 네이버와 다음을 비교했었는데요.

지금와서 다시 이 두 포탈의 모바일 웹사이트를 보면 많이 변모한 모습을 보여줍니다. 특히 다음의 모바일웹은 크게 발전했으며 오히려 네이버가 많이부족한 모습을 보여줍니다.

**1. 스크롤을 줄이다.**
** **이전의 다음의 가장 큰 불만은 무지막지하게 세로로 큰 페이지였습니다. 네이버 역시 컨텐츠를 전면 배치함에 따라 스크롤이 늘어났고,  반대로 다음은  컨텐츠의 줄수를 조절하여 스크롤을 줄였습니다. 이제 어느정도 적정수준으로 들었다고 보며 다음도 길다는 느낌은 들지 않더군요.

{{IMG:1}}

**과거에는 수배 까지 차이나던 스크롤이 약간 다음이 큰 수준으로 변경 ]**

**2. 사용자의 이용빈도에 따른 배치
**과거에는 뒤에 숨어있던 만화같은 읽을거리를 전면에 배치했으며, 많이 쓰는 서비스 아이콘몇개를 전면에 배치했습니다. 사용자의 이용빈도에 따른 배치가 아닌가 싶네요.

{{IMG:2}}
****
**3. 가독성이 높은 모바일 웹 **
네이버의 경우 웹의 폰트들이 대체적으로 밑밑합니다. 그에 반해 다음은 볼드체와 색상을 적절히 섞어써 좀 더 화려하게 보입니다. 일반적으로 야외에서 lcd를 보는 모바일환경을 본다고 가정할때, 네이버의 가독성은 상대적으로 낮은것 같습니다. 네이버와 다음의 명암 색분포도와 폰트 크기 분포 같은것을 조사해보면 재밌는 결과가 나올것도 같은데 말입니다.

Previously, I compared Naver and Daum's mobile web.

Looking at the mobile websites of these two portals again now, they show a greatly transformed appearance. In particular, Daum's mobile web has significantly evolved, while Naver appears to be lacking in many aspects.

**1. Reduced Scrolling.**
Previously, the biggest complaint about Daum was its excessively long vertical pages. Naver also saw an increase in scrolling due to placing content upfront, whereas Daum reduced scrolling by adjusting the number of content lines. I believe it has now reached an appropriate level, and Daum no longer feels long.

{{IMG:1}}
**[ Comparison of Naver and Daum's mobile page scrolling,**
**Scrolling, which used to differ by several times in the past, has changed to a level where Daum is slightly larger ]**

**2. Layout based on user frequency of use**
In the past, readable content like comics, which used to be hidden, is now placed upfront, and several frequently used service icons are also prominently displayed. I suspect this layout is based on user frequency of use.

{{IMG:2}}
****
**3. Highly Readable Mobile Web**
In Naver's case, the web fonts are generally plain. In contrast, Daum uses a suitable mix of bold text and colors, making it appear more vibrant. Assuming a mobile environment where one views an LCD screen outdoors, Naver's readability seems relatively low. It would be interesting to investigate things like the contrast color distribution and font size distribution of Naver and Daum; I imagine some interesting results would emerge.

**4. Good Visibility on Android as well**
Previously, there were clear signs that Android browsers weren't given much consideration, but these days, they look very good. In the case of comics, despite showing larger images compared to Naver, there's less resolution blurring. Android browsers forcibly reduce the resolution of images exceeding a certain size.

{{IMG:3}} {{IMG:4}}

Comparison of image files on Naver and Daum comic mobile pages
Since only one was randomly selected, the results below cannot be considered absolute.

| Comic Investigated | Image Size (px) | File Size | Pixels per 1KB | col5 |

| Daum | Deadman Wakeup | 700*1924 | 111kb | 12133px |
| Naver | Gyeonwoo and Jiknyeo | 600*1702 | 116kb | 8803px |

Looking at the results above, there's about a 30% difference in compression ratio. Since JPG compression usually varies depending on the complexity of the target image, it's too early to conclude if this is due to the above findings. As this is not my main profession, I regret not being able to compare and analyze a wider variety of images. If you have time, please try comparing them in more diverse ways. Or perhaps my phone is uniquely messing up only Naver.

**5. Enhanced Focus on the Search Bar**
When entering a query in the search bar, the design achieves a higher level of focus than Naver's orange border appearing on focus. On general browsing pages, not the main page, content is darkened, and only the sections and search bar are visible, greatly increasing focus. It felt like a very good feature when using it.

{{IMG:5}}
**[Daum's mobile web as seen during a general search; the dark area is actually due to an alpha effect.**
**I used OTP, so the capture didn't work well. This is not reproducible on PC.]**
****
**6. Conclusion**
I believe that mobile pages should focus less on delivering a lot of content and more on ensuring users perceive that they are accurately performing their intended actions. This is because actions are often taken in shaky environments or places with poor lighting. I think this is what Daum is doing better than Naver.
These features and designs likely emerged because extensive user analysis preceded them. I wish Naver good luck as well.
