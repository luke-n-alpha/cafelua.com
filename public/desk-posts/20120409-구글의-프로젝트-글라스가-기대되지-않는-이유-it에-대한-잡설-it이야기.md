구글의 프로젝트 글래스 영상이 올라옴에 따라 사람들의 반응이 참 뜨겁습니다.

AR기술을 이용한 구글의 프로젝트 글래스는 그다지 새로운 기술은 아닙니다. 음성인식과 AR은 꽤 역사가 된 기술이기 때문이죠. 터미네이터 안경이라고 불리는 이 안경은 구글에 실존한다고 까지 합니다. 가격이 400~600달러 선이라는 이야기도 있고.. 하지만, 개인적으로는 그렇게 기대되지는 않습니다. 그 이유를 말씀드려볼까 합니다.
{{IMG:1}}

** 2. 구글의 제품은 플랫폼이지 완성품이 아니었다.** 기술적 만족도를 둘째 치고, 그동안 구글의 제품들은 플랫폼이었지, 완성품이 아니었습니다. 이번 프로젝트 글래스의 영상은 이러한 구글과는 상당히 동떨어져 보입니다. 구글이 처음 공개한 안드로이드 컵케이크는 어떤 모습이었을까요 ? 이를 완성도 있게 바꾼것은 삼성, HTC, 소니입니다. 역시 증명이라도 하듯이 구글TV의 UX는 극악이었죠. 프로젝트 글래스를 애플이 발표했다면, 시연 동영상과 크게 차이나지 않았을겁니다. 애플은 언제나 엔드유저 입장의 기기를 만드는 회사니까요. 구글의 프로젝트 글래스 역시 마찬가지 일겁니다. 구글이라는 이름만으로도 굉장히 geek한 기기가 나올지 모른다는 느낌이 드네요.

**[ 구글 글래스의 패러디 MS 글래스, 하지만 구글 글래스도 실제는 이럴지도 모릅니다. ]**

** 3. 그러나 모바일플랫폼의 미래를 가이드한 구글** 하지만, 진짜 의미는 바로 여기에 있습니다. 구글이 만든 프로젝트 글래스는 모바일의 미래를 가이드하고 있습니다. 그리고, 아마도 어쩌면 이에 맞는 플랫폼을 구상중이라는 의미일수도 있습니다. 안드로이드를 확장할 건지, 아니면 또 별도의 OS나 플랫폼을 개발하는 알 수 없죠. 달이나 아마존의 지도까지 그리는 구글이기에 이러한 시도를 해볼 수 있는지도 모르겠습니다. 그리고 돈 안되보이는 이러한 기술들이지만, 언젠가는 구글의 이러한 시도는 알게 모르게 세상을 바꾸어놓을지도 모릅니다. 우주선을 쏘는 프로젝트라면, 애플보다는 구글이 어울리는것은 현재로서는 분명합니다. 사용자가 만족도가 낮은 부족한 기술들에 돈을 부어 이러한 미래를 꿈꿔볼수 있는 구글이 무척 부럽다는 생각이 드네요. 돈안되면 안하는 기업들이 대다수인데 말이죠.

As Google's Project Glass video was uploaded, people's reactions are incredibly enthusiastic.

Google's Project Glass, which uses AR technology, is not a particularly new technology. This is because voice recognition and AR are technologies with a considerable history. This eyewear, dubbed 'Terminator glasses,' is even said to exist at Google. There are also rumors of its price being around $400-$600... However, personally, I'm not that excited about it. I'd like to explain why.
{{IMG:1}}

**1. Related Technologies with Low User Satisfaction**
The satisfaction level with voice recognition technology is actually not very high. Many people were impressed by SIRI's cool promotional videos, but the satisfaction with SIRI on iPhones is not that high. Google's Android voice input was also the same. This indicates that the technical satisfaction with voice recognition technology is still not very high. In fact, Nuance, SIRI's parent company, is practically the progenitor of voice recognition technology. Google also pours a lot of money into such pattern recognition technologies, and these technologies are even used in places like NASA to observe stars. However, the biggest limitation of these pattern recognition technologies is their low satisfaction in real-world applications. Voice search from Google or Naver is useful for short, simple queries in quiet places, but its satisfaction is terrible on subways or outdoors. This is because human ears selectively discern the sounds they want to hear, whereas machines cannot. The same applies to the video used in AR. Cameras are fundamentally single-focus. Humans, on the other hand, can freely shift focus in real-time, quickly identifying what they want to see. This process involves dozens of interactions between the brain and eyes within seconds. However, machines cannot do that. If you look at a flowerpot placed in front of a window, it's difficult for the machine to even determine whether the user looked outside the window or at the flowerpot in front of it. If I just wanted to see a pretty flowerpot, but weather information popped up, it would be annoying. Push notifications are annoying enough, but pop-ups floating in front of your eyes are pollution. The battery issue, which everyone talks about, also has technical limitations.
**2. Google's Products Were Platforms, Not Finished Products.**
Putting technical satisfaction aside, Google's products have always been platforms, not finished products. The video for this Project Glass seems quite detached from this typical Google approach. What did Android Cupcake look like when Google first unveiled it? It was Samsung, HTC, and Sony that refined it into a complete product. As if to prove the point, Google TV's UX was notoriously bad. If Apple had announced Project Glass, it wouldn't have differed much from the demo video. Apple always creates devices from an end-user perspective. Google's Project Glass will likely be the same. Just the name Google gives the impression that a very geeky device might emerge.

**[ Google Glass parody MS Glass, but Google Glass might actually be like this. ]**

**3. However, Google Guided the Future of Mobile Platforms**
However, the true meaning lies right here. Google's Project Glass is guiding the future of mobile. And perhaps, it might mean that they are conceptualizing a platform suited for it. Whether they will expand Android or develop a separate OS or platform is unknown. Perhaps it's because Google maps the moon and even the Amazon that they can attempt such endeavors. And while these technologies may not seem profitable, Google's attempts might, someday, change the world in subtle ways. If it's a project to launch a spaceship, Google is clearly a better fit than Apple for now. I feel very envious of Google, which can pour money into imperfect technologies with low user satisfaction to dream of such a future. Most companies wouldn't do it if it didn't make money.
