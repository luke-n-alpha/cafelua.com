** 2. 모두들 기대하지 않았던 안드로원의 업그레이드**
저는 안드로원의 업글에 관해서는 기대하고 있지 않았습니다. 판매대수도 다른 안드로이드기종에 비해 많지 않았고, 무엇보다도 가장 먼저 나왔고 옵티머스q와 z를 출시한만큼 안드로원에 대한 리소스확보는 쉽지 않을거라는 추측때문이었습니다. 그러나 제 예상이 보기좋게 빗나가고 lg는 안드로이드원의 추가 업그레이드를 공식적으로 약속했습니다. 비록 hw가 2.2에 맞지 않음에도 불구하고도 2.2업글을 진행해주겠다는 의미는 2.2로 마켓이 대부분 옮겨갈것이라는것과 부족한 성능은 프로요를 통해 대폭 개선할 수 있으리라는 예측 때문일것 같습니다.
얼마전에 한 기사에는 최신버젼인 프로요가 오히려 저가형 안드로이드폰에 구원자가 될거라는 이야기가 있었는데 현실이 되는듯합니다.

** 2. The Andro-One Upgrade Nobody Expected**
I wasn't expecting an upgrade for the Andro-One. Its sales volume wasn't high compared to other Android models, and more importantly, it was released first, and with the launch of Optimus Q and Z, I speculated that securing resources for Andro-One would be difficult. However, my prediction was pleasantly wrong, and LG officially promised an additional upgrade for the Andro-One. Even though the hardware isn't perfectly suited for 2.2, the decision to proceed with the 2.2 upgrade likely stems from the expectation that most of the market will shift to 2.2, and that the lacking performance can be significantly improved through Froyo.
Recently, an article suggested that Froyo, the latest version, could actually be a savior for low-end Android phones, and it seems that's becoming a reality.
 {{IMG:1}}
**** 
** 3. Korean Companies' Answer to Users Distrusting Android Upgrades**
Furthermore, the significance of this announcement could be an answer to the distrust users and Apple supporters have continuously raised regarding device manufacturers' lack of upgrade support. The fact that the first Android phone released in Korea will support 2.2 sounds almost like news that all Android phones will support the latest version. Clearly, other device manufacturers cannot help but be stimulated by LG's decision. Especially encouraging is the promise of support despite the hardware specifications not being perfectly aligned.

**** 
** 4. Listening to Customer Voices is a Survival Strategy in the 2.0 Era**
I've always thought this, but I believe there are few foolish companies that would maintain pointed-out flaws and weaknesses. Low-resolution iPhones upgraded to high resolution, and developers who lacked faith in version upgrades now provide them. It's normal corporate behavior to fix issues that have been brought to light. Otherwise, it will clearly lead to customer churn. At least, the iPhone 4's antenna issue regarding lack of version upgrade support for Android, which hasn't widely adopted 2.2, is a hardware structural problem, but software issues are simply a matter of allocating resources, so they aren't actually a big problem.
What's more problematic is the behavior of excluding communication and pushing only with general advertising strategies, or telling consumers, "It's your fault for writing such complaints," or "Didn't you read the terms and conditions?" Such "sell it and wash your hands of it" attitudes will be a long-term negative for companies.

**** 
** 5. LG in Crisis, Hoping for a Counterattack.**
In fact, despite releasing decent devices like the Optimus Q among Android devices including the Galaxy S, LG Electronics doesn't seem to have seen much success, as LGT has been marginalized in the smartphone battle between SKT and KT. Of course, this is more of a U+ issue than an LG Electronics issue, but since LG Electronics and U+ are not number one in Korea, it's true that more effort is needed. And isn't Android and smartphones a new market, a good opportunity to actually change the game? Personally, I hope the smartphone landscape becomes a market with the fun of choosing, like Baskin Robbins 31, rather than a Samsung vs. Apple dichotomy, and for that reason, I support LG. (Also because it's the best fit for me in terms of price-to-performance ratio. ^^)
 
** 6. You'll do well with Optimus Q too, right? Please maintain quality.**
Some users are asking for quick support for the Opti-Q, and I'm also eagerly awaiting Eclair, which is said to be coming in August.
However, software development isn't something that happens just because the boss says, "Develop it by next week." The "ppalli-ppalli" (hurry-hurry) culture has also contributed to the current state of the Korean software industry.
As someone who is very satisfied with and uses the Optimus Q, I will look forward to and await the version upgrade.
