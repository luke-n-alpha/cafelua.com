There was talk that Google I/O was unremarkable, but I recently heard amazing news from Google. The fact that Google's photo storage service has become free. In fact, I recently compared cloud storage capacities, and at the time, I chose Microsoft's OneDrive, which offers 1 terabyte of space along with Office 365. Besides that, Amazon was already offering near-free cloud space, but I didn't choose it because Amazon boasted terrible speeds in Korea.

**1. Why I chose Google Photos instead of OneDrive: Free and Fast!**
However, OneDrive has been uploading 450GB for two weeks now. The speed is truly terrible. But recently, Google introduced free photo storage. I tested it, and it boasts a much more comfortable speed than OneDrive. Of course, there's one limitation: it's restricted to 16-megapixel photos and Full HD videos. First, for Full HD, most smartphones and cameras have a maximum resolution around that level, so it won't be a big problem. However, a 16-megapixel camera is a resolution that applies to the latest smartphones, but it's a bit lacking for DSLR users or Galaxy Zoom 2 users like me. Still, if you think about it, once you reach around 16 megapixels, other factors become more important than pixels, so I think it's pretty decent.

**2. Smartphone Settings: Photos taken go straight to the cloud.**
You can download the app below.

https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.photos
                                [**Photos - Android Apps on Google Play** play.google.com](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.photos)

If your family wants to use the same photo space, you can create a separate Google ID. On my wife's smartphone, only the Google Photos app is logged in with my ID. However, you need to be careful here: if two accounts are set up on an Android smartphone, a disaster can occur where all schedules and phone numbers download and get mixed up, so please turn off all synchronization when initially setting up the secondary account. Synchronization is set by default to only occur when using Wi-Fi, and if you set it to high quality, it seems to automatically resize and upload based on 16 megapixels.

3) Automatic Photo Classification

{{IMG:3}}

It automatically classifies photos. Deep learning technology for photo classification seems to already be integrated (which is why some misidentifications are visible). While previous services that offered cloud and free photo storage merely emphasized cloud space, Google seems to have created the ultimate solution in the photo field, offering seamless integration between mobile and PC, easy photo management, and even automation technology for photo sharing. Actually, the Google Stories feature is said to have been around for quite some time, but as word-of-mouth about the free photo space spreads, this excellent technology seems to be gaining attention once again.

**4. Crisis for Domestic Photo Services**
N Drive began charging for storage, while Daum Cloud shut down its cloud business. The situation is evolving into a battle that demands powerful infrastructure and high technological expertise. Personal photo services are very important. Images contain more information than text, and by interpreting them, much more can be done. I believe Google's attack on the Korean home market has begun. First, the most nervous will likely be direct competitors like N Drive and indirect competitors like KakaoStory. After shutting down Daum Cloud, how will Kakao defend KakaoStory? Kakao Album seems to be already in a somewhat bad state, so it would be difficult to compete with that. I have a slight feeling that it's highly likely to go towards an integrated service of KakaoStory and Kakao Album, and in Naver's case, while they are aggressively starting with Pola, it won't be easy given the sluggish social graph. So, will they adopt an unlimited photo policy like Google? I think we'll have to wait and see. Anyway... I think this is a moment when Google, having laid the groundwork with Android, is trying to reap the harvest. I plan to write an additional post on this separately.
