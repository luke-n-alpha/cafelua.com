삼성의 갤럭시 S3이후, 메이저 모델 중 제 관심을 끌었던 적이 없습니다.S4나 S5나 차이를 별반 느끼지 못했습니다. 오히려 줌2같은 변태적인 모델이 제 관심을 끌었습니다. 그런데, 이번 S6 edge는 오래간만에 제 관심을 끌었습니다. 일단 다른건 다 모르겠고,edge라는 이름이 드러냈듯이 좌우면에 커브드 글래스를 통한 디자인이 확 눈에 들어왔습니다. 과연 갤럭시 S6가 최근 어닝쇼크까지 맞은 삼성의 구원투수가 될 수 있을까요? 그 이야기를 해보려고 합니다.{{IMG:1}}
**1. 먼저 프리미엄 스마트폰으로 디자인은 합격점** 디자인은 합격점입니다. 고만고만한 스마트폰 디자인 시장에 커브드글래스로 포인트를 준 것은 매우고무적인 일입니다. 아마도 갤럭시S6 를 구매하는 사용자는 프리미엄 모델의 유니크함을 즐기는 사용자일 가능성이 높아보입니다.
**3. 일체형 배터리, 마이크로SD 카드 미체택, 이제는 단점도 아님** 그러면 혹시 특별한 단점이라도 있을까요? 일체형 배터리와 마이크로  SD카드 미체택은 그동안 갤럭시와 아이폰의 차이처럼 여기시는 분들 도 있을지 모르겠지만, 이제는 중요한 사항은 아니라고 봅니다. 그동안 안드로이드의 충분한 전력 사용 개선, 그리고 배터리 자체의 성능 개선으로 이미 2년간 폰바꿀때까지는 넉넉히 쓰는듯합니다. 저도 갤럭시 줌2를 쓰고 있는데, 배터리 한개인데 불편함을 못 느낍니다. SD카드 미체택은 메모리 가격하락으로 인해 이미 64gb 이상의 메모리 체택을 하더라도 제조사로 하여금 큰 부담을 느끼지 않을 뿐더러 윈도우즈도 아닌데 128 gb이상의 메모리를 요구하지도 않습니다. 초기 아이폰에서 용량크기가 중요했던 이유는 네트워크 인프라가 충분히 갖추어지지 않은 시점에 PC와 연결해서 음악이나 동영상을 충분히 다운 받기 위해서였는데, 광대역 LTE 와 기가와이파이 까지 논하는 상황에서 기계의 스토리지 확장성은 그다지 장점이 되지 못합니다. 그러니 그것 뺐다고 단점이 되지도 않죠.
**4. 핵심인 엣지, 커브드 글래스 얘기해볼까?** 먼저 이 엣지가 가지는 디자인적 성공과 유니크함의 획득과 함께, 반대 급부의 위험도 가지고 있습니다. 엣지의 디자인의 가장 큰 영향은 하드웨어와 소프트웨어의 서드파티에 대한 영향입니다.
** 1) 하드웨어 서드파티의 어려움 - 케이스는 어찌 만들라고?**먼저 손쉽게 하드웨어 부분에서는 케이스 업체로서는 엣지의 디자인이 참 골치아픈 디자인일겁니다. 엣지의 강점은 모서리인데, 대체적으로 이부분은 범퍼로 사용되던 영역입니다. 이를 가리고 케이스를 만들수는 없겠죠. 물론, 창의적인 발상으로 인해 어떻게든 나올것으로 예상되지만, 케이스 만드는 업체 입장 상아직은 신중한 단계일것 같습니다. 기본적으로 엣지의 느낌은 나 예쁘니까 더이상 케이스로 씌우지 마. 하는 느낌입니다. 스티브잡스도 왜 예쁜 아이폰을 케이스에 넣는지 불만이었다는 소문도 있었지만, 아이러니하게 최대 케이스시장은 아이폰이 가지고 있었죠. 이는 디자인에 대한 욕구가 많은 사용층은 예쁜 폰과 함께 다양한 서드파티 하드웨어 시장을 원한다는 얘기 같습니다.
**6. 삼성에 다른 게임을 주문한다.** 과거 S전자 출신의 한 지인이 이런 이야기를 하시더군요. 삼성전자도 이 위기를 알고 있을 것이다. 삼성전자도 룰체인지를 준비하고 있을것이다. 그리고 그 방법은 어쩌면 아예 스마트폰이 아닌 다른게임을 이미 선택했는지도 모른다. 사실 삼성의 차세대 동력 언급에서 스마트폰이 빠진지는 꽤 오래되었다. 그러면서 예를 든 것이 아마 의료를 얘기하셨습니다. 삼성SDS의 의료SW구축 실력과 삼성전자의 IoT, 삼성의료원의 의료서비스, 이 삼각편대가 시너지를 발휘하지 않을까 하는 이야기를 잠시 나누었죠. 생각해보니 제 뒷통수를 때리더군요. 저도 그 동안 너무 삼성에게 편협한 요구만 하지 않았는지. 삼성은 전자만 있는 것도 아니고, 잘할 수 있는 분야들이 충분히 있는데 말입니다. 요즘 IoT나 SW는 오프라인 접점을 기반으로 이루어지고 있습니다. 이런 상황에서 삼성이 가지고 있는 전자는 어쩌면 삼성이 가지고 있는 아주 일부의 능력일 뿐인지도 모르겠다는 생각도 들었습니다. 한가지는 분명합니다. 삼성은 더 이상 스마트폰의 삼성전자로는 답이 어려움은 이번 S6로 더 확인사살한 느낌입니다. 물론, S6 edge는 한동안 삼성전자의 몰락을 완화 혹은 잠시 반등 시켜줄 수 있을지 모르겠습니다. 하지만 s6 edge에서 본 삼성전자의 행보는 여전히 중국경쟁 업체들 대비 이기기 힘든 싸움을 하고 있다는 생각이 듭니다. 그리고 만약 삼성전자가 이 중국 경쟁 업체들의 위협에도 불구하고 살아남는 방법은 중국 경쟁 업체와 아예 다른 영역에서 다른 게임을 하는것일겁니다. 물론, 그게 빵집은 아닐거라는건 삼성에서도 잘 아실거라고 믿습니다.

Since Samsung's Galaxy S3, no major model has captured my interest. I didn't feel much difference between the S4 and S5. Instead, quirky models like the Zoom 2 caught my eye. However, this S6 edge has captured my interest after a long time. First of all, I don't know about anything else, but as the name 'edge' suggests, the design with curved glass on both sides immediately caught my eye. Can the Galaxy S6 truly be the savior for Samsung, which recently faced an earnings shock? I'd like to discuss that.
{{IMG:1}}
**1. First, as a premium smartphone, the design gets a passing grade**
The design gets a passing grade. Giving a point with curved glass in the mediocre smartphone design market is a very encouraging development. It seems likely that users who purchase the Galaxy S6 are those who enjoy the uniqueness of a premium model.

**2. Performance... gets a passing grade, but now it's irrelevant**
Improved, faster, higher resolution... It's good. It's fine. However, with FHD and 3GB of memory secured, I wonder if such competition still holds meaning. Of course, a good camera also gets a passing grade. It's fine. But the problem is that Samsung's Galaxy S4 and S5 were never bad. They were always good. It's certain that this alone won't solve Samsung's problems.

**3. Non-removable battery, no microSD card slot – no longer disadvantages**
So, are there any particular disadvantages? While some might have considered the non-removable battery and lack of a microSD card slot as a key difference between Galaxy and iPhone, I don't think they are important issues anymore. With sufficient power usage improvements in Android and performance enhancements in batteries themselves, it seems like they last comfortably for two years until you replace your phone. I also use a Galaxy Zoom 2, and with just one battery, I don't feel any inconvenience. The lack of an SD card slot is no longer a big burden for manufacturers, even if they adopt 64GB or more memory, due to falling memory prices, and it's not Windows, so it doesn't demand 128GB or more memory. The reason storage capacity was important in early iPhones was to download enough music or videos by connecting to a PC when network infrastructure wasn't fully established. In a situation where broadband LTE and gigabit Wi-Fi are being discussed, the storage expandability of a device is no longer much of an advantage. So, removing it isn't a disadvantage.

**4. Let's talk about the core feature: the Edge, curved glass?**
First, along with the design success and unique appeal that the Edge brings, it also carries risks as a trade-off. The biggest impact of the Edge's design is on third-party hardware and software.

**1) Hardware Third-Party Difficulties - How to make cases?**
First, on the hardware side, for case manufacturers, the Edge's design will be quite a headache. The strength of the Edge is its curved sides, which are generally areas used for bumpers. You can't cover them up and make a case, can you? Of course, it's expected that something will emerge through creative ideas, but from the perspective of case manufacturers, it seems like they are still in a cautious phase. Basically, the Edge gives off a vibe of 'I'm beautiful, so don't cover me with a case anymore.' There were rumors that Steve Jobs was also dissatisfied with why beautiful iPhones were put in cases, but ironically, the largest case market belonged to the iPhone. This seems to suggest that users with a strong desire for design want a beautiful phone along with a diverse third-party hardware market.

**2) Software Third-Party Difficulties - Agh! The buttons are folded!**
Secondly, for software companies, the Edge is also a very perplexing design. I was going to give quite different scores depending on whether this Edge design aimed for a zero-bezel or intended to create a new UX area, but the unveiled Galaxy S6 Edge appears to target a zero-bezel. However, from the perspective of software developers, it's not a zero-bezel but a minus-bezel. Generally, the edges are areas where control buttons are precisely located, a battleground of 1 pixel. You need to display as much content as possible in the center while securing enough button area to prevent accidental presses. In the case of games, they are played in landscape mode, and that area is precisely the control area. It seems like it will be a concern for software third-parties whether they need to create a separate design for the Galaxy S6. Fortunately, the Galaxy S6 is sufficiently large and has a high resolution, so software companies have room to maneuver through customization. However, many apps that are not customized risk becoming a bad experience from the S6's perspective, lowering the usability of the S6.

**5. Is Samsung's return to hardware okay?**
I believe the S6's purpose and message are much clearer than emphasizing software that couldn't achieve its goals in the S4 or S5. A camera that takes good pictures at night, beautiful selfies. Very clear, right? Therefore, it can get good marks for its keynote or presentation. However, I believe this will be difficult to become Samsung's savior. If Samsung's enemy had been the iPhone, it might have become a savior. Of course, even so, it's difficult to expect a popularity surge comparable to the iPhone 6. As I wrote before, the iPhone 6's surge in popularity was an expression of latent desire for the iPhone, not because the iPhone 6 was better than the Galaxy, so it's not a fair comparison. Samsung's enemy is now Chinese companies. Recently, I imported a Chinese smartpad directly from overseas. It features a Bay Trail processor, multi-OS with Android and Windows 8.1, 1 year free Office 365, 64GB storage, an 8.9-inch QHD-level display, weighs 530g, and has a design and thinness similar to an iPad, all for 300,000 won including customs fees and domestic quality inspection. No other modifiers are needed. Everything is settled at the price of 300,000 won. The monster Samsung has to fight now is exactly this kind of device. It's a situation where a 900,000 won S6 Edge has to fight against a 180,000 won Chinese S5. At least the iPhone has iTunes. In this situation, even if Samsung increases sales with the S6, its profits are likely to continue to worsen. Instead, perhaps targeting VR, like with the VR Gear, for the S6 announcement might allow for a new game. However, that strategy is still very underdeveloped.

**6. Ordering a different game for Samsung.**
An acquaintance who used to work at S Electronics once told me this: Samsung Electronics must also be aware of this crisis. Samsung Electronics must be preparing for a rule change. And perhaps they have already chosen a different game, one that isn't even smartphones. In fact, it's been quite a while since smartphones were omitted from Samsung's discussions of next-generation growth engines. And the example they gave was probably healthcare. We briefly discussed whether the synergy of Samsung SDS's medical software development capabilities, Samsung Electronics' IoT, and Samsung Medical Center's medical services could create a powerful trio. Thinking about it, it hit me like a ton of bricks. Had I also been making too narrow demands of Samsung all this time? Samsung isn't just about electronics, and there are plenty of areas where they can excel. Nowadays, IoT and software are built upon offline touchpoints. In this situation, I also thought that the electronics Samsung possesses might just be a very small part of Samsung's overall capabilities. One thing is clear. It feels like the S6 has further confirmed that Samsung can no longer find answers as 'Samsung Electronics, the smartphone company.' Of course, the S6 edge might alleviate or temporarily rebound Samsung Electronics' decline for a while. However, Samsung Electronics' trajectory as seen in the S6 edge still suggests they are fighting a difficult battle against Chinese competitors. And if Samsung Electronics is to survive despite the threat from these Chinese competitors, it will be by playing a different game in an entirely different arena than its Chinese rivals. Of course, I believe Samsung itself knows that won't be a bakery.
