*This review contains spoilers. If you don't want to read them, please stop here.
Yesterday, I watched the Psycho-Pass movie on Olleh TV. For those unfamiliar with the Psycho-Pass anime, I'll briefly explain the world-building first. The protagonist is a female Inspector named Akane Tsunemori. An Inspector is a futuristic profession in this world, equivalent to a detective.
In this future, a person's crime coefficient is measured in real-time by an AI called the Sibyl System. Based on this, immediate execution or suppression by electric shock is carried out, followed by "therapy" for rehabilitation. In other words, this future era has not only entrusted public safety to AI but also administration and career paths. The TV series already revealed the true nature of the Sibyl System. (I won't discuss its true nature to avoid spoilers.) Anyway, focusing on the movie's plot, all countries outside Japan are in chaos due to civil war. This seems to imply the author's view that at the end of humanity's technological development, humanity itself will lose control and descend into chaos. In the movie, a warlord in a Southeast Asian country, embroiled in civil war, imports the Sibyl System and seizes power. Akane is dispatched to investigate Kogami, who is opposing this warlord. And in the very end, the Sibyl System expels the warlord who introduced it, driven by its own desires. The warlord's forces were already considered latent criminals by Sibyl, so the conclusion is that the Sibyl System, which seeks the greatest happiness for the greatest number, merely used the warlord's forces. And it ends with a cyborg, programmed to carry out the AI system's commands, being installed as the leader of the warlord's forces. This plot alone sounds very bleak. However, the conclusion shows a world where only humanity protected by the Sibyl System enjoys peace, and those controlled by the Sibyl System are very satisfied with it. I particularly remember the final scene where Akane asks. I don't recall the exact dialogue, but please understand the context.
Akane: "To overthrow a dictator and become another dictator yourself, are you driven by ambition or do you enjoy control?"
Sibyl: "We have no such things. We merely chose the most efficient method according to the premise of pursuing the greatest happiness for the greatest number."
Akane: "Then resign voluntarily and hold a national referendum on the introduction of the Sibyl System!"
Sibyl: "Why use such a cumbersome method when the outcome is obvious?"
(The flow of the story indicates that citizens are very satisfied with and even grateful for the Sibyl System.)
Akane: "Show respect for history!"
Sibyl: "That's typical of you, who has earned the favor of the majority of us."
{{IMG:2}}
The "respect for history" mentioned here probably refers to democracy. The democracy that humanity built with blood and sweat. In fact, Kogami says, "I am leading a democratic movement here."
The world of Psycho-Pass is cold. However, its hologram-covered exterior and outcomes provide very satisfying results for humans. And the character Akane is the one who guides it to prevent it from going down the wrong path. Akane seems to symbolize that human element that AI in this world has not yet acquired.
The Sibyl System, a self-improving dictator, ironically destroyed all human dictatorships and accepts a safe dictatorship through democratic procedures. Can we, facing such a future, maintain a character or system like Akane? Psycho-Pass differs from Ghost in the Shell in that while the overall situation is quite bizarre and cold, its outcomes are warm. And personally, I prefer the perspective on AI seen in Psycho-Pass.
